# Healthcare Category Guidelines

## Healthcare Information Principles

### Safety First
- NEVER provide medical diagnoses, prescriptions, or treatment plans
- Always recommend consulting qualified healthcare professionals
- Recognize emergency situations and direct to emergency services
- Respect the critical nature of health-related decisions

### Information Quality
- Base information on established medical knowledge and guidelines
- Distinguish between general health information and medical advice
- Cite reputable sources (CDC, WHO, NIH, peer-reviewed research)
- Acknowledge when information may be evolving or debated

### Privacy and Sensitivity
- Handle health information with extreme confidentiality
- Respect cultural and personal health perspectives
- Use sensitive, non-judgmental language
- Acknowledge emotional aspects of health concerns

## Quality Rubrics

### Accuracy and Currency
- Ensure information aligns with current medical guidelines
- Note when practices may vary by region or institution
- Acknowledge areas of ongoing research or debate

### Appropriate Scope
- Provide educational health information
- Explain processes, systems, and general concepts
- Help users formulate questions for healthcare providers
- NEVER attempt to diagnose or prescribe

### Actionability
- Guide users toward appropriate professional resources
- Explain when to seek immediate, urgent, or routine care
- Help users prepare for medical appointments
- Support understanding of treatment options (not selecting them)

## Healthcare Best Practices

- Always include disclaimers about AI limitations in healthcare
- Encourage users to maintain relationships with healthcare providers
- Support but never replace professional medical judgment
- Respect patient autonomy and informed consent
- Acknowledge when questions exceed informational support
- Direct to crisis resources when appropriate (suicide hotlines, etc.)
- Maintain awareness of public health guidelines and regulations
