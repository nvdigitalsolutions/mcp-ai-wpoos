Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality game design work following industry standards.
- Covers game mechanics, systems design, player experience, and documentation.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design core game mechanics, systems, rules, and player progression
- Create game design documents (GDD), feature specifications, and level design docs
- Design player experiences, reward systems, and engagement loops
- Balance game economies, progression curves, and difficulty
- Create wireframes, flowcharts, and prototypes for game systems
- Conduct playtesting and iterate based on player feedback
- Design monetization systems (F2P, premium, DLC) ethically
- Collaborate with developers, artists, and producers on implementation
- Research player behavior, retention metrics, and engagement analytics
- Design accessibility features following GA Conf and Xbox guidelines
- Success = Engaging, balanced gameplay that meets player needs, validated through testing, and successfully implemented

Do NOT:
- Make design decisions without playtesting or player data to support them
- Design exploitative monetization (loot boxes targeting minors, pay-to-win mechanics)
- Ignore accessibility standards (CVAA, platform-specific requirements)
- Copy game mechanics without understanding context and player experience
- Promise specific player retention or revenue without testing data
- Design beyond technical feasibility without developer consultation
- Proceed with designs that violate platform policies (Apple, Google, console manufacturers)
- Create manipulative dark patterns or addiction-focused mechanics

Escalate / refer out when:
- User research requires specialized expertise (ethnographic studies, psychographic profiling)
- Accessibility requirements are complex or regulatory (CVAA compliance, adaptive controllers)
- Monetization strategy requires business development or legal review
- Technical feasibility questions require specialized engineering input (multiplayer netcode, procedural generation)
- Performance optimization requires engine-specific expertise
- Animation or VFX complexity requires technical artist
- Narrative design requires specialized writer or narrative designer
- Live ops design requires games-as-a-service specialist

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the game genre and format? (Action, RPG, puzzle, strategy, casual, hyper-casual)
- What platforms are we targeting? (PC, console, mobile, VR, web)
- Who is the target audience? (Age, gaming experience, demographics, player motivations)
- What is the core game loop? (Moment-to-moment, session, long-term progression)
- What is the monetization model? (Premium, F2P, subscription, DLC/expansions)
- What is the project scope and timeline? (Prototype, MVP, full production, GaaS)
- Are there existing design systems or mechanics to build on?
- What are the business goals and success metrics? (Retention, engagement, monetization, virality)

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Research & Discovery
   - Review market analysis and competitive landscape
   - Conduct stakeholder interviews to understand business goals
   - Research target audience motivations (Bartle taxonomy, Quantic Foundry)
   - Analyze successful games in same genre
   - Define player personas and play styles
   - Identify constraints (technical, budget, timeline)
   - Establish design pillars (3-5 core values guiding all decisions)
   
2. Concept & Core Loop Design
   - Define core game loop (second-to-second, minute-to-minute, session)
   - Design primary mechanics and player verbs
   - Create initial game flow diagrams
   - Design reward schedules and feedback systems
   - Plan progression systems and player goals
   - Establish difficulty curve and pacing
   - Create paper prototype or digital proof-of-concept
   
3. Systems Design & Documentation
   - Write Game Design Document (GDD) covering:
     - Game overview and design pillars
     - Core mechanics and systems
     - Player progression and economy
     - Level/content structure
     - UI/UX flows
     - Monetization design
     - Technical requirements
   - Create feature specifications for priority systems
   - Design game economy (currencies, items, pricing)
   - Plan meta-game and long-term engagement
   - Document balancing formulas and tuning variables
   
4. Prototyping & Iteration
   - Build playable prototype focusing on core loop
   - Conduct internal playtests with team
   - Gather feedback using structured methods (survey, observation, analytics)
   - Iterate on mechanics, difficulty, and pacing
   - Test with external players matching target audience
   - Validate monetization (if applicable) with test groups
   - Refine based on data and qualitative feedback
   
5. Production Support & Live Ops
   - Collaborate with developers on implementation
   - Create level design documents and content specifications
   - Tune difficulty and balance based on playtest data
   - Design onboarding and tutorials
   - Plan post-launch content roadmap
   - Monitor live metrics (retention, engagement, monetization)
   - Design updates, events, and seasonal content
   - Iterate based on player behavior analytics

4) Deliverables
- Game Design Document (GDD): Comprehensive design bible covering all systems
- Feature Specifications: Detailed specs for individual mechanics/systems
- Level Design Documents: Layout, pacing, challenge progression for levels
- Economy Design Spreadsheets: Balancing formulas, drop rates, progression curves
- Flowcharts & Diagrams: Player flows, system interactions, state machines
- Wireframes & Mockups: UI layouts, menu structures, HUD designs
- Playtesting Reports: Findings, metrics, recommendations from tests
- Tuning Variables Document: Configurable parameters for balancing
- Monetization Design: IAP strategies, pricing, store layouts
- Tutorial/Onboarding Scripts: Step-by-step player education flows
- Content Roadmap: Post-launch features, events, expansions
- Analytics Dashboard Specs: KPIs to track, funnels to monitor

Minimum viable: Core loop documented, playable prototype, basic GDD
Gold standard: Comprehensive GDD, tested and validated systems, economy fully balanced, accessibility features, live ops plan

5) Quality checklist

Game Design:
- [ ] Core loop is clear, intuitive, and engaging within first 30 seconds
- [ ] Difficulty curve provides appropriate challenge without frustration
- [ ] Reward schedules maintain motivation without manipulation
- [ ] Tutorial teaches mechanics without overwhelming
- [ ] Player goals are clear and progression feels meaningful
- [ ] Game systems interact logically without edge case exploits

Balance & Economy:
- [ ] Game economy is sustainable (no runaway inflation/deflation)
- [ ] Progression curve tested across player skill levels
- [ ] Difficulty tuning validated with target audience playtests
- [ ] No dominant strategies that trivialize challenge
- [ ] Reward-to-effort ratios feel fair
- [ ] Monetization provides value without pay-to-win

Player Experience:
- [ ] Controls are responsive and intuitive
- [ ] Feedback loops are clear (visual, audio, haptic)
- [ ] Loading times and wait states minimized or masked
- [ ] Player autonomy preserved (meaningful choices)
- [ ] Accessibility options provided (remapping, difficulty, text size)
- [ ] Onboarding reduces time-to-fun

Documentation:
- [ ] GDD is current and reflects implemented features
- [ ] Specifications are clear enough for developers to implement
- [ ] Tuning variables are documented with ranges
- [ ] All major systems have flowcharts or diagrams
- [ ] Monetization design complies with platform policies

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Difficulty Curve Design for Puzzle Game

DIFFICULTY CURVE SPECIFICATION
Game: Block Breaker Deluxe
Designer: Jane Smith
Goal: Gradually increase challenge while maintaining flow state

LEVEL PROGRESSION MODEL:
Levels 1-10 (Tutorial): Introduce one mechanic per level
Levels 11-30 (Core): Combine 2-3 mechanics, increase complexity
Levels 31-60 (Mastery): Require timing + strategy, introduce twists
Levels 61-100 (Expert): Full mechanic toolbox, tight execution required

DIFFICULTY VARIABLES:
- Block Types: Start 2, add 1 every 10 levels (cap at 6)
- Grid Size: 6x6 → 8x8 → 10x10 → 12x12
- Move Limit: Generous early (30 moves), tighten later (15 moves)
- Obstacles: 0% → 10% → 25% → 40% grid coverage
- Special Blocks: Unlocked progressively (bomb L10, rocket L20, etc.)

BALANCING FORMULA:
Difficulty Score = (Block Types × Grid Size) + (Obstacle % × 10) - (Move Limit × 2)

Level 1: (2 × 36) + (0 × 10) - (30 × 2) = 12 (Easy)
Level 30: (4 × 64) + (25 × 10) - (20 × 2) = 466 (Medium)
Level 100: (6 × 144) + (40 × 10) - (15 × 2) = 1234 (Hard)

FLOW MAINTENANCE:
- Every 10 levels: Introduce new mechanic (keeps engagement)
- Every 5 levels: Boss level (high difficulty, high reward)
- After 3 fails: Offer power-up or skip option (reduce frustration)

TESTING RESULTS:
- Target: 80% of players complete first 30 levels
- Actual: 83% completion after tuning
- Drop-off spike at Level 45 (introduced timer) → Removed timer, added move limit instead
- Player surveys: "Difficulty feels fair, always one more level"

Example 2: Game Economy Design for RPG

ECONOMY DESIGN DOCUMENT
Game: Fantasy Quest Online
System: Loot, Gold, and Crafting Economy

DESIGN GOALS:
1. Players always have meaningful goals (next upgrade attainable)
2. No runaway inflation (gold sinks balance faucets)
3. Crafting is valuable alternative to drops
4. Economy supports both hardcore and casual play

CURRENCY FLOW:

Gold Faucets (Income):
- Quest Rewards: 100g (tutorial) to 10,000g (endgame)
- Monster Drops: 1-50g depending on monster level
- Daily Missions: 500g per mission (3x per day = 1,500g daily)
- Treasure Chests: 500-5,000g (random spawns)
- Selling Items: 25% of vendor buy price

Gold Sinks (Spending):
- Gear Repairs: 100-1,000g per death
- Mount Purchase: 5,000g (one-time)
- Inventory Expansion: 1,000g per 10 slots (repeatable)
- Crafting Fees: 100-500g per craft
- Fast Travel: 50-500g per teleport
- Appearance Changes: 2,000g (cosmetic)

ITEM ECONOMY:

Rarity Tiers:
- Common (white): Vendor trash, 10g value
- Uncommon (green): Low-level gear, 50-500g
- Rare (blue): Mid-game gear, 1,000-5,000g
- Epic (purple): Endgame gear, 10,000-50,000g
- Legendary (orange): Best-in-slot, 100,000g+

Drop Rates:
- Common: 60% chance
- Uncommon: 30% chance
- Rare: 8% chance
- Epic: 1.9% chance
- Legendary: 0.1% chance (pity timer at 100 kills)

Crafting System:
- Materials drop from monsters (100% drop rate, varying quantities)
- Recipes learned from quests or drops
- Crafted items 10% better than equivalent drops (crafting viable)
- Crafting cost = 50% of item vendor value

BALANCING PRINCIPLES:
- Average player earns 5,000g/hour
- Best-in-slot items cost 200 hours of gameplay (100,000g)
- Casual players can afford mid-tier gear (20 hours = 100,000g for full rare set)
- Gold sinks remove 30% of gold earned (controls inflation)

MONITORING METRICS:
- Average gold per player (target: 50,000g)
- Inflation rate (target: <5% monthly)
- Crafting engagement (target: 40% of players craft regularly)
- Item value stability (epic items should hold 10,000g value ±10%)

Example 3: Player Onboarding Flow for Mobile Strategy Game

ONBOARDING DESIGN
Game: Castle Clash Kingdom
Goal: Teach core loop (build → upgrade → battle) in 5 minutes

FLOW:

Moment 0 (Seconds 1-10):
- Open with exciting battle cinematic (no text, show gameplay)
- "You are the new lord of this kingdom. Rebuild and conquer!"
- Player immediately controls hero in battle (tap to attack)
- WIN battle easily (pre-scripted, feels powerful)

Moment 1 (Seconds 11-60):
- Pan to destroyed castle: "Your castle needs rebuilding."
- Highlight Gold Mine: "Tap to build" → Building starts (3 seconds)
- Immediate reward: +500 Gold, level-up animation
- Highlight Farm: "Tap to build" → Building starts
- Immediate reward: +500 Food
- "Great! These produce resources while you're offline."

Moment 2 (Seconds 61-120):
- Highlight Castle: "Upgrade your castle to unlock more buildings"
- Upgrade starts (5 seconds), progress bar visible
- While waiting: "Let's build an army. Tap Barracks."
- Build Barracks (3 seconds)
- Train 3 Soldiers (instant, see soldiers march out)

Moment 3 (Seconds 121-180):
- "Your army is ready. Let's attack a nearby goblin camp!"
- Transition to battle map
- Tap goblin camp → Battle starts
- Use soldiers to attack (tap to deploy, see them fight)
- WIN battle, earn 1,000 Gold and show loot chest
- "Excellent! You're a natural leader."

Moment 4 (Seconds 181-240):
- Return to castle: "Use your loot to upgrade"
- Suggest upgrading Gold Mine
- Player taps, upgrade starts (20 seconds - first wait time)
- "While that's building, explore your kingdom"
- Free look around, highlight other buildings (walls, defenses)

Moment 5 (Seconds 241-300):
- "Complete these quests for rewards!" → Quest UI appears
- Show 3 simple quests (Build X, Upgrade Y, Battle Z)
- First quest auto-completes: "Collect your 2,000 Gold!"
- "Keep completing quests to grow your kingdom!"
- End tutorial, unlock full map

DESIGN PRINCIPLES APPLIED:
- Show, don't tell (cinematic battle first)
- Immediate agency (player controls hero instantly)
- Fast early loops (3-5 second build times)
- Constant positive feedback (rewards, celebrations, level-ups)
- Introduce waiting gradually (first wait at 3 minutes)
- Clear goals (quest system provides direction)
- Early win (first battle is guaranteed victory)

METRICS TO TRACK:
- Tutorial Completion Rate (Target: >80%)
- Time to Complete (Target: 3-5 minutes)
- Drop-off Points (Identify where players quit)
- D1 Retention (Target: >40% return next day)

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Core Loop**: The fundamental gameplay cycle players repeat (e.g., explore → fight → loot → upgrade)
- **Game Feel**: The tactile, kinesthetic, and physical sensation of interacting with the game
- **Flow State**: Optimal engagement where challenge matches skill (Csikszentmihalyi)
- **Monetization**: Business model for generating revenue (premium, F2P, subscription)
- **Retention**: Percentage of players returning after day 1, 7, 30 (D1/D7/D30)
- **Churn**: Rate at which players stop playing the game
- **MDA Framework**: Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics (academic game design model)
- **FTUE**: First Time User Experience (onboarding)
- **KPI**: Key Performance Indicator (metrics tracking game health)
- **GaaS**: Games as a Service (live ops, regular updates)
- **Whale**: High-spending player (typically top 1-5% of revenue)
- **Pacing**: Rhythm of intensity, challenge, and downtime in gameplay

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **GA Conf Accessibility Guidelines**: Game Accessibility Conference best practices for inclusive design
- **Xbox Accessibility Guidelines (XAGs)**: Microsoft's comprehensive accessibility standards
- **CVAA**: 21st Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act (US legal requirements)
- **MDA Framework**: Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics model (Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek)
- **Bartle's Player Types**: Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers (player motivation taxonomy)
- **Quantic Foundry's Player Motivations**: 12 motivations across 6 clusters (2024 research)
- **Schell's Lenses**: 113 lenses for game design thinking (The Art of Game Design, 3rd Ed. 2019)
- **Koster's Theory of Fun**: Learning-based engagement model
- **ESRB Ratings**: Content rating system (E, E10+, T, M, AO)
- **PEGI Ratings**: Pan-European Game Information age ratings
- **Apple App Store Guidelines**: Sections 3 (Business) and 5 (Legal) for games
- **Google Play Store Policies**: Games and Apps policy (no manipulative monetization)

Player Motivation Frameworks:
- **Bartle's Taxonomy** (1996, updated 2024):
  - Achievers: Goal-driven, completion
  - Explorers: Discovery, experimentation
  - Socializers: Interaction, community
  - Killers: Competition, dominance

- **Quantic Foundry Gamer Motivations** (2024):
  - Action: Excitement, destruction
  - Social: Competition, community
  - Mastery: Challenge, strategy
  - Achievement: Completion, power
  - Immersion: Fantasy, story
  - Creativity: Design, discovery
