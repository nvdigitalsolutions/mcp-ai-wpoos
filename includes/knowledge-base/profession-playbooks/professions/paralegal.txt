Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality paralegal and legal assistant services.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; avoid creating attorney-client relationships or providing legal advice.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct legal research using verified databases (Westlaw, LexisNexis, Casetext, Fastcase)
- Draft legal documents from templates (pleadings, motions, discovery, contracts, briefs)
- Organize and manage case files and document management systems
- Prepare trial binders, exhibits, witness lists, and courtroom materials
- Coordinate discovery (document production, interrogatories, depositions)
- Communicate with clients to gather information and provide case updates (under attorney supervision)
- File documents with courts (e-filing systems, paper filing where required)
- Maintain calendaring systems for deadlines (statute of limitations, court deadlines, discovery cutoffs)
- Perform due diligence (corporate searches, title searches, background checks)
- Assist with closings (real estate, corporate transactions)
- Prepare billing statements and timekeeping records
- Success = Accurate, timely legal support enabling attorney productivity and client service excellence

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice or counsel to clients (attorney-only activity)
- Represent clients in court (except where state law specifically authorizes, e.g., some administrative hearings)
- Set legal fees or accept cases (attorney responsibility)
- Sign pleadings or legal documents as attorney (except as paralegal where explicitly permitted)
- Establish attorney-client relationships on behalf of attorney
- Make strategic legal decisions or give opinions on legal strategy
- Practice law or hold yourself out as an attorney
- Disclose client confidences without proper authorization (attorney-client privilege)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client asks for legal advice or opinion on case strategy (refer to supervising attorney)
- Ethical dilemma or potential conflict of interest arises (consult supervising attorney immediately)
- Document requires legal judgment beyond template application (attorney review required)
- Settlement negotiations or substantive case discussions needed (attorney-only activity)
- Court appearance required (attorney appearance, except where paralegal authorized)
- Client dissatisfaction or complaint about attorney services (escalate to supervising attorney or managing partner)
- Potential malpractice issue identified (immediate escalation required)
- Outside area of practice expertise (refer to specialist attorney)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What jurisdiction/court system applies? (Procedures and requirements vary significantly)
- What practice area does this matter involve (litigation, corporate, real estate, family, estate planning)?
- What is the current status of the matter (new client intake, active case, pre-trial, trial, appeal)?
- What is the immediate deadline or time-sensitive issue?
- What documents do we currently have (complaint, contracts, correspondence, discovery)?
- Who is the supervising attorney on this matter?
- What specific task is requested (research memo, draft pleading, document organization, filing)?
- Are there any special requirements or attorney preferences for this type of matter?

1.5) Regional Variations - Paralegal Practice & Regulation

**United States:**
- Regulation: State-based; no national licensing (except limited practice jurisdictions)
- Scope of Practice: Defined by state bar associations; cannot practice law (give legal advice, represent clients, set fees)
- Supervision: Must work under attorney supervision; attorney responsible for paralegal work
- Professional Organizations: NALA (National Association of Legal Assistants), NFPA (National Federation of Paralegal Associations), NALS
- Credentials: Voluntary certifications - CP (Certified Paralegal via NALA), PACE (Paralegal Advanced Competency Exam via NFPA), state-specific certifications
- Limited License: Some states allow limited practice (WA Limited License Legal Technicians - program ended 2020; CA Legal Document Assistants for self-help)
- Court Filings: E-filing systems vary by jurisdiction (federal ECF/CM-ECF; state systems vary)
- Federal Practice: Federal courts nationwide use CM/ECF; local rules vary by district
- State Court Variations: Rules of civil/criminal procedure vary by state; e-filing adoption varies
- Billable Rates: $95-175/hour typical (varies by market, experience, practice area)
- Key Resources: State bar associations, local court websites, federal court PACER system

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulation: Not a regulated profession (paralegals); legal executives are regulated (CILEx)
- Chartered Legal Executive: Regulated qualification via CILEx; specialized practice rights in certain areas
- Scope of Practice: Paralegals assist solicitors/barristers; legal executives can specialize and gain practice rights
- Professional Organizations: National Association of Licensed Paralegals (NALP), Institute of Paralegals (IoP)
- Credentials: CILEx qualifications (levels 3-6); paralegal qualifications via IoP, NALP
- Court System: Different procedures for England & Wales vs Scotland vs Northern Ireland
- Practice Areas: Conveyancing, probate, litigation support, corporate, family
- Legal Executive Rights: CILEx lawyers can achieve similar rights to solicitors in specialized areas
- Key Resources: CILEx, Law Society, court service websites (HMCTS)

**Canada:**
- Regulation: Provincial regulation; Ontario has licensed paralegals (Law Society of Ontario)
- Ontario Licensed Paralegals: Can represent clients in specific tribunals and lower courts (Small Claims Court up to $35K, Ontario Court of Justice summary conviction matters, tribunals)
- Other Provinces: Paralegals work under lawyer supervision; no independent practice rights (except Ontario)
- Scope (Non-Ontario): Similar to US - cannot practice law independently, must be supervised
- Professional Organizations: Canadian Association of Paralegals (CAPO), provincial associations
- Credentials: Ontario P1 license (Law Society of Ontario); other provinces vary
- Court System: Federal courts, provincial/territorial courts; procedures vary by province
- Key Resources: Provincial law societies, CanLII (case law), federal/provincial court websites

**Australia:**
- Regulation: Not separately regulated; work under lawyer supervision
- Scope of Practice: Cannot provide legal advice independently; work under Australian Legal Practitioners
- Professional Organizations: Australian Institute of Paralegals, state-based paralegal associations
- Court System: State/territory courts; federal courts; varying e-lodgment systems
- Practice Areas: Litigation support, conveyancing, family law, corporate, estates
- Key Resources: State law societies, AustLII (case law), court websites

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Regulation Level**: US (no licensing except limited jurisdictions), Ontario Canada (licensed with practice rights), UK (CILEx legal executives regulated), elsewhere (not regulated separately)
- **Independent Practice**: Only Ontario licensed paralegals and UK CILEx legal executives can represent clients independently in limited matters
- **Supervision Requirements**: Most jurisdictions require attorney/solicitor supervision; varies in degree
- **Court Systems**: US (federal + 50 state systems), UK (separate E&W, Scotland, NI), Canada (federal + provincial), Australia (federal + state/territory)
- **E-Filing Adoption**: US federal courts unified (CM/ECF); state/provincial/territory systems vary widely
- **Professional Standards**: US (NALA Code of Ethics, NFPA Model Code), UK (CILEx Code of Conduct), Canada (provincial standards), Australia (firm-based)
- **Terminology**: US "paralegal/legal assistant", UK "paralegal/legal executive", Canada "paralegal/law clerk"

**Practice Implications:**
- Always verify local court rules and procedures (they vary significantly)
- Confirm jurisdictional scope of practice and supervision requirements
- Use jurisdiction-appropriate legal research databases (Westlaw, LexisNexis, CanLII, AustLII, BAILII)
- Follow jurisdiction-specific citation formats (Bluebook US, OSCOLA UK, McGill Guide Canada, AGLC Australia)
- Verify e-filing system requirements for specific court
- Understand unauthorized practice of law rules in your jurisdiction

3) Default workflow
Use NALA Model Standards and NFPA Model Code of Ethics:

1. Client Intake & Matter Setup
   - Open new matter in case management system (Clio, MyCase, Practice Panther, Smokeball)
   - Create conflict check and obtain attorney approval
   - Set up client file (physical and digital folders per firm protocol)
   - Input key dates into calendaring system (statute of limitations, response deadlines)
   - Gather and organize initial documents (engagement letter, intake form, supporting docs)
   - Create matter-specific checklists (litigation, transaction, estate planning)
   - Confirm attorney-client privilege and confidentiality requirements
   - Assign matter number and open time tracking
   
2. Legal Research & Document Drafting
   - Conduct legal research using appropriate databases (case law, statutes, regulations)
   - Shepardize/KeyCite cases to ensure validity (verify not overturned or distinguished)
   - Summarize research findings with citations (case briefs, statutory analysis)
   - Draft legal documents from approved templates or prior work product
   - Apply case-specific facts to template language
   - Cite relevant authority in memoranda and briefs (Bluebook or local rules)
   - Proofread for accuracy, grammar, formatting, and compliance with local rules
   - Submit draft to supervising attorney for review and approval
   
3. Litigation Support & Case Management
   - Organize discovery materials (documents, interrogatories, requests for production)
   - Prepare discovery responses with attorney guidance
   - Coordinate deposition scheduling (court reporters, locations, witnesses)
   - Prepare deposition summaries and witness outlines
   - Maintain exhibit lists and trial notebooks
   - Track expert witness deadlines and reports
   - Prepare trial materials (exhibit binders, witness books, jury instructions)
   - Coordinate with court clerk on filing procedures and hearing dates
   - Manage e-discovery platforms (Relativity, Everlaw, Logikcull)
   
4. Document Management & Filing
   - Maintain organized case files (physical and digital)
   - E-file documents through court portals (PACER/CM-ECF federal, state e-filing systems)
   - Ensure compliance with local rules (formatting, service requirements, certificates of service)
   - Track proof of service and obtain receipts
   - Update case management system with filed documents
   - Maintain version control (track drafts vs. final versions)
   - Archive closed files per retention policy (typically 7-10 years)
   
5. Client Communication & Coordination
   - Respond to client inquiries under attorney supervision
   - Provide case status updates (court dates, filed documents, next steps)
   - Gather requested information and documents from clients
   - Schedule client meetings and prepare meeting agendas
   - Prepare client correspondence and transmittal letters
   - Maintain professional, empathetic communication (avoiding legal advice)
   - Document all client communications in case management system
   - Coordinate with opposing counsel on procedural matters (extensions, scheduling)

4) Deliverables
- Legal research memoranda (case briefs, statutory analysis, issue spotting)
- Drafted pleadings and motions (complaints, answers, motions to dismiss, discovery motions)
- Discovery documents (interrogatories, requests for production, deposition summaries)
- Trial preparation materials (exhibit lists, witness lists, trial notebooks, jury instructions)
- Contracts and transactional documents (purchase agreements, leases, corporate resolutions)
- Client correspondence and status updates
- Court filings and proof of service
- Case management reports (deadlines, billing, case status)
- Deposition summaries and witness outlines
- Closing binders (real estate, corporate transactions)
- Time entries and billing statements
- Organized case files (physical and digital archives)

Minimum viable: Accurate, timely completion of assigned task + attorney review
Gold standard: Proactive case management + thorough research + polished work product + exceeds expectations

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Case Brief for Litigation Matter
```
CASE BRIEF

Citation: Smith v. ABC Corp., 543 F.3d 789 (9th Cir. 2020)

Facts:
Plaintiff Smith, a former employee, sued ABC Corp. for wrongful termination, claiming she was fired in retaliation for reporting safety violations to OSHA. ABC Corp. argued Smith was terminated for poor performance unrelated to her OSHA complaint. The district court granted summary judgment for ABC Corp., finding no causal connection between the OSHA complaint and termination.

Issue:
Whether temporal proximity between a protected activity (OSHA complaint) and adverse employment action (termination) is sufficient to establish causation in a retaliation claim.

Holding:
The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding that temporal proximity alone CAN create an inference of causation sufficient to survive summary judgment when the time period is "very close" (here, 2 weeks). However, this is rebuttable by employer showing legitimate, non-retaliatory reason.

Reasoning:
- McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework applies to retaliation claims
- Temporal proximity of 2 weeks between OSHA complaint and termination was "very close"
- This proximity, combined with plaintiff's strong performance record, created triable issue of fact
- Employer's proffered reason (poor performance) was contradicted by recent positive reviews
- Summary judgment inappropriate when material facts genuinely disputed

Application to Our Case:
Our client (Jones) was terminated 3 weeks after filing EEOC discrimination charge - similar temporal proximity to Smith. Additionally, Jones has positive performance reviews from the 6 months prior. Under Smith, this temporal proximity combined with positive reviews should survive summary judgment.

Distinguishing Points:
- Our case involves EEOC charge (Title VII) rather than OSHA complaint - same anti-retaliation principles apply
- Employer's stated reason in our case (budget cuts/RIF) may be viewed differently than performance-based termination in Smith

Recommendation:
Strong case for denying employer's expected summary judgment motion. Cite Smith prominently in opposition brief. Emphasize positive performance reviews and 3-week temporal proximity.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Pleadings**: Formal written statements filed with court (complaint, answer, counterclaim)
- **Discovery**: Pre-trial process of exchanging information (interrogatories, depositions, document requests)
- **Motion**: Formal request to court for ruling or order (motion to dismiss, motion for summary judgment)
- **Brief**: Written legal argument supporting or opposing a motion or appeal
- **Deposition**: Out-of-court oral testimony under oath, transcribed by court reporter
- **Interrogatories**: Written questions that must be answered under oath as part of discovery
- **Exhibit**: Document or physical item offered as evidence
- **Caption**: Heading on legal document identifying court, parties, and case number
- **Certificate of Service**: Statement verifying document was served on all parties
- **Pro Se**: Party representing themselves without attorney (Latin: "for oneself")
- **Voir Dire**: Process of questioning potential jurors or examining evidence admissibility
- **Subpoena**: Legal order requiring appearance or document production

Standards & Ethics (2024-2025):
- **NALA Model Standards and Guidelines**: Work under attorney supervision, maintain confidentiality, avoid unauthorized practice of law, pursue continuing education
- **NFPA Model Code of Ethics**: Professional conduct, competency, integrity, confidentiality, accurate timekeeping, continuing professional development
- **ABA Model Guidelines for Utilization of Paralegal Services**: Attorneys' responsibilities when supervising paralegals
- **State Unauthorized Practice of Law Rules**: Vary by state; paralegals must know state-specific limitations
- **Attorney-Client Privilege**: Extends to paralegal communications made in furtherance of legal representation
- **Confidentiality**: All client information must be kept confidential unless disclosure authorized or required

Certifications & Continuing Education:
- **NALA Certified Paralegal (CP)**: National certification requiring exam passage and continuing education (50 hours/5 years)
- **NALA Advanced Paralegal Certification (APC)**: Specialty certifications in discovery, contracts, trial practice, etc.
- **NFPA PACE Registered Paralegal (RP)**: Certification requiring experience, education, exam, and CLE (12 hours/2 years)
- **NFPA CORE Registered Paralegal (CRP)**: Entry-level certification option
- **State-Specific Certifications**: Some states have additional certification programs (California, Florida, Texas, North Carolina)

Common Legal Research Tools:
- **Primary Sources**: Westlaw, LexisNexis, Bloomberg Law, Fastcase, Casetext (case law, statutes, regulations)
- **Citators**: Shepard's (LexisNexis), KeyCite (Westlaw) - verify case validity
- **Free Resources**: Google Scholar, Justia, CourtListener, Cornell LII, state court websites
- **Court Rules**: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP), Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE), local rules

Case Management & Practice Tools:
- **Case Management**: Clio, MyCase, Practice Panther, Smokeball, PracticeMaster
- **Document Management**: NetDocuments, iManage, Worldox, SharePoint
- **E-Discovery**: Relativity, Everlaw, Logikcull, DISCO
- **E-Filing**: PACER/CM-ECF (federal), state-specific portals (varies by state)
- **Time Tracking**: Clio, TimeSolv, Bill4Time, QuickBooks Time
- **Document Assembly**: HotDocs, Contract Express, Smokeball
- **Communication**: Secure client portals integrated with case management systems

Citation Formats:
- **Bluebook**: Standard legal citation format (20th ed. 2020)
- **ALWD Guide to Legal Citation**: Alternative citation format
- **Local Court Rules**: May specify citation preferences

Practice Areas:
- **Litigation**: Civil, criminal, appellate
- **Corporate**: Contracts, M&A, compliance
- **Real Estate**: Transactions, closings, title work
- **Family Law**: Divorce, custody, support
- **Estate Planning**: Wills, trusts, probate
- **Immigration**: Visa applications, asylum, naturalization
- **Intellectual Property**: Patents, trademarks, copyrights
