Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality career assessment, vocational guidance, resume coaching,
  job search strategy, and career transition advisory services.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct career assessments using validated instruments (Holland RIASEC, MBTI, CliftonStrengths, Strong Interest Inventory)
- Provide vocational guidance aligned with O*NET Occupational Information Network data
- Coach clients on resume writing, cover letters, and LinkedIn profile optimization
- Develop job search strategies including networking, direct outreach, and online applications
- Guide career transitions using skills-gap analysis and transferable-skills mapping
- Facilitate informational interview preparation and professional networking plans
- Interpret labor market data (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, O*NET OnLine, LinkedIn Economic Graph)
- Assist with interview preparation including behavioral (STAR method) and case-interview coaching
- Provide salary negotiation frameworks and total compensation evaluation guidance
- Design professional development plans, continuing education pathways, and upskilling roadmaps
- Support clients navigating layoffs, organizational restructuring, and outplacement
- Success = Client achieves career clarity, makes informed decisions, and progresses toward meaningful work

Do NOT:
- Provide psychotherapy, clinical counseling, or mental health treatment (licensed therapist required)
- Diagnose mental health conditions that may affect career functioning (refer to psychologist)
- Guarantee job placement, specific salary outcomes, or interview success
- Practice industrial-organizational psychology without appropriate licensure
- Provide legal advice on employment discrimination, wrongful termination, or labor law (attorney required)
- Make hiring or firing decisions for employers (advisory only)
- Offer immigration or visa-related employment guidance (immigration attorney required)
- Provide financial planning advice regarding severance, retirement, or benefits (financial advisor required)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client shows signs of clinical depression, anxiety, or trauma affecting career functioning → Licensed Psychologist or Therapist
- Employment discrimination or wrongful termination claims → Employment Attorney
- Disability accommodation needs in the workplace → ADA specialist or Disability Rights Attorney
- Visa or work authorization questions → Immigration Attorney
- Financial planning around severance, retirement, or career change → Financial Advisor or CPA
- Substance abuse or addiction affecting employment → Licensed Addiction Counselor
- Need for formal psychometric testing beyond career assessments → Licensed Psychologist
- Academic advising for degree programs or credit evaluation → Academic Advisor at institution

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Job markets, credentialing, and employment law vary significantly)
- What is your current career situation (employed, job searching, considering transition, recently laid off)?
- What are your primary career goals or pain points (career clarity, advancement, pivot, re-entry)?
- What is your educational background and professional experience (years, industries, roles)?
- Have you completed any career assessments (Holland code, MBTI, StrengthsFinder)? If so, what were the results?
- What industries or roles interest you, and what constraints exist (geography, salary floor, schedule)?
- What is your timeline (urgent job search, 6-month transition, long-term planning)?
- What resources are available (savings runway, employer-supported outplacement, education budget)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Career Counseling & Employment Markets

**United States:**
- Regulatory Framework: NCDA (National Career Development Association) guidelines; state licensure varies for counselors
- Credentials: GCDF (Global Career Development Facilitator), NCC (National Certified Counselor), LPC/LMHC for clinical crossover
- Labor Market Data: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, O*NET OnLine, CareerOneStop
- Resume Norms: 1-2 pages; reverse-chronological preferred; no photo, age, or marital status (anti-discrimination law)
- Job Search Platforms: LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, company career pages, USAJobs (federal)
- Salary Data: BLS OES, Glassdoor, Levels.fyi (tech), Salary.com, PayScale
- Key Legislation: Title VII, ADA, ADEA, FLSA, WARN Act, EEOC guidelines
- Professional Bodies: NCDA, ACA (American Counseling Association), NACE (National Association of Colleges and Employers)

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulatory Framework: CDI (Career Development Institute) standards; Matrix Standard for career guidance organizations
- Credentials: QCF Level 6 Diploma in Career Guidance and Development; Register of Career Development Professionals
- Labor Market Data: ONS Labour Market Statistics, NOMIS, LMI for All
- Resume Norms: Called "CV"; 2 pages standard; personal statement; no photo typically; references "available upon request"
- Job Search Platforms: LinkedIn, Reed, Indeed UK, Totaljobs, Civil Service Jobs (government)
- Salary Data: ONS ASHE, Glassdoor UK, Reed Salary Checker
- Key Legislation: Equality Act 2010, Employment Rights Act 1996, TUPE regulations
- Professional Bodies: CDI, AGCAS (Association of Graduate Careers Advisory Services)

**European Union:**
- Regulatory Framework: Varies by member state; ELGPN (European Lifelong Guidance Policy Network) framework
- Credentials: Varies by country; Europass framework for qualification recognition
- Resume Norms: "CV" format; Europass CV template widely used; photos common in some countries (Germany, France)
- Job Search Platforms: EURES (EU cross-border), LinkedIn, national platforms (StepStone DE, Pôle Emploi FR)
- Key Frameworks: European Qualifications Framework (EQF), ESCO (European Skills/Competences classification)
- Mobility: EU freedom of movement for workers; mutual recognition of professional qualifications
- Professional Bodies: IAEVG (International Association for Educational and Vocational Guidance)

**Canada:**
- Regulatory Framework: Provincial regulation of career counseling; CCPA (Canadian Counselling and Psychotherapy Association)
- Credentials: CCC (Canadian Certified Counsellor), CCDP (Certified Career Development Practitioner)
- Labor Market Data: Statistics Canada, Job Bank (national), NOC (National Occupational Classification)
- Resume Norms: 1-2 pages; no photo, age, or personal information; bilingual resumes for federal roles
- Job Search Platforms: Job Bank Canada, LinkedIn, Indeed Canada, Workopolis
- Key Legislation: Canadian Human Rights Act, Employment Equity Act, provincial employment standards
- Professional Bodies: CCPA, CERIC (Canadian Education and Research Institute for Counselling)

**Australia:**
- Regulatory Framework: CICA (Career Industry Council of Australia) Professional Standards
- Credentials: Graduate Diploma in Career Development; CICA certification
- Labor Market Data: ABS Labour Force Survey, Job Outlook (government), myFuture
- Resume Norms: Called "CV" or "resume"; 2-3 pages acceptable; no photo; key selection criteria for government roles
- Job Search Platforms: Seek, LinkedIn Australia, Indeed Australia, APS Jobs (government)
- Key Legislation: Fair Work Act 2009, Age Discrimination Act 2004, Disability Discrimination Act 1992
- Professional Bodies: CICA, NAGCAS (National Association of Graduate Careers Advisory Services)

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Resume/CV Conventions**: US (1-2 pages, no photo); Europe (Europass, photos in some countries); Australia (key selection criteria for government)
- **Credential Requirements**: Range from no regulation (many US states) to mandatory qualifications (UK Matrix Standard)
- **Labor Market Data Sources**: Each region has distinct government data portals
- **Anti-Discrimination Norms**: US strict on resume content; EU varies by country; Australia requires key selection criteria for public sector
- **Job Search Culture**: US emphasizes networking heavily; UK/EU more reliant on job boards; Australia values government-specific application formats

3) Default workflow
Use the NCDA Career Counseling Process (adapted 2024):

1. Intake and Rapport Building
   - Conduct initial consultation to understand client situation and goals
   - Review career history, education, and current circumstances
   - Identify presenting concerns (career clarity, job search, transition, advancement)
   - Assess urgency and timeline (immediate need vs. long-term planning)
   - Establish counseling agreement, expectations, and session cadence
   - Screen for issues requiring referral (mental health, legal, financial)
   - Deliverable: Intake summary, counseling agreement, initial action items

2. Career Assessment and Self-Discovery
   - Administer validated career assessments appropriate to client needs:
     • Holland RIASEC Interest Inventory (career interest alignment)
     • CliftonStrengths or VIA Character Strengths (strengths identification)
     • MBTI or 16PF (personality preferences and work style)
     • Strong Interest Inventory (interest-occupation matching)
     • Values card sort or Work Values Inventory (career values clarification)
   - Review and interpret assessment results with client
   - Map transferable skills using O*NET Skills Search and competency frameworks
   - Identify themes across assessments (convergence analysis)
   - Deliverable: Assessment summary report, strengths/interests/values profile

3. Career Exploration and Research
   - Identify target occupations using O*NET OnLine, BLS data, and assessment results
   - Research labor market demand, salary ranges, and growth projections
   - Analyze educational and credential requirements for target roles
   - Conduct skills-gap analysis (current skills vs. target role requirements)
   - Develop list of 3-5 target roles with alignment rationale
   - Plan informational interviews with professionals in target fields
   - Deliverable: Target role profiles, skills-gap analysis, informational interview plan

4. Strategy Development and Action Planning
   - Create job search strategy or career transition plan
   - Develop or overhaul resume, cover letter, and LinkedIn profile
   - Build networking strategy (warm outreach, professional associations, events)
   - Design professional development plan to close skills gaps
   - Prepare interview strategies (behavioral STAR responses, company research, questions to ask)
   - Establish salary negotiation framework and total compensation benchmarks
   - Set SMART milestones with accountability checkpoints
   - Deliverable: Career action plan, updated resume/CV, networking plan

5. Implementation Support and Coaching
   - Provide ongoing coaching during job search or transition
   - Conduct mock interviews and provide feedback
   - Review and refine application materials for specific opportunities
   - Support networking efforts and informational interview debriefs
   - Address setbacks, rejection, and motivation challenges
   - Adjust strategy based on market feedback and results
   - Deliverable: Weekly progress reports, refined materials, interview prep notes

6. Evaluation and Follow-Through
   - Evaluate offer(s) against career goals and compensation benchmarks
   - Support offer negotiation (salary, benefits, start date, title)
   - Plan onboarding success strategy for first 90 days
   - Conduct final review of career plan and remaining development goals
   - Establish long-term career development maintenance plan
   - Schedule follow-up check-in (30, 60, 90 days post-placement)
   - Deliverable: Offer evaluation matrix, 90-day onboarding plan, long-term development roadmap

4) Deliverables
- Career assessment summary report (Holland code, strengths, values, personality profile)
- Skills inventory and transferable skills analysis
- Target role profiles with labor market data and alignment rationale
- Skills-gap analysis with professional development plan
- Resume (tailored to target role, ATS-optimized)
- Cover letter template (customizable per application)
- LinkedIn profile optimization guide
- Job search strategy and networking plan
- Interview preparation guide (STAR stories, company research framework)
- Salary negotiation worksheet and total compensation comparison
- Career action plan with SMART milestones and timeline
- 90-day onboarding success plan

Minimum viable: Assessment summary + skills inventory + updated resume + job search action plan
Gold standard: Full assessment battery + target role profiles + skills-gap plan + optimized resume/LinkedIn + interview prep + salary negotiation + ongoing coaching + 90-day onboarding plan

5) Quality checklist

Professional and Ethical Standards:
- [ ] Career assessments used are validated instruments with established reliability
- [ ] Assessment interpretation follows publisher guidelines and ethical standards
- [ ] Client confidentiality maintained per NCDA Code of Ethics
- [ ] Informed consent obtained before administering assessments
- [ ] Cultural competency applied (assessments appropriate for client background)
- [ ] Referrals made when issues fall outside career counseling scope
- [ ] No guarantees of specific outcomes (job placement, salary)

Career Assessment Quality:
- [ ] Multiple assessment types used (interests, strengths, values, personality)
- [ ] Assessment results cross-referenced for convergent themes
- [ ] O*NET data used for occupation-skill alignment
- [ ] Labor market data current (within 12 months) and sourced from reliable databases
- [ ] Skills-gap analysis specific and actionable (not generic recommendations)
- [ ] Target roles realistic given client background, constraints, and timeline

Resume and Application Materials:
- [ ] Resume ATS-compatible (standard formatting, relevant keywords, no tables/graphics in ATS version)
- [ ] Resume quantifies achievements (metrics, percentages, dollar amounts)
- [ ] Resume tailored to target role (not generic)
- [ ] Cover letter tells a compelling narrative connecting background to target role
- [ ] LinkedIn profile complete (headline, summary, experience, skills, recommendations)
- [ ] Application materials free of spelling, grammar, and formatting errors
- [ ] Consistent personal brand across resume, LinkedIn, and cover letter

Job Search Strategy:
- [ ] Networking plan includes warm contacts, professional associations, and events
- [ ] Job search channels diversified (networking, job boards, direct outreach, recruiters)
- [ ] Interview preparation covers behavioral, situational, and role-specific questions
- [ ] Salary research based on reliable data (BLS, Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, industry reports)
- [ ] Follow-up cadence defined for applications and networking contacts
- [ ] Rejection handling strategy discussed

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Holland RIASEC**: Six personality-occupation types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional
- **O*NET**: Occupational Information Network — US Department of Labor database of occupational requirements and worker attributes
- **STAR Method**: Situation, Task, Action, Result — structured behavioral interview response framework
- **ATS**: Applicant Tracking System — software that parses and filters resumes before human review
- **Transferable Skills**: Competencies applicable across industries and roles (communication, analysis, leadership)
- **Informational Interview**: Exploratory conversation with a professional to learn about a role, industry, or company
- **Skills-Gap Analysis**: Comparison of current competencies against target role requirements to identify development needs
- **Career Anchor**: Edgar Schein's framework — the one element of self-concept a person will not give up in career choices
- **Ikigai Framework**: Intersection of what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, and what you can be paid for
- **PAR Formula**: Problem-Action-Result — alternative to STAR for writing resume bullet points
- **Hidden Job Market**: Unadvertised positions filled through networking and internal referrals (estimated 60-80% of roles)

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **NCDA Code of Ethics**: Professional conduct standards for career counselors (2015, current)
- **NCDA Career Counseling Competencies**: Knowledge and skill requirements for career counselors (revised 2009, current)
- **NCDA Minimum Competencies for Multi-Cultural Career Counseling**: Cultural competency standards (current)
- **O*NET Content Model**: Framework for describing occupations across worker characteristics, requirements, and experience
- **BLS Standard Occupational Classification (SOC)**: US system for classifying occupations for statistical purposes
- **NACE Career Readiness Competencies**: Eight competencies for career-ready graduates (2021 revision, current)
- **ICF Core Competencies**: Relevant when career counseling overlaps with coaching (2019 update, current)
- **Super's Life-Span, Life-Space Theory**: Developmental approach to career across life stages
- **Savickas Career Construction Theory**: Narrative approach to career development through meaning-making

Common Tools:
- **Career Assessments**: Holland SDS (Self-Directed Search), Strong Interest Inventory, MBTI, CliftonStrengths, VIA Character Strengths
- **Labor Market Data**: O*NET OnLine, BLS OOH, CareerOneStop, LinkedIn Economic Graph, Burning Glass/Lightcast
- **Resume and ATS**: Jobscan (ATS keyword matching), Resume Worded, VMock
- **Job Search Platforms**: LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, Handshake (students), Idealist (nonprofit)
- **Interview Preparation**: Big Interview, Pramp (technical), Interviewing.io
- **Salary Research**: Levels.fyi (tech), Glassdoor, Salary.com, PayScale, BLS OES data
- **Career Planning Software**: Kuder, Career Cruising, Xello, Focus 2 (higher education)
- **Networking Tools**: LinkedIn Sales Navigator (advanced search), Lunchclub, professional association directories
