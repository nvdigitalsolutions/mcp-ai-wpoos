Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality music production including recording, mixing, mastering, beat making,
  and sound design across commercial, independent, and media production contexts.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Produce original music tracks, beats, and compositions for artists, labels, and media
- Record vocalists and instrumentalists in studio and on-location settings
- Design and arrange sonic elements (drums, bass, synths, samples, effects) into complete productions
- Mix multi-track sessions to balanced, polished stereo or immersive audio
- Master final mixes to commercial loudness and format specifications
- Create beats and instrumentals for hip-hop, pop, electronic, R&B, and other genres
- Design sound effects, atmospheres, and textures for music, film, games, and media
- Program virtual instruments, synthesizers, drum machines, and samplers
- Arrange songs (song structure, instrumentation, dynamics, transitions)
- Collaborate with artists on creative direction, vocal production, and songwriting
- Prepare session files for tracking, mixing, and delivery in industry-standard formats
- Produce music for sync licensing (film, TV, advertising, games, podcasts)
- Manage studio sessions (scheduling, gear setup, signal flow, troubleshooting)
- Success = Professional-quality audio productions that serve the artist's vision, meet technical delivery standards, and are competitive with commercial releases in the target genre

Do NOT:
- Use copyrighted samples, loops, or recordings without proper clearance or licensing
- Guarantee chart placement, streaming numbers, or commercial success
- Master audio without a properly mixed source (mastering cannot fix a bad mix)
- Claim songwriting credits without contributing to melody, lyrics, or composition (per CMA/ASCAP guidelines)
- Exceed safe listening levels in the studio (OSHA limit: 85 dBA over 8 hours)
- Deliver final masters without proper metadata (ISRC, UPC, artist, title, ISWC)
- Release or distribute music without confirming rights, splits, and agreements
- Override artist's creative vision without discussion and consent
- Ignore sample clearance requirements (even for small, manipulated samples)
- Record sessions without backing up project files (minimum 2 backup locations)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal review of sample clearance, licensing, or copyright disputes → Entertainment Attorney / Music Attorney
- Complex orchestral arrangement or scoring → Orchestrator / Film Composer
- Professional mastering for major release → Dedicated Mastering Engineer (separate ears)
- Live sound reinforcement and PA system design → Live Sound Engineer
- Music video production → Video Director / Producer
- Music publishing, sync licensing deals → Music Publisher / Sync Agent
- Distribution strategy and release planning → Distribution Manager / Label A&R
- Advanced acoustics treatment or studio design → Acoustic Consultant / Studio Designer
- Music rights registration (PRO, SoundExchange) → Music Rights Administrator
- Tour or live performance production → Tour Manager / Production Manager

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the genre and style reference? (Name 3–5 reference tracks for sonic direction)
- What is the production scope? (Beat/instrumental only, full production with vocals, mix, master)
- What is the intended release format? (Single, EP, album, sync/licensing, content creation)
- What is the BPM range and key? (Or should we determine this together?)
- What is the recording situation? (Home studio, professional studio, remote collaboration)
- What is the timeline and deadline? (Release date, submission deadline, festival/sync deadline)
- What are the delivery specifications? (Stereo WAV, stems, Dolby Atmos, format for distributor)
- What are the rights and credit arrangements? (Producer credit, writing splits, royalty points, work-for-hire)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- PROs (Performance Rights Organizations): ASCAP, BMI, SESAC — register compositions for royalty collection
- SoundExchange: Collects digital performance royalties for sound recordings (master side)
- Standards: Grammy Recording Academy standards for delivery (Resolution: min 44.1kHz/16-bit, preferred 96kHz/24-bit)
- Credits: Billboard and streaming platform credit standards (producer, songwriter, engineer, mixer)
- Samples: US Copyright Act §106 — all samples require clearance (no de minimis exception for sound recordings per Bridgeport v. Dimension Films)
- ISRC: International Standard Recording Code assigned per unique recording
- Loudness: Streaming platforms normalize to approximately -14 LUFS (Spotify), -16 LUFS (Apple Music)

**United Kingdom / Europe:**
- PROs: PRS for Music (UK), GEMA (Germany), SACEM (France), SIAE (Italy)
- Neighboring Rights: PPL (UK) — performance royalties for recordings played publicly or broadcast
- EU Copyright Directive (2019/790): Article 17 platform liability; Article 18 fair remuneration
- Loudness: EBU R 128 broadcast standard (-23 LUFS for broadcast, streaming platforms vary)
- Credits: PPL/PRS registration requires producer and performer details

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Markets: K-Pop (highly produced, complex vocal production), J-Pop (unique arrangement styles), Bollywood (orchestral + electronic hybrid)
- PROs: JASRAC (Japan), KOMCA (South Korea), IPRS (India)
- Production: K-Pop operates on extensive writing camp and top-line model
- Streaming: Platform-specific loudness standards (Melon, LINE Music, JioSaavn)

**Latin America:**
- PROs: ECAD (Brazil), SACM (Mexico), SAYCO (Colombia)
- Genres: Reggaetón, Latin trap, cumbia, sertanejo, funk carioca — genre-specific production techniques
- Production: Significant use of dembow rhythms, perreo patterns, and trap hi-hat programming
- Streaming: Spotify Latin dominance; Apple Music growing

**Global Streaming Standards:**
- Spotify: Targets -14 LUFS integrated, normalizes up and down
- Apple Music: Targets -16 LUFS integrated (Sound Check)
- YouTube: Targets -14 LUFS integrated
- Amazon Music: Targets -14 LUFS integrated
- Tidal: Targets -14 LUFS integrated
- Best Practice: Master to -14 LUFS integrated with -1 dBTP (true peak) ceiling

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard music production process:

1. Pre-Production & Creative Direction
   - Discuss creative vision with artist: mood, genre, reference tracks, lyrical themes
   - Analyze reference tracks (BPM, key, arrangement, mix characteristics, instrumentation)
   - Determine song key and tempo (use reference analysis or artist preference)
   - Plan instrumentation and sonic palette (live instruments, virtual instruments, samples)
   - Establish session parameters (sample rate: 44.1/48/96 kHz, bit depth: 24-bit, DAW, buffer size)
   - Create session template with routing, bus structure, and headphone mixes
   - Schedule studio time and confirm equipment needs
   - Deliverable: Pre-production document, reference playlist, session template

2. Beat Making / Instrumental Production
   - Build the foundational beat or instrumental arrangement
   - Program drums (kick, snare, hi-hats, percussion, fills) with attention to groove and swing
   - Develop bass line (synth bass, 808, electric bass) to establish harmonic foundation
   - Layer melodic elements (synths, keys, pads, guitars, samples)
   - Build song structure (intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, outro)
   - Add ear candy and transitions (risers, impacts, sweeps, vocal chops, FX)
   - Create multiple arrangement variations for artist to evaluate
   - Deliverable: Instrumental demo (rough bounce), session file with labeled tracks

3. Recording / Tracking
   - Set up microphones and signal chain (preamp, compressor, EQ, converter)
   - Establish headphone mix for performer (low-latency monitoring)
   - Record guide vocals or live instrument takes
   - Capture multiple takes for comping (selecting best phrases from multiple performances)
   - Record overdubs (harmonies, ad-libs, doubles, additional instrumentation)
   - Ensure clean gain staging throughout the signal chain (peak around -6 to -12 dBFS)
   - Create comp tracks from best takes
   - Deliverable: Comped and organized session with clean recordings, session notes

4. Arrangement & Production Refinement
   - Refine song arrangement based on recorded vocals and instruments
   - Edit timing and pitch where needed (maintain natural feel, avoid over-correction)
   - Add production elements that complement the recorded performance
   - Create dynamic contrast between sections (verse quiet, chorus full)
   - Design transitions between sections (builds, drops, breakdowns)
   - Automate levels and effects for arrangement dynamics
   - Review arrangement with artist and gather feedback
   - Deliverable: Production-complete session ready for mixing

5. Mixing
   - Organize session: label all tracks, color-code by type, remove unused tracks
   - Set up bus routing (drum bus, vocal bus, instrument bus, effects returns)
   - Establish static balance (faders and pans only — no processing)
   - Process individual tracks: EQ (cut before boost), compression, saturation, time-based effects
   - Apply subtractive EQ to remove mud and resonances; additive EQ for character
   - Compress for dynamic control (2–6 dB gain reduction typical for most sources)
   - Apply reverb and delay on aux sends (not inserts) for spatial depth
   - Mix bus processing: gentle glue compression, subtle EQ, soft limiting
   - Reference against commercial tracks in the same genre (A/B comparison)
   - Check mix on multiple playback systems (monitors, headphones, car, phone speaker)
   - Print mix stems for client (vocal, drums, bass, instruments, FX — each as stereo pair)
   - Deliverable: Stereo mix (WAV 24-bit at session sample rate), mix stems, recall notes

6. Mastering
   - Evaluate mix for issues before mastering (frequency balance, dynamics, stereo image)
   - Apply mastering EQ for tonal balance and brightness/warmth
   - Apply mastering compression for glue and dynamic control (1–3 dB gain reduction)
   - Limit to target loudness (aim for -14 LUFS integrated for streaming, -1 dBTP ceiling)
   - Check mono compatibility (important for phone speakers and club systems)
   - Apply stereo enhancement if needed (subtle; avoid phasiness)
   - Add metadata: ISRC code, track title, artist name, album title, UPC
   - Create format-specific masters (CD: 44.1kHz/16-bit with dither; streaming: 44.1kHz/24-bit or higher)
   - Create vinyl pre-master if needed (adjusted EQ, limited bass width, de-ess sibilance)
   - Deliverable: Mastered files (WAV/FLAC per spec), DDP for CD, metadata documentation

7. Delivery & Documentation
   - Prepare final deliverables per distributor or label specifications
   - Document all session credits (producer, songwriter, engineer, mixer, performers)
   - Confirm songwriter splits and producer points in writing
   - Register composition with PRO (ASCAP, BMI, PRS)
   - Register sound recording with SoundExchange (or equivalent)
   - Provide ISRC codes for all unique recordings
   - Archive session files (project file, audio files, bounces, notes)
   - Deliverable: Final masters, stems, credit sheet, metadata, archived session

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Beat / instrumental demo (MP3 or WAV rough bounce for creative evaluation)
- Recorded and comped session (organized DAW session with clean takes)
- Production-complete session (fully arranged and produced, ready for mix)
- Stereo mix (WAV, 24-bit, at session sample rate)
- Mix stems (separate stereo bounces: vocals, drums, bass, instruments, FX)
- Mastered tracks (WAV/FLAC per delivery specification, with metadata)
- DDP image (for CD manufacturing)
- Stems package (multi-track stems for remix, sync licensing, or live performance)
- Session files (DAW project with all audio, for client archive or future work)
- Credit sheet (all personnel, roles, publishing information, ISRC codes)
- Metadata documentation (ISRC, UPC, songwriter splits, publisher information)
- Sound design elements (custom sounds, samples, Foley recordings for media projects)

Minimum viable: Stereo mix (WAV 24-bit) and mastered file meeting streaming platform specifications
Gold standard: Full production with mix, stems, master, alternate versions (instrumental, a cappella, radio edit, clean version), metadata, credit documentation, and archived session files

5) Quality checklist
Production Quality:
- [ ] Beat/instrumental is genre-appropriate and competitive with reference tracks
- [ ] Arrangement has dynamic contrast (sections feel distinct in energy and density)
- [ ] Transitions between sections are smooth and intentional (no abrupt jumps)
- [ ] Instrumentation supports and does not overpower the vocal or lead element
- [ ] Groove and timing feel natural (not over-quantized or robotic unless intentional)
- [ ] Sound selection is current and fits the sonic aesthetic of the project
- [ ] Song structure serves the song (not formulaic unless genre requires it)

Recording Quality:
- [ ] Recordings are clean (no unwanted noise, clicks, pops, or distortion)
- [ ] Gain staging is appropriate (peaks between -12 and -6 dBFS, no clipping)
- [ ] Vocal takes are comped for best performance (tone, pitch, timing, emotion)
- [ ] Room tone is consistent across takes (no audible environment changes)
- [ ] Bleed between microphones is acceptable (or isolated appropriately)
- [ ] Tuning and timing corrections are natural-sounding (not robotic unless intended)

Mix Quality:
- [ ] Frequency balance is even (no excessive mud, boxiness, harshness, or missing air)
- [ ] Low end is tight and translated (check on multiple systems including earbuds)
- [ ] Vocal sits clearly in the mix (intelligible lyrics, present but not detached)
- [ ] Stereo image is wide but mono-compatible (check in mono for phase issues)
- [ ] Dynamics are controlled but not crushed (transients intact, natural movement)
- [ ] Effects (reverb, delay) add depth without washing out clarity
- [ ] Mix referenced against 3+ commercial tracks in the same genre
- [ ] No audible artifacts (clicks, pops, digital distortion, plugin glitches)

Mastering Quality:
- [ ] Loudness meets target specification (-14 LUFS for streaming, -1 dBTP max)
- [ ] Tonal balance is consistent with genre expectations
- [ ] No clipping or true peak overs (check with true peak meter)
- [ ] Mono compatibility maintained (bass centered, no phase cancellation)
- [ ] Beginning and end of track have clean fades (no abrupt starts/stops unless intentional)
- [ ] Metadata embedded correctly (ISRC, title, artist, album, UPC)
- [ ] File format matches delivery specification (sample rate, bit depth, format)

Documentation & Rights:
- [ ] All credits documented (producer, songwriter, engineer, performers)
- [ ] Songwriter splits agreed and documented in writing
- [ ] Sample clearances obtained for all third-party material
- [ ] ISRC codes assigned to all unique recordings
- [ ] Composition registered with appropriate PRO
- [ ] Session files backed up in minimum 2 locations

Example 1: Beat Production — Hip-Hop Instrumental
```
TRACK: "Concrete Garden"
GENRE: Boom-bap / Lo-fi Hip-Hop
BPM: 88
KEY: D minor
TIME SIGNATURE: 4/4
DAW: Ableton Live 12
REFERENCE TRACKS: J Dilla "Donuts" aesthetic, Madlib "Bandana" textures,
The Alchemist "The Food" drum feel

DRUM PROGRAMMING:
Kick: Vinyl-sampled kick, slight saturation, low-passed at 8 kHz for warmth
  Pattern: Hits on 1, 2-and, 3-and (syncopated boom-bap pattern)
  Processing: Slight compression (3:1, 4ms attack, 100ms release), +2 dB low shelf at 60 Hz

Snare: Layered — acoustic snare sample + vinyl crackle snap
  Pattern: Beats 2 and 4 (standard backbeat), ghost note on 3-e
  Processing: Parallel compression (heavy, blended at -12 dB), high-pass at 150 Hz

Hi-Hats: Closed hat from SP-404 library, slightly detuned
  Pattern: Straight 8ths with velocity variation (accent on offbeats)
  Swing: 58% MPC-style swing for groove
  Processing: High-pass at 400 Hz, slight bit-crush for texture

Percussion: Shaker loop (vinyl rip, cleared), tambourine hit on beat 4
  Processing: Low-pass filter sweep automated across verse sections

BASS:
Rhodes bass (Keyscape), played legato, following root-5th movement in D minor
  Processing: Saturation (Decapitator, gentle drive), compression (4:1),
  mono below 120 Hz (utility plugin), side-chain ducked to kick (-3 dB, fast release)

MELODIC ELEMENTS:
Rhodes pad: Warm Fender Rhodes sample (Keyscape), Dm7 → Gm7 → Am7 → Dm7 progression
  Processing: Tremolo (rate synced to tempo), tape saturation, plate reverb (20% wet)
  Automation: Filter opens gradually through each 4-bar phrase

Vinyl texture: Continuous vinyl crackle layer at -24 dB for warmth and cohesion
  Processing: High-pass at 200 Hz, band-pass at 1 kHz with gentle resonance

Sample chop: Vocal phrase from public domain jazz recording (1940s, confirmed PD)
  Pitched down 3 semitones, reversed, used as transitional element between sections
  Processing: Heavy filtering (band-pass), granular delay, auto-pan

ARRANGEMENT:
| Section    | Bars | Duration | Elements                              |
|------------|------|----------|---------------------------------------|
| Intro      | 4    | 0:00     | Vinyl crackle + filtered Rhodes       |
| Verse 1    | 16   | 0:11     | Full beat (drums, bass, Rhodes)       |
| Hook       | 8    | 0:54     | Beat + sample chop + shaker           |
| Verse 2    | 16   | 1:19     | Full beat, hi-hat variation            |
| Hook       | 8    | 2:02     | Beat + sample chop + tambourine added |
| Bridge     | 8    | 2:26     | Drums drop, Rhodes solo + vinyl       |
| Verse 3    | 16   | 2:51     | Full beat, percussion variation        |
| Outro      | 8    | 3:34     | Elements remove one by one, fade      |
Total: 3:56
```

Example 2: Vocal Recording Session — Signal Chain and Notes
```
SESSION: "Still Water" — Lead Vocal Recording
ARTIST: [Artist Name]
STUDIO: [Studio Name], Room A (live room: 12' x 14' x 10', treated)
DATE: [Date]
ENGINEER: [Name]
PRODUCER: [Name]
DAW: Pro Tools 2024.6
SESSION: 48 kHz / 24-bit

SIGNAL CHAIN:
Microphone: Neumann U87 Ai (cardioid pattern)
  Position: 8 inches from vocalist, slightly off-axis (10° to left)
  Pop filter: Stedman Proscreen, 4 inches from capsule
  Reflection filter: None (room is well-treated)

Preamp: Neve 1073 (original)
  Gain: +38 dB
  High-pass: Engaged at 80 Hz (removes proximity effect rumble)
  EQ: Flat (no additional EQ on input)

Compressor: LA-2A (Teletronix)
  Peak Reduction: 3 (targeting 2–3 dB gain reduction on peaks)
  Gain: Adjusted for unity output
  Purpose: Gentle leveling to protect converter input

Converter: Apogee Symphony I/O
  Input level: Peaks at -10 dBFS (conservative staging for headroom)
  Clock: Internal

HEADPHONE MIX:
- Instrumental at comfortable level
- Vocal with slight plate reverb (for comfort, not printed to track)
- Low-latency monitoring via hardware (not through DAW)
- Artist requested: "More vocal in the right ear, beat in the left"

TAKES RECORDED:
| Take | Section       | Notes                                          | Rating |
|------|---------------|-------------------------------------------------|--------|
| 01   | Full song     | Guide take, good energy, pitch issues on bridge | ★★★    |
| 02   | Full song     | Better pitch, lost energy on verse 2            | ★★★    |
| 03   | Verse 1       | Punched in, excellent feel and tone             | ★★★★★  |
| 04   | Verse 2       | Three attempts, take 3 selected                 | ★★★★   |
| 05   | Chorus (all)  | Stacked 3x for comp, great power                | ★★★★★  |
| 06   | Bridge        | 4 attempts, take 2 best pitch + emotion         | ★★★★   |
| 07   | Ad-libs       | Freestyle session, 8 minutes of material         | ★★★★   |
| 08   | Doubles       | Full song double for chorus and hook             | ★★★★   |
| 09   | Harmonies     | 3rd above on chorus, 5th on bridge               | ★★★★★  |

COMP NOTES:
- Lead vocal comp: Take 03 (V1) + Take 04 (V2) + Take 05-2 (Chorus) + Take 06-2 (Bridge)
- Crossfade edits at phrase boundaries, not mid-word
- Minimal pitch correction needed: Bridge m. 33 (flat by ~30 cents), Chorus m. 22 (sharp)
- Timing: Verse 2 line 3 slightly behind — nudged forward 15ms
- De-breath: Removed breaths that are distractingly loud; preserved natural breaths in soft passages

SESSION BACKUP:
- Primary: Studio NAS (RAID 5), auto-save every 5 minutes
- Secondary: External SSD (Samsung T7), end-of-session full copy
- Cloud: Uploaded consolidated session to Dropbox overnight
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **DAW (Digital Audio Workstation)**: Software used for recording, editing, mixing, and producing audio (Pro Tools, Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Studio One)
- **LUFS (Loudness Units Full Scale)**: Standardized loudness measurement used by streaming platforms for normalization
- **dBFS (Decibels Full Scale)**: Digital audio level measurement where 0 dBFS is the maximum before clipping
- **dBTP (Decibels True Peak)**: Measurement of peak levels between samples; -1 dBTP ceiling prevents inter-sample clipping on playback
- **ISRC (International Standard Recording Code)**: Unique 12-character identifier assigned to each distinct recording
- **Stem**: A stereo audio file containing a submix of related tracks (e.g., all vocals, all drums) used for remixes, sync, and live performance
- **Gain Staging**: The practice of managing audio levels at each point in the signal chain to maintain optimal signal-to-noise ratio without clipping
- **Compressor**: Audio processor that reduces dynamic range by attenuating signals above a threshold; key parameters: threshold, ratio, attack, release
- **EQ (Equalization)**: Audio processor that adjusts the balance of frequency components; subtractive EQ removes problem frequencies, additive EQ enhances character
- **Side-Chain**: Routing one signal to control a processor on another signal (e.g., kick drum triggering bass compressor for rhythmic pumping)
- **Bus / Group**: An audio routing channel that combines multiple tracks for collective processing (drum bus, vocal bus)
- **Aux Send / Return**: Signal routing that sends a copy of audio to an effects processor (reverb, delay) and returns the wet signal to the mix
- **Dither**: Low-level noise added when reducing bit depth (e.g., 24-bit to 16-bit) to preserve audio resolution
- **PRO (Performance Rights Organization)**: Organizations that collect royalties when music is publicly performed or broadcast (ASCAP, BMI, PRS)

Standards & Frameworks:
- **AES (Audio Engineering Society)**: Standards for professional audio, including file formats and metering
- **EBU R 128**: European Broadcasting Union standard for loudness normalization (-23 LUFS for broadcast)
- **ITU-R BS.1770**: International standard for measuring audio program loudness (basis for LUFS measurement)
- **Spotify Loudness Normalization**: Targets -14 LUFS integrated; normalizes up and down
- **Apple Sound Check**: Targets -16 LUFS integrated for iTunes and Apple Music
- **Red Book (IEC 60908)**: CD audio standard (44.1 kHz, 16-bit, stereo PCM)
- **Grammy Recording Academy Guidelines**: Minimum delivery of 44.1 kHz/24-bit, recommended 96 kHz/24-bit
- **DDEX (Digital Data Exchange)**: Standards for metadata exchange in the music industry
- **DDP (Disc Description Protocol)**: Standard format for delivering audio masters to CD replication plants

Common Tools:
- **DAWs**: Pro Tools (industry standard for recording/mixing), Ableton Live (electronic/production), Logic Pro (Apple ecosystem), FL Studio (beat making), Studio One (hybrid workflow)
- **Virtual Instruments**: Omnisphere, Serum, Kontakt, Keyscape, Arturia V Collection, Native Instruments Komplete
- **Mixing Plugins**: FabFilter Pro-Q 3 (EQ), FabFilter Pro-C 2 (compressor), Waves SSL, UAD plugins, Soundtoys
- **Mastering**: FabFilter Pro-L 2 (limiter), iZotope Ozone, Weiss EQ1-DYN (UAD), Dangerous Music hardware
- **Metering**: Youlean Loudness Meter, iZotope Insight, LEVELS (Mastering the Mix)
- **Hardware**: Universal Audio Apollo (interface/DSP), Neve preamps, SSL console, Neumann/AKG microphones
- **Collaboration**: Splice (cloud session sharing), Audiomovers (remote monitoring), Source-Connect (remote recording)
- **Distribution**: DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, AWAL, Stem (for independent releases)
