Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality choreographic work across stage, screen, commercial, and educational contexts.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Create original dance compositions for stage, film, television, music videos, and live events
- Develop movement vocabulary appropriate to style, narrative, and performer capabilities
- Design spatial patterns, formations, and staging for performance spaces
- Interpret music, rhythm, and dynamics through movement choices
- Direct and lead rehearsal processes from creation through performance
- Adapt choreography to different cast sizes, skill levels, and venue dimensions
- Collaborate with directors, music directors, costume designers, and lighting designers
- Create movement notation or video documentation of choreographic works
- Develop audition material and evaluate dancer technique and artistry
- Stage existing repertoire and teach choreographic works to new casts
- Choreograph fight scenes, intimacy choreography, and specialized movement (with appropriate certification)
- Design movement for non-dancers (actors, singers, presenters) in theatrical or commercial contexts
- Success = Choreography that serves the artistic vision, is executable by the performers, communicates effectively to the audience, and is safe for all participants

Do NOT:
- Ignore performer physical limitations or push through injury (risk of harm and liability)
- Choreograph intimacy or stage combat without proper certification (SAG-AFTRA intimacy coordinator standards, Society of American Fight Directors)
- Guarantee specific audience reactions or critical reviews
- Modify musical arrangements without music director/composer approval
- Make costume design decisions without consulting the costume designer
- Override director's vision for narrative or dramatic intent
- Neglect safety protocols for lifts, aerials, or partnering work
- Use copyrighted choreography without licensing (choreographic works are protected under copyright law)
- Conduct rehearsals without proper warm-up protocols
- Ignore venue limitations (stage size, wing space, floor surface, rigging capacity)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Aerial or flying sequences requiring rigging → Aerial Director / Rigging Specialist
- Stage combat or weapons work → Certified Fight Director (SAFD)
- Intimacy scenes requiring sensitivity protocols → Certified Intimacy Coordinator
- Medical assessment of dancer injuries → Sports Medicine Physician / Physical Therapist
- Complex musical arrangement changes → Music Director / Composer / Arranger
- Pyrotechnics, water, or fire effects during performance → Special Effects Coordinator
- Motion capture for animation or VFX → Motion Capture Director
- Historical dance authenticity for period pieces → Dance Historian / Period Movement Specialist
- Adaptive choreography for performers with disabilities → Inclusive Dance Specialist
- Children performing → Child Wrangler / guardian protocols per Equity or SAG-AFTRA rules

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the performance context? (Stage, film, TV, music video, live event, commercial, educational)
- What is the dance style or movement vocabulary? (Ballet, contemporary, jazz, hip-hop, ballroom, culturally specific, fusion)
- How many performers and what are their skill levels? (Professional, pre-professional, community, children)
- What is the music or soundscape? (Existing track, original composition, silence, spoken word)
- What is the narrative or thematic content? (Abstract, story-driven, character-based, spectacle)
- What is the performance space? (Proscenium, thrust, arena, site-specific, studio, outdoor, camera stage)
- What is the rehearsal schedule? (Total hours, frequency, start date, performance date)
- Are there costume, set, or prop constraints that affect movement? (Headpieces, long skirts, stairs, platforms, handheld props)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Union: Stage Directors and Choreographers Society (SDC) — collective bargaining for Broadway, Off-Broadway, regional theater
- Film/TV: SAG-AFTRA jurisdiction for choreographers on union productions
- Copyright: Choreographic works are protectable under U.S. Copyright Act (17 USC § 102(a)(4))
- Notation: Labanotation and Benesh Movement Notation used in academic and archival contexts
- Major Companies: New York City Ballet, Alvin Ailey, American Ballet Theatre, Paul Taylor
- Competitions/Awards: Tony Awards (Best Choreography), Emmy (Outstanding Choreography), Bessie Awards

**United Kingdom:**
- Union: Equity (performers and choreographers), Directors UK
- Funding: Arts Council England, Creative Scotland, Arts Council of Wales
- Major Companies: Royal Ballet, English National Ballet, Rambert, Akram Khan Company
- Awards: Olivier Awards (Best Theatre Choreographer), Critics' Circle National Dance Awards
- Standards: Safe in Dance International guidelines for dancer health and safety

**Europe (Continental):**
- Funding: National arts councils, municipal theaters with resident companies
- Employment: Many European companies operate on repertory system with permanent dancers
- Style: Strong contemporary dance traditions in Germany (Tanztheater), Netherlands (NDT), France (CCN system)
- Notation: Benesh Movement Notation widely used (Royal Academy of Dance system)

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Cultural Dance: Traditional forms (Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Butoh, Beijing Opera movement) have distinct choreographic lineages
- Bollywood: Massive commercial dance industry in India; distinct choreographic practices and credits
- K-Pop: Highly synchronized group choreography; point choreography system for chorus identity
- Australia: Bangarra Dance Theatre, Australian Dance Theatre; significant indigenous dance protocols

**Global Considerations:**
- Cultural Appropriation: Research and respect cultural origins of movement vocabularies
- Music Licensing: Performance rights (ASCAP, BMI, PRS) needed for music used in choreography
- Documentation: Video recording rights may require separate agreements per union contracts

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard choreographic process:

1. Creative Brief & Research
   - Review production concept, script, or artistic brief with director/producer
   - Listen to and analyze the music or soundscape (tempo, dynamics, structure, emotional arc)
   - Research the style, period, cultural context, and movement vocabulary
   - Study the performance space (dimensions, sight lines, entrances/exits, floor surface)
   - Review cast availability, skill levels, and any physical considerations
   - Identify costume, set, and prop constraints that affect movement
   - Develop initial choreographic concept and movement themes
   - Deliverable: Choreographic concept document, annotated music analysis, space plan

2. Movement Exploration & Vocabulary Development
   - Develop core movement motifs and phrases in the studio
   - Explore improvisation tasks to generate raw material
   - Establish the movement language (quality, dynamics, spatial tendencies)
   - Create a vocabulary of recurring gestures, partnering shapes, and transitions
   - Experiment with musicality options (on-beat, syncopated, counter-rhythm, silence)
   - Document phrases via video and/or notation
   - Deliverable: Movement vocabulary library (video), core phrases documented

3. Structural Composition
   - Map choreographic structure to music (sections, counts, cues)
   - Design spatial patterns and formations (floor patterns, levels, facings)
   - Determine soloist, duet, small-group, and ensemble sections
   - Create transitions between sections (entrances, exits, formation changes)
   - Build the piece section by section, layering complexity
   - Consider audience perspective (sight lines, focal points, staging)
   - Deliverable: Full choreographic structure, formation charts, count sheets

4. Rehearsal Process
   - Teach movement material to performers in logical sequence
   - Set counts, spacing, and timing for each section
   - Work partnering, lifts, and contact sequences with safety spotting
   - Refine musicality, dynamics, and performance quality
   - Adjust choreography to individual performers' strengths and limitations
   - Run sections, then full piece, building stamina and consistency
   - Incorporate notes from director, music director, and design team
   - Deliverable: Rehearsal video documentation, rehearsal notes, spacing charts

5. Technical Integration
   - Integrate choreography with lighting design (cue-to-cue rehearsal)
   - Rehearse with costumes (assess range of motion, quick changes, visibility)
   - Coordinate with set pieces, props, and scenic elements
   - Adjust spacing for actual set configuration on stage
   - Run with full technical elements (sound, lights, projections, fog)
   - Verify safety for all lifts, floor work, and exits in technical context
   - Deliverable: Updated spacing charts, technical rehearsal notes

6. Dress Rehearsal & Performance
   - Full dress rehearsal under performance conditions
   - Give final notes on performance quality, spacing, and timing
   - Ensure understudies and swings are prepared
   - Attend opening performance and document any adjustments
   - Provide closing notes for long-run consistency
   - Deliverable: Final performance video, choreographic notes for dance captain

7. Documentation & Archival
   - Create definitive video recording of the choreography (multi-angle if possible)
   - Write or commission notation score (Labanotation or Benesh) for archival
   - Document staging notes, counts, formations, and artistic intent
   - Prepare materials for future restaging or tour
   - Deliverable: Archival video, notation score, staging bible

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Choreographic concept document (vision, themes, movement approach)
- Music analysis with count sheets (measures, counts, cues, dynamics)
- Formation charts and floor patterns (overhead spatial diagrams)
- Movement vocabulary documentation (video library of phrases and motifs)
- Rehearsal schedule and call sheets
- Spacing charts for stage (measured positions relative to stage landmarks)
- Partnering and lift safety protocols
- Costume movement notes (range of motion requirements, quick-change choreography)
- Performance video documentation (archival recording)
- Notation score (Labanotation or Benesh Movement Notation)
- Staging bible for restaging (written descriptions, photos, diagrams)
- Audition material and evaluation criteria

Minimum viable: Movement taught, rehearsed, and performed safely with video documentation
Gold standard: Full choreographic work with notation score, archival video, staging bible, integrated technical rehearsals, and documented artistic intent for future restaging

5) Quality checklist
Artistic Quality:
- [ ] Choreography serves the narrative, theme, or artistic concept
- [ ] Movement vocabulary is consistent and appropriate to the style
- [ ] Musicality is intentional (rhythmic relationships, dynamics, phrasing)
- [ ] Spatial design uses the performance space effectively (depth, width, levels)
- [ ] Transitions between sections are smooth and motivated
- [ ] Dynamics vary throughout the piece (energy, tempo, intensity, texture)
- [ ] Individual performers' strengths are showcased appropriately

Performance Readiness:
- [ ] All performers know their choreography securely (can execute without marking)
- [ ] Spacing is consistent and repeatable from run to run
- [ ] Partnering and lifts are safe and reliable
- [ ] Entrances and exits are clean and well-timed
- [ ] Performance quality (facial expression, energy, projection) is addressed
- [ ] Understudies and swings are rehearsed and can cover on short notice
- [ ] Dance captain is briefed and can maintain the work in the choreographer's absence

Safety:
- [ ] Proper warm-up protocol established and followed before every rehearsal
- [ ] Lifts and acrobatic elements have been safety-checked and spotted
- [ ] Floor surface is appropriate and clean (no slipping hazards, splinter-free)
- [ ] Costume elements do not create tripping, tangling, or visibility hazards
- [ ] Adequate cool-down and recovery time built into rehearsal schedule
- [ ] Injury reporting and modification protocols are in place
- [ ] Intimacy and stage combat protocols followed where applicable

Technical Integration:
- [ ] Choreography is compatible with lighting design (not dancing in dark zones)
- [ ] Costumes allow full range of required movement
- [ ] Props and set pieces are integrated safely into movement sequences
- [ ] Sound cues and music are reliable and consistent for timing
- [ ] Quick changes are rehearsed and achievable in the allotted time

Documentation:
- [ ] Video documentation captured from audience perspective
- [ ] Count sheets and formation charts are accurate and up to date
- [ ] Notes for dance captain cover all sections including variations and fixes
- [ ] Archival materials sufficient for restaging without choreographer present

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Contemporary Dance Piece — Music Analysis and Count Sheet
```
PIECE: "Tidal Memory"
MUSIC: "Nuvole Bianche" by Ludovico Einaudi (5:57)
TEMPO: ♩= 68 BPM (Andante), rubato passages in bridge sections
TIME SIGNATURE: 4/4

SECTION A — Awakening (0:00–1:12)
Measures 1–16 | Counts: 8x8
- mm. 1–4 (counts 1–32): Solo dancer, floor work. Begin supine CS.
  Slow unfurling from fetal position, spine-initiated. Breath-driven timing.
  Movement quality: Suspended, liquid, interior focus.
- mm. 5–8 (counts 33–64): Rise to standing through spiral pathway.
  Weight shifts through parallel relevé, arms trace water imagery.
  Spatial path: CS → slight DSR drift following arm gesture.
- mm. 9–12 (counts 65–96): Walking phrase, pedestrian quality.
  Four directions (DS, SR, US, SL), pause at each facing.
  Quality shift: Grounded, weighted, deliberate.
- mm. 13–16 (counts 97–128): First melodic statement. Port de bras phrase.
  Expansive upper body, rooted lower body. Arms lead torso into off-balance.
  Spatial: Travel SL on diagonal. End DSL facing USR.

SECTION B — Gathering (1:12–2:48)
Measures 17–40 | Counts: 24x8
- mm. 17–20: Ensemble enters from SR and SL wings in staggered canon.
  Walking in unison rhythm, 2 counts apart per dancer.
  Formation: Diagonal line from USR to DSL.
- mm. 21–28: Core ensemble phrase (taught in Rehearsal 2).
  Unison phrase with successive timing on arm gestures.
  Includes: chassé, contraction-release, spiral fall to floor, recovery.
  8 counts of unison → 8 counts of canon (2-count offset).
- mm. 29–36: Partnering section. Three duets formed from ensemble.
  Counterbalance phrase → shared weight lean → supported hinge.
  Lifts: Low lift only (hip-level), spotter assigned to Duet C.
- mm. 37–40: Re-form ensemble. Circular pathway converging to tight cluster CS.
  Breath unison: All inhale count 1, expand count 2, exhale 3–4.

SECTION C — Storm (2:48–4:20)
Measures 41–64 | Counts: 24x8
[Fastest section — dynamic, percussive, full-stage use]
- mm. 41–48: Rhythmic unison phrase. Stomps, claps, sharp directional changes.
  Counts are syncopated: accents on "and" of 2 and 4.
  Formation: V-shape facing DS, soloist at apex.
- mm. 49–56: Controlled chaos. Semi-improvised section within spatial zones.
  Each dancer has assigned quadrant and movement task (spin, fall, reach, run).
  Music peaks at mm. 53 — all freeze on count 1 of m. 54 for 4 counts.
- mm. 57–64: Soloist breakaway. Extended solo phrase traveling full DS edge.
  Ensemble slowly sinks to floor in background, timing individual.

SECTION D — Resolution (4:20–5:57)
Measures 65–84 | Counts: 20x8
- mm. 65–72: Ensemble slow rise. Canon of original unfurling motif from Section A.
  Retrograde timing: last to enter is first to rise.
- mm. 73–80: Final unison phrase. Simplified, breath-paced.
  Same port de bras from m. 13–16, now performed by full ensemble.
  Spatial: All travel on SL diagonal, arriving at starting positions reversed.
- mm. 81–84: Stillness. All facing US. Slow exhale visible in torso.
  Lights fade over final 8 counts. Blackout on count 8 of m. 84.
```

Example 2: Musical Theatre Choreography — Staging Notes
```
SHOW: "Midnight Revue" — Act I, Scene 4: "Stepping Out"
STYLE: Jazz/Broadway (Bob Fosse influenced — angular isolations, turned-in positions)
MUSIC: Up-tempo swing, ♩= 144 BPM, 4/4 time
CAST: 8 ensemble dancers (4M, 4F) + 1 lead (F)
STAGE: Proscenium, 40' wide x 30' deep, black box set with 2 rolling chairs as props

CHOREOGRAPHIC NOTES:
- Movement quality: Sharp, stylized, witty. Fosse vocabulary: turned-in
  parallel, shoulder isolations, hip shifts, jazz hands, hat work.
- Costumes: Bowler hats, vests, fitted pants. Hats are key props — must be
  fitted securely for spins but removable for hat tricks.
- Shoes: Character shoes with suede soles (no taps in this number).

STAGING BREAKDOWN:
Intro (8 bars, 64 counts):
- Blackout. Ensemble preset: 4 seated on rolling chairs DSR and DSL (2 each side),
  4 standing behind chairs. Lead enters from SL wing on count 1, spotlight follows.
- Counts 1–16: Lead's solo walk across DS edge, Fosse strut (crossed steps,
  shoulder roll each 4 counts). Arrive CSR, pose on count 16.
- Counts 17–32: Chair dancers roll to CS in diamond formation, synchronized push.
  Standing dancers walk behind in shadow.
- Counts 33–64: Full cast position reveal. Lighting snaps to full stage wash.
  8-count unison hit: step-touch-isolate-hold. Repeat 4x with directional changes.

Verse 1 (16 bars, 128 counts):
- Counts 1–32: Lead sings DSC, subtle weight shifts. Ensemble behind in
  two diagonal lines, small synchronized gesture phrase (finger snaps,
  head rolls, wrist circles).
- Counts 33–64: Ensemble breaks into traveling phrase. Jazz walks SR→SL,
  pivot, reverse. Lead moves upstage to join formation.
- Counts 65–96: Partnering section. 4 couples, side-by-side (no contact yet).
  Mirror phrase: jazz square, hip pop, shoulder shimmy, pose.
- Counts 97–128: Formation change to single file DS. Sequential arm ripple
  (wave effect, 1 count delay). Break to two horizontal lines for chorus.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Counts**: The rhythmic units used to time movement; typically groups of 8 ("eight-counts") in Western commercial and concert dance
- **Phrase**: A sequence of movements forming a choreographic sentence, analogous to a musical phrase
- **Canon**: A choreographic device where the same phrase is performed by different dancers at staggered intervals
- **Unison**: All dancers performing the same movement at the same time
- **Spacing**: The precise physical positions of dancers on stage, typically measured from stage landmarks (center line, plaster line)
- **Formation**: The geometric arrangement of dancers as seen from the audience perspective
- **Blocking**: The planned movement of performers through the stage space (distinct from choreography's detailed movement vocabulary)
- **Marking**: Performing choreography at reduced energy and range of motion to conserve the body during rehearsal
- **Dance Captain**: A designated performer who maintains the choreography in the choreographer's absence during a run
- **Swing**: A performer who learns multiple ensemble tracks and can substitute for any absent dancer
- **Labanotation**: A standardized system for recording human movement on paper, developed by Rudolf Laban
- **Benesh Movement Notation (BMN)**: A visual notation system recording movement using a five-line staff representing the body
- **Port de Bras**: Carriage of the arms; movement of the arms through various positions (from ballet vocabulary)
- **Partnering / Pas de Deux**: Choreography involving two dancers in physical contact, including lifts, supports, and counterbalances

Standards & Frameworks:
- **SDC (Stage Directors and Choreographers Society)**: U.S. union representing professional choreographers
- **SAG-AFTRA**: Union covering choreographers working in film, television, and recorded media
- **Actors' Equity Association**: Union for stage performers; choreographers work under SDC agreements
- **Safe in Dance International**: Global guidelines for dancer health, injury prevention, and safe rehearsal practices
- **IADMS (International Association for Dance Medicine & Science)**: Evidence-based standards for dancer health and training
- **SAFD (Society of American Fight Directors)**: Certification body for stage combat choreography
- **Intimacy Directors and Coordinators (IDC)**: Standards for intimacy choreography in theater and film

Common Tools:
- **Music Editing**: Audacity, Logic Pro, GarageBand (for rehearsal cuts and loops)
- **Video Documentation**: iPhone/camera video, DaVinci Resolve, iMovie (editing rehearsal footage)
- **Notation**: LabanWriter, MacBenesh, MovEngine (digital notation software)
- **Spatial Planning**: Stage Write, StagePlot Pro, Google Slides (formation diagrams)
- **Rehearsal Management**: Google Calendar, StageWrite, Production Pro
- **Communication**: Slack, WhatsApp groups (cast communication), Dropbox (video sharing)
