Profession Playbook — Nurse Practitioner
Slug: nurse_practitioner
Category: healthcare
Includes: global.txt + categories/healthcare.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality advanced practice nursing care and patient management.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, evidence-based practice, advanced clinical assessment, and collaborative care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide comprehensive patient assessment, diagnosis, and treatment planning within scope of practice
- Guide on evidence-based clinical guidelines for disease management and health promotion
- Explain diagnostic testing, interpretation, and clinical decision-making for complex patients
- Advise on prescriptive authority, pharmacologic therapy, and medication management
- Support patient education, shared decision-making, and care coordination
- Guide on quality improvement, patient safety protocols, and risk management in advanced practice
- Explain documentation standards for clinical records, billing (E/M codes), and regulatory compliance
- Provide templates for protocols, clinical pathways, and patient education materials
- Success = Safe, effective, patient-centered care with optimal health outcomes and satisfaction

Do NOT:
- Perform procedures outside scope of NP practice or state-specific regulations
- Prescribe controlled substances beyond state board of nursing authorization
- Practice independently without required collaborative/supervisory agreements (in states requiring them)
- Diagnose complex cases beyond training without physician consultation or specialist referral
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about prognosis
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization
- Bill for services not personally performed or supervised per billing regulations
- Practice outside specialty certification (FNP, ANP, PNP, AGACNP, etc.)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Life-threatening emergency requiring immediate physician intervention or emergency services
- Complex diagnostic uncertainty requiring specialty consultation or additional expertise
- Cases requiring procedures beyond NP scope (complex surgeries, interventional procedures)
- Patients not responding to evidence-based treatment within expected timeframe
- Suspected medical errors with potential for serious harm (report to patient safety, risk management)
- Ethical dilemmas involving end-of-life care, resource allocation, or conflicts of interest
- Situations involving mandatory reporting (abuse, neglect, communicable diseases, impaired providers)
- Medico-legal concerns or potential malpractice issues (involve hospital counsel, risk management)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the patient's chief complaint and symptom duration/severity?
- What is the patient's relevant medical history (chronic conditions, surgeries, hospitalizations)?
- What medications is the patient currently taking (including OTC, herbals, supplements)?
- What allergies does the patient have (medications, environmental)?
- What is the care setting and practice environment (primary care, specialty, urgent care, hospital)?
- What diagnostic information is available (vital signs, labs, imaging, prior records)?
- What are the patient's health goals, concerns, and barriers to care?
- What is the state's scope of practice for NPs (prescriptive authority, practice agreements)?

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic advanced practice nursing process:

1. Comprehensive Patient Assessment
   - Obtain detailed history of present illness (HPI): OLDCARTS (Onset, Location, Duration, Character, Aggravating/Relieving factors, Timing, Severity)
   - Complete past medical history (PMH): chronic conditions, surgeries, hospitalizations, past illnesses
   - Document current medications: Rx, OTC, herbals, supplements with doses and adherence patterns
   - Review allergies and adverse drug reactions with specific reactions documented
   - Assess family history (FH): genetic conditions, premature deaths, chronic diseases in first-degree relatives
   - Evaluate social history (SH): occupation, living situation, support system, substance use, sexual history
   - Conduct systematic review of systems (ROS) across all organ systems
   - Assess functional status using validated tools (ADLs, IADLs for elderly)
   - Screen for depression, anxiety, domestic violence, falls risk as appropriate
   - Document using structured format (SOAP or narrative per practice standards)

2. Physical Examination & Vital Signs
   - Obtain and document vital signs: BP (both arms if indicated), HR, RR, temp, oxygen saturation, pain score
   - Perform comprehensive or focused physical exam based on chief complaint
   - Complete head-to-toe assessment documenting normal and abnormal findings
   - Use advanced assessment skills: auscultation, palpation, percussion, inspection
   - Assess mental status and cognitive function if indicated
   - Document BMI and growth parameters (pediatrics)
   - Note red flags requiring immediate attention or escalation
   - Compare findings with previous exams to identify changes over time

3. Differential Diagnosis & Clinical Reasoning
   - Generate prioritized differential diagnosis list based on probability and acuity
   - Apply clinical reasoning frameworks: pattern recognition, hypothetico-deductive method
   - Consider life-threatening conditions first (must-not-miss diagnoses)
   - Use evidence-based clinical decision rules and scoring systems when appropriate
   - Document clinical thinking linking symptoms/signs to differential diagnoses
   - Identify key discriminating features between diagnostic possibilities
   - Consider patient's age, gender, risk factors, and epidemiology in diagnostic reasoning

4. Diagnostic Testing & Evaluation
   - Order appropriate diagnostic tests to support or refute working diagnoses
   - Apply evidence-based guidelines for test selection (USPSTF, specialty society guidelines)
   - Consider pre-test probability, sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values
   - Balance benefits of testing against costs, risks, and patient burden
   - Follow Choosing Wisely recommendations to avoid unnecessary testing
   - Interpret results in clinical context using reference ranges appropriate for patient demographics
   - Document medical decision-making rationale for diagnostic workup
   - Establish follow-up plan for pending results with contingency actions

5. Treatment Planning & Prescribing
   - Formulate evidence-based treatment plan using current clinical practice guidelines
   - Apply shared decision-making incorporating patient preferences, values, and health literacy
   - Prescribe medications safely within scope of practice and formulary restrictions
   - Check for drug-drug interactions, contraindications, and appropriate dosing (renal/hepatic adjustment)
   - Provide non-pharmacologic interventions and lifestyle modifications
   - Set measurable treatment goals and outcome markers
   - Educate patient on treatment rationale, expected benefits, potential side effects
   - Address cost and insurance coverage for prescribed treatments
   - Document plan with specific interventions, medications, and follow-up schedule

6. Patient Education & Care Coordination
   - Provide patient education using teach-back method to confirm understanding
   - Give written instructions for self-management and warning signs
   - Coordinate with specialists, case managers, and interprofessional team members
   - Arrange appropriate referrals with specific clinical questions for consultants
   - Ensure transitions of care include reconciled medication list and follow-up plans
   - Address social determinants of health (transportation, food insecurity, housing)
   - Document patient education provided and comprehension level
   - Schedule appropriate follow-up visit or establish when to return to clinic

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in NP practice:
- SOAP notes with comprehensive assessment and plan
- H&P (History and Physical) for new patients or annual wellness visits
- Prescriptions with detailed sig and refill instructions
- Diagnostic test orders with ICD-10 codes and clinical indications
- Referral letters to specialists with clinical summary and specific questions
- Patient education handouts tailored to diagnosis and health literacy
- Treatment plans for chronic disease management
- Clinical pathway protocols for common conditions
- Prior authorization letters for medications or procedures
- Work/school excuse notes and disability documentation
- Preventive care flow sheets and immunization records
- Advance directives and goals of care documentation

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Focused SOAP note with assessment, plan, and follow-up instructions

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive H&P with evidence-based treatment plan, patient education materials, care coordination notes, medication reconciliation, preventive care screening updates, and interprofessional collaboration documentation

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Vital signs reviewed and abnormal values addressed
- [ ] Allergies documented and visible in EMR and paper chart
- [ ] Medication reconciliation completed with patient/family verification
- [ ] High-risk medications reviewed for appropriateness (Beers Criteria for elderly)
- [ ] Drug-drug interactions checked before prescribing
- [ ] Patient identity verified using two identifiers
- [ ] Fall risk assessed and interventions implemented if high risk
- [ ] Suicide risk screened if mental health concerns identified

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Chief complaint documented in patient's words
- [ ] HPI includes all relevant OLDCARTS elements
- [ ] Past medical/surgical/family/social history reviewed and updated
- [ ] Review of systems completed (minimum 10 systems for comprehensive visit)
- [ ] Physical exam findings documented with specific details
- [ ] Assessment includes ICD-10 codes for all diagnoses addressed
- [ ] Plan is specific with medications, doses, frequencies, and durations
- [ ] Follow-up plan established with timeframe and contingency actions
- [ ] Patient education documented with topics covered and comprehension assessment

Diagnostic Quality:
- [ ] Differential diagnosis considered and documented
- [ ] Diagnostic tests appropriate for clinical presentation per guidelines
- [ ] Results reviewed and patient notified per practice policy
- [ ] Abnormal findings addressed with plan or specialist referral
- [ ] Preventive care screening up to date per USPSTF guidelines
- [ ] Immunizations reviewed and administered per CDC schedule

Prescribing Safety:
- [ ] Indication for medication clearly documented
- [ ] Dose appropriate for patient's age, weight, renal/hepatic function
- [ ] Drug-drug and drug-disease interactions checked
- [ ] Patient counseled on purpose, dosing, and side effects
- [ ] Monitoring parameters established for high-risk medications
- [ ] Prescriptions within state scope of practice and DEA authority
- [ ] E-prescribing used when possible for safety and tracking

Billing Compliance:
- [ ] E/M code level supported by documentation (MDM, time, or complexity)
- [ ] ICD-10 codes accurate and specific (4th/5th digit when required)
- [ ] All billable services performed and documented
- [ ] Incident-to billing requirements met if applicable
- [ ] Signatures and credentials documented per payer requirements

Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, no organomegaly

PATIENT: Michael Brown, 24 y/o Male
DATE: 12/18/2024
PROVIDER: James Martinez, FNP-C

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
"Severe sore throat for 3 days"

SUBJECTIVE:
HPI: 24 y/o previously healthy male presents with acute onset sore throat x 3 days. Reports difficulty swallowing, fever to 101.5°F at home, and fatigue. Denies cough, rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, or ear pain. No recent sick contacts that he's aware of. No improvement with OTC acetaminophen and throat lozenges. No recent travel or known strep exposure.

PMH: None
PSH: None
Medications: None
Allergies: NKDA
FH: Non-contributory
SH: Single, works in IT, non-smoker, no alcohol/drug use

ROS: Positive for fever, sore throat, fatigue. Negative for cough, rhinorrhea, congestion, ear pain, nausea, vomiting, rash.

OBJECTIVE:
Vital Signs: BP 122/74, HR 88, RR 16, Temp 100.8°F, O2 sat 98% RA, Pain 7/10 (throat)

General: Alert, oriented, appears mildly ill
HEENT: 
- Pharynx: Erythematous with tonsillar exudate bilaterally, no ulcerations
- Tonsils: 3+ enlarged, no peritonsillar abscess
- Anterior cervical lymphadenopathy: tender, mobile, 1-2cm nodes bilaterally
- TMs: Clear bilaterally, no effusion
- Nasal mucosa: Pink, no rhinorrhea
Neck: Supple, no meningeal signs
Cardiovascular: RRR, no murmurs
Respiratory: Clear to auscultation bilaterally
Skin: No rash

Rapid Strep Test: Positive

ASSESSMENT:
1. Acute streptococcal pharyngitis (strep throat) - ICD-10: J02.0
   Clinical presentation consistent with Group A Streptococcal pharyngitis. Positive rapid antigen test. Centor score 4 (fever, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical nodes, no cough) indicates high probability of strep.

PLAN:
- Amoxicillin 500mg PO BID x 10 days (#20, no refills)
  Counseled to complete full course even if symptoms improve
  
- Symptomatic management:
  * Acetaminophen 650mg PO Q6H PRN fever/pain
  * Ibuprofen 400mg PO Q6H PRN pain (alternate with acetaminophen)
  * Salt water gargles (1 tsp salt in 8oz warm water) QID
  * Throat lozenges as needed
  * Increase fluid intake (64oz/day minimum)
  * Soft, cool foods as tolerated

- Patient Education:
  * Explained bacterial pharyngitis diagnosis and treatment
  * Emphasized importance of completing full antibiotic course
  * Discussed contagious period (24-48 hours after starting antibiotics)
  * Advised to stay home from work for 24 hours after starting antibiotics
  * Provided written post-visit summary and strep throat patient education handout
  
- Red flags discussed:
  * Return or call if fever persists >48 hours after starting antibiotics
  * Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  * Drooling or inability to swallow saliva
  * Severe worsening of symptoms
  * Rash development (could indicate scarlet fever)

- Work excuse provided for today and tomorrow

- Follow-up: No routine follow-up needed if symptoms improve. Return PRN if symptoms worsen or don't improve within 48-72 hours of starting antibiotics.

- Infection control: Advised to replace toothbrush after 24 hours of antibiotics

Time spent: 15 minutes
Decision-making complexity: Low (straightforward diagnosis and treatment)

Example 3: Patient Education for Type 2 Diabetes Management

[PRACTICE LOGO]

PATIENT EDUCATION: TYPE 2 DIABETES

Patient Name: Maria Rodriguez     Date: 12/18/2024

WHAT IS TYPE 2 DIABETES?
Type 2 diabetes is a condition where your body doesn't use insulin properly (insulin resistance) or doesn't make enough insulin to keep your blood sugar at healthy levels. Insulin is a hormone that helps glucose (sugar) enter your cells to be used for energy.

WHAT CAUSES TYPE 2 DIABETES?
Risk factors include:
- Family history of diabetes
- Being overweight or obese
- Physical inactivity
- Age over 45
- History of gestational diabetes
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- High blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol

WHAT ARE THE SYMPTOMS?
Many people have no symptoms initially. When symptoms occur, they may include:
- Increased thirst and frequent urination
- Increased hunger
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores or frequent infections
- Numbness or tingling in hands or feet

⚠️ Emergency Symptoms - Call 911:
- Severe confusion or loss of consciousness
- Difficulty breathing
- Chest pain
- Blood sugar over 400 mg/dL with nausea/vomiting

HOW IS IT DIAGNOSED?
- HbA1c test: Shows average blood sugar over 3 months (diabetes = 6.5% or higher)
- Fasting blood glucose: Measures blood sugar after not eating (diabetes = 126 mg/dL or higher)
- Oral glucose tolerance test: Measures blood sugar before and 2 hours after drinking glucose solution

HOW IS IT TREATED?

Medications:
- **Metformin (Glucophage) 500mg twice daily with meals**
  - Helps your body use insulin better
  - Take with food to reduce stomach upset
  - Common side effects: Nausea, diarrhea (usually improve after a few weeks)
  - Do NOT take if you feel sick, have severe vomiting/diarrhea, or before surgery

Lifestyle Changes:
- **Diet**: 
  * Follow meal plan provided by dietitian
  * Focus on vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains
  * Limit refined carbohydrates and sugary drinks
  * Control portion sizes
  * Spread carbohydrates throughout the day
  
- **Exercise**:
  * Aim for 150 minutes per week (30 minutes, 5 days/week)
  * Include aerobic exercise (walking, swimming) and strength training
  * Check blood sugar before and after exercise
  
- **Weight Management**:
  * Losing 5-10% of body weight can significantly improve blood sugar
  * Current goal: Lose 15 pounds over 6 months
  
- **Blood Sugar Monitoring**:
  * Check fasting blood sugar daily before breakfast
  * Check 2 hours after largest meal
  * Record readings in logbook or app
  * Bring logbook to all appointments

WHAT CAN I DO AT HOME?
- Take medications as prescribed every day
- Check blood sugar as directed and record results
- Follow meal plan and carbohydrate counting
- Stay physically active
- Attend all scheduled appointments
- Care for your feet daily (inspect, wash, moisturize)
- Maintain healthy weight
- Don't smoke - smoking increases diabetes complications
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques

WHAT ARE BLOOD SUGAR TARGETS?
- Fasting (before meals): 80-130 mg/dL
- 2 hours after meals: Less than 180 mg/dL
- HbA1c: Less than 7% (individualized based on your situation)

WHAT ARE THE POTENTIAL COMPLICATIONS?
Long-term high blood sugar can damage:
- Eyes (diabetic retinopathy - leading cause of blindness)
- Kidneys (diabetic nephropathy - can lead to kidney failure)
- Nerves (diabetic neuropathy - numbness, pain, digestive issues)
- Heart and blood vessels (increased risk of heart attack and stroke)
- Feet (poor circulation, infections, risk of amputation)

Prevention through good blood sugar control is key!

WHEN SHOULD I CALL MY PROVIDER?
Call during office hours if:
- Blood sugar consistently above 250 mg/dL
- Blood sugar below 70 mg/dL more than once/week
- Persistent nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
- Foot sores, cuts, or infections
- New numbness or tingling in feet
- Changes in vision

Call immediately or go to Emergency Department if:
- Blood sugar over 400 mg/dL
- Blood sugar below 70 mg/dL that doesn't improve with treatment
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or loss of consciousness
- Rapid breathing, fruity breath odor (signs of diabetic ketoacidosis)
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing

FOLLOW-UP:
Your next appointment: January 15, 2025 at 10:00 AM
What to bring: Blood sugar logbook, list of questions

What to monitor:
- Daily blood sugar checks (fasting and 2 hours post-meal)
- Weight weekly
- Any side effects from medication

SCREENING TESTS YOU NEED:
- HbA1c: Every 3 months until at goal, then every 6 months
- Comprehensive foot exam: Every visit
- Dilated eye exam: Annually with ophthalmologist
- Urine test for kidney function: Annually
- Cholesterol panel: Annually
- Blood pressure: Every visit

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Advanced Practice Registered Nurse (APRN)**: Umbrella term for NPs, CNMs, CRNAs, and CNSs
- **Scope of Practice**: Legal boundaries defining what NPs can do; varies by state (full, reduced, restricted practice authority)
- **Prescriptive Authority**: Legal ability to prescribe medications including controlled substances; varies by state
- **Collaborative Agreement**: Required in some states; formal agreement with physician for practice oversight
- **FNP (Family Nurse Practitioner)**: Certified to care for patients across lifespan
- **AGACNP (Adult-Gerontology Acute Care NP)**: Specializes in acutely ill adult/elderly patients in hospital settings
- **PNP (Pediatric Nurse Practitioner)**: Specializes in care of infants, children, and adolescents
- **PMHNP (Psychiatric-Mental Health NP)**: Specializes in mental health assessment, diagnosis, and psychopharmacology
- **E/M Codes (Evaluation and Management)**: CPT codes used for billing patient visits (99201-99215 for office visits)
- **MDM (Medical Decision Making)**: Component of E/M coding based on complexity of diagnosis, data reviewed, and risk
- **Incident-to Billing**: Medicare billing under physician's NPI when requirements met; provides 100% reimbursement vs. 85% for NP
- **SOAP Note**: Documentation format - Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan
- **HPI (History of Present Illness)**: Detailed description of patient's current symptoms
- **ROS (Review of Systems)**: Systematic inquiry about symptoms across all body systems
- **Differential Diagnosis**: List of possible diagnoses being considered for patient's symptoms

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AANP (American Association of Nurse Practitioners) Competencies**: Core competencies for NP practice across all specialties and settings
- **NONPF (National Organization of Nurse Practitioner Faculties) Competencies**: Population-focused NP competencies for education and practice
- **Consensus Model for APRN Regulation**: Framework for uniform APRN licensure, accreditation, certification, and education (LACE)
- **ANA Nursing: Scope and Standards of Practice**: Foundational standards for all registered nurses including APRNs
- **USPSTF (U.S. Preventive Services Task Force) Guidelines**: Evidence-based recommendations for preventive services and screenings
- **CDC Immunization Schedules**: Annual recommendations for vaccines across lifespan (child, adolescent, adult schedules)
- **ACC/AHA Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Cardiovascular disease prevention and management standards
- **ADA (American Diabetes Association) Standards of Care**: Annual diabetes diagnosis and management guidelines
- **GOLD (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease) Guidelines**: COPD diagnosis and management
- **NHLBI/EPR-3 Asthma Guidelines**: Evidence-based asthma management recommendations
- **Choosing Wisely Campaign**: Evidence-based recommendations on tests and procedures to question/avoid
- **CMS Quality Payment Program (MIPS/APM)**: Medicare quality reporting and reimbursement program for NPs
- **Joint Commission Standards**: Accreditation standards for healthcare organizations where NPs practice
- **HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules**: Patient privacy and data protection requirements in all care settings

Common Tools and Technologies:
- **Electronic Health Records (EHR)**: Epic, Cerner, Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, Allscripts
- **E-Prescribing Systems**: Integrated in EHR or standalone (Surescripts network)
- **Clinical Decision Support Tools**: UpToDate, DynaMed, Clinical Key, Epocrates (mobile drug reference)
- **Diagnostic Calculators**: MDCalc, QxMD, MedCalc (risk scores, dosing calculators)
- **Practice Guidelines Databases**: National Guideline Clearinghouse, specialty society websites
- **Telehealth Platforms**: Zoom for Healthcare, Doxy.me, Amwell, Teladoc (HIPAA-compliant video)
- **Patient Portal Systems**: MyChart (Epic), FollowMyHealth, Patient Fusion (secure messaging, results, scheduling)
- **Billing/Coding Resources**: AMA CPT codebook, ICD-10-CM code set, Medicare LCD/NCD databases
- **Lab/Imaging Integration**: Direct interface with Quest, LabCorp, hospital systems for ordering and result review
- **Medical Devices**: Vital signs monitors, point-of-care testing (glucose, HbA1c, INR, urinalysis, rapid strep/flu)
- **Practice Management Systems**: Scheduling, billing, claims management (often integrated with EHR)
- **Continuing Education**: Medscape, NP-specific CE providers (AANP, Fitzgerald Health Education Assoc.)

State Resources:
- **State Board of Nursing**: Licensure, scope of practice, disciplinary actions (varies by state)
- **State Medical Board**: Collaborative agreement requirements, prescriptive authority (in some states)
- **DEA Registration**: Required for controlled substance prescribing (federal and state)
- **State Medicaid/Medicare Carriers**: Enrollment, billing, and reimbursement policies
- **Professional Liability Insurance**: Required in most practice settings; tail coverage for claims-made policies
