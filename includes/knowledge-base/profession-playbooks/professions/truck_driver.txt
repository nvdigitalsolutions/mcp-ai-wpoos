Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for commercial truck drivers (CDL Class A/B), containing actionable  
  instructions, SOPs, checklists, and templates for safe freight transportation, HOS compliance, and ELD usage.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety, FMCSA compliance, load securement, Hours of Service management, and professional conduct.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Operate commercial trucks safely with valid CDL (Class A for tractor-trailers, Class B for straight trucks)
- Conduct mandatory pre-trip and post-trip vehicle inspections per FMCSA 49 CFR Part 396
- Manage Hours of Service (HOS) compliance using Electronic Logging Devices (ELDs) per 49 CFR Part 395
- Secure cargo properly using appropriate load securement techniques (FMCSA 49 CFR Part 393)
- Navigate efficiently using trip planning, GPS, and routing tools while monitoring weather/road conditions
- Maintain accurate records (logbook, fuel receipts, bills of lading, inspection reports)
- Report mechanical defects, accidents, and incidents promptly to dispatch/carrier
- Submit to DOT drug and alcohol testing per FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse requirements
- Practice defensive driving and follow all traffic laws
- Success = Safe, on-time delivery with zero accidents, HOS compliance, and professional conduct

Do NOT:
- Operate without valid CDL or proper endorsements (H for hazmat, T for tankers, X for both)
- Exceed Hours of Service limits (11-hour drive limit, 14-hour work window, 60/70-hour weekly limit)
- Drive with critical safety defects (brakes, steering, lights, tires below legal minimums)
- Falsify logbooks or ELD records (federal offense with severe penalties)
- Transport hazardous materials without proper placarding and hazmat endorsement
- Ignore load securement requirements (risk of cargo shift, rollovers, falling cargo)
- Drive under influence of alcohol or drugs (zero tolerance, immediate disqualification)
- Use handheld electronic devices while operating (texting/calling without hands-free)
- Bypass weigh stations or DOT inspection stations

Escalate / refer out when:
- Mechanical breakdown requires roadside service or tow (contact dispatch, arrange repair)
- Accident occurs with injuries, fatalities, or significant property damage (call 911, secure scene, document)
- HOS violation unavoidable due to adverse driving conditions (document thoroughly, report to carrier)
- Cargo damage discovered during delivery (document with photos, notify shipper/receiver, file claim)
- Hazmat incident or spill occurs (call CHEMTREC 1-800-424-9300, evacuate area, follow emergency response)
- DOT inspection results in out-of-service order (cannot drive until violations corrected)
- Drug/alcohol test positive or refusal (immediate removal from service, follow return-to-duty protocol)
- Medical certification expires or health condition disqualifies driving (obtain new medical exam)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of trucking operation (OTR long-haul, regional, local delivery, dedicated route)?
- What CDL class and endorsements required (Class A/B, hazmat, tanker, doubles/triples)?
- What cargo types are transported (dry van, refrigerated, flatbed, tanker, oversized)?
- What are the Hours of Service requirements (interstate vs. intrastate, short-haul exemption)?
- What load securement methods are needed (straps, chains, tarps, dunnage, edge protection)?
- What ELD system is in use (what brand, how to log in/out, transfer data)?
- What are the route requirements (states crossed, permits needed, restricted routes)?
- What documentation is required (bills of lading, delivery receipts, inspection reports)?

3) Default workflow
Use FMCSA regulations and trucking industry best practices:

1. Trip Planning & Preparation
   - Review load assignment (pickup/delivery locations, cargo details, appointment times)
   - Verify CDL, medical certificate, and endorsements valid and current
   - Check ELD functionality (proper login, vehicle connection, data synchronized)
   - Plan route considering HOS availability, truck restrictions, weigh station locations
   - Review weather forecast and road conditions (winter weather, construction zones)
   - Ensure adequate rest before starting shift (10 consecutive hours off-duty required)
   - Gather documentation (bills of lading, permits, emergency contact numbers)

2. Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection (FMCSA 49 CFR Part 396.13)
   - Conduct systematic walk-around inspection (follow CDL test procedure)
   - Engine compartment: fluid levels, belts, hoses, leaks
   - Air brake system: pressure build (95-125 psi), leak test, warning devices functional
   - Wheels/tires: tread depth (4/32" minimum steer, 2/32" other), inflation, damage, lug nuts secure
   - Lights/reflectors: brake lights, turn signals, markers, reflective tape
   - Coupling: fifth wheel securely locked, kingpin undamaged, air/electrical lines connected
   - Cargo securement: load properly distributed, secured per 49 CFR Part 393, not overweight
   - Emergency equipment: fire extinguisher, warning triangles, spare fuses
   - Documentation: complete written inspection report, note defects, place out of service if critical

3. Driving Operations & HOS Management
   - Log status changes accurately in ELD (driving, on-duty not driving, sleeper berth, off-duty)
   - Monitor HOS clock continuously (drive time remaining, 14-hour window, 70-hour weekly limit)
   - Take required 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving (short-haul exempt)
   - Practice defensive driving (maintain following distance, scan mirrors, anticipate hazards)
   - Adjust speed for conditions (weather, traffic, road surface, visibility)
   - Weigh vehicle at scales, obtain permits as required by states
   - Manage fuel efficiently (plan fuel stops, monitor fuel economy, idle reduction)
   - Communicate with dispatch (on-time delivery updates, delays, issues)

4. Loading, Securement & Delivery
   - Inspect cargo before loading (damage, quantity correct, proper handling required)
   - Verify weight distribution (avoid overloading axles, maintain proper balance)
   - Apply load securement per FMCSA standards (number of tie-downs by weight, working load limits)
   - Re-check securement after first 50 miles and periodically during trip
   - Use tarps/protection for weather-sensitive or uncovered loads
   - Document delivery (obtain signatures, note exceptions/damages, take photos if needed)
   - Obtain bills of lading or delivery receipts as proof of delivery

5. Post-Trip & Record Keeping
   - Complete post-trip inspection, report any new defects or damage
   - Submit all required documentation (BOLs, fuel receipts, weigh tickets, inspection reports)
   - Review ELD logs for accuracy, make annotations as needed
   - Clean cab and trailer (remove trash, organize paperwork)
   - Park securely (well-lit area, trailer doors locked, air line disconnected)
   - Plan next day's rest and trip requirements

4) Deliverables
- Daily Vehicle Inspection Reports (DVIR) - pre-trip and post-trip documentation
- Hours of Service (HOS) logs via ELD - complete driving records with duty status changes
- Bills of Lading (BOL) - cargo documentation with pickup/delivery signatures
- Load securement documentation - photos of properly secured cargo
- Fuel receipts and expense reports - IFTA fuel tax documentation
- Accident/incident reports - detailed documentation of any safety events
- Weigh station tickets and permit documentation
- Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse compliance records
- Delivery appointment confirmations and proof of delivery

Minimum viable: Safe, on-time delivery with clean DVIR, HOS compliance, accurate documentation
Gold standard: Exemplary safety record (no accidents/violations), fuel efficiency leadership, customer commendations, mentor to new drivers

5) Quality checklist

Safety & Compliance:
- [ ] Valid CDL with appropriate class and endorsements, medical certificate current
- [ ] Pre-trip inspection completed and documented, no critical defects
- [ ] HOS limits followed (11 driving, 14 on-duty, 60/70 weekly, 30-min break)
- [ ] ELD functioning properly, all duty status changes logged accurately
- [ ] Cargo properly secured per FMCSA Part 393, weight limits not exceeded
- [ ] Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse status current

Operational Excellence:
- [ ] Route planned efficiently, appointments met or delays communicated proactively
- [ ] Defensive driving practiced (safe following distance, speed for conditions)
- [ ] Fuel management optimized (efficient routing, idle reduction)
- [ ] Vehicle maintenance concerns reported promptly
- [ ] DOT inspections passed without violations

Documentation & Communication:
- [ ] BOLs accurate and signed, delivery exceptions documented
- [ ] Inspection reports complete and submitted
- [ ] Expense documentation (fuel receipts, weigh tickets) maintained
- [ ] Dispatch communications timely and professional
- [ ] Customer interactions courteous and professional

Example 2: HOS Management - Adverse Driving Conditions Exception
```
HOURS OF SERVICE - Adverse Driving Conditions Exception Invoked
Date: 2024-12-18 | Driver: Martinez, Ana | CDL: A-67890-CA | Truck #5521

SITUATION:
Encountered unexpected severe weather conditions that were not apparent at trip start.
Original ETA to delivery: 22:00 (with 11 hours drive time available)
Adverse conditions extended drive time, requiring HOS extension.

ADVERSE CONDITIONS DOCUMENTED:
- Heavy snowfall began at 14:30 MT (Highway 70 through Colorado Rockies)
- Visibility reduced to less than 200 feet
- Road conditions: snow-covered, multiple vehicles in ditches
- Speed reduced from 65 mph to 25-30 mph for safety
- Colorado DOT issued travel advisory (no travel recommended for light vehicles)

HOS TIMELINE:
- Started shift: 08:00 (after 10 hours off-duty)
- Reached adverse conditions: 14:30 (6.5 hours driving)
- 14-hour window expires: 22:00
- Delivery location: 2 hours driving remaining at normal speed
- With adverse conditions: 4+ hours driving needed

DECISION:
Invoked adverse driving conditions exception (FMCSA 49 CFR 395.1(b)(1))
- Extended drive time by 2 additional hours (13 hours total driving)
- Extended on-duty time to 16-hour window
- Delivered safely at 23:45 (within extended limits)

ELD ANNOTATION:
"Adverse driving conditions - heavy snow, reduced visibility, CO Hwy 70.
Extended drive time to 13 hrs and duty day to 16 hrs per 395.1(b)(1).
Conditions not foreseeable at trip start. Safe delivery prioritized."

DOCUMENTATION:
- ELD logs with annotation
- Weather.gov screenshots showing snowfall advisory
- Colorado DOT travel advisory notification
- Photo of road conditions (timestamp 15:20)
- Dispatch communication log confirming conditions

COMPLIANCE:
Exception properly invoked per FMCSA guidelines. Cannot use exception again until after next 10-hour rest period.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **CDL (Commercial Driver's License)**: Federal license for commercial vehicles - Class A (tractor-trailers), Class B (straight trucks)
- **HOS (Hours of Service)**: FMCSA regulations limiting driving/on-duty time (11-hour drive, 14-hour window, 60/70-hour weekly)
- **ELD (Electronic Logging Device)**: Mandated device for automatic HOS recording (FMCSA compliant)
- **DVIR (Driver Vehicle Inspection Report)**: Daily pre-trip and post-trip written inspection documentation
- **FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration)**: DOT agency regulating commercial motor vehicle safety
- **BOL (Bill of Lading)**: Cargo documentation showing shipper, receiver, commodity, weight
- **GVW (Gross Vehicle Weight)**: Total weight of vehicle + cargo (80,000 lbs interstate limit)
- **WLL (Working Load Limit)**: Minimum strength rating for load securement equipment (chains, straps)
- **CSA (Compliance, Safety, Accountability)**: FMCSA system tracking carrier/driver safety performance
- **Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse**: FMCSA database tracking driver testing violations
- **Out of Service**: Vehicle or driver status prohibiting operation until violation corrected
- **Adverse Driving Conditions Exception**: HOS extension allowed for unforeseen weather/emergency (2-hour extension)
- **Medical Examiner's Certificate**: DOT physical required for CDL drivers (2-year validity typical)

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **49 CFR Part 383**: Commercial Driver's License Standards
- **49 CFR Part 391**: Qualifications of Drivers (medical, employment verification)
- **49 CFR Part 392**: Driving of Commercial Motor Vehicles (operational rules)
- **49 CFR Part 393**: Parts and Accessories Necessary for Safe Operation (cargo securement)
- **49 CFR Part 395**: Hours of Service of Drivers (11-hour drive limit, ELD mandate)
- **49 CFR Part 396**: Inspection, Repair, and Maintenance (DVIR requirements)
- **49 CFR Part 382**: Controlled Substances and Alcohol Use and Testing
- **49 CFR Part 397**: Transportation of Hazardous Materials (routing, placarding)
- **FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse (2020, updated 2024)**: Mandatory reporting database
- **CSA Safety Measurement System (SMS)**: BASIC categories for carrier safety scoring

Common Tools:
- ELD providers: Omnitracs, KeepTruckin, Samsara, PeopleNet, Garmin eLog
- GPS/routing: Rand McNally TND, Garmin Dezl, Trimble CoPilot, Google Maps (truck mode)
- Load boards: DAT, Truckstop.com, 123Loadboard (owner-operators)
- Weigh station apps: Drivewyze PreClear, Bestpass, NORPASS
- Inspection tools: Tire pressure gauge, tread depth gauge, flashlight, binder wrenches
- Load securement: Chains (Grade 70/80), ratchet straps, binders, edge protection, tarps
- Communication: CB radio (channel 19), Qualcomm/mobile data terminal, smartphone
