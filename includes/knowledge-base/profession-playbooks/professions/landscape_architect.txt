Profession Playbook — Landscape Architect
Slug: landscape_architect
Category: creative
Includes: global.txt + categories/creative.txt + this file

Intent
- This playbook provides comprehensive guidance for landscape architecture, site design, planting design, and outdoor space development.
- Covers residential, commercial, institutional, and public space projects.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design site plans, grading, and drainage systems for sustainable water management
- Develop comprehensive planting plans with climate-appropriate species selection
- Design hardscape elements (paving, walls, steps, structures, site furnishings)
- Plan stormwater management systems (bioswales, rain gardens, permeable paving)
- Create landscape master plans for large-scale development projects
- Prepare construction documents for landscape implementation (grading plans, planting plans, irrigation plans, lighting plans)
- Coordinate with civil engineers, architects, and other design professionals
- Specify irrigation and landscape lighting systems
- Design accessible outdoor spaces meeting ADA/AODA requirements
- Conduct site analysis including soils, topography, vegetation, hydrology, microclimates
- Prepare cost estimates and phasing plans for landscape construction
- Perform construction administration and site observation during implementation
- Success = Sustainable, beautiful outdoor spaces that function properly, meet codes, enhance the built environment, and thrive long-term

Do NOT:
- Seal plans without landscape architect license (required in most US states/Canadian provinces)
- Design structural retaining walls over 4' height without structural engineer involvement
- Ignore environmental regulations, wetlands, or protected species habitats
- Specify inappropriate plants for climate zone, soil conditions, or maintenance capacity
- Design grading that creates drainage problems or violates local codes
- Promise construction costs without proper quantity takeoffs and contractor pricing
- Overlook utility conflicts (underground power, gas, water, telecom)
- Design without understanding maintenance requirements and client capacity

Escalate / refer out when:
- Retaining walls over 4' height require structural engineering (most jurisdictions)
- Wetlands, streams, floodplains, or protected habitats involved (environmental engineer/specialist)
- Complex grading or stormwater engineering beyond basic surface drainage (civil engineer)
- Historic landscapes or preservation sites (historical landscape architect specialist)
- Specialized systems like green roofs, living walls, rooftop gardens (specialist consultant)
- Arboriculture issues requiring certified arborist (tree risk assessment, preservation plans)
- Ecological restoration requiring restoration ecologist
- Large-scale civil infrastructure (roads, utilities, major earthwork) needs civil engineer

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the project type and site location? (Residential, commercial, park, campus; urban/suburban/rural)
- What climate zone and USDA Plant Hardiness Zone? (Affects all plant selection and design strategies)
- What are site constraints? (Slopes, drainage issues, poor soils, existing vegetation, utilities)
- What functions are needed? (Recreation, circulation, stormwater management, habitat, aesthetics)
- What is the maintenance expectation and capacity? (Low/moderate/high maintenance acceptable)
- What is the project budget and timeline? (Affects scale, materials, phasing)
- Any environmental concerns or regulations? (Wetlands, tree preservation, stormwater requirements)
- What is the design aesthetic preference? (Formal/informal, native/ornamental, modern/traditional)

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Site Analysis and Inventory
   - Conduct site visit and document existing conditions (topography, vegetation, structures)
   - Survey utilities (locate underground services with utility locates)
   - Analyze soils (test for drainage, pH, compaction, composition)
   - Document sun/shade patterns and microclimates
   - Identify views, focal points, and site opportunities
   - Note constraints (slopes, wet areas, poor soils, invasive species)
   - Research zoning, setbacks, and site development regulations
   - Review environmental regulations (wetlands, tree protection, stormwater)
   - Photograph site thoroughly from multiple angles and vantage points

2. Programming and Conceptual Design
   - Meet with client to clarify program requirements and priorities
   - Develop functional diagrams showing space relationships
   - Create bubble diagrams exploring different layout options
   - Sketch preliminary concepts (2-3 options) showing overall organization
   - Develop conceptual grading and drainage strategies
   - Identify appropriate plant palette for climate and site conditions
   - Create preliminary cost estimate ranges for different concepts
   - Present concepts to client with pros/cons of each approach
   - Gather feedback and select preferred direction

3. Design Development
   - Refine selected concept with detailed design decisions
   - Create preliminary site plan showing hardscape, planting areas, structures
   - Develop grading plan with spot elevations and slope analysis
   - Design drainage system (surface and subsurface as needed)
   - Create preliminary planting plan with plant groupings
   - Design irrigation zones and water source planning
   - Specify materials (paving, walls, fences, site furnishings)
   - Develop lighting concept for safety and aesthetics
   - Create 3D visualization or perspective sketches to communicate design
   - Refine cost estimate with quantity takeoffs
   - Present design development package for client approval

4. Construction Documents
   - Prepare detailed site plan with all dimensions, materials, notes
   - Create grading and drainage plan with contours, spot elevations, drainage structures
   - Develop detailed planting plan with plant schedule (botanical/common names, sizes, quantities)
   - Design irrigation plan with zones, heads, pipe sizes, controllers, backflow prevention
   - Create lighting plan with fixture types, locations, wiring, and controls
   - Prepare construction details (wall sections, paving details, edging, steps, etc.)
   - Write specifications for materials and installation methods
   - Coordinate with consultants (structural, electrical, irrigation)
   - Submit for permits (building, grading, tree removal if required)
   - Prepare bid documents for contractor pricing

5. Bidding and Contractor Selection
   - Distribute construction documents to qualified contractors for bidding
   - Conduct pre-bid meeting and site walk with contractors
   - Answer contractor questions and issue addenda if needed
   - Review and analyze bids for completeness and accuracy
   - Assist client with contractor selection
   - Review contractor's means and methods submittals
   - Finalize contract documents

6. Construction Administration and Observation
   - Conduct preconstruction meeting with contractor and client
   - Review shop drawings and product submittals
   - Conduct site observations at key milestones (layout, grading, installation)
   - Respond to contractor RFIs (requests for information)
   - Review and approve payment applications
   - Document construction progress with photography
   - Prepare punch list of incomplete or deficient work
   - Conduct final inspection and issue certificate of substantial completion
   - Provide maintenance guidelines to client

7. Post-Construction Follow-up
   - Revisit site during first growing season to assess plant establishment
   - Provide recommendations for maintenance or adjustments
   - Document completed project with professional photography
   - Update record drawings showing as-built conditions if changes occurred

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Site analysis diagrams and written reports
- Conceptual design sketches and bubble diagrams (2-3 options)
- Design development site plan with materials and planting concept
- Construction site plan with dimensions, materials, notes (scaled 1"=10' or 1"=20')
- Grading and drainage plan with contours, spot elevations, drainage details
- Detailed planting plan with plant schedule (botanical names, common names, sizes, spacing, quantities)
- Irrigation plan showing zones, heads, pipe sizes, controllers, backflow prevention
- Lighting plan showing fixtures, locations, wiring paths, electrical requirements
- Construction details (wall sections, paving patterns, edging, steps, handrails, fences, etc.)
- Technical specifications for materials and installation methods
- Cost estimates (preliminary, design development, detailed quantity takeoff)
- Maintenance guidelines for client
- As-built record drawings (if changes occurred during construction)

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Site analysis and constraints map
- Conceptual design plan showing layout and major elements
- Preliminary planting plan with plant palette
- Basic cost estimate

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive site analysis with soils report and environmental assessment
- Multiple conceptual options with 3D visualizations
- Complete construction document set (site plan, grading, planting, irrigation, lighting, details)
- Technical specifications book
- Detailed quantity takeoff and cost estimate with phasing options
- Construction administration services through project completion
- Professional photography of completed project
- Maintenance plan with seasonal calendar

5) Quality checklist
Create a profession-specific QA gate:

Site Analysis & Planning:
- [ ] Utilities located (call 811 before digging) and shown on plans
- [ ] Existing trees surveyed and preservation plan developed if needed
- [ ] Soils tested and amendments specified if needed
- [ ] Drainage patterns analyzed and problems identified
- [ ] Zoning setbacks and building codes researched and met
- [ ] Environmental constraints identified (wetlands, steep slopes, protected species)
- [ ] Accessibility requirements identified (ADA/AODA slopes, paths, parking)
- [ ] Sun/shade patterns documented and considered in plant selection

Grading & Drainage:
- [ ] Positive drainage away from buildings (minimum 2% slope for 10')
- [ ] Slopes do not exceed 3:1 (33%) without retaining walls
- [ ] Maximum 2% cross-slope on accessible paths
- [ ] Spot elevations shown at key locations (building corners, grade changes, etc.)
- [ ] Drainage structures sized adequately (catch basins, area drains, dry wells)
- [ ] Stormwater directed to appropriate discharge points
- [ ] Erosion control measures specified for construction phase
- [ ] Grading plan coordinates with architectural foundation elevations

Planting Design:
- [ ] All plants appropriate for USDA Hardiness Zone and climate
- [ ] Plants matched to site conditions (sun/shade, wet/dry, soil pH)
- [ ] Mature sizes considered—adequate spacing for growth
- [ ] Native and/or climate-adapted species prioritized
- [ ] Invasive species avoided (check local/regional invasive lists)
- [ ] Diversity of species to avoid monocultures and disease susceptibility
- [ ] Seasonal interest considered (flowers, foliage, winter structure)
- [ ] Maintenance requirements align with client capacity
- [ ] Deer resistance considered if deer pressure exists
- [ ] Salt tolerance specified for plants near roads/parking in snow climates

Irrigation:
- [ ] Irrigation zones separate plants with different water needs
- [ ] Coverage is adequate but not redundant (avoid overwatering)
- [ ] Backflow prevention device specified and meets local codes
- [ ] Controller is programmable and appropriate for system complexity
- [ ] Water source identified and adequate pressure/flow confirmed
- [ ] Mainline and lateral pipe sizes calculated for pressure requirements
- [ ] System designed for efficient water use (drip for shrub beds, appropriate sprinkler spacing)
- [ ] Rain sensors or smart controllers specified where required by code

Hardscape & Materials:
- [ ] Paving materials appropriate for use (vehicular vs. pedestrian loads)
- [ ] Accessible routes meet ADA/AODA requirements (width, slope, surface)
- [ ] Steps have proper rise/run ratios (max 7" rise, min 11" run)
- [ ] Handrails specified where required (typically 4+ risers)
- [ ] Walls under 4' do not require structural engineer (varies by jurisdiction)
- [ ] Materials are durable and appropriate for climate (freeze/thaw resistance)
- [ ] Slip-resistant surfaces specified for walking areas
- [ ] Edging details prevent hardscape migration

Lighting:
- [ ] Adequate lighting for safety on paths, steps, entrances
- [ ] Fixtures are dark-sky compliant where required (downward-facing, shielded)
- [ ] Electrical requirements meet code (GFCI, proper conduit depths)
- [ ] Lighting zones allow separate control for efficiency
- [ ] Fixtures appropriate for wet locations (outdoor rating)
- [ ] Lamp types specified (LED recommended for efficiency)

Construction Documents:
- [ ] All dimensions are clear and not ambiguous
- [ ] North arrow and scale shown on all plans
- [ ] Plant botanical names spelled correctly (genus and species)
- [ ] Details are coordinated with plans (reference numbers match)
- [ ] Notes are clear and enforceable (avoid vague terms like "as approved")
- [ ] Specifications reference industry standards (ANSI, ASTM, etc.)
- [ ] Permitting requirements identified and addressed
- [ ] Drawings are clearly organized and easy to follow for contractors

## Planting Notes
### Trees
- Dig planting hole 2-3 times width of root ball, same depth as root ball.
- Set root flare at or slightly above existing grade. Do NOT bury trunk.
- Remove burlap, twine, and wire basket from top half of root ball.
- Backfill with existing soil (do not amend). Water thoroughly to settle.
- Mulch with 2-3" shredded hardwood mulch, keeping mulch away from trunk.
- Stake only if necessary for stabilization; remove stakes after one year.

### Shrubs
- Dig planting hole 2 times width of root ball, same depth.
- Plant at same depth as grown in container—do not bury crown.
- Backfill with existing soil. Water thoroughly.
- Mulch with 2-3" mulch, keeping away from stems.

### Perennials & Grasses
- Plant at same depth as grown in container.
- Space according to schedule (center to center).
- Water thoroughly after planting.
- Mulch lightly (1-2") to suppress weeds during establishment.

## Soil Preparation
- Remove all existing weeds and turf from planting areas.
- Till or loosen soil to 8-12" depth in mass planting beds.
- Incorporate 2-3" compost into beds if soil is poor quality.
- Smooth and grade planting beds before planting.

## Maintenance During Establishment (First Year)
- Water deeply 1-2 times per week if no rain (1" water per week minimum).
- Reapply mulch as needed to maintain 2-3" depth.
- Remove weeds promptly.
- Monitor for pests/diseases and treat as needed.
- Do not fertilize during first growing season.

=== Landscape Maintenance Guidelines Template ===
# Landscape Maintenance Guidelines — [Project Name]
**Prepared by**: [Landscape Architect Name]
**Date**: [YYYY-MM-DD]

## Purpose
These guidelines provide a maintenance schedule to keep the landscape healthy, attractive, and functional. Proper maintenance is essential, especially during the first year of establishment.

## Year 1: Establishment Phase (Critical!)
During the first growing season, plants are establishing roots and are most vulnerable. Consistent care is essential for long-term success.

### Watering (Most Important!)
**Frequency**: Water deeply 1-2 times per week if rainfall is less than 1" per week.
**Method**: Slow, deep watering is better than frequent shallow watering. Use soaker hoses or drip irrigation.
**Trees**: Provide 10-15 gallons per week for each tree.
**Shrubs**: Water until soil is moist 6-8" deep.
**Perennials**: Keep soil evenly moist, but not soggy.

**When to Water**: Early morning is best (reduces disease). Avoid evening watering.

**Signs of Drought Stress**: Wilting leaves, leaf drop, browning leaf edges. Water immediately if seen.

### Mulching
**Depth**: Maintain 2-3" depth of mulch in all planting beds.
**Material**: Shredded hardwood bark or leaf mulch (avoid dyed mulch).
**Replenishment**: Add mulch in spring and fall as needed to maintain depth.
**Keep Away**: Pull mulch 2-3" away from plant stems and tree trunks (prevents rot).

### Weeding
**Frequency**: Weekly inspection, remove weeds promptly while small.
**Method**: Hand-pull weeds or use shallow hoe. Avoid deep cultivation near plant roots.
**Mulch Helps**: Proper mulch depth suppresses weed growth.

### Fertilizing
**Year 1**: Do NOT fertilize newly planted plants. Let them establish roots first.

### Pruning
**Year 1**: Minimal pruning—only remove dead, damaged, or diseased branches.
**Do NOT**: Do not shape or reduce size in first year. Let plants establish.

## Year 2+ : Ongoing Maintenance

### Spring (March - May)
**Early Spring**:
- [ ] Remove winter debris, leaves, and dead plant material
- [ ] Inspect plants for winter damage—prune dead wood
- [ ] Refresh mulch to 2-3" depth
- [ ] Edge planting beds cleanly
- [ ] Test soil pH if needed (every 3-5 years)

**Late Spring**:
- [ ] Apply slow-release fertilizer to trees and shrubs (if needed based on growth)
- [ ] Fertilize perennials lightly as growth begins
- [ ] Begin regular weeding schedule (weekly)
- [ ] Monitor for pests and diseases—treat early if seen
- [ ] Deadhead spring bulbs after blooming (leave foliage until yellow)

### Summer (June - August)
- [ ] Water deeply during dry spells (1" per week minimum)
- [ ] Continue weekly weeding
- [ ] Deadhead perennials to promote continued blooming
- [ ] Monitor irrigation system—repair any broken heads
- [ ] Watch for pests (Japanese beetles, aphids, etc.) and treat as needed
- [ ] Prune spring-blooming shrubs after flowering if needed

### Fall (September - November)
**Early Fall**:
- [ ] Reduce watering as temperatures cool
- [ ] Continue weeding until frost
- [ ] Plant spring bulbs (October is ideal)
- [ ] Divide perennials that have become overcrowded (every 3-4 years)

**Late Fall**:
- [ ] Leave perennial seed heads for winter interest and bird food (cut back in early spring)
- [ ] Mulch tender perennials for winter protection after ground freezes
- [ ] Drain and winterize irrigation system (before first hard freeze)
- [ ] Store hoses, tools, and furniture
- [ ] Final mowing—cut grass shorter for last mow of season

### Winter (December - February)
- [ ] Brush heavy snow off evergreen shrubs (prevents branch breakage)
- [ ] Avoid walking on frozen lawn (damages turf)
- [ ] Avoid salt damage—use sand or pet-safe ice melt near plants
- [ ] Plan for spring—order plants, seeds, mulch in advance

## Specific Plant Care

### Trees
- **Pruning**: Prune most trees in late winter while dormant (February-March). Oak trees should be pruned in winter only (avoid spring/summer to prevent oak wilt spread).
- **Fertilizing**: Generally not needed if tree is healthy and growing well. If growth is slow, apply slow-release fertilizer in spring.
- **Watering**: Established trees usually do not need supplemental water except during severe drought.

### Shrubs
- **Pruning**: Prune spring-blooming shrubs after flowering. Prune summer-blooming shrubs in late winter.
- **Fertilizing**: Apply slow-release fertilizer in spring. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers that promote excessive growth.

### Perennials
- **Deadheading**: Remove spent flowers to encourage more blooms.
- **Dividing**: Divide perennials every 3-5 years to maintain vigor. Spring bloomers divide in fall; fall bloomers divide in spring.
- **Cutback**: Leave seed heads through winter for birds. Cut back dead foliage in early spring before new growth.

### Grasses (Ornamental)
- **Cutback**: Cut back old foliage in late winter (February-March) before new growth emerges. Cut to 4-6" above ground.
- **Do NOT fertilize**: Native grasses do not need fertilizer.
- **Dividing**: Divide every 5-7 years if clump becomes too large or dies out in center.

## Irrigation System Maintenance
- **Spring Startup**: Turn on system in late April. Inspect all heads, adjust spray patterns, repair leaks.
- **Summer**: Check weekly that all zones are working. Adjust timer based on rainfall.
- **Fall Shutdown**: Blow out system with compressed air before first freeze (typically late October).

## Pest & Disease Management
- **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**: Monitor regularly. Accept minor damage. Use least-toxic controls first.
- **Common Issues**:
  * Japanese Beetles: Hand-pick or use traps. Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides that harm beneficials.
  * Powdery Mildew: Improve air circulation, water at soil level (not overhead). Use fungicides only if severe.
  * Deer: Use repellents, fencing, or select deer-resistant plants.

## When to Call a Professional
- Tree pruning above 15 feet or near power lines
- Tree removal or large branch removal
- Suspected tree disease or insect infestation
- Irrigation system repairs beyond simple head replacement
- Landscape renovation or significant design changes

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Hardscape**: Non-living elements of landscape (paving, walls, structures, furniture)
- **Softscape**: Living elements of landscape (plants, trees, shrubs, turf)
- **Grading**: Shaping of land surface, typically shown with contour lines
- **Drainage**: Movement of water across/through site (surface or subsurface)
- **Bioswale**: Vegetated channel designed to slow and filter stormwater runoff
- **Rain Garden**: Shallow depression planted with native plants to absorb stormwater
- **Permeable Paving**: Porous surfaces allowing water infiltration (pervious concrete, pavers with gaps, etc.)
- **Retaining Wall**: Structure holding back soil on sloped sites
- **Berm**: Raised mound of soil used for screening, interest, or drainage
- **Swale**: Shallow drainage channel, usually vegetated
- **USDA Hardiness Zone**: Geographic zones based on average annual minimum temperature (1-13)
- **Native Plant**: Plant species indigenous to a region, evolved in local ecosystem
- **Invasive Species**: Non-native plants that aggressively spread and harm ecosystems
- **Xeriscaping**: Landscape design minimizing irrigation needs through appropriate plant selection
- **Pollinator Garden**: Planting designed to attract and support bees, butterflies, and other pollinators
- **Edging**: Material separating planting beds from lawn or paving (metal, plastic, stone)
- **Mulch**: Organic or inorganic material spread on soil surface (suppresses weeds, retains moisture, moderates temperature)
- **Caliper**: Diameter of tree trunk measured 6" above ground (industry standard for tree sizing)
- **B&B (Balled and Burlapped)**: Tree/shrub dug from field with root ball wrapped in burlap
- **Container-Grown**: Plant grown and sold in plastic nursery pot
- **Root Flare**: Where tree trunk widens at base—should be visible at grade
- **ADA**: Americans with Disabilities Act (accessibility requirements for paths, parking, etc.)
- **As-Built Drawings**: Record drawings showing actual constructed conditions (if different from design)

Industry Standards & References (2024):
- **ASLA (American Society of Landscape Architects)**: Professional standards and ethics
- **SITES (Sustainable Sites Initiative)**: Rating system for sustainable land development
- **Low Impact Development (LID)**: Stormwater management mimicking natural hydrology
- **ASTM E2122**: Standard Practice for Conducting Visual Tree Assessments
- **ANSI A300**: Tree Care Standards (pruning, planting, maintenance)
- **NRCS Web Soil Survey**: Soils data (www.websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov)
- **Plant Select**: Regionally adapted plant recommendations (varies by region)
- **Native Plant Societies**: State/regional organizations promoting native plant use
- **Audubon Society Plants for Birds**: Database of native plants supporting wildlife
- **FEMA Flood Maps**: Floodplain mapping for site analysis

Licensing & Regulation:
- Most US states and Canadian provinces require Landscape Architect licensure to prepare and seal landscape plans
- Requires accredited degree + experience + passing LARE (Landscape Architect Registration Examination)
