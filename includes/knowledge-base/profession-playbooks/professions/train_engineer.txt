Profession Playbook — Train Engineer
Slug: train_engineer
Category: transportation  
Includes: global.txt + categories/other.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for railroad locomotive engineers, containing actionable instructions,
  SOPs, checklists, and templates for safe train operations, FRA certification compliance, and operating rule adherence.

Category guidance
- Emphasize Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) regulations, operating rules, safety protocols, and certification requirements.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Operate locomotives safely per FRA certification (49 CFR Part 240) and railroad operating rules
- Conduct pre-departure locomotive inspections and testing per FRA requirements
- Control train movement according to signal indications, speed restrictions, and track warrants
- Communicate clearly with conductors, dispatchers, and other railroad personnel
- Monitor locomotive systems (air brakes, power, speed, warning systems) continuously
- Respond appropriately to emergencies (derailments, equipment failures, trespasser incidents)
- Maintain FRA-required proficiency and pass periodic rules examinations
- Document train operations, defects, and incidents accurately
- Comply with Hours of Service (HOS) regulations for railroad employees
- Success = Safe, on-time train operations with zero preventable incidents, full regulatory compliance, proficiency maintained

Do NOT:
- Operate locomotive without valid FRA certification or expired medical certificate
- Exceed authorized speed limits or disregard signal indications
- Operate while fatigued or under influence of alcohol/drugs (FRA zero tolerance)
- Bypass required safety tests (air brake test, locomotive inspection, radio check)
- Fail to report defects, near-misses, or safety hazards
- Violate Hours of Service limits (12 hours on-duty maximum, rest requirements)
- Make unapproved modifications to locomotive equipment or bypass safety systems
- Ignore track bulletins, slow orders, or maintenance-of-way restrictions
- Use personal electronic devices while operating locomotive (FRA ban on cell phones)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Major equipment failure requires assistance (loss of brakes, engine failure, signal system malfunction)
- Accident/derailment occurs (notify dispatcher immediately, secure scene, protect tracks)
- Trespasser strike or fatality (emergency stop, call 911, preserve scene for investigation)
- Hazardous materials release or spill (evacuate area, notify CHEMTREC, follow emergency response)
- Signal system failure or conflicting indications (stop, contact dispatcher, receive authority to proceed)
- Medical emergency (crew member or passenger) requires EMS
- Criminal activity or security threat observed (notify railroad police, law enforcement)
- FRA Inspector boards for inspection (comply fully, provide requested documentation)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What class of railroad (Class I freight, regional, short line, passenger/commuter)?
- What type of train operations (freight, passenger, switching, yard operations)?
- What FRA certification level (engineer, conductor, both)?
- What territory/subdivision (specific operating rules, signal systems, speed restrictions)?
- What locomotive types and controls (diesel-electric, electric, PTC-equipped)?
- What are the Hours of Service requirements (12-hour limit, rest period mandates)?
- What signal system is in use (ABS, CTC, dark territory, PTC overlay)?
- What are the key operating rules and railroad-specific procedures?

3) Default workflow
Use FRA regulations (49 CFR Parts 217, 218, 240, 242, 243) and railroad operating rules:

1. Pre-Duty Preparation & Fitness
   - Verify FRA certification valid and medical certificate current
   - Ensure adequate rest per HOS requirements (10 hours off-duty minimum for freight, 8 for passenger)
   - Review duty assignment (train number, origin/destination, schedule, locomotive consist)
   - Check for any medical conditions or medications affecting fitness for duty
   - Arrive at reporting location with sufficient time for pre-departure procedures
   - Obtain and review all relevant bulletins (track bulletins, general orders, special instructions)
   - Conduct job briefing with conductor/crew (discuss route, hazards, special instructions)

2. Locomotive Inspection & Testing (49 CFR Part 229)
   - Conduct systematic walk-around inspection of locomotive exterior
     * Check for fluid leaks (oil, coolant, fuel)
     * Inspect trucks, wheels, and brake rigging
     * Verify marker lights, ditch lights, and horn operational
     * Check for loose or damaged parts
   - Enter cab and test all safety systems
     * Alerter system functional (acknowledges required)
     * Dead man pedal or equivalent operative
     * Speedometer accurate
     * Air brake gauges reading correctly
     * Radio communication tested with dispatcher/yard
     * PTC (Positive Train Control) initialized if equipped
   - Perform air brake test (Class I, Class IA, or Class II per FRA requirements)
     * Verify brake pipe pressure (typically 90 psi)
     * Apply and release brakes, confirm proper response
     * Test emergency brake application
     * Document test results and defects
   - Review locomotive consist (number of units, horsepower available, limitations)
   - Verify all required paperwork (train orders, track warrants, consist information)

3. Train Movement & Operations
   - Obtain authority to occupy main track (track warrant, clearance, signal indication)
   - Call dispatcher for any restrictions or updates before departing
   - Operate train in accordance with signal aspects and speed limits
     * Clear (green): Proceed at authorized speed
     * Approach (yellow): Prepare to stop at next signal
     * Stop (red): Must stop before passing signal
   - Monitor track ahead for obstructions, maintenance crews, equipment
   - Maintain continuous air brake monitoring (pressure, application, leakage)
   - Respond to track side warning systems (hot box detectors, dragging equipment)
   - Control train speed using dynamic brakes and air brakes appropriately
   - Manage slack action (gentle throttle and brake application to minimize train forces)
   - Communicate with conductor regularly (location updates, speed restrictions ahead)
   - Whistle/horn for grade crossings (two longs, one short, one long - repeated as needed)
   - Comply with all slow orders, work zones, and restricted speed areas

4. Emergency Response Procedures
   - Emergency brake application: Place brake handle in full emergency, notify dispatcher
   - Derailment: Stop immediately if safe, secure scene, protect adjacent tracks, notify emergency services
   - Trespasser strike: Emergency stop, call 911, do not move train, preserve scene for investigation
   - Signal failure: Stop short of red signal, contact dispatcher, receive verbal authority if required
   - PTC enforcement: If PTC initiates penalty brake, acknowledge, determine cause, report to dispatcher
   - Equipment failure: Assess severity, determine if train can continue safely, request assistance if needed
   - Hazmat release: Evacuate area, notify CHEMTREC (1-800-424-9300), follow emergency response guide
   - Medical emergency: Request EMS via dispatcher, provide first aid if trained, document incident

5. Post-Trip & Documentation
   - Complete end-of-run air brake test and secure locomotive
   - Document all defects, unusual occurrences, and operational issues
   - Report any near-misses or safety concerns to dispatcher and safety department
   - Complete required timekeeping and HOS records
   - Participate in any required post-incident drug/alcohol testing
   - Debrief with crew on any operational issues or lessons learned
   - Secure locomotive (remove reverser key if required, apply handbrakes, shut down per procedure)

6. Ongoing Training & Proficiency
   - Pass annual rules examination (operating rules, FRA regulations, territory familiarization)
   - Complete periodic skills assessments (signal recognition, train handling, emergency response)
   - Maintain territory qualification (ride over subdivisions regularly, pass territory tests)
   - Attend required safety training (updated FRA regulations, new equipment, incident reviews)
   - Participate in simulator training if available (emergency scenarios, unusual conditions)
   - Review accident/incident reports to learn from others' experiences

4) Deliverables
- Locomotive Inspection Reports - pre-departure and post-trip documentation
- Air Brake Test Reports - required before each trip and after setouts/pickups
- Train Operation Reports - documenting speed restrictions complied with, delays, unusual events
- Defect Reports - mechanical issues requiring maintenance attention
- Incident/Accident Reports - derailments, grade crossing incidents, trespasser strikes
- Hours of Service Records - documenting on-duty and off-duty time for FRA compliance
- Territory Qualification Records - demonstrating proficiency on specific subdivisions
- Rules Examination Scores - annual testing results showing continued competency
- Drug & Alcohol Testing Documentation - random and post-incident testing records

Minimum viable: Safe train operations with no incidents, all FRA requirements met, certification maintained
Gold standard: Exemplary safety record, fuel-efficient train handling, mentorship of new engineers, leadership in safety culture

Example 3: PTC (Positive Train Control) System Intervention
```
OPERATIONAL REPORT - PTC Penalty Brake Application
Date: 2024-12-18 | Time: 11:42 | Subdivision: Eastern District, MP 89.2
Train: Q-LACDAL-16A (Intermodal) | Engineer: Kim, David | Cert #: E-2024-CA-3382
Locomotive: BNSF 4021 (PTC-equipped) | Speed: 58 MPH | Authorized: 60 MPH

SITUATION:
Approaching CP (Control Point) Adams with signal displaying Approach (yellow) aspect.
Requirement: Reduce speed to no more than 30 MPH by next signal (CP Baker, 2 miles ahead).

PTC INTERVENTION:
11:42:15 - PTC display shows "ENFORCE APPROACH - SLOW TO 30 MPH BY MP 91.2"
11:42:20 - Engineer begins dynamic brake application to comply
11:42:35 - Speed reducing (currently 52 MPH, target 30 MPH ahead)
11:42:45 - PTC determines deceleration rate insufficient to meet target
11:42:46 - PTC initiates PENALTY BRAKE APPLICATION (automatic emergency brake)
11:42:50 - Engineer acknowledges penalty brake, train speed 48 MPH and decelerating rapidly

ENGINEER ACTIONS:
- Acknowledged PTC penalty immediately
- Allowed train to slow below 30 MPH (reached 28 MPH at MP 91.0)
- Reset PTC system after penalty condition cleared
- Contacted dispatcher to report PTC penalty and reason
- Documented event in train operations log

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS:
Engineer initiated braking at appropriate location for typical train handling.
However, with 78 loaded intermodal cars (8,124 tons), deceleration rate was slower than
PTC algorithm calculated as necessary. PTC enforced penalty brake as designed to ensure
compliance with 30 MPH restriction before next signal.

LESSONS LEARNED:
- Heavier trains require earlier brake initiation when PTC-enforced restrictions apply
- PTC calculations are conservative - manual braking should begin earlier than minimum
- For Approach signals with PTC, initiate braking immediately rather than waiting

CORRECTIVE ACTIONS:
- Will initiate braking earlier on future Approach signals with heavy tonnage
- Reviewed PTC enforcement algorithms with Road Foreman of Engines
- No safety violation occurred - PTC system functioned as designed
- Event used as training opportunity for other engineers handling heavy intermodal trains

REPORTING:
- Event logged in PTC system (automatic recording)
- Dispatcher notified per protocol
- Road Foreman reviewed event recorder data (no rule violation found)
- Used as positive example in next safety meeting: "PTC as safety backup system"

COMPLIANCE VERIFICATION:
✓ No signal violation (speed reduced to 28 MPH before CP Baker)
✓ PTC system operational and functioning correctly
✓ Engineer acknowledged penalty and reset system appropriately
✓ No FRA reportable event (penalty brake with no accident/injury)
✓ Crew proficiency maintained (appropriate response to PTC intervention)

OUTCOME:
Train delayed 3 minutes due to penalty brake recovery.
No safety violation. System functioned as designed to ensure signal compliance.

Engineer: David Kim | Cert #: E-2024-CA-3382 | Date/Time: 2024-12-18 12:15
Road Foreman Review: Wilson, R. | Findings: No violation, PTC functioned correctly | Date: 2024-12-18
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **FRA (Federal Railroad Administration)**: DOT agency regulating railroad safety, engineer certification
- **Engineer Certification (49 CFR Part 240)**: Federal requirement for locomotive engineers including training, testing, medical exam
- **Operating Rules**: Railroad-specific procedures governing train movement, signal compliance, communication
- **Signal Aspect**: Visual indication of track conditions ahead (Clear/Green, Approach/Yellow, Stop/Red)
- **Track Warrant**: Written authority from dispatcher to occupy main track in non-signaled (dark) territory
- **PTC (Positive Train Control)**: GPS-based system preventing train-to-train collisions and overspeed derailments
- **Air Brake Test**: Required inspection verifying brake system functionality (Class I, IA, II, or III)
- **Alerter System**: Safety device requiring periodic engineer acknowledgment to prevent unattended operation
- **Hours of Service (HOS)**: FRA limits on railroad employee work hours (12 hours on-duty maximum, rest requirements)
- **Dark Territory**: Track without signal system, requiring track warrant authority for train movement
- **Consist**: Locomotive configuration (number and arrangement of units in a locomotive set)
- **Dynamic Brakes**: Electrical braking system using traction motors as generators (rheostatic or regenerative)
- **Grade Crossing (49 CFR Part 222)**: At-grade intersection of railroad track and roadway

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **49 CFR Part 240**: Qualification and Certification of Locomotive Engineers
- **49 CFR Part 242**: Qualification and Certification of Conductors  
- **49 CFR Part 217**: Railroad Operating Rules (railroad must adopt and maintain)
- **49 CFR Part 218**: Railroad Operating Practices (safety rules for employees)
- **49 CFR Part 229**: Railroad Locomotive Safety Standards (inspection, maintenance)
- **49 CFR Part 243**: Training, Qualification, and Oversight for Safety-Related Railroad Employees
- **49 CFR Part 219**: Control of Alcohol and Drug Use (testing requirements)
- **49 CFR Part 225**: Accident/Incident Reporting (reporting thresholds and procedures)
- **49 CFR Part 236**: Rules, Standards, and Instructions Governing Railroad Signal Systems
- **NORAC Operating Rules**: Widely used operating rules system (Northeast Operating Rules Advisory Committee)
- **GCOR (General Code of Operating Rules)**: Operating rules system used by many western railroads

Common Tools:
- Locomotive controls: Throttle, dynamic brake, independent/automatic air brake handles
- Communication: Locomotive radio (AAR channels), mobile phone (restricted use)
- Navigation: GPS systems, track charts, employee timetables, subdivision maps
- PTC display: Onboard computer showing speed limits, authorities, restrictions
- Event recorder: "Black box" recording speed, brake applications, horn use, throttle position
- Rules books: Operating rules, timetables, special instructions, bulletins
- Personal protective equipment: Safety vest, hard hat, safety glasses, hearing protection
