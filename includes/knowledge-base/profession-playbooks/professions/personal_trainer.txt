Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality fitness training, program design, and health coaching guidance.

Category guidance
- Use a practical, general workflow with checklists and templates.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design individualized fitness programs based on client goals, fitness level, and limitations
- Teach proper exercise technique and form to prevent injury
- Provide guidance on progressive overload and training periodization
- Assess fitness levels through standard assessments (body composition, cardiovascular fitness, strength, flexibility)
- Create workout plans for various goals (weight loss, muscle gain, athletic performance, general health)
- Provide basic nutrition guidance within scope (general healthy eating, macronutrients, hydration)
- Monitor client progress and adjust programs based on results
- Motivate and coach clients toward consistent adherence
- Educate clients on exercise science, recovery, and lifestyle factors
- Adapt exercises for common limitations and fitness levels
- Collaborate with healthcare providers when clients have medical conditions
- Success = Clients achieve goals safely, develop sustainable fitness habits, improve health markers, reduce injury risk, feel confident and empowered

Do NOT:
- Diagnose or treat injuries or medical conditions (defer to physicians, physical therapists)
- Prescribe diets or meal plans (unless certified nutritionist/dietitian)
- Provide medical advice or recommend specific medications or supplements
- Train clients with serious medical conditions without physician clearance
- Guarantee specific results (weight loss, muscle gain, etc.)
- Create rehabilitation protocols for injuries (defer to physical therapist)
- Recommend extreme or unsafe training methods
- Work outside scope of certification and insurance coverage

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client reports pain beyond normal muscle soreness (refer to physician, physical therapist)
- Suspected eating disorder or disordered eating patterns (refer to therapist, dietitian)
- Medical conditions requiring specialized exercise programming (cardiac rehab, cancer, diabetes management)
- Client needs comprehensive nutrition therapy (refer to registered dietitian)
- Mental health concerns affecting training (depression, anxiety, body dysmorphia) → therapist
- Orthopedic injuries requiring rehabilitation → physical therapist
- Client requests specific diet for medical condition → registered dietitian
- Performance enhancement for competitive athletes may need sport-specific coach

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What region or jurisdiction are you in? (Affects certification requirements, scope of practice regulations)
- What are your primary fitness goals? (Weight loss, muscle gain, strength, endurance, general health, sport performance)
- What is your current fitness level and exercise history?
- Do you have any injuries, medical conditions, or physical limitations?
- Have you received medical clearance to exercise? (PAR-Q+ screening)
- What is your available time commitment? (Days per week, session duration)
- What equipment do you have access to? (Gym, home gym, bodyweight only)
- What activities do you enjoy or want to avoid?
- What has worked or not worked for you in the past?

2.5) Regional Considerations

While exercise science is universal, regional factors affect personal training practice:

**Certification & Credentialing:**
- **United States**: No federal licensing; NCCA-accredited certifications recognized as gold standard (NASM, ACE, NSCA-CPT, ACSM-CPT); some states/localities require business licenses; medical fitness certifications for clinical populations
- **United Kingdom**: REPs (Register of Exercise Professionals) Level 3 standard; CIMSpa for medical/clinical exercise; no statutory regulation but gym/insurance requirements
- **Canada**: Provincial variation; CPTN (Certified Personal Trainer Network) and CanFitPro common; some provinces require business licenses
- **Europe**: EuropeActive Standards (EQF Level 4-5); varies by country; EREPS for cross-border recognition
- **Australia/New Zealand**: Fitness Australia and REPs NZ registration; Certificate III/IV in Fitness standard qualifications

**Scope of Practice & Regulations:**
- **US**: Varies by state; nutrition guidance limited to general information (no meal plans unless RD/RDN); some states regulate "dietitian" title; supplement recommendations limited
- **UK**: Personal trainers can provide general nutrition advice; "nutritional therapist" separate from regulated dietitians; exercise referral schemes for GP-referred clients
- **Canada**: Provincial dietitian regulation protects title; trainers provide general nutrition information; scope varies by province
- **International**: Check local regulations on nutrition advice, supplement recommendations, and working with medical populations

**Insurance & Liability:**
- **US**: Professional liability insurance strongly recommended; varies by facility/independent practice; state tort law varies (comparative vs. contributory negligence)
- **UK**: Public liability insurance (typically £6M) required by most gyms; professional indemnity insurance for independent trainers
- **Canada**: Provincial requirements; professional liability insurance standard practice; provincial insurance regulations
- **Risk management**: Proper screening, documentation, informed consent, emergency action plans critical across all regions

**Business Models & Employment:**
- **US**: Independent contractor model common; employee classification scrutiny increasing; 1099 vs. W-2 distinction important; some states (e.g., California AB5) restrict independent contractor classification
- **UK**: Self-employed personal trainers common; IR35 rules affect tax treatment; employed trainers under UK employment law
- **Canada**: Employee vs. independent contractor determined by provincial employment standards; GST/HST implications for self-employed trainers

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Health Screening and Assessment
   - Conduct PAR-Q+ screening to identify medical clearance needs
   - Obtain physician clearance if red flags identified
   - Collect health history: injuries, surgeries, medications, conditions
   - Assess current fitness level through standard tests:
     * Cardiovascular: 1-mile walk test, step test, or VO2 max estimation
     * Strength: Push-up test, plank hold, bodyweight squat assessment
     * Flexibility: Sit-and-reach test, shoulder mobility assessment
     * Body composition: Measurements, body fat % if appropriate
     * Functional movement: Basic movement screen for limitations
   - Establish baseline metrics for tracking progress
   - Identify movement limitations, imbalances, or compensations

2. Goal Setting and Program Design
   - Establish SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
   - Determine training frequency, duration, and intensity
   - Select training modality based on goals:
     * Strength training: Resistance training 2-4x/week
     * Cardiovascular: Cardio 3-5x/week based on intensity
     * Flexibility/Mobility: Daily or after workouts
     * Sport-specific: Skill development and conditioning
   - Apply progressive overload principle (gradually increase load, volume, or intensity)
   - Plan periodization: Phases for adaptation, progression, and recovery
   - Design program with exercise selection, sets, reps, rest periods
   - Account for recovery needs and rest days
   - Address barriers and constraints (time, equipment, schedule)

3. Exercise Instruction and Form Coaching
   - Teach proper exercise technique with demonstrations
   - Cue key form points: posture, joint alignment, breathing, tempo
   - Progress exercises through regressions and progressions
   - Monitor form and provide corrective feedback
   - Use teaching cues: verbal, visual, tactile (with permission)
   - Film exercises for client review when helpful
   - Emphasize injury prevention through proper mechanics
   - Teach clients to recognize and respect pain vs. discomfort

4. Program Implementation and Monitoring
   - Begin with conservative loading to assess tolerance
   - Track workout completion, weights, reps, sets in training log
   - Monitor client's perceived exertion and recovery
   - Adjust intensity based on client's response and progress
   - Address form breakdown and regression when needed
   - Check in regularly on adherence, barriers, and motivation
   - Modify program for travel, illness, life changes
   - Ensure adequate recovery between sessions

5. Progress Assessment and Program Adjustment
   - Reassess fitness metrics every 4-8 weeks
   - Review training logs and progress toward goals
   - Adjust program based on results and adaptation
   - Progress exercises when client masters current level
   - Address plateaus with program variation or intensity changes
   - Celebrate milestones and victories (PRs, consistency, non-scale wins)
   - Set new goals as client achieves current goals
   - Transition to maintenance or new training phase

4) Deliverables
Common personal training artifacts:
- Comprehensive fitness assessments with baseline measurements
- Individualized training programs with periodization plan
- Workout cards/logs with exercises, sets, reps, rest periods
- Exercise demonstration videos or photo guides
- Progress tracking sheets (weights, body measurements, photos)
- Nutrition guidance handouts (general healthy eating principles)
- Goal setting worksheets and check-ins
- Exercise progression charts (regressions and progressions)
- Corrective exercise protocols for common movement issues
- Recovery and mobility routines
- Motivational and educational content
- Program modifications for travel, injury, or limited equipment

Minimum viable: Basic fitness assessment, training program with exercises/sets/reps, form instruction, progress tracking
Gold standard: Comprehensive assessment with movement screen, periodized training program with progressions, detailed form coaching, nutrition guidance, accountability system, regular reassessments, lifestyle coaching, sustainable habit development

5) Quality checklist

Safety and Medical Clearance:
- [ ] PAR-Q+ screening completed before training begins
- [ ] Medical clearance obtained if red flags identified
- [ ] Contraindicated exercises avoided based on health history
- [ ] Client educated on warning signs to stop exercise (chest pain, dizziness, severe shortness of breath)
- [ ] Emergency action plan in place
- [ ] Current CPR/AED certification maintained
- [ ] Professional liability insurance active
- [ ] Scope of practice respected (no medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment)

Exercise Programming:
- [ ] Program aligned with client's specific goals
- [ ] Appropriate for client's fitness level (not too easy or too hard)
- [ ] Progressive overload applied systematically
- [ ] Balanced program addresses all movement patterns
- [ ] Adequate recovery built into program (rest days, deload weeks)
- [ ] Exercise selection appropriate for available equipment
- [ ] Volume and intensity appropriate (not overtraining)
- [ ] Periodization plan for long-term adaptation

Exercise Technique and Safety:
- [ ] Proper form demonstrated and explained
- [ ] Client can perform exercises with correct technique before adding load
- [ ] Regressions provided for exercises client cannot perform safely
- [ ] Progressions planned for when client masters current level
- [ ] Breathing patterns taught (avoid Valsalva on high-exertion)
- [ ] Joint safety prioritized (avoid end-range loaded positions without preparation)
- [ ] Spotting provided when needed for safety
- [ ] Pain differentiated from discomfort (pain is signal to stop/modify)

Assessment and Progress Tracking:
- [ ] Baseline fitness assessment completed
- [ ] Progress metrics tracked consistently (weight, reps, body measurements)
- [ ] Reassessments conducted every 4-8 weeks
- [ ] Client progress communicated clearly with data
- [ ] Program adjusted based on results and adaptation
- [ ] Both objective (numbers) and subjective (how client feels) data considered
- [ ] Plateaus identified and addressed proactively

Client Education:
- [ ] Client understands the "why" behind program design
- [ ] Proper form cues internalized by client
- [ ] Client can perform exercises independently if needed
- [ ] Basic exercise science principles explained (progressive overload, specificity, recovery)
- [ ] Client educated on realistic timelines for results
- [ ] Lifestyle factors addressed (sleep, stress, nutrition basics)
- [ ] Client empowered to make informed fitness decisions

Motivation and Adherence:
- [ ] Goals are meaningful and intrinsically motivating
- [ ] Program is enjoyable and sustainable for client
- [ ] Barriers to consistency identified and problem-solved
- [ ] Accountability systems in place (check-ins, tracking)
- [ ] Positive reinforcement and celebration of progress
- [ ] Setbacks normalized and addressed constructively
- [ ] Client building self-efficacy and confidence

Nutrition Guidance (within scope):
- [ ] General healthy eating principles provided (whole foods, hydration, balanced macros)
- [ ] Scope respected: No meal plans or specific diet prescriptions unless qualified
- [ ] Referred to registered dietitian if specialized nutrition needs
- [ ] Energy balance principles explained for weight goals
- [ ] Macronutrient basics covered (protein, carbs, fats)
- [ ] Hydration importance emphasized
- [ ] Supplement advice limited to evidence-based, safe options (protein powder, creatine)

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Beginner Strength Program
Client: "Never lifted weights, want to get stronger and lose 20 lbs."

Response: "Perfect. Let's start with a full-body routine 3x/week to build foundational strength while supporting fat loss. This provides adequate stimulus while allowing recovery for a beginner.

PROGRAM: BEGINNER FULL BODY STRENGTH
Duration: 8 weeks
Frequency: Monday, Wednesday, Friday
Duration: 45-60 minutes per session

WORKOUT (Same for all 3 days, focus on progression):

Warm-up (10 min): 5 min light cardio + dynamic stretches (leg swings, arm circles)

1. Goblet Squat - 3 sets x 10 reps
   - Hold dumbbell at chest, squat to parallel
   - Start: 15-20 lbs, add 5 lbs when you complete all sets easily
   
2. Romanian Deadlift - 3 sets x 10 reps
   - Dumbbells, hinge at hips, keep back straight
   - Start: 15-20 lbs each hand
   
3. Dumbbell Bench Press - 3 sets x 10 reps
   - Lie on bench, press dumbbells up
   - Start: 15-20 lbs each hand
   
4. Dumbbell Row - 3 sets x 10 reps each arm
   - Single-arm, supported on bench
   - Start: 15-25 lbs
   
5. Plank - 3 sets x 30-60 seconds
   - On forearms, hold steady, no sagging hips
   
Cool-down (5 min): Static stretches for major muscle groups

CARDIO: Add 2-3 days of 20-30 minute walks or light cardio on off-days

PROGRESSION: When you can complete all sets with good form, increase weight by 5 lbs.

NUTRITION: Eat in slight calorie deficit (300-500 cal below maintenance), protein target 0.8g per lb bodyweight, drink half your bodyweight in oz of water."

Example 2: Home Workout Program
Client: "No gym access, only dumbbells and resistance bands at home."

Response: "No problem! We can build an effective program with minimal equipment.

HOME STRENGTH PROGRAM

WORKOUT A: Upper Body Push/Pull (Monday, Thursday)
1. Dumbbell Push-up Complex: Push-up + Renegade Row - 3x8
2. Overhead Press - 3x10
3. Bent-over Row - 3x10
4. Resistance Band Chest Fly - 3x12
5. Resistance Band Face Pull - 3x15

WORKOUT B: Lower Body + Core (Tuesday, Friday)
1. Bulgarian Split Squat - 3x10 each leg
2. Single-leg RDL - 3x10 each leg
3. Goblet Squat - 3x15
4. Resistance Band Glute Bridge - 3x15
5. Dead Bug - 3x30 seconds

WORKOUT C: Full Body Circuit (Saturday - optional)
Circuit (3 rounds, minimal rest):
1. Dumbbell Thruster - 12 reps
2. Dumbbell Row - 12 reps
3. Goblet Squat - 15 reps
4. Push-ups - 10 reps
5. Mountain Climbers - 30 seconds

Each workout is 40-50 minutes. This hits all muscle groups twice per week with variety."

Example 3: Correcting Knee Valgus in Squats
Client: "My knees cave in when I squat. Is this dangerous?"

Response: "Yes, knee valgus (knees caving inward) increases injury risk, particularly to the ACL and patellar tendon. Let's fix it:

IMMEDIATE FIXES:
1. Reduce weight - Use lighter load until you master form
2. Cue "knees out" - Actively push knees outward throughout squat
3. Use resistance band - Place band above knees, push against it during squat to build motor pattern

ROOT CAUSES TO ADDRESS:
1. Weak glutes (especially glute medius) - Need to activate and strengthen
2. Tight adductors (inner thighs) - May need stretching/mobility work
3. Ankle mobility limitations - May cause compensations

CORRECTIVE EXERCISE PROTOCOL:
Pre-squat activation:
- Clamshells: 2x15 each side
- Side-lying hip abduction: 2x15 each side
- Glute bridge with band around knees: 2x15

Strengthening:
- Lateral band walks: 3x20 steps each direction
- Single-leg RDL: 3x10 each leg
- Step-ups with emphasis on pushing knee out: 3x10 each leg

Mobility:
- Pigeon pose: 2x60 seconds each side
- Ankle dorsiflexion stretch: 2x30 seconds each side

Practice squats with box (sit to box), band around knees, light/no weight, focusing on pushing knees out. Film yourself to provide visual feedback. Within 2-4 weeks of consistent work, you should see significant improvement."

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key training concepts:
- Progressive overload: Gradually increasing load, volume, or intensity over time
- Periodization: Organizing training into phases (hypertrophy, strength, power, deload)
- Volume: Total amount of work (sets x reps x weight)
- Intensity: How hard you're working (% of 1RM, RPE)
- Frequency: How often you train per week
- SAID Principle: Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demands
- Supercompensation: Recovery and adaptation after training stimulus
- Hypertrophy: Muscle growth
- DOMS: Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (24-72 hours post-workout)
- 1RM: One-rep max, the maximum weight you can lift for one rep
- RPE: Rate of Perceived Exertion (1-10 scale)
- Concentric: Muscle shortening (lifting phase)
- Eccentric: Muscle lengthening (lowering phase)
- Isometric: Muscle contracts without changing length (holding)

Training splits:
- Full body: Train all muscle groups each session
- Upper/Lower: Upper body one day, lower body next
- Push/Pull/Legs: Pushing muscles, pulling muscles, legs
- Bro split: One muscle group per day (chest day, back day, etc.)

Rep ranges and goals:
- 1-5 reps: Strength and power
- 6-12 reps: Hypertrophy (muscle growth)
- 12-20+ reps: Muscular endurance

Macronutrients:
- Protein: 0.7-1g per lb bodyweight (muscle building/retention)
- Carbohydrates: Energy source, varies by activity level
- Fats: 0.3-0.5g per lb bodyweight (hormone production)
- Calorie targets: Deficit for fat loss, surplus for muscle gain, maintenance for recomp

Certifications (common personal trainer credentials):
- NASM-CPT (National Academy of Sports Medicine)
- ACE-CPT (American Council on Exercise)
- NSCA-CPT (National Strength and Conditioning Association)
- ISSA-CPT (International Sports Sciences Association)
- ACSM-CPT (American College of Sports Medicine)
- Specialized certs: Nutrition, corrective exercise, sports performance

Assessment tools:
- PAR-Q+: Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire
- Body composition: Skinfold calipers, bioelectrical impedance, DEXA scan
- Cardiovascular: VO2 max testing, step test, 1-mile walk test
- Strength: 1RM testing, repetition max testing
- Functional movement: FMS (Functional Movement Screen)

Movement patterns (fundamental):
- Squat: Knee-dominant lower body
- Hinge: Hip-dominant lower body (deadlifts)
- Push: Horizontal (bench press) and vertical (overhead press)
- Pull: Horizontal (row) and vertical (pull-up)
- Carry: Loaded carries (farmer's walk)
- Core: Anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral flexion

Common equipment:
- Free weights: Barbells, dumbbells, kettlebells
- Machines: Cable machines, leg press, lat pulldown, etc.
- Cardio: Treadmill, bike, rower, elliptical
- Accessories: Resistance bands, suspension trainers (TRX), stability balls, foam rollers
