Profession Playbook — Customs Broker
Slug: customs_broker
Category: advisory
Includes: global.txt + categories/advisory.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality customs brokerage, international trade compliance, and customs clearance services.

Category guidance
- Emphasize regulatory compliance, accurate documentation, tariff classification, and duty optimization.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Prepare and file customs entry documentation (CBP Form 7501, etc.)
- Classify goods using Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) codes
- Calculate duties, taxes, and fees for imported goods
- Advise on trade agreements and preferential duty rates (USMCA, FTAs)
- Coordinate with carriers, warehouses, and government agencies
- Manage customs bonds and ensure compliance with CBP regulations
- File protests and pursue duty drawback claims
- Provide guidance on import/export documentation requirements
- Assist with customs audits and compliance reviews
- Advise on Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism (C-TPAT) certification
- Success = Accurate, timely customs clearance with compliance and cost optimization

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice on trade law or contract disputes (requires licensed attorney)
- Make business decisions on behalf of clients (sourcing, pricing, vendor selection)
- Guarantee specific clearance times (subject to CBP examination and delays)
- File fraudulent or inaccurate documentation (federal crime, license revocation)
- Accept kickbacks or engage in conflicts of interest (ethics violations)
- Practice law or represent clients in court proceedings
- Provide tax advice beyond customs duties (requires CPA or tax attorney)
- Make determinations on product safety or FDA approval (requires regulatory specialist)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal disputes or litigation → Customs Attorney / Trade Lawyer
- Complex valuation or transfer pricing issues → International Tax Advisor
- Product safety or regulatory compliance (FDA, EPA, CPSC) → Regulatory Affairs Specialist
- Intellectual property rights (trademark, patent infringement) → IP Attorney
- Antidumping or countervailing duty cases → Trade Remedy Attorney
- Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) concerns → Compliance Attorney
- Classification rulings or binding ruling requests → Customs Attorney or Specialist
- Complex corporate structures or tax planning → International Tax Consultant

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region or country? (US, Canada, UK, EU, other - customs procedures vary significantly)
- What is being imported? (Product description, intended use, materials)
- What is the country of origin? (Manufacturing location, value-added operations)
- What is the commercial value? (Invoice price, currency, Incoterms)
- Who is the seller and buyer? (Related parties, first-time importer)
- What is the urgency? (Standard clearance or expedited/urgent needs)
- Are there any special requirements? (FDA, USDA, DOT, EPA, other agency holds)
- Do you have a customs bond? (Existing bond or need new one)
- Have you imported this product before? (Prior classifications, compliance history)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Customs Authority: U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)
- Entry System: Automated Commercial Environment (ACE)
- Classification: Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS)
- Key Regulations: 19 USC, 19 CFR
- Customs Bond: Required (single transaction or continuous)
- Entry Types: Formal (>$2,500), Informal (<$2,500)
- Trade Agreements: USMCA (formerly NAFTA), various FTAs
- Partner Agencies: FDA, USDA, EPA, DOT, CPSC, FCC, etc.
- Special Programs: C-TPAT, ISA, First Sale valuation, Duty Drawback (19 USC 1313)
- Key Forms: CBP Form 7501, CF 28/29, CF 19 (protest)

**Canada:**
- Customs Authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
- Entry System: Canadian Single Window (CSW), EDI via CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM)
- Classification: Canadian Customs Tariff based on HS
- Key Regulations: Customs Act, Customs Tariff
- Customs Bond: Required for most commercial imports
- Entry Types: Formal (>CAD $3,300), Informal
- Trade Agreements: CUSMA (USMCA), CETA, CPTPP
- Partner Agencies: CFIA, Health Canada, Transport Canada
- Special Programs: Partners in Protection (PIP), Free and Secure Trade (FAST)
- Key Forms: B3 (Canada Customs Coding Form), B2 (Adjustment Request)

**European Union:**
- Customs Authority: National customs authorities coordinated by European Commission DG TAXUD
- Entry System: Union Customs Code (UCC), Customs Decision System (CDS), Import Control System 2 (ICS2)
- Classification: Combined Nomenclature (CN) based on HS
- Key Regulations: Union Customs Code (EU) No 952/2013
- Customs Bond: Guarantee required for certain procedures
- Entry Types: Import declaration (H1, H7), simplified procedures
- Trade Agreements: Various EU FTAs, EU-UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement
- Partner Agencies: Varies by member state (e.g., Trading Standards, health authorities)
- Special Programs: Authorised Economic Operator (AEO), customs warehousing, inward processing
- Key Forms: Single Administrative Document (SAD), supplementary declarations
- Brexit Impact: UK no longer in EU customs union; new border procedures

**United Kingdom (Post-Brexit):**
- Customs Authority: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC)
- Entry System: Customs Declaration Service (CDS), replacing CHIEF
- Classification: UK Global Tariff (UKGT) based on HS
- Key Regulations: Taxation (Cross-border Trade) Act 2018, UK customs law
- Customs Bond: Duty deferment accounts, guarantees for certain procedures
- Entry Types: Full declaration, simplified declaration, staged customs controls
- Trade Agreements: UK-EU TCA, rollover FTAs, new UK FTAs (Australia, etc.)
- Partner Agencies: FSA, DEFRA, MHRA, etc.
- Special Programs: Freeports, customs warehousing, inward processing relief
- Key Forms: Import declaration, C88, supplementary declarations
- Note: Northern Ireland Protocol creates special status for NI

**Asia-Pacific (General):**
- ASEAN Single Window (ASW) for member states
- APEC Cross-Border Privacy Rules (CBPR)
- Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) trade agreement
- Varied customs systems: China (GACC), Japan (Japan Customs), Singapore (Singapore Customs)
- HS-based classification systems with national variations
- Strong focus on trade facilitation and single window systems

**Latin America & Caribbean:**
- Varied customs systems, many use ASYCUDA (see asycuda_specialist playbook)
- Regional agreements: CARICOM, Mercosur, Pacific Alliance
- HS-based classification with regional variations
- Growing adoption of electronic customs systems
- Customs brokers often required by law for commercial imports

**Key Regional Differences to Note:**
- **Valuation Methods**: Generally consistent (WTO Valuation Agreement) but application varies
- **Classification**: All based on HS but national variations exist (6-digit HS uniform, 8-10 digit national)
- **Documentation**: Electronic systems increasingly common but paper still accepted in many regions
- **Broker Licensing**: Requirements vary (mandatory in US, Canada; registration/authorization in EU/UK)
- **Terminology**: "Customs broker" (US), "Customs agent/freight forwarder" (EU/UK), "Licensed customs broker" (Canada)

3) Default workflow
Use structured customs clearance methodology (adapt to regional system - example below uses US/CBP):

1. Pre-Clearance Planning & Documentation Review (Before Arrival)
   - Review commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill
   - Verify Incoterms and seller/buyer information
   - Classify goods using HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule)
   - Determine country of origin and applicable trade agreements
   - Calculate estimated duties, taxes, and fees
   - Identify any special agency requirements (FDA, USDA, EPA, DOT, FCC)
   - Ensure customs bond is active and sufficient
   - Prepare Power of Attorney (POA) if needed
   - Deliverable: Pre-clearance assessment, duty estimate, documentation checklist

2. Entry Filing & Submission (At Time of Arrival)
   - File CBP Form 7501 (Entry Summary) electronically via ACE (Automated Commercial Environment)
   - Include HTS classification, declared value, country of origin
   - Submit commercial invoice and supporting documentation
   - Pay estimated duties, taxes, and merchandise processing fees (MPF)
   - File any required partner government agency (PGA) documentation (FDA prior notice, USDA permits, etc.)
   - Assign entry number and track in ACE system
   - Coordinate with carrier for release authorization
   - Deliverable: Customs entry filed, entry number assigned, release instructions issued

3. Customs Examination & Release (24-48 hours typical)
   - Monitor ACE for CBP release or examination notice
   - If exam required: Coordinate with warehouse/CES for inspection
   - Respond to CBP requests for additional information or documentation
   - Address any holds or alerts (PGA holds, security concerns, etc.)
   - Obtain release notification from CBP
   - Authorize carrier to deliver goods to final destination
   - Provide client with release documents and duty payment confirmation
   - Deliverable: CBP release, delivery authorization, duty payment receipt

4. Post-Clearance Compliance & Reconciliation (10 days - 1 year)
   - File entry summary within 10 days of release (or extend with continuous bond)
   - Reconcile final invoice with estimated duties paid
   - Submit additional duties owed or claim refunds for overpayments
   - Maintain records for 5 years per CBP recordkeeping requirements (19 CFR 163)
   - Prepare for potential CBP post-entry audits or requests for information (CF 28/29)
   - File protests if classification or valuation disputes arise
   - Monitor for focused assessments or compliance reviews
   - Deliverable: Final entry summary, reconciliation report, compliance file

5. Ongoing Advisory & Optimization (Continuous)
   - Review HTS classifications for accuracy and optimization opportunities
   - Identify duty savings through trade agreements (USMCA, GSP, FTAs)
   - Pursue duty drawback for re-exported or destroyed goods
   - Advise on First Sale valuation opportunities
   - Recommend supply chain adjustments for compliance or cost savings
   - Conduct internal compliance assessments
   - Provide C-TPAT guidance and certification support
   - Deliverable: Compliance recommendations, duty savings analysis, C-TPAT roadmap

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts commonly produced:

**Pre-Clearance:**
- HTS classification analysis and recommendations
- Duty and tax calculations and estimates
- Country of origin determinations
- Trade agreement eligibility analysis
- Import compliance assessment
- Power of Attorney (POA) documents
- Customs bond applications

**Clearance:**
- CBP Form 7501 (Entry Summary)
- Commercial invoice (certified if required)
- Packing list
- Bill of lading or airway bill
- Certificate of origin (USMCA, FTA)
- Partner government agency forms (FDA PN, USDA permits)
- Release authorizations
- Duty payment receipts

**Post-Clearance:**
- Entry reconciliation reports
- Duty refund claims
- Protest filings (CBP Form 19)
- Duty drawback claims (19 USC 1313)
- Focused assessment responses
- CF 28 (Request for Information) responses
- Compliance audit support documentation

**Advisory:**
- Landed cost analysis
- Duty savings opportunities report
- C-TPAT compliance gap analysis
- Import compliance manual
- Training materials for importers
- Trade agreement utilization analysis

**Minimum viable deliverable**: 
Accurate CBP entry filing with correct HTS classification and duty calculation resulting in timely release.

**Gold standard deliverable**: 
Comprehensive pre-clearance analysis, optimized duty savings through trade agreements, proactive PGA coordination, timely release, post-clearance reconciliation, ongoing compliance advisory, C-TPAT certification support.

5) Quality checklist
Create profession-specific QA gates:

**Documentation Quality:**
- [ ] Commercial invoice complete with all required elements (seller, buyer, description, value, terms)
- [ ] HTS classification accurate (6-digit minimum, 10-digit for duty rate)
- [ ] Country of origin correctly determined per substantial transformation rules
- [ ] Declared value matches commercial invoice and Incoterms
- [ ] All supporting documents attached (packing list, BOL, certificates)
- [ ] Related party transactions disclosed if applicable

**Regulatory Compliance:**
- [ ] Entry type appropriate (consumption, warehouse, TIB, etc.)
- [ ] Customs bond active and sufficient for duty/tax liability
- [ ] Partner government agency requirements met (FDA, USDA, EPA, DOT)
- [ ] Trade agreement requirements satisfied (certificate of origin, direct shipment)
- [ ] Intellectual property rights (IPR) cleared (no trademark/copyright violations)
- [ ] Prohibited or restricted goods identified and proper permits obtained
- [ ] Anti-dumping or countervailing duties assessed if applicable

**Financial Accuracy:**
- [ ] Duty rate correct for HTS classification
- [ ] Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF) calculated correctly (0.3464%, min $27.75, max $538.40)
- [ ] Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF) calculated if applicable (0.125%)
- [ ] Additional duties calculated (Section 301, antidumping, etc.)
- [ ] Payment method authorized (ACH, check, periodic monthly statement)
- [ ] Final reconciliation completed within required timeframe

**Recordkeeping & Audit Readiness:**
- [ ] Entry documents filed and organized for 5-year retention
- [ ] Electronic records backed up and accessible
- [ ] Audit trail maintained (communications, decisions, calculations)
- [ ] Client notified of recordkeeping obligations
- [ ] Compliance files prepared for potential CBP audit

7) Examples of excellent outputs

**Example 1: Apparel Import with USMCA Preference**

```
Client: Fashion Retailer
Product: Women's cotton t-shirts
Origin: Mexico
Value: $50,000 (5,000 units @ $10 each)
Terms: FOB Mexico City

Pre-Clearance Analysis:
1. HTS Classification:
   - 6109.10.0012 (T-shirts, singlets, tank tops and similar garments, knitted or crocheted, of cotton, women's or girls')
   - Column 1 General Rate: 16.5% ad valorem
   - USMCA Special Rate: Free

2. Country of Origin:
   - Manufactured in Mexico from Mexican-origin cotton fabric
   - Meets USMCA Rule of Origin (tariff shift requirement in Chapter 61)
   - Certificate of Origin obtained from Mexican manufacturer

3. Duty Calculation (without USMCA):
   - Entered Value: $50,000
   - Duty (16.5%): $8,250
   - MPF (0.3464%, max $538.40): $173.20 (capped)
   - Total: $8,423.20

4. Duty Calculation (with USMCA):
   - Entered Value: $50,000
   - Duty (Free under USMCA): $0
   - MPF (0.3464%, max $538.40): $173.20
   - Total: $173.20

5. Savings: $8,250 (100% duty savings)

Entry Filing:
- Entry Type: 01 (Consumption Entry)
- Filed via ACE with USMCA Certificate of Origin
- Claimed Special Program Indicator (SPI): CA (USMCA)
- Entry released within 24 hours
- Total duties paid: $173.20 (MPF only)

Post-Clearance:
- Entry summary filed within 10 days
- USMCA certification retained for 5 years
- Recommended annual audit of Mexican supplier to ensure continued USMCA compliance

Outcome:
- Client saved $8,250 per shipment
- Annual savings: $165,000 (20 shipments/year)
- Landed cost reduced from $58,423 to $50,173 (14% reduction)
```

**Example 2: Electronics Import with Section 301 Duties**

```
Client: Electronics Distributor
Product: Wireless Bluetooth speakers
Origin: China
Value: $100,000 (10,000 units @ $10 each)
Terms: CIF Los Angeles

Pre-Clearance Analysis:
1. HTS Classification:
   - 8518.21.0000 (Loudspeakers, single, mounted in enclosures)
   - Column 1 General Rate: 4.9% ad valorem
   - Section 301 List 3: Additional 25% (imposed 2018-2019 trade war)

2. Country of Origin:
   - Manufactured in China
   - Section 301 tariffs applicable (China-origin goods)
   - No tariff exclusion available for this product

3. Partner Government Agency Requirements:
   - FCC: Equipment authorization required (FCC ID on product)
   - FCC Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (SDoC) obtained
   - No FDA or other agency holds

4. Duty Calculation:
   - Entered Value: $100,000
   - Duty (4.9%): $4,900
   - Section 301 Additional Duty (25%): $25,000
   - MPF (0.3464%, max $538.40): $346.40 (not capped)
   - HMF (0.125%): $125
   - Total: $30,371.40

Entry Filing:
- Entry Type: 01 (Consumption Entry)
- Filed via ACE with FCC Supplier's Declaration of Conformity
- Section 301 duties automatically assessed by ACE system
- Entry released after FCC hold cleared (24 hours)
- Total duties paid: $30,371.40

Advisory Recommendations:
1. Country of Origin Diversification:
   - Sourcing from Vietnam or Malaysia could eliminate $25,000 Section 301 duty
   - Estimated landed cost savings: 20% if sourcing shifted
   - Client exploring alternative suppliers

2. Duty Drawback Opportunity:
   - If goods are re-exported or incorporated into exported products, 99% of duties can be recovered
   - Client selling 20% of goods to Canadian customers
   - Potential drawback claim: $6,074 per year

Outcome:
- Compliant import despite high duty burden
- Client awareness of total landed cost: $130,371 ($13.04/unit vs. $10 invoice price)
- Strategic sourcing recommendations provided for future cost reduction
```

**Example 3: Food Import with FDA and USDA Requirements**

```
Client: Food Importer
Product: Frozen beef (boneless)
Origin: Australia
Value: $75,000 (15,000 lbs @ $5/lb)
Terms: CFR Los Angeles (Cost and Freight)

Pre-Clearance Analysis:
1. HTS Classification:
   - 0202.30.8000 (Meat of bovine animals, frozen, boneless, other than high-quality beef)
   - Column 1 General Rate: 4.4 cents/kg (specific duty)
   - Quantity: 15,000 lbs = 6,803.9 kg

2. Partner Government Agency Requirements:
   - **FDA Prior Notice**: Required for all food imports (must file before arrival)
   - **USDA FSIS**: Meat and poultry products require USDA import inspection
   - **USDA APHIS**: Veterinary certificate required from Australian authorities
   - **Country of Origin Labeling (COOL)**: Beef must be labeled with country of origin for retail sale

3. Regulatory Compliance:
   - Australia is approved source country for beef (USDA FSIS)
   - Australian establishment number must be on FSIS-approved list
   - Veterinary health certificate obtained from Australian Department of Agriculture
   - Product must be inspected at USDA-approved port (Los Angeles qualified)

4. Duty Calculation:
   - Entered Value: $75,000
   - Specific Duty (4.4 cents/kg × 6,803.9 kg): $299.37
   - MPF (0.3464%, max $538.40): $259.80
   - HMF (0.125%): $93.75
   - Total: $652.92

Entry Filing Process:
1. FDA Prior Notice filed 2 hours before arrival (via FDA PNSR system)
2. Customs entry filed via ACE with USDA/FSIS affirmation
3. USDA notified of arrival for reinspection scheduling
4. Product held at port-approved cold storage facility
5. USDA inspector conducts physical examination (2-5% sampling)
6. USDA releases shipment after inspection approval
7. CBP releases entry after USDA clearance

Timeline:
- Day 1: Arrival, FDA Prior Notice filed, customs entry filed
- Day 2: USDA inspection scheduled
- Day 3: USDA inspection conducted, passed
- Day 4: CBP release issued, delivery authorized
- Total: 4 days from arrival to delivery

Post-Clearance:
- Entry summary filed within 10 days
- USDA and FDA documentation retained for 2 years (FDA) and 5 years (CBP)
- Recommended quarterly compliance review with Australian supplier

Outcome:
- Compliant import with FDA and USDA requirements met
- 4-day clearance (typical for perishable food with inspection)
- Total landed cost: $75,653 ($5.04/lb including duties and fees)
- Client advised to maintain USDA-approved supplier list and current veterinary certificates
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule)**: US tariff classification system based on international HS codes
- **CBP (Customs and Border Protection)**: US government agency enforcing customs and immigration laws
- **ACE (Automated Commercial Environment)**: CBP's electronic system for entry filing and cargo tracking
- **Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501)**: Primary document for customs clearance and duty payment
- **Country of Origin**: Country where goods were manufactured or substantially transformed
- **Incoterms**: International commercial terms defining buyer/seller responsibilities (FOB, CIF, DDP, etc.)
- **MPF (Merchandise Processing Fee)**: Ad valorem fee (0.3464%) charged for customs processing
- **HMF (Harbor Maintenance Fee)**: Fee (0.125%) on cargo arriving by vessel
- **Duty Drawback**: Refund of duties paid on imported goods that are re-exported
- **C-TPAT (Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism)**: Voluntary supply chain security program
- **Related Party Transaction**: Import between parties with business or family relationship (affects valuation)

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **19 USC (United States Code Title 19)**: Primary customs law (Tariff Act of 1930, as amended)
- **19 CFR (Code of Federal Regulations Title 19)**: CBP regulations implementing customs law
- **HTSUS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States)**: 2024 edition, updated annually
- **USMCA (US-Mexico-Canada Agreement)**: Free trade agreement effective July 2020 (replaced NAFTA)
- **WCO (World Customs Organization) Harmonized System**: International tariff nomenclature (6-digit codes)
- **INCOTERMS 2020**: ICC commercial terms (FOB, CIF, DDP, etc.)
- **CBP Informed Compliance Publications**: Guidance on classification, valuation, country of origin
- **Section 321 De Minimis**: Exemption for shipments valued under $800
- **Section 301 Tariffs**: Additional duties on China-origin goods (List 1, 2, 3, 4)

**Common Tools:**
- **ACE (Automated Commercial Environment)**: CBP's electronic entry filing system
- **ACE Portal**: Web interface for entry filing, status tracking, account management
- **ACE Reports**: ABI (Automated Broker Interface) data transmission
- **ABI (Automated Broker Interface)**: Electronic data interchange for entry filing
- **CBP CROSS**: CBP's online search system for rulings and decisions
- **USITC Tariff Database**: Interactive HTS lookup with duty rates and trade data
- **FDA PNSR (Prior Notice System Record)**: FDA's electronic prior notice filing system
- **USDA APHIS VIPS**: Veterinary import permit system
- **Flexport, Freightos, Cargowise**: Customs brokerage and freight forwarding software
- **ACE Secure Data Portal**: Document imaging and submission to CBP
