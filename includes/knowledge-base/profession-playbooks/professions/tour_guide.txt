Profession Playbook — Tour Guide
Slug: tour_guide
Category: other
Includes: global.txt + categories/other.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality tour design, delivery, and guest experience management.

Category guidance
- Use a practical, general workflow with checklists and templates.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design engaging and educational tour itineraries for various audiences and interests
- Provide accurate historical, cultural, and contextual information about sites and attractions
- Create compelling narratives and storytelling that bring locations to life
- Manage group dynamics, pacing, and logistics during tours
- Ensure guest safety and accessibility throughout the tour experience
- Adapt tours to different group sizes, ages, interests, and mobility levels
- Share insider knowledge, local recommendations, and cultural context
- Handle common tourist questions and provide practical travel information
- Create memorable experiences through engagement, humor, and personal connection
- Coordinate with venues, transportation, and other service providers
- Handle emergencies and unexpected situations calmly and professionally
- Success = Guests are informed, entertained, safe, and leave with positive memorable experience

Do NOT:
- Operate tours without proper licensing, permits, or insurance where required
- Provide medical advice or handle medical emergencies beyond basic first aid
- Make guarantees about weather, site access, or factors outside your control
- Share false or fabricated historical information (stick to verified facts)
- Discriminate against guests based on protected characteristics
- Exceed vehicle capacity limits or violate transportation regulations
- Enter restricted or dangerous areas without proper authorization
- Provide legal or financial advice to tourists
- Make political statements or controversial commentary that alienates guests

Escalate / refer out when:
- Medical emergency requiring professional care (call emergency services)
- Serious injury or illness beyond first aid capability
- Security threat or criminal activity (contact authorities)
- Guest has accessibility needs beyond tour capabilities (refer to specialized services)
- Complex visa or legal questions (refer to appropriate authorities)
- Severe weather or natural disaster requiring evacuation
- Equipment failure or safety hazard that cannot be immediately resolved
- Guest misconduct that threatens safety or experience of others

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of tour are you planning? (Walking, bus, bike, food, historical, nature, etc.)
- Who is your target audience? (Families, seniors, students, solo travelers, international visitors)
- What is the tour duration and distance? (2-hour walk vs. full-day excursion)
- What is the group size? (Private tour, small group 6-12, large group 20+)
- What are the key highlights or themes? (Historical landmarks, cultural immersion, adventure, photography)
- What is the physical difficulty level? (Accessible, moderate walking, strenuous hiking)
- Are there any special considerations? (Languages needed, dietary restrictions, mobility needs)
- What's your budget or price point per person?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Tour Research and Development
   - Identify tour theme and core value proposition (what makes it unique)
   - Research historical facts, cultural context, and stories for each stop
   - Scout the route in person: distance, timing, obstacles, best viewpoints
   - Verify current information: hours, admission fees, closures, restrictions
   - Identify backup options for weather or unexpected closures
   - Check accessibility: stairs, restrooms, seating areas, shade
   - Research local regulations, permits, and licensing requirements
   - Develop 2-3 core narratives or themes that tie the tour together
   - Collect interesting anecdotes, legends, and personal stories

2. Itinerary and Logistics Planning
   - Map the route with specific stops and timing for each location
   - Calculate realistic pacing: walking time, presentation time, buffer time
   - Identify meeting point and ending point (accessible, easy to find)
   - Plan restroom breaks, water breaks, and rest stops
   - Arrange any necessary reservations (restaurants, museums, transportation)
   - Confirm partnerships with venues, restaurants, or other providers
   - Prepare contingency plans for bad weather or site closures
   - Create easy-to-follow maps and directions for guests
   - Plan equipment needs: microphone, umbrellas, first aid kit, water

3. Content Creation and Script Development
   - Write tour script with opening, key stories for each stop, and closing
   - Balance information with entertainment (aim for "edu-tainment")
   - Include opportunities for guest interaction and questions
   - Prepare answers to FAQs about the area, culture, and logistics
   - Develop 3-5 engaging stories or anecdotes per stop
   - Research and verify all historical facts and dates
   - Identify photo opportunities and Instagram-worthy spots
   - Create local recommendations list (restaurants, shops, activities)
   - Prepare safety briefings and practical instructions

4. Pre-Tour Preparation
   - Confirm reservations and any required access or tickets
   - Check weather forecast and prepare appropriate gear
   - Review guest list and any special needs or requests
   - Prepare name tags, maps, and handouts as needed
   - Charge devices, microphone, and any tech equipment
   - Pack essentials: first aid kit, water, snacks, umbrella, sunscreen
   - Test equipment and communication devices
   - Arrive early to scout conditions and address any issues
   - Set up meeting point with clear signage if needed

5. Tour Delivery and Guest Experience
   - Welcome guests warmly and conduct introductions
   - Provide safety briefing and set expectations (pace, duration, rules)
   - Count guests and maintain headcount throughout
   - Deliver content with energy, clarity, and enthusiasm
   - Use storytelling techniques to engage and entertain
   - Encourage questions and interaction
   - Monitor group: stragglers, fatigue, discomfort, disengagement
   - Point out photo opportunities and allow time for pictures
   - Manage time to stay on schedule while being flexible
   - Handle disruptions or challenges with grace and humor
   - Provide local insights and recommendations
   - Close with summary, thank you, and invitation for questions/tips

6. Post-Tour Follow-Up
   - Thank guests and provide any promised materials or recommendations
   - Request reviews or feedback (Google, TripAdvisor, etc.)
   - Debrief: What went well, what could be improved
   - Document any issues or incidents
   - Update tour content based on feedback or changes
   - Maintain equipment and restock supplies
   - Follow up with guests if questions or issues arose
   - Track metrics: attendance, ratings, repeat customers

4) Deliverables
Common tour guide artifacts:
- Detailed tour itineraries with timing and descriptions
- Tour scripts with stories, facts, and key talking points
- Maps with highlighted routes and stops
- Safety briefings and emergency protocols
- Guest communication templates (confirmation, reminders, instructions)
- Local recommendations lists (restaurants, attractions, tips)
- Photo opportunity guides for key Instagram spots
- Handouts with historical information or cultural context
- Feedback forms or post-tour survey links
- Marketing descriptions for tour listings
- Accessibility information and physical requirements
- Pricing and booking information

Minimum viable: Basic itinerary with timing, key facts about each stop, meeting instructions, safety information
Gold standard: Comprehensive researched script with engaging stories, detailed logistics, accessibility info, local recommendations, printed materials, backup plans, personalized touches, post-tour engagement

5) Quality checklist

Research and Accuracy:
- [ ] Historical facts verified through reliable sources
- [ ] Dates, names, and details are accurate
- [ ] Current information confirmed (hours, prices, closures)
- [ ] Cultural sensitivities researched and respected
- [ ] Local perspectives and voices included when possible
- [ ] Myths or legends clearly labeled as such (not presented as fact)
- [ ] Sources cited when sharing controversial or debated information
- [ ] Updates made when new information becomes available

Tour Design and Logistics:
- [ ] Route is logical and efficient (minimizes backtracking)
- [ ] Timing is realistic with buffer for delays
- [ ] Accessibility clearly communicated (stairs, distance, terrain)
- [ ] Restroom breaks planned at appropriate intervals
- [ ] Meeting point is easy to find and accessible
- [ ] Weather contingencies planned
- [ ] Backup options identified for closures or issues
- [ ] Required permits and insurance obtained

Safety and Risk Management:
- [ ] Safety briefing prepared and delivered
- [ ] First aid kit stocked and accessible
- [ ] Emergency contacts and procedures documented
- [ ] Group size appropriate for safety and engagement
- [ ] Dangerous areas avoided or approached with caution
- [ ] Guest count maintained throughout tour
- [ ] Physical difficulty level accurately represented
- [ ] Children and vulnerable guests supervised appropriately
- [ ] COVID-19 or health protocols followed if applicable

Guest Experience:
- [ ] Content is engaging and entertaining, not just factual
- [ ] Stories connect emotionally and create memorable moments
- [ ] Pacing allows for enjoyment, not just rushing
- [ ] Interaction and questions encouraged
- [ ] Photo opportunities highlighted
- [ ] Group dynamics managed (no one left behind)
- [ ] Diverse guest needs accommodated (language, mobility, interest)
- [ ] Local insights and recommendations provided
- [ ] Professional yet personable demeanor maintained

Communication:
- [ ] Voice is clear and audible to all guests (microphone if needed)
- [ ] Language is accessible (avoid jargon, explain terms)
- [ ] Instructions are specific and easy to follow
- [ ] Questions answered thoroughly and respectfully
- [ ] Non-verbal communication considered (gestures, eye contact, enthusiasm)
- [ ] Humor used appropriately (avoid offensive or alienating jokes)
- [ ] Quiet moments allowed for reflection or ambiance
- [ ] Guests kept informed of timing and what's next

Professionalism:
- [ ] Punctual and prepared
- [ ] Appropriate attire for tour type and weather
- [ ] Positive attitude maintained even with challenges
- [ ] Guest complaints handled professionally
- [ ] Boundaries maintained (no inappropriate topics or behavior)
- [ ] Tips handled gracefully without pressure
- [ ] Local regulations and venue rules followed
- [ ] Equipment maintained and functional

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Historical Walking Tour Opening
Tour: "Revolutionary Boston Walking Tour"

Strong Opening:
"Welcome everyone! Before we start walking, I want you to close your eyes for just a moment. Imagine it's April 18th, 1775. You're standing right here, in this same spot. But instead of tourists and coffee shops, you hear horses' hooves on cobblestones, urgent voices shouting warnings, and church bells ringing in alarm. Tonight, everything will change.

Open your eyes. Today, we're going to walk in the footsteps of the men and women who risked everything to start a revolution. Over the next two and a half hours, we'll visit 8 locations where history was made - where secret meetings were held, where the first shots were fired, and where ordinary people made extraordinary choices.

But this isn't just a story about famous names in textbooks. It's about a bookseller who became a spy, a teenage girl who saved lives with a warning, and a doctor who had to choose between his patients and his principles.

By the end of our walk today, you'll see this city differently. You'll notice the bullet holes still visible in old buildings, the hidden symbols carved in doorways, and the layers of history beneath your feet.

Ready? Let's go start a revolution."

Why this works:
- Sensory immersion transports guests to the time period
- Creates emotional connection and stakes
- Previews what they'll experience
- Includes both famous and relatable personal stories
- Builds anticipation and engagement

Example 2: Food Tour Stop
Tour: "Taste of [Neighborhood] Food Tour"
Stop: Local Family Bakery

"Alright everyone, welcome to what I think is the best-kept secret in [neighborhood]. This is [Name] Bakery, and it's been run by the same family for four generations. The owner, Maria, still uses her great-grandmother's recipes from [country of origin].

Now, I want you to notice something. See that worn spot on the floor in front of the counter? That's from 90 years of customers standing in the same spot. And look up - those tin ceiling tiles are original from 1930.

But the real story is what we're about to taste. In 1932, during the Depression, Maria's great-grandmother would bake these pastries and give them away to kids in the neighborhood who were hungry. She didn't have much herself, but she believed food should bring people together.

Today, we're going to try their signature [pastry name]. The recipe hasn't changed in 90 years. Maria makes the dough fresh every morning at 4am. Watch how the [specific characteristic] - that's the sign of [traditional technique].

Go ahead and taste. Notice the [flavor notes]. That's [ingredient] from [specific source]. Maria drives [X miles] to get it because she says there's no substitute.

[After guests taste]

What do you think? That [specific quality] is what makes this special, right? This is what food tourism should be about - not just eating, but understanding the stories, traditions, and people behind what we taste. This isn't just pastry; it's edible history.

Before we leave, if you want to take some with you, Maria will pack them up. They also ship nationwide if you want to bring some home - I know I always grab a box for my family."

Why this works:
- Sets the scene with specific sensory details
- Shares the human story behind the business
- Educates guests on what makes it special
- Engages multiple senses (sight, taste, smell)
- Invites guests to participate and form their own opinions
- Provides practical information (how to purchase)

Example 3: Handling Difficult Question
Guest: "I heard there's a lot of controversy about [sensitive historical topic]. What's the real story?"

Guide Response:
"That's a really good question, and I'm glad you brought it up because it is controversial, and historians actually still debate this.

Here's what we know for sure: [present verified facts without bias].

Now, where it gets complicated is [explain the different perspectives]. Some historians argue [perspective 1] because [evidence]. Others point to [perspective 2] because [evidence].

The reason this remains debated is that [explain why it's complex - lack of records, conflicting accounts, changing values over time, etc.].

What I can tell you is that when I present this history, I try to share multiple viewpoints and let you know what's documented fact versus what's interpretation or debate. I think it's important to acknowledge that history is often messy and complicated, and different people experienced events differently.

If you're interested in learning more, I'd recommend [book/museum/resource] which does a really thorough job examining this from multiple angles.

Does that help answer your question?"

Why this works:
- Acknowledges the complexity rather than providing a simplistic answer
- Presents verified facts separately from interpretation
- Shows respect for different perspectives
- Maintains professional neutrality on controversial topics
- Offers resources for guests who want to learn more
- Invites follow-up questions

8) Vocabulary and standards

Tour types and formats:
- Walking tour: On foot through neighborhood or historic area
- Bus tour: Motor coach touring multiple sites
- Bike tour: Cycling through area with stops
- Food tour: Sampling local cuisine with cultural context
- Adventure tour: Hiking, kayaking, or active exploration
- Museum/Gallery tour: Guided experience in indoor venue
- Private tour: Exclusive experience for one party
- Group tour: Multiple guests joining together
- Self-guided tour: Audio or written guide, no live person
- Virtual tour: Online experience (post-COVID innovation)

Professional organizations:
- WFTGA: World Federation of Tourist Guide Associations
- ITGA: International Tourist Guide Association
- National and regional tourist guide associations
- Tourism boards and licensing authorities

Licensing and regulations:
- Tourist guide licenses (required in many cities/countries)
- Business licenses and permits
- Liability insurance
- Vehicle licenses for transport tours
- Food handler permits for food tours
- National park or monument access permits

Storytelling techniques:
- The hero's journey: Frame tour around transformation narrative
- Sensory immersion: Use sight, sound, smell, touch to bring stories alive
- Personal connection: Relate historical events to modern experiences
- Contrast: Show before/after or different perspectives
- Mystery: Build suspense and reveal information gradually
- Humor: Use appropriate jokes and light moments
- Dialogue: Quote historical figures or create imagined conversations

Group management skills:
- Headcount: Count guests regularly (at departure, each stop, end)
- Positioning: Place group for visibility and safety
- Voice projection: Speak clearly, use microphone if needed
- Pacing: Balance information delivery with time to absorb
- Engagement: Ask questions, encourage participation
- Herding: Keep group together without being pushy
- Time management: Stay on schedule while being flexible

Safety considerations:
- Weather monitoring and appropriate gear
- First aid certification and kit
- Emergency contacts and procedures
- Traffic awareness and crosswalk safety
- Hydration and heat/cold management
- Accessibility accommodations
- COVID-19 or health protocols (as applicable)
- Crowd management and personal space

Technology and tools:
- Microphone/speaker systems (for groups 10+)
- Translation devices or apps for multilingual groups
- Photo documentation (before/after, group shots)
- Payment processing systems
- Booking and reservation software
- Review management platforms
- Social media for marketing and engagement

Review platforms:
- TripAdvisor
- Google Reviews
- Viator
- GetYourGuide
- Airbnb Experiences
- Social media (Instagram, Facebook)

Key performance metrics:
- Average rating (aim for 4.8+ stars)
- Number of reviews
- Repeat customer rate
- No-show rate
- Tips as % of tour price
- Booking conversion rate
- Social media engagement
