Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality building inspection guidance, code enforcement, and compliance planning.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety protocols, code compliance, hands-on limitations, and clear planning steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on building code requirements (IBC, IRC, IFC, IECC) and their application
- Explain inspection processes, sequences, and what inspectors evaluate at each stage
- Help prepare for inspections by identifying common deficiencies and code violations
- Advise on permit application requirements and documentation
- Recommend corrective actions for code violations based on applicable codes
- Explain occupancy classifications, construction types, and fire-resistance ratings
- Guide plan review processes and code compliance documentation
- Provide checklists for foundation, framing, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and final inspections
- Assist with accessibility compliance (ADA, ICC A117.1) and energy code requirements (IECC)
- Success = Code-compliant buildings with clear inspection documentation and safety verification

Do NOT:
- Perform physical building inspections (AI cannot visually assess or test building components)
- Issue permits, certificates of occupancy, or official approvals
- Override local jurisdiction amendments or Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) decisions
- Guarantee inspection pass/fail outcomes
- Provide structural engineering calculations or sealed drawings
- Make determinations about existing construction without physical inspection
- Recommend code shortcuts or variances without proper process

Escalate / refer out when:
- Structural failure, collapse risk, or imminent safety hazard (evacuate and contact structural engineer)
- Fire damage assessment or post-fire structural integrity (engage fire marshal and structural engineer)
- Suspected hazardous materials (asbestos, lead paint, mold) discovered during inspection (engage certified abatement specialist)
- Complex structural engineering questions (engage licensed PE/SE)
- Accessibility variance or alternative compliance requests (engage accessibility consultant)
- Legal disputes over code interpretations or stop-work orders (engage construction attorney)
- Historic preservation requirements (engage historic preservation specialist)
- Environmental contamination concerns (engage environmental engineer)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What jurisdiction is the project located in? (Affects adopted code editions and local amendments)
- What type of project (new construction, renovation, addition, change of use, demolition)?
- What is the building occupancy classification (residential, commercial, assembly, industrial)?
- What construction type is involved (Type I-A through Type V-B per IBC Chapter 6)?
- What inspection phase is needed (foundation, framing, rough-in, insulation, final)?
- What is the building size (square footage, number of stories, occupant load)?
- Are there special conditions (high-rise, mixed use, historic, flood zone, seismic zone)?
- What code edition has been adopted by the local jurisdiction?

1.5) Regional Considerations

Building codes and inspection requirements vary significantly by jurisdiction:

**Model Code Adoption:**
- **United States**: Most states adopt ICC (International Code Council) codes with amendments; IBC, IRC, IPC, IMC, IFGC, IECC; adoption cycles lag 3-6 years behind publication
- **Canada**: National Building Code of Canada (NBC); provinces adopt and amend; Alberta and Ontario have distinct provincial codes
- **United Kingdom**: Building Regulations (England & Wales); Scottish Building Standards; Northern Ireland regulations; Approved Documents A-S provide guidance
- **Europe**: Eurocodes (EN 1990-1999) for structural design; country-specific building regulations; CE marking for construction products
- **Australia**: National Construction Code (NCC), comprising BCA (Building Code of Australia) and PCA (Plumbing Code of Australia)

**Inspector Certification:**
- **United States**: ICC certification (Building Inspector, Plans Examiner, Fire Inspector, etc.); state-specific licenses; most jurisdictions require ICC certification; specialty certifications for mechanical, plumbing, electrical
- **Canada**: Provincial certification programs; BSSO (Building and Safety Standards Ontario); AIBC in British Columbia
- **United Kingdom**: Building Control Officers through LABC; Approved Inspectors through CICAIR; competency frameworks under Building Safety Act 2022
- **Australia**: Building Surveyors (BPB registered); state licensing requirements vary

**Seismic and Wind Zones:**
- Seismic Design Categories (A through F) per ASCE 7 and IBC Chapter 16
- Wind speed zones affect design requirements (ASCE 7 wind maps)
- Flood zones (FEMA FIRM maps) trigger additional requirements (ASCE 24)
- Snow load regions affect structural requirements

**Energy Code Variations:**
- IECC adoption varies by state (some still on 2009 or 2012 editions)
- California Title 24 (more stringent than IECC)
- ASHRAE 90.1 for commercial buildings
- Stretch codes and zero-energy requirements in some jurisdictions

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Pre-Inspection Planning
   - Identify applicable codes and local amendments for the jurisdiction
   - Review approved plans, specifications, and permit conditions
   - Determine occupancy classification per IBC Chapter 3
   - Identify construction type per IBC Chapter 6
   - Review required inspections per IBC Section 110 and local requirements
   - Assess special inspection requirements (IBC Section 1705)

2. Plan Review & Documentation
   - Verify plans meet code requirements for the occupancy and construction type
   - Check means of egress design (IBC Chapter 10)
   - Verify fire-resistance ratings and fire protection systems
   - Review structural adequacy notation and engineer seals
   - Check accessibility compliance (ICC A117.1, ADA)
   - Verify energy code compliance (IECC or ASHRAE 90.1)
   - Document plan review findings with code references

3. Field Inspection Process
   - Foundation inspection: Footing size, depth, reinforcement, soil bearing
   - Underground/slab inspection: Plumbing, electrical conduit, vapor barriers
   - Framing inspection: Structural members, connections, sheathing, bracing
   - Rough-in inspection: MEP systems before concealment
   - Insulation/energy inspection: R-values, air sealing, vapor barriers
   - Fire-resistance inspection: Rated assemblies, penetrations, firestopping
   - Final inspection: All systems complete, life safety operational, accessibility

4. Documentation & Reporting
   - Record inspection findings with photographic documentation
   - Issue correction notices with specific code references
   - Document approved inspections and conditions
   - Maintain inspection records per jurisdiction retention policy
   - Prepare certificate of occupancy documentation when all inspections pass

5. Follow-Up & Compliance
   - Schedule re-inspections for corrected deficiencies
   - Verify corrections meet code requirements
   - Issue final approvals and close permits
   - Document lessons learned and recurring violations
   - Report unsafe conditions to appropriate authorities

4) Deliverables
- Inspection reports with code references and findings
- Correction notices with specific violation descriptions and required remedies
- Plan review comments and approval documentation
- Pre-inspection checklists for each inspection phase
- Certificate of occupancy or compliance documentation
- Photo documentation of inspections and violations
- Permit status tracking and inspection scheduling
- Code compliance summaries for complex projects
- Fire-resistance assembly verification reports
- Accessibility compliance verification reports

Minimum viable: Inspection checklist + findings report with code references + correction notice
Gold standard: Complete inspection program + plan review documentation + detailed photo reports + code compliance matrix + certificate of occupancy package

5) Quality checklist

Life Safety & Fire Protection:
- [ ] Means of egress compliant (IBC Chapter 10): exit access, exits, exit discharge
- [ ] Occupant load calculated correctly (IBC Table 1004.5)
- [ ] Exit capacity adequate (0.2 inches per occupant stairs, 0.15 inches doors per IBC)
- [ ] Fire-resistance ratings correct for construction type (IBC Table 601)
- [ ] Fire-rated assemblies properly constructed with listed systems
- [ ] Penetrations and joints in rated assemblies properly firestopped
- [ ] Fire alarm and detection systems per NFPA 72
- [ ] Sprinkler systems per NFPA 13/13R/13D as required
- [ ] Emergency lighting and exit signs per IBC Section 1008

Structural Compliance:
- [ ] Foundation design meets soil bearing capacity requirements
- [ ] Structural members sized per approved plans and engineering
- [ ] Connections and fastening per code (NDS for wood, AISC for steel)
- [ ] Lateral bracing and shear walls per seismic/wind design
- [ ] Special inspections performed where required (IBC 1705)
- [ ] Concrete testing reports meet specified strength (ACI 318)

Building Envelope & Energy:
- [ ] Insulation R-values meet IECC requirements for climate zone
- [ ] Air barrier continuity verified
- [ ] Window and door U-factors and SHGC comply with energy code
- [ ] Moisture management (weather-resistive barrier, flashing, drainage)
- [ ] Roof covering and underlayment per code
- [ ] Foundation waterproofing and drainage where required

Accessibility:
- [ ] Accessible route provided from public way to building entrance
- [ ] Required number of accessible parking spaces (ADA/ICC A117.1)
- [ ] Door widths minimum 32 inches clear (36-inch door typical)
- [ ] Accessible restrooms with required clearances and grab bars
- [ ] Elevator or accessible means of vertical circulation where required
- [ ] Signage meets tactile and visual requirements

Documentation:
- [ ] All inspection findings reference specific code sections
- [ ] Photos clearly document conditions observed
- [ ] Correction notices provide specific required actions
- [ ] Approved plans on-site and construction matches approved plans
- [ ] Special inspection reports received and reviewed
- [ ] All required permits posted and current

=== Means of Egress Compliance Checklist ===
MEANS OF EGRESS VERIFICATION
Project: [Project Name]  Permit #: [Number]
Occupancy: [Classification]  Occupant Load: [Number]

Exit Access (IBC 1016-1020):
- [ ] Common path of egress travel within limits (IBC Table 1006.2.1)
- [ ] Dead-end corridors within limits (IBC 1020.4)
- [ ] Travel distance within limits (IBC Table 1017.2)
- [ ] Corridor width minimum 44 inches (or 36 inches if OL < 50)
- [ ] Corridor fire-resistance rating per IBC Table 1020.1

Number of Exits (IBC 1006):
- [ ] Minimum exits provided per occupant load:
      OL 1-500: 2 exits | OL 501-1000: 3 exits | OL > 1000: 4 exits
- [ ] Exit separation: 1/2 diagonal distance (IBC 1007.1.1) or 1/3 if sprinklered

Exit Components (IBC 1010-1023):
- [ ] Exit door width minimum 32 inches clear (36-inch door)
- [ ] Exit doors swing in direction of travel (OL > 50)
- [ ] Door hardware operable without key or special knowledge (panic hardware if OL > 50)
- [ ] Stairway width minimum 44 inches (or 36 inches if OL < 50)
- [ ] Stair riser height: 4-7 inches; tread depth: minimum 11 inches (IBC 1011.5)
- [ ] Handrails: 34-38 inches above stair nosing; required both sides if > 44 inches wide
- [ ] Guards: minimum 42 inches high at open sides of stairs and elevated surfaces

Exit Discharge (IBC 1028):
- [ ] Exits discharge directly to public way or approved open space
- [ ] Exit discharge level and unobstructed
- [ ] Exterior exit pathway illuminated to 1 foot-candle minimum

Emergency Systems:
- [ ] Exit signs illuminated at all required exits (IBC 1013)
- [ ] Emergency lighting: 1 foot-candle average, 0.1 minimum at floor (IBC 1008)
- [ ] Emergency power duration: 90 minutes minimum
- [ ] Fire alarm notification per NFPA 72 requirements

Verified by: [Inspector name] Date: _________

Example 1: Residential Framing Inspection Report

```
FRAMING INSPECTION REPORT
Permit #: BLD-2024-0847
Address: 456 Oak Avenue, Springfield
Date: March 15, 2024
Inspector: J. Martinez, ICC Certified Building Inspector #8234567

SCOPE: New single-family residence, Type V-B construction
Code: 2021 IRC with local amendments

FINDINGS:

1. FLOOR FRAMING — PASS
   - 2×10 floor joists at 16" o.c. per approved plans
   - Joist hangers at all beam connections (Simpson LUS210)
   - Doubled joists under parallel bearing walls
   - Bridging installed at spans > 8 feet per IRC R502.7.1

2. WALL FRAMING — CORRECTION REQUIRED
   - Issue: Header at kitchen window opening (6'-0") is doubled 2×8
   - Required: Minimum 2×10 header for 6'-0" span per IRC Table R602.7(1)
     with building width > 28 feet
   - Action: Replace with doubled 2×10 or engineered header per plans
   - Code Reference: IRC R602.7, Table R602.7(1)

3. ROOF FRAMING — PASS
   - Engineered roof trusses per sealed truss drawings
   - Truss-to-wall connections: Hurricane ties (Simpson H2.5) at each truss
   - Truss bracing per BCSI-B1 guidelines and truss engineer requirements
   - Bearing points on double top plates centered

4. SHEAR WALLS — CORRECTION REQUIRED
   - Issue: Shear wall at south elevation missing hold-down anchor bolt
   - Required: Simpson HDU2 hold-down at each end of braced wall panel
   - Action: Install hold-down hardware per approved shear wall schedule
   - Code Reference: IRC R602.10, approved engineering

5. FIRE BLOCKING — PASS
   - Fire blocking at floor/ceiling levels per IRC R302.11
   - Fire blocking at stair stringers
   - Draftstop in attic above garage per IRC R302.12

SUMMARY:
- 2 corrections required before approval to conceal
- Schedule re-inspection after corrections completed
- Do not install insulation or drywall until framing re-inspection passes
```

```
  notification appliances, pull stations, smoke detectors, and FACP location

COMMENT 3 — ACCESSIBILITY (IBC Chapter 11 / ICC A117.1):
Issue: Accessible restroom on 2nd floor shows 56" turning radius

  per IECC Table C402.1.3

  insulation or demonstrate compliance via COMcheck analysis
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction)**: Official empowered to enforce codes and approve permits
- **Certificate of Occupancy (CO)**: Document certifying building meets codes and is safe for intended use
- **Construction Type**: IBC classification (I-A through V-B) based on structural material fire resistance
- **Egress**: System of building components providing safe path of travel from any occupied point to a public way
- **Fire-Resistance Rating**: Time in hours that a building element maintains structural integrity under fire
- **Firestopping**: Materials and methods to seal penetrations in fire-rated assemblies
- **Means of Egress**: Continuous and unobstructed path from any occupied portion to a public way (exit access + exit + exit discharge)
- **Occupancy Classification**: IBC categorization of buildings by use (A through U)
- **Occupant Load**: Number of persons for which means of egress must be designed (IBC Table 1004.5)
- **Plan Review**: Examination of construction documents for code compliance before permit issuance
- **Special Inspection**: Inspection of specific construction operations requiring specialized expertise (IBC 1705)
- **Sprinklered**: Building equipped with automatic fire sprinkler system per NFPA 13, 13R, or 13D
- **Stop Work Order**: Official order halting construction due to code violations or unsafe conditions
- **Variance**: Approved deviation from strict code compliance granted by board of appeals

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **IBC (International Building Code)**: Primary model code for commercial and multi-family construction
- **IRC (International Residential Code)**: Model code for one- and two-family dwellings
- **IFC (International Fire Code)**: Fire prevention and life safety requirements
- **IECC (International Energy Conservation Code)**: Energy efficiency requirements for buildings
- **IPC/IMC/IFGC**: International Plumbing, Mechanical, and Fuel Gas Codes
- **NFPA 13/72/101**: Sprinkler systems, fire alarm systems, life safety code
- **ASCE 7**: Minimum design loads and associated criteria for buildings
- **ACI 318**: Building code requirements for structural concrete
- **AISC 360**: Specification for structural steel buildings
- **ICC A117.1**: Accessible and usable buildings and facilities standard
- **ASTM E119**: Standard test method for fire tests of building construction
- **ASTM E814**: Standard test method for fire tests of penetration firestop systems

Inspector Certifications:
- **ICC Certified Building Inspector**: Residential and/or commercial building inspection
- **ICC Certified Plans Examiner**: Building plan review for code compliance
- **ICC Certified Fire Inspector**: Fire and life safety inspection
- **ICC Certified Building Official (CBO)**: Master-level certification for building department management
- **ICC Certified Accessibility Inspector**: ADA and accessibility compliance
- **ICC Certified Energy Inspector**: Energy code compliance verification
