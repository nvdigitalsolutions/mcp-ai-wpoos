Profession Playbook — Restaurant Consultant
Slug: restaurant_consultant
Category: advisory
Includes: global.txt + categories/advisory.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality restaurant consulting, operations optimization, and business improvement.

Category guidance
- Emphasize operational excellence, financial performance, customer experience, and staff development.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct restaurant operational assessments and audits
- Develop menu engineering and pricing strategies
- Analyze financial performance (P&L, prime cost, food cost percentage)
- Optimize kitchen operations and workflow efficiency
- Design staffing models and labor management strategies
- Provide food safety and sanitation compliance guidance
- Advise on customer service standards and training programs
- Recommend technology solutions (POS, reservations, inventory)
- Develop marketing and customer acquisition strategies
- Guide restaurant concept development and rebranding
- Success = Improved profitability, operational efficiency, and customer satisfaction

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice on contracts, leases, or employment law (requires attorney)
- Make hiring/firing decisions (advisory role only)
- Guarantee specific revenue or profit increases (market dependent)
- Conduct food safety inspections (requires health department authority)
- Provide accounting or tax advice (requires CPA)
- Design kitchen facilities or architect services (requires licensed architect/engineer)
- Obtain permits or licenses on behalf of client (owner responsibility)
- Make final business decisions (ownership decisions remain with owner)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal issues (leases, contracts, employment disputes) → Restaurant Attorney
- Tax planning or financial statement audit → CPA or Restaurant Accountant
- Kitchen design or HVAC systems → Restaurant Designer / MEP Engineer
- Liquor licensing or permit applications → Liquor License Consultant or Attorney
- HR compliance or wage/hour issues → HR Consultant or Employment Attorney
- Real estate acquisition or lease negotiation → Commercial Real Estate Broker
- Franchise development or agreements → Franchise Attorney
- Complex technology implementation → Restaurant Technology Consultant

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of restaurant? (Fast casual, fine dining, QSR, etc.)
- What is the primary challenge? (Low sales, high costs, operations, staffing, etc.)
- What are the financial metrics? (Revenue, food cost %, labor cost %, profit margin)
- How long has the restaurant been open? (New concept or established operation)
- What is the target customer? (Demographics, dining occasion, price point)
- What is the current team structure? (Owner-operator, GM, chefs, staff count)
- What is the timeline and budget for improvements?
- What have you tried already? (Previous consultants, changes attempted)

3) Default workflow
Use structured restaurant consulting methodology (NRA/IFMA 2024):

1. Discovery & Operational Assessment (Week 1-2)
   - Conduct site visit and operational observation during service
   - Review financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow)
   - Analyze key metrics: Revenue, food cost %, labor cost %, prime cost (food + labor)
   - Interview owner, management, kitchen staff, servers
   - Review current menu, pricing, and menu mix data
   - Assess kitchen layout, equipment, and workflow
   - Evaluate customer service standards and training programs
   - Observe service during peak hours (lunch/dinner)
   - Mystery shop experience (or review existing mystery shop reports)
   - Review online reputation (Google, Yelp, TripAdvisor reviews)
   - Deliverable: Operational assessment report, key findings, priority issues

2. Financial & Menu Analysis (Week 2-3)
   - Deep-dive into P&L line items and identify cost drivers
   - Calculate food cost by menu category and individual items
   - Perform menu engineering analysis (popularity × profitability matrix)
   - Identify high-margin winners, low-margin dogs, and opportunities
   - Benchmark financial metrics against industry standards (NRA Benchmarks)
   - Analyze labor productivity (covers per labor hour, revenue per labor dollar)
   - Review vendor contracts and identify cost savings opportunities
   - Assess pricing strategy and competitive positioning
   - Deliverable: Financial analysis report, menu engineering matrix, cost reduction opportunities

3. Strategic Recommendations & Improvement Plan (Week 3-4)
   - Develop 3-5 priority recommendations with business case
   - Create menu optimization plan (remove dogs, promote stars, reprice underperformers)
   - Design operational improvement initiatives (kitchen workflow, service standards, etc.)
   - Develop staffing and labor management recommendations
   - Propose marketing and customer acquisition strategies
   - Identify technology solutions (POS upgrades, inventory management, reservations)
   - Create financial projections showing impact of recommendations
   - Prepare implementation roadmap with phases and timelines
   - Deliverable: Strategic recommendations deck, implementation roadmap, financial projections

4. Implementation Support (Month 2-3)
   - Train management team on new processes and standards
   - Roll out new menu (revised items, pricing, design)
   - Implement operational changes (kitchen workflow, service procedures)
   - Install technology solutions and train staff
   - Launch marketing campaigns (promotions, social media, loyalty programs)
   - Monitor early results and adjust as needed
   - Conduct weekly check-ins with management team
   - Deliverable: Training materials, operational manuals, progress dashboards

5. Performance Monitoring & Continuous Improvement (Month 4-6)
   - Track KPIs weekly (revenue, food cost %, labor cost %, customer count)
   - Compare actual results to projections
   - Identify variances and root causes
   - Refine strategies based on performance data
   - Conduct quarterly operational audits
   - Provide ongoing coaching to management team
   - Celebrate wins and address persistent challenges
   - Deliverable: Monthly performance reports, quarterly business reviews

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts commonly produced:

**Assessment Phase:**
- Operational assessment report
- Financial analysis (P&L review, key metrics)
- Menu engineering analysis (popularity × profitability)
- Competitive analysis (local market, pricing, positioning)
- Customer experience audit (service, ambiance, online reputation)
- SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats)

**Strategy Phase:**
- Strategic recommendations presentation
- Menu optimization plan (add, remove, reprice items)
- Cost reduction opportunities report
- Labor optimization plan (staffing models, scheduling)
- Marketing and customer acquisition strategy
- Technology recommendations (POS, inventory, reservations)
- Financial projections (pro forma P&L with recommendations)

**Implementation Phase:**
- Revised menu (items, pricing, descriptions, design)
- Standard operating procedures (SOPs) for kitchen and service
- Training manuals and checklists
- Staff training sessions (on-site or virtual)
- Marketing materials (promotions, social media content, email campaigns)
- Technology implementation plans

**Performance Monitoring:**
- Weekly KPI dashboards
- Monthly financial performance reports
- Quarterly business review presentations
- Customer satisfaction survey results
- Vendor cost tracking and benchmarking

**Minimum viable deliverable**: 
Operational assessment with 3-5 prioritized recommendations and high-level implementation plan.

**Gold standard deliverable**: 
Comprehensive assessment, data-driven menu engineering, financial projections, detailed implementation roadmap, staff training, ongoing performance monitoring with monthly reporting and quarterly business reviews.

Create profession-specific QA gates:

- [ ] Food cost calculated by category and item (ideally with recipe costing)
- [ ] Labor cost analyzed by role and shift

- [ ] Pricing analyzed against competitors and value perception
- [ ] Cost reduction opportunities quantified (vendor negotiations, waste reduction)

**Recommendations Quality:**
- [ ] Prioritized by impact and feasibility (quick wins vs. strategic initiatives)
- [ ] Financial impact quantified for each recommendation

- [ ] Change management considerations addressed (staff buy-in, training needs)
- [ ] Risks and mitigation strategies identified
- [ ] Success metrics defined for each initiative

**Implementation Quality:**
- [ ] Training materials clear, practical, and tailored to restaurant
- [ ] New menu tested and priced correctly (recipes, photos, descriptions)
- [ ] SOPs documented with step-by-step instructions
- [ ] Staff trained and competent in new procedures
- [ ] Technology implemented and staff proficient
- [ ] Marketing campaigns launched with tracking mechanisms

=== Menu Engineering Analysis Template ===
**Menu Engineering Analysis**
**Restaurant:** [Name]
**Period:** [Date Range]
**Prepared by:** [Consultant Name]

**Methodology:**
Menu items are classified based on two factors:
1. **Popularity**: Menu Mix % (item sales ÷ total category sales)
   - High Popularity: Above 70% of average (e.g., if avg is 10%, high is >7%)
2. **Profitability**: Contribution Margin (selling price − food cost)
   - High Profitability: Above average contribution margin for category

**Classification Matrix:**
- **Stars** (High Popularity + High Profitability): Keep and promote
- **Plowhorses** (High Popularity + Low Profitability): Consider repricing or reducing costs
- **Puzzles** (Low Popularity + High Profitability): Market better or re-engineer
- **Dogs** (Low Popularity + Low Profitability): Remove or drastically change

**Appetizers Analysis:**

| Item | Sales | Menu Mix % | Selling Price | Food Cost | CM | CM % | Classification | Action |
|------|-------|------------|---------------|-----------|----|----|----------------|---------|
| Buffalo Wings | 120 | 30% | $12 | $3.60 | $8.40 | 70% | Star | Promote heavily, feature on menu |
| Calamari | 60 | 15% | $14 | $4.20 | $9.80 | 70% | Star | Keep, consider price increase to $15 |
| Spinach Dip | 80 | 20% | $10 | $3.50 | $6.50 | 65% | Plowhorse | Increase price to $11 or reduce cost |
| Bruschetta | 40 | 10% | $11 | $3.00 | $8.00 | 73% | Puzzle | Market better, add to combo deals |
| Shrimp Cocktail | 30 | 7.5% | $16 | $6.00 | $10.00 | 63% | Dog | Remove or reformulate with cheaper protein |

**Category Average:**
- Average Menu Mix %: 10% (5 items, so 100% ÷ 5 = 20% would be perfect distribution; 70% of 20% = 14% is "high")
- Average Contribution Margin: $8.54
- Average CM %: 68%

**Recommendations:**
1. **Promote Stars**: Feature Buffalo Wings and Calamari prominently on menu, consider combo deals
2. **Reprice Plowhorses**: Increase Spinach Dip from $10 to $11 (10% increase, still good value)
3. **Market Puzzles**: Add Bruschetta to "Happy Hour" promotion or photo on menu
4. **Remove Dogs**: Discontinue Shrimp Cocktail, replace with higher-margin seafood appetizer

**Expected Impact:**
- Removing Dog saves $180/month in wasted food cost (30 sales × $6 food cost)
- Repricing Plowhorse adds $80/month revenue (80 sales × $1 increase)
- Promoting Stars increases sales 20%, adding $1,008/month contribution margin (36 additional sales × average $8.40 CM)
- **Total Monthly Impact: +$1,268 profit**

7) Examples of excellent outputs

**Example 1: Casual Dining Restaurant Turnaround**

```
Client: Family-owned Italian restaurant (80 seats)
Challenge: Declining revenue, high food cost, low profitability
Baseline Metrics:
- Annual Revenue: $600,000 (down 15% from prior year)
- Food Cost: 38% (benchmark: 30%)
- Labor Cost: 35% (benchmark: 32%)
- Prime Cost: 73% (benchmark: 62%)
- Net Profit: 2% (benchmark: 10-12%)

Assessment Findings:
1. Menu too large (65 items) with low-selling "dogs" and inconsistent food cost
2. Portion sizes inconsistent (no portion control tools used)
3. High waste from overproduction and spoilage (no FIFO system)
4. Overstaffed during slow periods (poor labor scheduling)
5. Minimal marketing and no online ordering (losing to competitors)
6. Outdated POS system (no sales mix reporting or inventory tracking)

Recommendations:
1. **Menu Engineering & Simplification**
   - Reduce menu from 65 to 40 items (remove 25 dogs and poor performers)
   - Focus on 8-10 signature dishes (Italian classics: pasta, chicken parm, etc.)
   - Repriced menu items based on 30% food cost target
   - Standardized recipes and implemented portion control
   - Expected impact: Food cost reduced from 38% to 30% (8 point improvement)

2. **Cost Control & Kitchen Operations**
   - Implemented FIFO system and waste tracking
   - Negotiated with vendors (5-8% cost reduction on key items)
   - Trained staff on portion control (scales, measuring cups, photo guides)
   - Expected impact: Additional 2% food cost reduction

3. **Labor Optimization**
   - Created data-driven labor schedules based on historical sales by daypart
   - Reduced staff during slow periods (Monday-Wednesday lunch)
   - Cross-trained servers and kitchen staff for flexibility
   - Expected impact: Labor cost reduced from 35% to 32% (3 point improvement)

4. **Revenue Growth Initiatives**
   - Launched online ordering via Toast POS integration
   - Started email marketing campaign (weekly specials, promotions)
   - Activated social media presence (Instagram, Facebook)
   - Implemented loyalty program (10% discount after 5 visits)
   - Expected impact: 10-15% revenue increase over 12 months

5. **Technology Upgrade**
   - Replaced outdated POS with Toast POS ($3,000 one-time + $165/month)
   - Gained sales mix reporting, inventory management, online ordering
   - Improved operational efficiency and data visibility

Implementation:
- Month 1: Menu redesign, vendor negotiations, staff training
- Month 2: Labor schedule optimization, POS implementation
- Month 3: Marketing launch (online ordering, social media, email)
- Month 4-12: Monitor, refine, and scale successful initiatives

Results After 12 Months:
- Revenue: $690,000 (+15% increase)
- Food Cost: 30% (8 point improvement)
- Labor Cost: 32% (3 point improvement)
- Prime Cost: 62% (11 point improvement)
- Net Profit: 12% (10 point improvement)
- Dollar Profit: $82,800 (vs. $12,000 prior year = +$70,800 improvement)

ROI:
- Consulting fees: $15,000
- Technology investment: $5,000 (POS + marketing tools)
- Total investment: $20,000
- Year 1 profit improvement: $70,800
- **ROI: 354% return in first year**
```

**Example 3: Food Cost Reduction Initiative**

```
Client: Upscale steakhouse (120 seats, $2M annual revenue)
Challenge: Food cost at 42% (benchmark for steakhouse: 35%)
Goal: Reduce food cost to 36% (6 point improvement = $120,000 annual savings)

Assessment:
1. Analyzed 3 months of invoice data and sales mix
2. Conducted kitchen observation and waste audit
3. Reviewed recipes and portion sizes
4. Interviewed kitchen staff on challenges

Root Causes:
1. **Over-portioning**: Steaks cut 2 oz heavier than menu specs (12 oz listed, serving 14 oz)
   - Impact: 17% more cost per steak (2 oz ÷ 12 oz)
   - 500 steaks/month × $3.50 extra cost = $1,750/month waste
2. **Produce waste**: Lettuce and tomatoes spoiling before use (25% waste rate)
   - Impact: $800/month
3. **Lack of recipe standards**: Chefs "eyeballing" ingredients (inconsistent)
   - Impact: Estimated 10% overcost on sauces, sides, etc. = $2,000/month
4. **Theft/comps**: Excessive staff meals and untracked comps
   - Impact: $1,500/month
5. **Vendor pricing**: Not benchmarking or negotiating contracts
   - Impact: 5% higher than market = $3,000/month

Recommendations:
1. **Portion Control Enforcement**
   - Purchased digital scales for grill station
   - Trained grill cooks on precise portioning
   - Implemented spot checks by sous chef
   - Savings: $1,750/month

2. **Produce Par Level Optimization**
   - Reduced par levels based on historical usage
   - Improved FIFO rotation and storage practices
   - Negotiated more frequent deliveries (3x/week vs. 2x/week)
   - Savings: $600/month (waste reduced from 25% to 10%)

3. **Recipe Standardization**
   - Documented all recipes with precise measurements
   - Created photo guides for plating consistency
   - Trained kitchen staff on new standards
   - Savings: $1,500/month

4. **Comps & Staff Meal Policy**
   - Implemented comp tracking in POS (manager approval required)
   - Created staff meal policy (designated items, one meal per shift)
   - Reduced discretionary comps by 60%
   - Savings: $900/month

5. **Vendor Negotiations**
   - Benchmarked pricing with 2 additional vendors
   - Negotiated volume discounts with primary vendor
   - Switched vendors for produce (8% savings)
   - Savings: $2,400/month

Total Monthly Savings: $7,150
Annual Savings: $85,800 (4.3 percentage point food cost reduction)

Implementation Timeline:
- Week 1: Portion control tools and training
- Week 2: Recipe documentation and staff training
- Week 3: Produce par level adjustments and vendor negotiations
- Week 4: Comp policy rollout and enforcement
- Month 2-3: Monitor, refine, ensure sustainment

Results After 3 Months:
- Food Cost: 37% (down from 42%, 5 point improvement)
- Close to 36% target, ongoing refinement expected to hit goal
- Savings on track: $7,000+/month average
- Kitchen staff bought into changes (clear standards, less guesswork)
- No customer complaints (portions still generous, just consistent)
- Profitability improved from 8% to 13% net profit margin
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **Food Cost Percentage**: Cost of food sold ÷ Food revenue (benchmark: 28-35% depending on concept)
- **Labor Cost Percentage**: Total labor ÷ Revenue (benchmark: 25-35%)
- **Prime Cost**: Food cost + Labor cost (benchmark: 60-65%)
- **Contribution Margin (CM)**: Selling price − Food cost (profit per item)
- **Menu Mix**: Sales distribution across menu items (percentage of total category sales)
- **Covers**: Number of meals served (customer count)
- **Average Check**: Total revenue ÷ Customer count
- **Revenue per Labor Hour**: Revenue ÷ Total labor hours worked
- **Table Turn**: Number of times a table is occupied during service period
- **FIFO (First In, First Out)**: Inventory rotation method to minimize spoilage

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **NRA (National Restaurant Association) Benchmarks**: Industry financial and operational standards
- **ServSafe Food Safety**: Food handler certification program (required in most states)
- **HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points)**: Food safety management system
- **FDA Food Code**: Federal food safety guidelines (adopted by most states)
- **OSHA Standards**: Workplace safety regulations (kitchen safety, slip/fall prevention)
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)**: Accessibility requirements for dining and restrooms
- **PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)**: Credit card data protection
- **Menu Engineering (Kasavana & Smith)**: Framework for menu optimization
- **IFMA (International Foodservice Manufacturers Association)**: Foodservice industry research

**Common Tools:**
- **POS Systems**: Toast, Square, Clover, Lightspeed, Micros Oracle
- **Reservation Systems**: OpenTable, Resy, Yelp Reservations
- **Inventory Management**: MarketMan, BlueCart, SimpleOrder, Avero
- **Scheduling & Labor**: 7shifts, HotSchedules, Deputy, Homebase
- **Accounting**: QuickBooks, Xero, Restaurant365
- **Online Ordering**: Toast, ChowNow, Grubhub, DoorDash, Uber Eats
- **Recipe & Cost Management**: PlateIQ, Kitchen CUT, meez
- **Customer Feedback**: Yelp, Google Reviews, TripAdvisor, SurveyMonkey
