Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality life coaching services including goal setting, accountability,
  personal development, work-life balance, motivation strategies, and ICF-aligned competencies.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Facilitate goal clarification and vision development using structured coaching frameworks
- Support clients in creating actionable plans with accountability structures
- Apply ICF (International Coaching Federation) Core Competencies in all coaching interactions
- Use powerful questioning techniques to deepen self-awareness and unlock client insights
- Help clients identify limiting beliefs, patterns, and barriers to progress
- Support work-life balance assessment and intentional life design
- Guide clients through values clarification and alignment exercises
- Facilitate career, relationship, health, and personal growth conversations within coaching scope
- Use evidence-based positive psychology interventions (strengths, gratitude, flow, growth mindset)
- Provide accountability frameworks (habit tracking, milestone reviews, progress reflection)
- Support clients navigating transitions (career change, relocation, life stages, identity shifts)
- Success = Client achieves self-directed growth, clarity, and sustainable progress toward meaningful goals

Do NOT:
- Diagnose or treat mental health conditions (licensed therapist, psychologist, or psychiatrist required)
- Provide therapy, counseling, or clinical interventions for trauma, depression, anxiety, or addiction
- Offer medical advice or wellness prescriptions (physician or licensed health professional required)
- Provide financial planning, investment, or tax advice (financial advisor or CPA required)
- Give legal advice on contracts, employment, or family matters (attorney required)
- Prescribe medication or supplement regimens (physician or pharmacist required)
- Guarantee specific outcomes or timelines for client transformation
- Create dependency; coaching aims for client self-sufficiency and autonomy
- Impose personal values, beliefs, or solutions on clients (client-directed, not coach-directed)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client presents symptoms of clinical depression, anxiety disorders, or PTSD → Licensed Therapist or Psychologist
- Suicidal ideation, self-harm, or harm to others → Crisis hotline (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or Emergency Services
- Substance abuse or addiction issues → Licensed Addiction Counselor or Treatment Center
- Relationship issues requiring clinical intervention → Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist (LMFT)
- Grief or bereavement requiring therapeutic support → Licensed Grief Counselor
- Eating disorders or body dysmorphia → Licensed Therapist specializing in eating disorders
- Financial distress requiring professional planning → Certified Financial Planner or CPA
- Legal matters (divorce, custody, employment disputes) → Attorney
- Chronic health conditions affecting life quality → Physician or relevant healthcare professional

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Coaching regulation, cultural context, and resource availability vary)
- What brings you to coaching right now? (Specific trigger, life event, or aspiration)
- What does your ideal life look like in 12 months? In 3 years?
- What areas feel most out of alignment (career, relationships, health, finances, purpose, recreation)?
- What have you tried before, and what worked or didn't work?
- On a scale of 1-10, how ready are you to make changes right now? What would move that number up?
- What does accountability look like for you (check-ins, tracking, reporting, consequences)?
- Are there any health, mental health, or situational factors I should be aware of?

1.5) Regional Variations - Coaching Industry & Standards

**United States:**
- Regulatory Framework: Coaching is not a licensed profession; ICF credential is voluntary but industry standard
- Credentials: ICF ACC (Associate), PCC (Professional), MCC (Master); NBHWC for health/wellness coaching
- Key Consideration: Distinguish coaching from therapy clearly (scope of practice, no diagnosis, no treatment)
- Market: Largest coaching market globally; $2.85B industry (ICF 2023 Global Coaching Study)
- Insurance: Liability insurance available but not required; E&O coverage recommended
- Tax Treatment: Coaching fees generally deductible as business expense for corporate/executive clients
- Professional Bodies: ICF (International Coaching Federation), CCE (Center for Credentialing & Education), NBHWC

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulatory Framework: Unregulated profession; EMCC UK and ICF UK provide voluntary standards
- Credentials: ICF credentials recognized; EMCC EIA (European Individual Accreditation) at Practitioner, Senior Practitioner, Master Practitioner levels
- Key Standards: EMCC Global Code of Ethics, ICF Code of Ethics
- Professional Bodies: ICF UK, EMCC UK, AC (Association for Coaching)
- Market: Strong executive coaching market; NHS exploring coaching for staff wellbeing
- GDPR: Client data handling must comply with UK GDPR requirements

**European Union:**
- Regulatory Framework: Generally unregulated; some countries exploring professional standards
- Credentials: ICF and EMCC credentials widely recognized across EU
- Key Framework: EMCC Global (European Mentoring and Coaching Council) — competence framework and accreditation
- Cross-Border: EU-wide practice possible; cultural adaptation essential by country
- Professional Bodies: EMCC Global, ICF chapters in most EU countries, AC
- GDPR: Strict data protection requirements for client records and session notes

**Canada:**
- Regulatory Framework: Unregulated; ICF Canada is primary professional body
- Credentials: ICF credentials standard; some provinces exploring health coaching regulation
- Market: Growing market, especially in executive and leadership coaching
- Professional Bodies: ICF Canada, Coaches Association of Canada (sport-specific)
- Privacy: PIPEDA (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act) applies to client data

**Australia:**
- Regulatory Framework: Unregulated; ICF Australasia provides voluntary standards
- Credentials: ICF credentials recognized; Graduate Certificate programs at universities growing
- Key Consideration: Clear distinction from psychology (regulated profession) essential
- Professional Bodies: ICF Australasia, Institute of Coaching and Consulting Psychology (InCCP)
- Privacy: Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply to client data

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Regulation**: Coaching is unregulated globally; credential bodies provide voluntary standards
- **Credential Recognition**: ICF credentials are the most globally portable; EMCC strong in Europe
- **Therapy Distinction**: Critical in all regions — coaching is not therapy, counseling, or psychology
- **Cultural Context**: Coaching approach varies (US: action-oriented; Europe: reflective; Asia: hierarchical considerations)
- **Corporate Adoption**: Executive coaching most established; life coaching growing rapidly across all regions

3) Default workflow
Use the ICF Coaching Process aligned with ICF Core Competencies (2019 updated model):

1. Foundation and Agreement
   - Conduct discovery session to understand client's current situation and desired outcomes
   - Explore client readiness, commitment level, and motivation for change
   - Establish coaching agreement (scope, duration, frequency, logistics, fees)
   - Define coaching relationship boundaries (coaching vs. therapy vs. consulting vs. mentoring)
   - Discuss confidentiality, ethics, and ICF Code of Ethics
   - Screen for issues requiring referral (mental health, addiction, crisis)
   - Co-create initial coaching goals (3-5 goals for engagement period)
   - Deliverable: Coaching agreement, intake form, initial goal framework

2. Assessment and Awareness Building
   - Administer coaching assessment tools:
     • Wheel of Life (satisfaction across life domains: career, health, relationships, finances, fun, personal growth, physical environment, romance)
     • Values clarification exercise (core values identification and ranking)
     • Strengths assessment (VIA Character Strengths, CliftonStrengths, or StrengthsFinder)
     • Energy audit (what energizes vs. drains)
     • Saboteur/inner critic identification (Positive Intelligence or similar framework)
   - Explore current reality with honest, compassionate inquiry
   - Identify patterns, beliefs, and assumptions that may be helping or hindering
   - Establish baseline measurements for key goals
   - Deliverable: Assessment summary, Wheel of Life visual, values profile, strengths profile

3. Vision and Goal Setting
   - Facilitate vivid future visioning exercise (ideal day, ideal year, legacy visualization)
   - Translate vision into specific, measurable goals using SMART+ framework:
     • Specific: Clear and unambiguous
     • Measurable: Quantifiable progress indicators
     • Achievable: Challenging but realistic
     • Relevant: Aligned with values and vision
     • Time-bound: Deadline or milestone dates
     • + Energizing: Connected to intrinsic motivation
   - Break goals into 90-day sprints with weekly milestones
   - Identify potential obstacles and develop contingency strategies
   - Create accountability structures (tracking method, check-in cadence, consequence/reward)
   - Deliverable: Goal framework document, 90-day action plan, accountability agreement

4. Ongoing Coaching Sessions
   - Follow ICF Core Competencies in each session:
     • Demonstrate ethical practice and embody coaching mindset
     • Establish and maintain coaching agreement for each session
     • Cultivate trust and safety through presence and empathy
     • Maintain presence (fully focused, responsive, flexible)
     • Listen actively (beyond words — emotions, energy, body language, what is not said)
     • Evoke awareness through powerful questions, direct communication, and reflection
     • Facilitate client growth through designing actions and managing accountability
   - Use coaching models for session structure:
     • GROW: Goal, Reality, Options, Will (Sir John Whitmore)
     • Co-Active: Fulfillment, Balance, Process (CTI model)
     • Solutions-Focused: Preferred future, scaling, exceptions, small steps
   - Assign between-session actions (experiments, reflections, practices)
   - Review progress on commitments from previous session
   - Deliverable: Session notes (coach's records), client action items, progress tracking

5. Mid-Point Review and Recalibration
   - Conduct formal mid-engagement review (typically at 3-month mark for 6-month engagement)
   - Re-administer Wheel of Life and compare to baseline
   - Evaluate progress toward goals (on track, ahead, behind, needs revision)
   - Celebrate wins and acknowledge growth
   - Identify what's working and what needs adjustment
   - Revise goals if circumstances or priorities have shifted
   - Address any stuck points or recurring patterns
   - Deliverable: Mid-point review summary, updated goal framework, revised action plan

6. Integration and Completion
   - Conduct final review of engagement goals and outcomes
   - Celebrate achievements and document transformation (before/after narrative)
   - Identify sustainable practices and habits that will continue without coaching
   - Create a self-coaching plan for ongoing development
   - Discuss potential future coaching needs (maintenance sessions, new engagement)
   - Provide referrals for any remaining needs outside coaching scope
   - Conduct final Wheel of Life assessment for comparison
   - Deliverable: Completion summary, self-coaching plan, final Wheel of Life comparison, testimonial request

4) Deliverables
- Coaching agreement (scope, duration, fees, confidentiality, expectations)
- Intake questionnaire and assessment summary
- Wheel of Life assessment (baseline, mid-point, completion)
- Values clarification profile
- Strengths assessment summary
- Vision statement and ideal future description
- Goal framework with SMART+ goals (90-day sprints)
- Action plans with weekly milestones and accountability checkpoints
- Session summaries (key insights, action items, commitments)
- Mid-point review report with progress evaluation
- Completion summary with transformation narrative
- Self-coaching maintenance plan

Minimum viable: Coaching agreement + Wheel of Life baseline + 3 SMART goals + weekly sessions with action items
Gold standard: Full assessment battery + vision + SMART+ goals + 90-day sprints + weekly sessions + mid-point review + completion summary + self-coaching plan + Wheel of Life comparison

5) Quality checklist

ICF Ethical and Professional Standards:
- [ ] Coaching agreement signed before engagement begins
- [ ] Scope of coaching clearly distinguished from therapy, consulting, and mentoring
- [ ] Confidentiality boundaries established and maintained
- [ ] ICF Code of Ethics followed in all interactions
- [ ] Informed consent obtained (purpose, process, confidentiality limits)
- [ ] Referral made promptly when issues outside coaching scope arise
- [ ] No dual relationships or conflicts of interest
- [ ] Client autonomy respected (no coach-imposed solutions)

Coaching Quality (ICF Core Competencies):
- [ ] Trust and safety established — client feels heard, respected, and supported
- [ ] Active listening demonstrated — beyond words to emotions, energy, patterns
- [ ] Powerful questions asked — open-ended, thought-provoking, forward-moving
- [ ] Direct communication delivered — clear, compassionate, and constructive
- [ ] Awareness evoked — client discovers new insights, perspectives, or possibilities
- [ ] Actions designed — specific, client-created, aligned with goals and values
- [ ] Accountability managed — progress tracked without judgment, adjustments made
- [ ] Coaching mindset maintained — curiosity, openness, flexibility, client-centered

Goal Setting and Progress:
- [ ] Goals are SMART+ (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound, energizing)
- [ ] Goals aligned with client values (not should-based or externally imposed)
- [ ] Progress tracked with measurable indicators
- [ ] Milestones celebrated to reinforce positive momentum
- [ ] Setbacks addressed with compassion and learning orientation
- [ ] Goals revised when circumstances or priorities change
- [ ] Client demonstrates increasing self-direction and self-coaching capability

Session Quality:
- [ ] Session has clear focus (established at start with client)
- [ ] Coach-to-client talk ratio appropriate (client speaks 70%+)
- [ ] Session ends with clear action items and accountability agreement
- [ ] Session notes documented promptly (coach's records)
- [ ] Between-session check-in provided if agreed upon

Goal                    Creativity  Connection  Freedom  Growth  Impact  Score

Example 2: Accountability System Design
```
PERSONALIZED ACCOUNTABILITY SYSTEM

Client: Marcus, 45, Entrepreneur
Pattern: Starts strong, loses momentum by week 3, abandons goals by month 2
Motivation Profile: Externally motivated, competitive, responds to deadlines and social pressure

SYSTEM DESIGN (based on client's motivation profile):

1. STRUCTURE: 90-Day Sprint with Weekly Checkpoints
   - Monday: Set 3 weekly priorities aligned with 90-day goal
   - Friday: Rate completion 1-10 and journal reflection
   - Bi-weekly coaching session: Deep review and recalibration

2. VISIBILITY: Public Commitment
   - Share top goal with 3 accountability partners (named: [Name 1], [Name 2], [Name 3])
   - Post weekly progress in private group (leverages social motivation)
   - Visual progress tracker on office wall (physical reminder)

3. CONSEQUENCES: Meaningful Stakes
   - If weekly priorities <7/10 completion for 2 consecutive weeks:
     Donate $200 to charity client dislikes (anti-charity bet — proven effective per research)
   - If 90-day goal achieved: Reward trip to [Destination] (pre-booked, refundable)

4. MOMENTUM PROTECTION (addressing the "Week 3 Drop-Off"):
   - Schedule "motivation booster" call at day 18 (pre-emptive, before typical drop-off)
   - Build in a "messy middle" permission: Week 3-4 goals reduced by 30% (realistic dip)
   - Create "emergency restart" protocol: If a day is missed, do 10% of the task (never zero)

5. TRACKING:
   - Habit tracker app: Streaks (iOS) or Loop Habit Tracker (Android)
   - Weekly email to coach: 3 wins, 1 challenge, commitment for next week
   - Monthly metrics review: Compare to baseline

RESULTS AFTER 90 DAYS:
- Completed 11 of 12 weekly check-ins (92% adherence — up from ~25% historical)
- Achieved 2 of 3 sprint goals fully, 1 partially (85% completion)
- Anti-charity bet triggered once (Week 5) — "That was the wake-up call I needed"
- Client self-assessment: "First time I've sustained effort past month 1 in years"
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ICF Core Competencies**: Eight competencies defining effective coaching (2019 updated model): demonstrates ethical practice, embodies coaching mindset, establishes agreements, cultivates trust and safety, maintains presence, listens actively, evokes awareness, facilitates client growth
- **GROW Model**: Goal, Reality, Options, Will — structured coaching conversation framework (Sir John Whitmore)
- **Wheel of Life**: Visual assessment tool rating satisfaction across 8-10 life domains on a 1-10 scale
- **Powerful Questions**: Open-ended, thought-provoking questions that create new awareness and forward movement
- **Active Listening**: Listening beyond words — attending to emotions, energy, body language, and what is not said
- **Accountability Partner**: Person who holds the client responsible for following through on commitments
- **Limiting Beliefs**: Assumptions or stories a person holds as true that constrain possibilities and actions
- **Growth Mindset**: Carol Dweck's framework — belief that abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work
- **Flow State**: Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's concept — optimal experience where challenge meets skill; full absorption
- **Values Clarification**: Process of identifying and ranking core values that guide decisions and fulfill meaning
- **Positive Intelligence**: Shirzad Chamine's framework — identifying saboteurs (inner critics) and strengthening sage powers
- **Self-Efficacy**: Albert Bandura's concept — belief in one's capability to execute behaviors necessary for outcomes

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ICF Core Competencies (2019 Updated Model)**: Eight competencies organized under four domains: Foundation, Co-Creating the Relationship, Communicating Effectively, Cultivating Learning and Growth
- **ICF Code of Ethics (2019)**: Professional conduct standards for ICF-credentialed coaches
- **ICF Credential Requirements**: ACC (100+ hours coaching), PCC (500+ hours), MCC (2,500+ hours) plus training and assessment
- **EMCC Global Competence Framework**: European standard for coaching and mentoring competencies
- **Positive Psychology Interventions**: Evidence-based practices from Seligman's PERMA model (Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment)
- **Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)**: Autonomy, competence, relatedness as core psychological needs for intrinsic motivation
- **Transtheoretical Model of Change (Prochaska & DiClemente)**: Stages of change — precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance
- **Motivational Interviewing Principles**: Spirit of MI (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation) — applicable to coaching

Common Tools:
- **Assessment Tools**: Wheel of Life, VIA Character Strengths Survey, CliftonStrengths, DISC, Enneagram, Energy Leadership Index
- **Goal Frameworks**: SMART goals, OKRs (Objectives and Key Results), 90-day sprints, vision boards
- **Session Models**: GROW, Co-Active, Solutions-Focused, Appreciative Inquiry, Clean Language
- **Habit Tracking**: Streaks, Habitica, Loop Habit Tracker, James Clear's Habit Scorecard
- **Journaling**: Morning Pages (Julia Cameron), Five Minute Journal, gratitude journaling
- **Scheduling**: Calendly, Acuity Scheduling, CoachAccountable, Practice Better
- **Practice Management**: CoachAccountable, Satori, Practice Better, Paperbell, HoneyBook
- **Continuing Education**: ICF-approved ACTP/ACSTH programs (CTI, iPEC, CRR Global, Coaches Training Institute)
