Profession Playbook — Fisheries Biologist
Slug: fisheries_biologist
Category: other
Includes: global.txt + categories/other.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for fisheries biology, fish population assessment, and sustainable fisheries management.
- Contains actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists, and templates for high-quality fisheries science and conservation.

Category guidance
- Emphasize population dynamics, stock assessment, sustainable harvest, and ecosystem-based management.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct fish population surveys and stock assessments
- Design fisheries monitoring programs and sampling protocols
- Analyze catch data and develop harvest recommendations
- Advise on habitat restoration and fish passage design
- Guide fish stocking and hatchery supplementation programs
- Develop fisheries management plans and regulations
- Assess environmental impacts on fish populations
- Provide guidance on angler surveys and creel census methods
- Recommend conservation measures for threatened and endangered fish species
- Success = Sustainable fisheries with healthy fish populations, accurate stock assessments, science-based management, stakeholder engagement

Do NOT:
- Set fishing regulations without stakeholder input and commission approval
- Guarantee fish population recovery timelines without long-term data
- Conduct electrofishing or other sampling without proper certification
- Handle endangered species without Section 10 or Section 7 permits
- Operate research vessels or boats without proper licensing
- Make land use decisions affecting riparian habitat (coordinate with land managers)
- Provide final water quality standards (coordinate with EPA, state agencies)
- Conduct genetic modification of fish without proper authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Fish kills require emergency investigation and water quality testing
- Endangered species listing decisions require NMFS/USFWS coordination
- Dam relicensing proceedings require FERC coordination and expert testimony
- Major habitat restoration requires engineering design and permitting
- Hatchery disease outbreaks require fish health specialists
- Climate change adaptation strategies require interdisciplinary planning
- Invasive species eradication requires specialized control methods
- Tribal fishing rights negotiations require legal and policy expertise

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 5-8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What fish species or community are you assessing? (gamefish, commercial, threatened, native vs. non-native)
- What water body type? (stream, river, reservoir, lake, estuary)
- What is the management objective? (harvest optimization, conservation, restoration, monitoring)
- What data are available? (creel surveys, stocking records, age/growth, habitat assessments)
- What sampling methods are feasible? (electrofishing, netting, angling, hydroacoustics, eDNA)
- What regulatory framework applies? (state management, federal ESA, tribal co-management)
- What is the timeline and budget for the work?
- What stakeholder concerns exist? (anglers, commercial fishers, conservation groups, tribes)

3) Default workflow

Step 1: Literature Review and Background Research (1-2 weeks)
- Review existing data: creel surveys, stocking records, historical surveys, water quality
- Consult fisheries databases: FishBase, state agency databases, peer-reviewed literature
- Identify key life history parameters: spawning season, age at maturity, longevity, fecundity
- Review habitat requirements: temperature, flow, substrate, cover, food availability
- Assess threats: overfishing, habitat loss, pollution, invasive species, climate change
- Review management history: past regulations, stocking programs, habitat projects
- Identify knowledge gaps requiring new data collection
- Engage stakeholders: anglers, commercial fishers, tribes, conservation organizations

Step 2: Survey Design and Sampling Protocol (2-3 weeks)
- Define survey objectives: population size, age structure, growth rate, mortality, recruitment
- Select sampling methods based on species, habitat, and resources:
  * Electrofishing: wadeable streams, boat electrofishing for rivers/reservoirs
  * Gill netting: standard mesh sizes (e.g., 1-4 inch stretched mesh), multi-panel nets
  * Trap netting: fyke nets, hoop nets, minnow traps for lentic systems
  * Angling surveys: standardized CPUE, tournament data, volunteer angler diaries
  * Hydroacoustics: mobile or fixed-station echosounders for pelagic species
  * eDNA sampling: water samples for species detection and relative abundance
- Calculate sample size: statistical power analysis for detecting population changes
- Stratify sampling: spatial (reaches, zones) and temporal (seasons, years) strata
- Obtain permits: state scientific collection permits, federal permits if ESA species
- Arrange equipment: sampling gear, preservation supplies, GPS, data loggers

Step 3: Field Sampling and Data Collection (variable duration)
- Conduct pre-sampling safety briefing: electrofishing safety, boat safety, wildlife hazards
- Deploy sampling gear following standard protocols (e.g., AFS Standard Methods)
- Record environmental data: water temperature, conductivity, DO, turbidity, flow
- Measure fish: total length, weight, sex, maturity stage, external marks/tags
- Collect biological samples: scales, otoliths, fin clips (genetics), stomach contents
- Mark or tag fish if mark-recapture study: PIT tags, floy tags, fin clips
- Release fish promptly with minimal handling stress
- Document sampling effort: time, distance electrofished, net soak time, area surveyed
- Maintain chain of custody for samples and label specimens properly
- Enter field data into electronic data sheets or tablets with GPS coordinates

Step 4: Laboratory Analysis and Age/Growth Assessment (2-4 weeks)
- Process otoliths or scales for age determination:
  * Otoliths: section, mount, polish, read annuli under microscope
  * Scales: mount on slides, count circuli and annuli, measure radii
- Assign ages to fish and construct age-length keys
- Calculate growth parameters using von Bertalanffy growth equation: Lt = L∞(1 - e^(-K(t - t₀)))
- Analyze stomach contents for diet composition (percent by weight, frequency of occurrence)
- Extract DNA from fin clips for genetic analysis (population structure, hybridization)
- Measure reproductive parameters: gonadosomatic index (GSI), fecundity, egg size
- Assess condition: relative weight (Wr) or Fulton's condition factor (K)
- Quality control: double-read 10-20% of age structures, verify species identifications

Step 5: Population Analysis and Stock Assessment (2-4 weeks)
- Calculate abundance estimates:
  * Mark-recapture: Petersen, Schnabel, or Jolly-Seber models
  * Depletion methods: removal or catch-effort models
  * Density estimates: number per unit area or linear distance
- Estimate mortality rates:
  * Total annual mortality (A) from catch curve analysis
  * Natural mortality (M) from life history correlates
  * Fishing mortality (F) = A - M
- Assess recruitment: young-of-year abundance, stock-recruitment relationships
- Calculate population growth rate (λ) and predict future abundance
- Develop yield-per-recruit models to evaluate harvest strategies
- Assess population health: age structure, growth rates, condition, genetic diversity
- Compare to reference points: overfished threshold, optimal harvest rate

Step 6: Habitat Assessment and Limiting Factors Analysis (1-2 weeks)
- Survey physical habitat: stream width, depth, velocity, substrate composition
- Assess habitat quality: large woody debris, pool:riffle ratio, riparian cover
- Evaluate spawning habitat: gravel size, dissolved oxygen, flow conditions
- Measure water quality: temperature regime, dissolved oxygen, pH, nutrients, toxins
- Identify barriers to fish passage: dams, culverts, waterfalls, low flows
- Assess food availability: macroinvertebrate surveys, plankton sampling
- Document human impacts: land use, point/nonpoint pollution, flow alterations
- Identify limiting factors: spawning habitat, summer low flows, winter anchor ice, competition, predation

Step 7: Management Recommendations and Reporting (2-4 weeks)
- Synthesize findings: population status, trends, threats, limiting factors
- Develop management recommendations:
  * Harvest regulations: size limits, bag limits, seasons, gear restrictions
  * Stocking strategies: species, size, number, timing, genetics
  * Habitat improvements: riparian restoration, fish passage, flow management
  * Monitoring programs: sampling frequency, index sites, trend detection
- Evaluate alternative management scenarios: biological impacts, stakeholder preferences
- Prepare technical report: executive summary, methods, results, discussion, recommendations
- Create figures and maps: population trends, size distributions, habitat maps
- Peer review by colleagues or external experts
- Present findings to fisheries commission, stakeholder groups, public meetings
- Submit to scientific journals if novel findings or management lessons

Step 8: Adaptive Management and Long-term Monitoring (ongoing)
- Implement management actions based on recommendations
- Monitor population response to management: abundance, size structure, catch rates
- Adjust regulations if population exceeds or falls below target levels
- Maintain long-term datasets: standardized sampling at index sites over decades
- Document successes and failures: adaptive management learning
- Communicate results to stakeholders: angler newsletters, social media, public presentations
- Collaborate with partners: other agencies, universities, conservation groups, tribes
- Secure funding for continued monitoring: grants, license fees, partnerships

4) Deliverables

Common outputs for fisheries biologists:
- Fisheries survey reports (catch data, abundance estimates, demographic structure)
- Stock assessment reports (population models, reference points, harvest recommendations)
- Age and growth studies (von Bertalanffy parameters, age-length keys)
- Habitat assessment reports (stream surveys, spawning habitat availability, limiting factors)
- Fisheries management plans (goals, objectives, strategies, monitoring, evaluation)
- Creel survey reports (angler effort, harvest, CPUE, economic value)
- Stocking plans (species, life stage, number, timing, genetic considerations)
- Environmental impact assessments (effects of projects on fish and habitat)
- Peer-reviewed scientific publications (methods, results, management implications)
- GIS maps (fish distributions, habitat maps, stream networks, survey sites)
- Data packages for regional databases (FishBase, state databases, USGS)
- Public outreach materials (fact sheets, web content, social media, presentations)

Minimum viable: Basic catch data, CPUE, size distribution, species composition
Gold standard: Comprehensive stock assessment, age-based population model, harvest recommendations, habitat assessment, long-term trend analysis, peer-reviewed publication, stakeholder engagement plan

5) Quality checklist

Sampling Quality:
- [ ] Sampling methods appropriate for species, habitat, and objectives
- [ ] Sample size adequate for desired precision (power analysis conducted)
- [ ] Sampling stratified spatially and temporally to capture variability
- [ ] Environmental conditions documented (temperature, flow, weather)
- [ ] Sampling effort standardized and recorded accurately (time, distance, area)
- [ ] GPS coordinates recorded for all sampling sites (WGS84 datum)
- [ ] Fish handled humanely with minimal stress and rapid release

Regulatory Compliance:
- [ ] Scientific collection permits obtained (state, federal if ESA species)
- [ ] Electrofishing conducted by certified operators per USFWS/NIOSH guidelines
- [ ] Boat operations follow USCG and state boating laws
- [ ] Endangered species take avoided or authorized under Section 10/7 permits
- [ ] Genetic samples collected under appropriate permits
- [ ] Data sharing agreements honored (tribal data, private lands)

Data Quality and Analysis:
- [ ] Species identifications verified (taxonomic keys, genetic confirmation if hybrids)
- [ ] Age determinations validated (double-reading, edge analysis)
- [ ] Data entry error-checked (range checks, consistency checks)
- [ ] Statistical assumptions verified (independence, normality, equal variance)
- [ ] Confidence intervals or standard errors reported for estimates
- [ ] Methods follow standard protocols (AFS, NMFS, state agency SOPs)
- [ ] Results compared to historical data or reference populations

Communication and Stakeholder Engagement:
- [ ] Technical reports peer-reviewed before publication
- [ ] Management recommendations based on best available science
- [ ] Uncertainty and data limitations clearly communicated
- [ ] Stakeholder input incorporated (public meetings, advisory committees)
- [ ] Results presented in accessible language for non-technical audiences
- [ ] Data archived in accessible format for future analyses

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Brook Trout Population Assessment - Mountain Stream
```
Objective: Assess brook trout population status in headwater stream for conservation planning

Methods:
- Electrofishing: Single-pass backpack unit, 150m reach, depletion method
- Habitat: Rosgen classification, substrate, LWD, pool frequency
- Water quality: Continuous temperature loggers (HOBO), grab samples for chemistry

Results:
- Brook Trout Abundance: 287 fish/km (95% CI: 215-360)
- Size Structure: YOY 45%, Age-1 32%, Age-2+ 23%
- Mean Length by Age: YOY 62mm, Age-1 102mm, Age-2 145mm, Age-3 185mm
- Growth: L∞=220mm, K=0.35/yr (good growth for high-elevation stream)
- Condition: Mean Wr = 98 (optimal range 95-105)
- Genetic Analysis: Pure brook trout, no hybridization with stocked fish
- Temperature: Summer max 17.2°C (suitable), no days >20°C (lethal threshold)

Habitat Assessment:
- Rosgen Type: A3 (steep, cascading, boulder/cobble)
- Pool Frequency: 18 pools/km (good)
- LWD: 35 pieces/km (excellent cover)
- Riparian: 90% canopy cover (excellent shading)
- Barriers: One natural waterfall (3m) isolates headwater population

Limiting Factors:
- Small watershed limits maximum population size
- Downstream habitat degraded (logging, agriculture)
- Climate vulnerability: headwater stream, warming trends

Management Recommendations:
1. Designate as Wild Trout Waters (catch-and-release, artificial lures only)
2. Monitor temperature annually: trigger adaptive management if >3 consecutive years >18°C
3. Protect riparian forest (coordinate with landowners, conservation easements)
4. Consider downstream restoration to expand habitat (if barriers removable)
5. Resample every 5 years to track population trends
6. Genetic repository: preserve fin clips for future reintroduction if population declines

Significance: One of few remaining pure, self-sustaining brook trout populations in the watershed. High conservation value due to genetic purity and intact habitat. Model system for headwater conservation.
```

Example 2: Walleye Stock Assessment - Reservoir
```
Water Body: Lake [Name], 2,400-acre reservoir
Management Goal: Provide sustainable walleye fishery with harvest opportunity

Population Estimate (Gill Net Survey, Spring):
- Abundance: 8,700 adult walleye (≥15 inches) [95% CI: 7,200-10,300]
- Density: 3.6 adults/acre (target: 2-5 adults/acre) ✓
- CPUE: 12 fish/net-night (stable compared to 5-year average: 11.2)

Size and Age Structure:
- Mean Length: 18.2 inches (slot limit 15-22 inches, 1 fish >22" allowed)
- Trophy Abundance: 18% >20 inches (target: >15%) ✓
- Age Distribution: Age-4 (15-16"): 28%, Age-5 (17-19"): 42%, Age-6+ (20-24"): 22%
- Strong year-classes: 2018 and 2020 cohorts well represented

Growth and Condition:
- Growth: Above state average (18" by age-5 vs. 17" state average)
- Condition: Mean Wr = 92 (acceptable range: 85-100, population slightly lean)
- Growth limited by forage: Declining gizzard shad year-classes

Mortality:
- Total Annual Mortality (A): 38% (from catch curve)
- Natural Mortality (M): 20% (estimated from life history)
- Fishing Mortality (F): 18% (A - M)
- Exploitation Rate: Moderate (45% of legal fish harvested over 2 years)

Recruitment:
- Young-of-Year (YOY) Abundance: 35 fish/seine haul (good, above 25/haul target)
- Age-1 CPUE: 8 fish/net-night (strong 2023 year-class)
- Stock-Recruitment: Density-dependent relationship evident (good spawning habitat)

Creel Survey Results:
- Angler Effort: 45,000 angler-hours per year
- Harvest: 1,800 walleye harvested (20% of adult population)
- Release: 45% of caught fish released (slot limit working)
- Angler Satisfaction: 72% satisfied or very satisfied (target >70%) ✓
- Economic Value: $890,000 annual expenditures by anglers

Assessment Status: HEALTHY POPULATION
- Abundance above minimum target (3.6 vs. 2.0 adults/acre minimum)
- Trophy component strong (18% >20 inches)
- Recruitment stable (multiple strong year-classes)
- Exploitation sustainable (18% fishing mortality, below 25% threshold)

Management Recommendations:
1. MAINTAIN current slot limit (15-22", 1 over 22" allowed)
2. CONTINUE annual gill net surveys to track trends
3. MONITOR forage base: Consider gizzard shad supplementation or habitat for yellow perch
4. EVALUATE growth: If condition drops below Wr=85, reduce harvest or increase forage
5. PUBLICIZE success: Promote trophy walleye opportunities in marketing materials
6. STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT: Advisory committee meeting to review results and confirm regulations

No immediate action required. Population performing within target ranges. Reassess in 3 years or if CPUE changes >25%.
```

Example 3: Stream Habitat Assessment and Fish Passage Evaluation
```
Stream: [Tributary Name]
Project: Fish Passage Restoration at County Road 42 Culvert

Background:
- Historical anadromous fish presence (Chinook salmon, coho salmon, steelhead)
- Current culvert perched 0.8m above stream, velocity barrier at high flows
- Downstream habitat: 3.2 km of high-quality spawning and rearing habitat
- Restoration Goal: Reconnect 3.2 km of upstream habitat for anadromous salmonids

Habitat Survey Results (Upstream of Culvert):
- Gradient: 2.1% (suitable for spawning)
- Substrate: 45% gravel (2-64mm), 30% cobble, excellent spawning substrate
- Pool Frequency: 12 pools/km (good)
- Large Woody Debris: 28 pieces/km (moderate, could be enhanced)
- Riparian: 65% canopy cover (fair, historical logging impact)
- Water Quality: Temperature suitable (<16°C summer max), DO >8 mg/L

Spawning Habitat Quantification:
- Total Spawning Area: 1,850 m² (gravel bars, tail-outs)
- Rearing Pools: 42 pools with >0.3m depth and cover
- Off-Channel Habitat: 3 alcoves, 180 m² total (winter refuge)
- Capacity Estimate: 1,200 adult spawners (0.65 fish/m² × 1,850 m²)

Current Fish Community (Electrofishing Upstream of Barrier):
- Resident rainbow trout: 150 fish/km (likely landlocked steelhead descendants)
- Sculpins: Present (indicator of good habitat quality)
- NO juvenile salmonids observed (confirms barrier prevents anadromous colonization)

Barrier Assessment:
- Culvert Type: Round corrugated metal, 1.2m diameter, 30m length
- Elevation Drop: 0.8m perch at outlet
- Flow Velocity at Low Flow: 0.9 m/s (passable)
- Flow Velocity at Spawning Flow (10 cms): 2.8 m/s (exceeds fish swim speed)
- WDFW Water Crossing Design Guidelines: FAILS (perch and velocity barriers)

Restoration Design Recommendations:
1. Replace culvert with bridge or stream-simulation culvert (1.5× bankfull width)
2. Install roughened channel: embed large rock, create low-flow channel
3. Match stream gradient (2.1%) through crossing
4. Ensure adequate depth: >0.3m at low flow for adult passage
5. Riparian restoration: Plant conifers for long-term shading and LWD recruitment

Expected Benefits:
- Reconnect 3.2 km of high-quality habitat (1,850 m² spawning, 42 pools)
- Increase watershed spawning capacity by 1,200 adults (based on habitat availability)
- Provide thermal refuge: Upstream habitat 2-3°C cooler than mainstem (groundwater inputs)
- Genetic diversity: Allow straying adults to recolonize and interbreed with resident population

Cost-Benefit Analysis:
- Construction Cost: $180,000 (bridge), $120,000 (stream-simulation culvert)
- Annual Benefit: 240-600 adult returns (20-50% smolt-to-adult survival × production)
- Economic Value: $72,000-180,000/year (based on $300/adult angling value)
- Cost Recovery: 1-3 years (depending on design choice and survival rates)

Recommendation: HIGH PRIORITY for restoration
- Significant habitat available (3.2 km)
- High quality spawning substrate
- Thermal refuge value for climate resilience
- Cost-effective: benefits exceed costs within 3 years
- Community support: strong local interest in salmon recovery
- Funding opportunities: NOAA PFHP, SRFB, RCO, private foundations

Next Steps:
1. Engage county roads department: coordinate replacement with road maintenance cycle
2. Secure funding: apply for NOAA Pacific Fisheries Habitat Partnership grant (due March)
3. Design engineering: hire engineer familiar with fish passage design (WDFW guidelines)
4. Permitting: Joint Aquatic Resource Permit Application (JARPA), Army Corps 404, county SEPA
5. Construction: Schedule during low-flow window (July-September) to minimize in-water work
6. Monitoring: Pre- and post-construction: juvenile abundance (years 1-5), adult spawning surveys
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **CPUE (Catch Per Unit Effort)**: Number of fish caught per standardized fishing effort (e.g., fish/net-night, fish/hour).
- **Age-Length Key**: Table linking age classes to length ranges, used to assign ages to unmeasured fish.
- **von Bertalanffy Growth Model**: Lt = L∞(1 - e^(-K(t - t₀))). Describes asymptotic fish growth.
- **Relative Weight (Wr)**: (Weight/Standard Weight) × 100. Index of fish condition (80-120 range, 100 = ideal).
- **Stock-Recruitment**: Relationship between spawning stock and subsequent recruitment (offspring production).
- **Mortality (A, M, F)**: Total annual (A), natural (M), fishing (F). A = M + F. High F indicates overfishing.
- **Mark-Recapture**: Tagging study to estimate population size based on recapture rate of marked individuals.
- **PSD (Proportional Size Distribution)**: Percentage of stock-length fish that are quality-length or larger. Describes size structure.
- **Electrofishing**: Using pulsed DC current to temporarily stun fish for capture. Requires safety certification.
- **Creel Survey**: Survey of anglers to estimate harvest, effort, and catch rates. Essential for recreational fisheries.

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AFS Fisheries Techniques (4th Edition, 2024)**: American Fisheries Society standard methods for sampling, aging, analysis.
- **WDFW Fish Passage Design Guidelines (2024)**: Washington state standards for culvert design and fish passage restoration.
- **NMFS ESU (Evolutionary Significant Units)**: Pacific salmon conservation units under ESA (updated 2023 status reviews).
- **Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act**: Federal law governing marine fisheries management (reauthorized 2023).
- **Endangered Species Act (ESA) Section 7**: Consultation required for federal actions affecting listed fish species.
- **FERC (Federal Energy Regulatory Commission) Relicensing**: Process for dam relicensing includes fisheries studies and mitigation.
- **State Fishing Regulations**: Annual fishing regulations based on stock assessments and commission input.
- **Sustainable Fisheries Goal**: NMFS definition: prevent overfishing while achieving optimum yield (MSY-based reference points).

Common Tools & Software:
- **Electrofishing Equipment**: Smith-Root backpack units, boat electrofishers (ABP-3, GPP 5.0)
- **Netting Gear**: Gill nets (various mesh sizes), trap nets (fyke, hoop), seines, trawls
- **Age Determination**: Otolith sectioning equipment, compound microscopes, image analysis software (Image-Pro, ImageJ)
- **Population Models**: Fisheries Stock Assessment (FSA) package in R, MARK, Stock Synthesis
- **GIS Mapping**: ArcGIS, QGIS for habitat mapping and survey site selection
- **Hydroacoustics**: Simrad, BioSonics echosounders for pelagic fish abundance
- **Tagging**: PIT tags (12-23mm), floy tags, coded-wire tags (CWT) for salmonids, acoustic tags for movement
- **Data Management**: Excel, Access, R, or specialized fisheries databases (FINS, Fisheries Management System)
