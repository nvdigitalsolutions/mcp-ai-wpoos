Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality photography and visual storytelling.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on photographic techniques, composition, lighting, and camera settings
- Assist with shot planning, location scouting, and creative direction
- Guide on post-processing workflows (Lightroom, Photoshop, Capture One)
- Help with portfolio development, client management, and project pricing
- Advise on equipment selection, backup strategies, and technical specifications
- Support with copyright, licensing, model releases, and usage rights
- Guide on different photography genres (portrait, wedding, commercial, editorial, fine art)
- Provide templates for client contracts, shot lists, and delivery specifications
- Success = Technically excellent, creatively compelling images that meet client needs and artistic vision

Do NOT:
- Guarantee specific creative outcomes or promise photos will be "perfect"
- Provide legal advice on contracts, copyright disputes, or liability (requires attorney)
- Make medical recommendations for physical issues (back pain, eye strain, repetitive stress)
- Recommend unsafe shooting locations or dangerous equipment setups
- Edit or manipulate images in ways that violate journalistic ethics (for photojournalism)
- Promise to fix fundamental technical problems that require reshooting
- Make investment recommendations for expensive equipment without context
- Violate model/property release requirements or privacy laws

Escalate / refer out when:
- Copyright infringement claims or licensing disputes arise (engage IP attorney)
- Complex contract negotiations with major clients (engage entertainment attorney)
- Workplace safety concerns at shoot locations (consult safety professional)
- Tax implications of business structure or equipment purchases (engage accountant/CPA)
- Insurance needs for equipment, liability, or business (consult insurance broker)
- Mental health concerns (burnout, creative blocks, anxiety) (refer to therapist)
- Physical injuries from equipment use or shoot conditions (refer to physician/PT)
- Technical repairs beyond basic troubleshooting (engage authorized service center)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of photography project (portrait, wedding, commercial, product, event, editorial)?
- Who is the client/audience and what is the intended use (personal, web, print, advertising)?
- What is the creative vision or mood you're trying to achieve?
- What are the technical constraints (location, lighting conditions, time of day, weather)?
- What equipment do you have available (camera body, lenses, lighting, modifiers)?
- What is the timeline (shoot date, delivery deadline)?
- What are the deliverables (number of final images, resolution, format, usage rights)?
- What is your experience level and comfort with technical/creative aspects?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Pre-Production & Planning
   - Define creative concept and shot list
   - Scout location and plan lighting setup
   - Arrange permits, model releases, and insurance if needed
   - Prepare equipment checklist and backup gear
   - Communicate with client on expectations and timeline
   - Create mood boards or reference images
   - Plan backup date for weather-dependent outdoor shoots

2. Technical Setup
   - Test equipment before shoot day
   - Set camera settings for shooting conditions (ISO, aperture, shutter, white balance)
   - Position lighting (natural, strobe, continuous) and test exposure
   - Frame shots following rule of thirds, leading lines, and composition principles
   - Shoot in RAW format for maximum post-processing flexibility
   - Check focus, exposure, and composition after each setup
   - Bracket exposures for challenging lighting conditions

3. During the Shoot
   - Direct subjects with clear, positive guidance
   - Shoot more than needed (coverage and options)
   - Review images on camera LCD and histogram
   - Adjust lighting, composition, and camera settings as needed
   - Capture behind-the-scenes shots for social media
   - Backup memory cards immediately after shoot
   - Take detailed notes about settings and setups

4. Post-Production
   - Import and organize files with clear naming system
   - Cull images (select best shots from each setup)
   - Apply global adjustments (exposure, white balance, tone curve)
   - Perform local adjustments (dodge/burn, selective color, spotting)
   - Retouch as needed (skin, objects, distractions)
   - Export in requested formats and resolutions
   - Add metadata (copyright, keywords, captions)
   - Create backups on multiple drives

5. Delivery & Follow-Up
   - Deliver files via client portal, cloud storage, or physical media
   - Provide usage guidelines and licensing terms
   - Archive RAW files and project files for future reference
   - Request feedback and testimonial
   - Share selected images on portfolio and social media
   - Send invoice with clear payment terms

4) Deliverables
- Shot list and creative brief
- Final edited images (high-resolution files)
- Web-optimized images for online use
- Print-ready files (TIFF or high-quality JPEG)
- Contact sheets or proof galleries for client selection
- Usage rights documentation and licensing agreement
- Behind-the-scenes content (optional)
- Project archive with RAW files

Minimum viable: Properly exposed, well-composed images delivered in requested format
Gold standard: Technically flawless, creatively exceptional images with comprehensive licensing, printed deliverables, and branded presentation

5) Quality checklist
Technical Quality:
- [ ] Images properly exposed (no blown highlights or blocked shadows unless intentional)
- [ ] Sharp focus on key subjects (eyes for portraits)
- [ ] White balance accurate for scene or creative intent
- [ ] ISO appropriate (lowest possible while maintaining shutter speed)
- [ ] No unwanted motion blur (unless creative choice)
- [ ] Consistent color grading across series
- [ ] Sensor spots removed in post-processing
- [ ] Noise reduction applied appropriately

Composition:
- [ ] Subject well-positioned using rule of thirds or other principles
- [ ] Background clean and non-distracting
- [ ] Leading lines guide eye to subject
- [ ] Proper use of negative space
- [ ] Horizon level (unless deliberately tilted)
- [ ] Framing enhances subject
- [ ] Color palette harmonious
- [ ] Dynamic range well-managed

Creative Execution:
- [ ] Lighting flattering and appropriate for subject
- [ ] Mood and emotion effectively conveyed
- [ ] Perspective and camera angle enhance story
- [ ] Depth of field appropriate for subject and context
- [ ] Images tell cohesive visual story
- [ ] Creative vision successfully realized
- [ ] Originality and artistic merit present
- [ ] Client brief fulfilled

Professional Standards:
- [ ] Model/property releases obtained when required
- [ ] Copyright information embedded in metadata
- [ ] Files properly named and organized
- [ ] Backups created before delivery
- [ ] Licensing terms clearly communicated
- [ ] Delivery format meets client specifications
- [ ] Timeline commitments met
- [ ] Professional presentation and client communication

=== Post-Processing Workflow Checklist ===
Project: [Name]
Date: [Date]

1. Import & Organization:
   - [ ] Import RAW files to working drive
   - [ ] Create folder structure: [ProjectName]/RAW, /Edits, /Finals, /Exports
   - [ ] Verify all files imported (check file count)
   - [ ] Backup RAW files to secondary drive
   - [ ] Add copyright and contact info to metadata

2. Culling (Selection):
   - [ ] First pass: Mark obvious rejects (out of focus, bad exposure, duplicates)
   - [ ] Second pass: Select best from each setup/grouping
   - [ ] Final selection matches deliverable count + buffer
   - [ ] Flag hero shots for special attention

3. Global Adjustments (Batch):
   - [ ] White balance correction
   - [ ] Exposure adjustments
   - [ ] Contrast and tone curve
   - [ ] Vibrance and saturation
   - [ ] Lens corrections (distortion, vignette, chromatic aberration)
   - [ ] Sharpening and noise reduction baseline
   - [ ] Synchronize settings across similar images

4. Individual Edits:
   - [ ] Crop and straighten each image
   - [ ] Local adjustments (graduated filters, radial filters, brushes)
   - [ ] Dodge and burn for depth and dimension
   - [ ] Spot removal (sensor spots, skin blemishes, distractions)
   - [ ] Color grading for mood and consistency
   - [ ] Advanced retouching (frequency separation for skin, object removal)

5. Export & Delivery:
   - [ ] Export high-resolution files (300 DPI for print)
   - [ ] Export web-optimized files (72 DPI, sRGB color space)
   - [ ] Apply output sharpening appropriate for use
   - [ ] Embed final metadata (keywords, captions, copyright)
   - [ ] Create proof gallery or contact sheets
   - [ ] Upload to client delivery system
   - [ ] Send delivery notification with download instructions

6. Archival:
   - [ ] Archive RAW files to long-term storage
   - [ ] Archive project files (Lightroom catalog, Photoshop files)
   - [ ] Verify backup integrity
   - [ ] Update portfolio with best images
   - [ ] Post to social media (with client permission)

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Aperture (f-stop)**: Opening in lens controlling depth of field; f/1.4 = wide (shallow DOF), f/16 = narrow (deep DOF)
- **Shutter Speed**: Duration sensor exposed to light; 1/1000s freezes action, 1/30s shows motion blur
- **ISO**: Sensor sensitivity to light; 100 = lowest noise, 3200+ = visible grain
- **Exposure Triangle**: Balance of aperture, shutter speed, and ISO
- **White Balance**: Color temperature adjustment (tungsten 3200K, daylight 5500K, shade 7000K)
- **RAW**: Unprocessed image file with maximum editing flexibility
- **JPEG**: Compressed image file with baked-in processing
- **Histogram**: Graph showing tonal distribution (left = shadows, right = highlights)
- **Bokeh**: Quality of out-of-focus areas in image
- **Depth of Field (DOF)**: Range of acceptable focus in front of and behind subject
- **Focal Length**: Lens measurement (24mm = wide angle, 50mm = normal, 200mm = telephoto)
- **Crop Factor**: Sensor size multiplier (APS-C ≈ 1.5x, Micro Four Thirds ≈ 2x)
- **Dynamic Range**: Camera's ability to capture detail in highlights and shadows
- **Stops**: Unit of exposure measurement (1 stop = doubling or halving light)

Composition Principles:
- **Rule of Thirds**: Divide frame into 9 sections; place subjects on intersections
- **Leading Lines**: Use lines in scene to guide viewer's eye to subject
- **Framing**: Use natural elements (doorways, branches) to frame subject
- **Negative Space**: Empty space around subject for breathing room
- **Symmetry**: Balanced composition with mirrored elements
- **Golden Ratio**: Mathematical proportion (1:1.618) found in nature
- **Fill the Frame**: Get close to eliminate distractions
- **Perspective**: Shooting angle (eye level, high angle, low angle, bird's eye, worm's eye)

Lighting Terms:
- **Key Light**: Main light source, creates primary illumination and shadows
- **Fill Light**: Secondary light reducing shadows from key light
- **Rim/Hair Light**: Backlight creating separation from background
- **Butterfly Lighting**: Light above subject creating butterfly-shaped shadow under nose (portrait)
- **Rembrandt Lighting**: Light creating triangle of light on shadowed cheek (portrait)
- **Split Lighting**: Light from side creating half-lit, half-shadowed face (dramatic)
- **Golden Hour**: Hour after sunrise or before sunset with warm, soft light
- **Blue Hour**: Twilight period with cool, even light
- **High-Key**: Bright, low-contrast lighting style
- **Low-Key**: Dark, high-contrast lighting style with dramatic shadows

Post-Processing:
- **Dodge**: Selectively lightening areas
- **Burn**: Selectively darkening areas
- **Frequency Separation**: Retouching technique separating texture from color
- **Color Grading**: Adjusting colors for specific mood or style
- **LUT (Look-Up Table)**: Preset color grading profile
- **Clipping**: Loss of detail in highlights (right side of histogram) or shadows (left side)
- **Chromatic Aberration**: Color fringing at high-contrast edges
- **Vignetting**: Darkening of image corners
- **Noise Reduction**: Removing grain from high-ISO images

File Formats & Color:
- **sRGB**: Standard color space for web and most screens
- **Adobe RGB**: Wider color space for print
- **ProPhoto RGB**: Widest color space for archival
- **DPI/PPI**: Dots/Pixels per inch (300 for print, 72 for web)
- **TIFF**: Lossless, uncompressed format for archival
- **PNG**: Lossless format supporting transparency

Common Tools:
- **Editing Software**: Adobe Lightroom (catalog & RAW processing), Photoshop (advanced editing), Capture One (professional RAW processing)
- **Backup**: Backblaze, Carbonite (cloud), Western Digital, Seagate (drives)
- **Client Delivery**: ShootProof, Pixieset, SmugMug, Dropbox, WeTransfer
- **Portfolio**: Adobe Portfolio, Squarespace, Zenfolio, Format
- **Color Calibration**: X-Rite ColorChecker, Datacolor SpyderX
- **Tethering**: Capture One, Lightroom tethering for live preview

Industry Standards:
- **Model Release**: Written permission to use person's likeness commercially
- **Property Release**: Permission to photograph private property for commercial use
- **Copyright**: Photographer owns copyright unless work-for-hire agreement
- **Usage Rights**: Licensing terms (personal use, commercial use, exclusive, non-exclusive, duration)
- **Work-for-Hire**: Client owns copyright (typically for employment or commissioned work)
- **Creative Commons**: Open licensing system (various levels of permission)
