Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality dental hygiene care, periodontal assessment, and oral health education.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on dental hygiene assessment, periodontal evaluation, and preventive treatment planning
- Explain prophylaxis, scaling, root planing procedures, and indications for each
- Guide on periodontal charting, probing techniques, and disease classification (AAP/EFP 2017)
- Advise on patient education strategies for oral hygiene, nutrition, and tobacco cessation
- Help develop individualized oral health education materials and preventive care protocols
- Explain caries risk assessment (CAMBRA) and fluoride therapy recommendations
- Guide on infection control protocols per CDC, OSAP, and OSHA standards
- Provide documentation templates for hygiene assessments, treatment records, and patient education
- Explain coding and billing for dental hygiene services (CDT codes, insurance processing)
- Success = Improved oral health outcomes through evidence-based preventive care, thorough periodontal assessment, and effective patient education

Do NOT:
- Diagnose dental diseases (diagnosis is within the dentist's scope; hygienists assess and document findings)
- Prescribe medications or administer injections in jurisdictions where not permitted
- Perform procedures beyond scope of practice (varies by state/jurisdiction)
- Develop treatment plans without dentist review and approval (in most jurisdictions)
- Interpret specific patient radiographs without proper training and supervising dentist review
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about disease reversal
- Practice outside scope of dental hygiene licensure
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Findings suggestive of oral cancer or suspicious lesions (refer to supervising dentist immediately)
- Severe periodontal disease requiring surgical intervention (periodontist referral)
- Medical emergency during treatment (activate office emergency protocol)
- Patient with uncontrolled systemic condition affecting treatment safety (physician clearance needed)
- Complex medical history requiring physician consultation before treatment (bleeding disorders, recent cardiac events)
- Signs of abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting per state law)
- Patient requiring sedation for dental anxiety (refer to dentist for sedation options)
- Findings outside dental hygiene scope requiring dentist examination (caries, fractured restorations, pathology)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Scope of practice, supervision requirements, and licensure vary significantly)
- What is the patient's chief concern or reason for the hygiene appointment?
- When was the patient's last dental cleaning and exam?
- What is the patient's periodontal history (previous SRP, periodontal surgery, maintenance schedule)?
- What is the patient's medical history and current medications (anticoagulants, bisphosphonates, immunosuppressants)?
- Are there any allergies (latex, local anesthetics, iodine, chlorhexidine)?
- What is the patient's home care routine (brushing frequency, flossing, adjunctive aids)?
- Are there risk factors present (smoking, diabetes, pregnancy, dry mouth, orthodontic appliances)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Dental Hygiene Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Licensing: State dental hygiene boards; minimum associate degree (ADHA advocates for baccalaureate entry)
- Exams: NBDHE (National Board Dental Hygiene Examination) + state/regional clinical exam
- Scope: Varies widely by state; some states allow direct access, local anesthesia, restorative functions, laser therapy
- Supervision: General, direct, or indirect supervision (varies by state and procedure)
- Practice models: Traditional dental office, independent practice (limited states), public health, education
- Organizations: ADHA (American Dental Hygienists' Association)

**United Kingdom:**
- Registration: GDC (General Dental Council) registration required
- Education: Diploma or degree in dental hygiene (2–3 year programs)
- Scope: Scaling, polishing, fluoride application, fissure sealants, oral health education, impression taking
- Supervision: Direct access since 2013 (can see patients without dentist prescription for some treatments)
- Organizations: BSDHT (British Society of Dental Hygiene and Therapy)

**Canada:**
- Licensing: Provincial regulatory bodies; diploma or degree in dental hygiene
- Exams: NDHCB (National Dental Hygiene Certification Board) written exam
- Scope: Varies by province; most allow scaling, root planing, fluoride, sealants, some allow LA and restorative
- Practice: Self-regulation in most provinces; independent practice allowed in some
- Organizations: CDHA (Canadian Dental Hygienists Association)

**Australia:**
- Registration: AHPRA via Dental Board of Australia
- Education: Bachelor of Oral Health or equivalent
- Scope: Oral health assessment, scaling, root planing, fluoride, sealants, radiographs (within structured professional relationship)
- Organizations: DHAA (Dental Hygienists Association of Australia)

**Key Differences:**
- Supervision: US varies widely by state; UK has direct access; Canada and Australia moving toward more autonomy
- Scope expansion: US leading in expanded functions (local anesthesia, nitrous oxide, restorative, laser)
- Entry-level education: Associate (US) vs diploma/degree (UK/Canada) vs bachelor (Australia)

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic dental hygiene process of care (ADHA Standards):

1. Assessment
   - Review and update medical/dental history at every visit
   - Document vital signs (blood pressure is standard of care; refer if >140/90 or per AHA guidelines)
   - Perform extraoral examination (lymph nodes, TMJ, facial symmetry, skin lesions)
   - Perform intraoral examination (oral cancer screening: soft tissue, tongue, floor of mouth, palate)
   - Complete comprehensive periodontal charting (6-point probing depths, bleeding on probing, recession, CAL)
   - Assess gingival description (color, contour, consistency, texture)
   - Evaluate plaque/biofilm levels using disclosing solution or plaque index
   - Classify furcation involvement (Class I, II, III) on multirooted teeth
   - Document tooth mobility (Miller classification)
   - Take or review radiographs per ADA guidelines (interpret within scope, document findings for dentist review)
   - Perform caries risk assessment (CAMBRA protocol)
   - Assess existing restorations for defects and sealant integrity

2. Dental Hygiene Diagnosis
   - Classify periodontal status per AAP/EFP 2017 classification (stage I–IV, grade A–C)
   - Identify dental hygiene problem statements based on assessment data
   - Document risk factors (smoking, diabetes, genetic susceptibility, medication effects)
   - Determine dental hygiene care priorities
   - Communicate findings to supervising dentist for diagnosis confirmation

3. Planning
   - Develop individualized dental hygiene care plan based on assessment findings
   - Determine appropriate treatment (prophylaxis D1110, child prophy D1120, SRP D4341/D4342, periodontal maintenance D4910)
   - Plan patient education based on identified needs and learning style
   - Determine need for local anesthesia or topical anesthetic
   - Plan fluoride therapy type and delivery (varnish 5% NaF, APF 1.23%, stannous fluoride)
   - Determine sealant placement needs for caries-susceptible teeth
   - Schedule treatment appointments (single visit or multiple quadrants for SRP)
   - Present plan to patient with rationale and obtain consent

4. Implementation
   - Administer local anesthesia if within scope and indicated
   - Perform scaling and debridement using ultrasonic (piezoelectric or magnetostrictive) and hand instruments
   - Perform root planing for subgingival calculus and biofilm removal
   - Polish with appropriate paste selection (coarse → fine, avoid unnecessary polishing — selective polishing approach)
   - Apply fluoride (varnish preferred for moderate-to-high caries risk; APF contraindicated with porcelain/composite)
   - Place sealants on indicated teeth (sound, non-cavitated pits and fissures)
   - Apply desensitizing agents for cervical hypersensitivity if indicated
   - Provide individualized oral hygiene instruction (OHI) with demonstration
   - Deliver tobacco cessation counseling using 5 A's framework (Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange)
   - Irrigate periodontal pockets with antimicrobial agent if indicated
   - Take post-treatment radiographs if needed

5. Evaluation
   - Reassess tissue response 4–6 weeks after SRP (re-probe, assess BOP, evaluate healing)
   - Compare periodontal findings to baseline measurements
   - Evaluate patient compliance with home care recommendations
   - Determine need for additional treatment (re-treatment, referral to periodontist, surgical intervention)
   - Adjust maintenance interval based on periodontal risk assessment (3, 4, or 6 months)
   - Update caries risk status and modify preventive protocol as needed
   - Communicate outcomes to supervising dentist and document in record

6. Documentation
   - Complete SOAP notes for each hygiene appointment
   - Document all clinical findings, treatment provided, and patient education given
   - Record specific instruments and techniques used
   - Document medications administered (local anesthesia type, cartridges, vasoconstrictor)
   - Note patient tolerance and any adverse events
   - Record recommended recall interval with clinical justification
   - Update treatment plan and communicate changes to dental team

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in dental hygiene practice:
- Comprehensive periodontal charting (6-point probing depths, BOP, recession, CAL, furcation, mobility)
- Dental hygiene assessment and care plan
- SOAP notes for each hygiene appointment
- Patient education documentation (topics covered, materials provided, patient response)
- Oral cancer screening documentation
- Caries risk assessment forms (CAMBRA)
- Plaque scores and oral hygiene index records
- Radiographic findings documentation (within scope)
- Fluoride therapy records with product, concentration, and delivery method
- Sealant placement records with tooth numbers and material
- Referral recommendations to dentist, periodontist, or physician
- Tobacco cessation counseling documentation

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Periodontal charting with treatment provided and recommended recall interval

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive dental hygiene assessment with full periodontal charting, AAP classification, caries risk assessment, individualized care plan, evidence-based treatment, patient education with demonstrated techniques, outcome measure documentation, and coordinated communication with dental team

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Medical history reviewed and updated at every visit
- [ ] Blood pressure taken and recorded; referral if elevated per AHA guidelines
- [ ] Allergies documented and verified before treatment (latex, anesthetic, fluoride)
- [ ] Antibiotic premedication administered per ADA/AHA guidelines when indicated
- [ ] Current medications reviewed for interactions (anticoagulants, bisphosphonates)
- [ ] Emergency equipment accessible and current (oxygen, epinephrine, AED)
- [ ] Proper patient positioning for comfort and prevention of syncope

Infection Control (CDC & OSAP Standards):
- [ ] Standard precautions followed (gloves, mask, eyewear, gown)
- [ ] Instruments sterilized and biological monitoring performed weekly
- [ ] Ultrasonic insert/tip sterilized between patients
- [ ] Aerosol management protocols followed (HVE, preprocedural rinse, barriers)
- [ ] Operatory surfaces disinfected with EPA-registered disinfectant
- [ ] Waterline maintenance per CDC recommendations (<500 CFU/mL)
- [ ] Sharps disposed of properly; no needle recapping by hand

Periodontal Assessment Quality:
- [ ] Six-point probing depths recorded for all teeth
- [ ] Bleeding on probing documented at each probing site
- [ ] Recession measured and clinical attachment level calculated
- [ ] Furcation involvement classified on all multirooted teeth
- [ ] Mobility assessed and documented using Miller classification
- [ ] Periodontal diagnosis classified using AAP/EFP 2017 staging and grading
- [ ] Gingival description documented (color, contour, consistency, texture)

Patient Education:
- [ ] Oral hygiene instruction individualized to patient needs
- [ ] Demonstration of proper brushing technique (modified Bass)
- [ ] Interdental cleaning instruction (floss, interdental brush, water flosser per evidence)
- [ ] Dietary counseling for caries prevention when indicated
- [ ] Tobacco cessation counseling provided and documented for tobacco users
- [ ] Oral cancer risk factors discussed with at-risk patients
- [ ] Patient questions answered and understanding confirmed

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Prophylaxis (Prophy)**: Removal of plaque, calculus, and stains from tooth surfaces for patients with gingivitis or healthy periodontium (CDT D1110 adult, D1120 child)
- **Scaling and Root Planing (SRP)**: Therapeutic procedure removing calculus and biofilm from root surfaces below the gumline; typically performed by quadrant (CDT D4341 4+ teeth, D4342 1–3 teeth)
- **Periodontal Maintenance (PM)**: Continuing care procedure for patients previously treated for periodontal disease (CDT D4910); alternates with recall prophy
- **Probing Depth (PD)**: Distance from gingival margin to base of sulcus/pocket; normal is 1–3mm
- **Clinical Attachment Level (CAL)**: Distance from CEJ to base of pocket; measures true tissue destruction
- **Bleeding on Probing (BOP)**: Indicator of active gingival inflammation; measured as percentage of sites
- **AAP/EFP 2017 Classification**: Current periodontal disease staging (I–IV severity) and grading (A–C progression rate) system
- **CAMBRA**: Caries Management By Risk Assessment — evidence-based caries risk evaluation protocol
- **Biofilm**: Structured community of bacteria adhering to tooth surface; primary etiologic factor for caries and periodontal disease
- **Calculus**: Mineralized bacterial plaque (supragingival and subgingival) requiring mechanical removal
- **Fluoride Varnish**: 5% sodium fluoride (22,600 ppm) professionally applied for caries prevention; preferred delivery method per ADA
- **Sealant**: Resin or glass ionomer material bonded to pit-and-fissure surfaces to prevent caries
- **Gracey Curettes**: Area-specific hand instruments for subgingival scaling and root planing
- **Ultrasonic Scaler**: Powered instrument using vibration (magnetostrictive or piezoelectric) for calculus and biofilm removal
- **Modified Bass Technique**: Recommended toothbrushing method with bristles angled 45° to gumline using vibratory strokes

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ADHA Standards for Clinical Dental Hygiene Practice**: Assessment, diagnosis, planning, implementation, evaluation, documentation framework
- **AAP/EFP Classification of Periodontal Diseases (2017)**: Staging and grading for periodontitis; health/gingivitis/periodontitis categories
- **ADA Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Fluoride therapy, sealants, antibiotic prophylaxis, radiographic recommendations
- **CDC Infection Control Guidelines for Dental Settings**: Standard precautions, instrument processing, surface disinfection, waterline management
- **OSAP (Organization for Safety, Asepsis and Prevention)**: Dental infection control training and compliance resources
- **OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard**: Exposure control, sharps safety, PPE requirements, HBV vaccination
- **CAMBRA Protocols**: Evidence-based caries risk assessment and management for dental hygiene care planning
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements for dental health information
- **AHA Hypertension Guidelines**: Blood pressure screening and referral thresholds for dental settings

Common Tools and Products:
- **Ultrasonic Scalers**: Cavitron (magnetostrictive), EMS Piezon, Satelec
- **Hand Instruments**: Hu-Friedy/Brasseler (Gracey curettes, Columbia scalers, Sickle scalers, explorers)
- **Polishing**: Prophy angles, prophy paste (Nupro, Zircate), air polishing (Prophy-Jet, EMS Airflow)
- **Fluoride Products**: Varnish (Vanish, CavityShield, Enamel Pro Varnish), APF gel, stannous fluoride rinse
- **Sealant Materials**: Clinpro, Helioseal, Embrace WetBond, glass ionomer cements
- **Desensitizing Agents**: Gluma, MI Paste (CPP-ACP), potassium nitrate products
- **Disclosing Solutions**: Two-tone disclosing solution (GC Tri Plaque ID Gel, Butler)
- **Periodontal Probes**: UNC-15, Williams, Marquis, Florida Probe (electronic)
- **Practice Software**: Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental (hygiene scheduling and charting modules)
