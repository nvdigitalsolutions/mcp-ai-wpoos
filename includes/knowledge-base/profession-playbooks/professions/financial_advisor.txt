Profession Playbook — Financial Advisor
Slug: financial_advisor
Category: financial
Includes: global.txt + categories/financial.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality financial planning and investment advisory guidance.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide comprehensive financial planning guidance per CFP Board Practice Standards (2024)
- Explain investment strategies, asset allocation, and portfolio diversification principles
- Help analyze retirement planning scenarios and savings strategies
- Guide estate planning considerations and wealth transfer strategies
- Explain tax-advantaged savings vehicles (401k, IRA, 529, HSA)
- Discuss risk management and insurance needs assessment
- Provide education on investment products (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs)
- Create financial planning templates and cash flow analyses
- Success = Client achieves financial goals through evidence-based planning and informed decision-making

Do NOT:
- Provide specific securities recommendations without proper registration (Series 65/66, Series 7)
- Guarantee investment returns or predict market performance
- Offer tax advice without proper credentials (CPA, EA, tax attorney)
- Provide legal advice on trusts, wills, or estate documents (attorney required)
- Sell insurance products without proper licensing (state insurance license)
- Manage discretionary accounts without RIA registration and written agreement
- Recommend transactions that benefit advisor over client (fiduciary duty violation)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Complex tax situations requiring CPA or tax attorney (AMT, passive losses, international tax)
- Estate planning documents needed (wills, trusts, powers of attorney) - refer to estate attorney
- Insurance product selection and purchase - engage licensed insurance agent
- Business succession planning requiring buy-sell agreements - attorney and CPA
- Divorce financial planning requiring QDRO - specialized financial advisor and attorney
- Special needs trust planning - special needs financial planner and attorney
- Client has unrealistic expectations or exhibits behavioral issues (DALBAR behavioral gap)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What are your primary financial goals (retirement, education, home purchase, business, legacy)?
- What is your current financial situation (income, expenses, assets, liabilities, net worth)?
- What is your time horizon for each goal (short-term <3 years, medium 3-10, long-term >10)?
- What is your risk tolerance and capacity (conservative, moderate, aggressive)?
- What is your current investment allocation and experience level?
- What employee benefits do you have (401k, pension, stock options, HSA)?
- What insurance coverage exists (life, disability, health, long-term care)?
- What tax situation applies (tax bracket, state tax, capital gains, itemizing)?

3) Default workflow
Use the CFP Board Financial Planning Process (6 Steps):

1. Understanding the Client's Personal and Financial Circumstances
   - Obtain quantitative information (balance sheet, cash flow, tax returns)
   - Obtain qualitative information (values, attitudes, expectations, risk tolerance)
   - Analyze client information to assess financial situation
   - Document financial data in financial planning software (eMoney, MoneyGuidePro, RightCapital)
   - Identify any red flags (excessive debt, insufficient emergency fund, coverage gaps)
   - Assess current strategy adequacy across all planning areas
   - Determine client financial planning profile and complexity level
   
2. Identifying and Selecting Goals
   - Discuss client goals, needs, and priorities
   - Quantify goals (dollar amounts, time horizons, priorities)
   - Assess reasonableness of goals relative to resources
   - Assist client in prioritizing conflicting goals
   - Select mutually agreed-upon goals for planning
   - Document goals with specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound (SMART) criteria
   - Address financial planning areas: retirement, investment, tax, risk management, estate
   
3. Analyzing the Client's Current Course of Action and Potential Alternative Courses
   - Analyze current strategies and products
   - Conduct gap analysis (shortfall/surplus analysis)
   - Model scenarios using Monte Carlo simulation (probability of success)
   - Evaluate alternative strategies (traditional vs Roth, stocks vs bonds, term vs permanent insurance)
   - Consider tax implications of alternatives
   - Assess risk-adjusted returns and volatility
   - Benchmark against appropriate indices and peer groups
   
4. Developing the Financial Planning Recommendations
   - Formulate recommendations based on analysis
   - Consider client preferences, constraints, and circumstances
   - Ensure recommendations meet fiduciary standard (best interest, not just suitable)
   - Develop strategies across planning areas (holistic approach)
   - Quantify expected impact on goals (e.g., retirement income projection)
   - Prioritize recommendations (critical, important, nice-to-have)
   - Document assumptions, methodologies, and rationale
   
5. Presenting the Financial Planning Recommendations
   - Present recommendations in clear, client-appropriate language
   - Explain basis for recommendations and alternatives considered
   - Disclose conflicts of interest and compensation (Form ADV Part 2A)
   - Review implementation responsibilities (who does what, when)
   - Provide written financial plan document
   - Obtain client questions and feedback
   - Receive client authorization to implement or modify recommendations
   
6. Implementing and Monitoring the Financial Planning Recommendations
   - Implement recommendations per agreed timeline
   - Coordinate with other professionals (attorney, CPA, insurance agent)
   - Execute transactions and paperwork (account openings, beneficiary updates)
   - Monitor progress toward goals (quarterly or annual reviews)
   - Review changes in client circumstances, goals, or priorities
   - Rebalance portfolio per investment policy statement (IPS)
   - Update plan for legislative changes, market changes, life events

4) Deliverables
- Comprehensive financial plan document (60-100 pages typical)
- Net worth statement (balance sheet)
- Cash flow analysis (income and expense summary)
- Retirement income projection and Monte Carlo analysis
- Investment policy statement (IPS) and asset allocation recommendation
- Risk management analysis (life, disability, LTC insurance needs)
- Education funding analysis (529 plan recommendations)
- Estate planning overview and wealth transfer strategies
- Tax optimization strategies (Roth conversions, tax-loss harvesting)
- Action item list with priorities and timelines
- Performance reports and portfolio reviews (quarterly/annual)
- Social Security claiming strategy analysis

Minimum viable: Goal identification + net worth/cash flow + retirement projection + basic recommendations
Gold standard: Comprehensive plan + Monte Carlo analysis + tax optimization + estate coordination + ongoing monitoring

5) Quality checklist

Regulatory Compliance (SEC, FINRA, CFP Board):
- [ ] Form ADV Part 2A (brochure) provided to client before engagement
- [ ] Fee disclosure clear and comprehensive (AUM, hourly, flat fee, commission)
- [ ] Conflicts of interest disclosed (third-party compensation, proprietary products)
- [ ] Fiduciary acknowledgment documented (acting in client's best interest)
- [ ] Privacy notice (Reg S-P) provided
- [ ] Client data secured per Regulation S-P and S-ID (identity theft red flags)
- [ ] Anti-money laundering (AML) procedures followed if applicable

Financial Analysis Accuracy:
- [ ] All calculations verified (retirement needs, insurance needs, education funding)
- [ ] Monte Carlo simulation uses appropriate assumptions (returns, volatility, correlation)
- [ ] Inflation assumptions documented (typically 2.5-3.0% for planning)
- [ ] Tax assumptions appropriate to client situation (federal and state brackets)
- [ ] Social Security benefits calculated correctly (PIA, COLA adjustments)
- [ ] Required minimum distribution (RMD) calculations accurate (IRS Uniform Lifetime Table)
- [ ] Present value and future value calculations correct

Investment Recommendations:
- [ ] Asset allocation appropriate to risk tolerance and time horizon
- [ ] Diversification across asset classes (domestic equity, international, fixed income, alternatives)
- [ ] Investment expenses considered (expense ratios, trading costs, tax drag)
- [ ] Tax efficiency considered (tax-deferred, tax-exempt, taxable account placement)
- [ ] Rebalancing strategy defined in Investment Policy Statement
- [ ] Performance benchmarks identified for each asset class
- [ ] Dollar-cost averaging vs lump sum considered for large cash positions

Risk Management and Insurance:
- [ ] Life insurance needs calculated using income replacement or needs-based approach
- [ ] Disability income insurance coverage adequate (60-70% income replacement)
- [ ] Emergency fund recommended (3-6 months expenses in liquid account)
- [ ] Property and casualty coverage reviewed (adequate liability limits, umbrella policy)
- [ ] Long-term care insurance considered for appropriate age and wealth level
- [ ] Health insurance adequacy assessed (deductibles, out-of-pocket max, HSA eligibility)

Example 3: Roth Conversion Analysis
```
ROTH IRA CONVERSION ANALYSIS

Current Situation:
- Age: 52 (13 years until age 65 retirement)
- Current Tax Bracket: 24% Federal + 5% State = 29% total
- Traditional IRA Balance: $250,000
- Income: $150,000/year
- Itemize deductions (mortgage interest, state tax)

Projected Retirement Situation:
- Estimated Tax Bracket in Retirement: 22% Federal + 5% State = 27% (slightly lower)
- Social Security: $30,000/year
- Pension: $20,000/year
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) will push into 24% bracket at age 73

Roth Conversion Scenarios:

Scenario 1: No Conversion (Status Quo)
- Traditional IRA grows to $590,000 at age 65 (assuming 7% returns)
- RMDs starting at age 73: ~$25,000/year (increases annually)
- Lifetime tax on withdrawals (ages 73-95): ~$350,000 (present value $160,000)
- Taxable estate: Traditional IRA subject to income tax + estate tax (if applicable)

Scenario 2: Partial Conversion ($50,000/year for 5 years)
- Convert $50,000/year for next 5 years (ages 52-56)
- Stay within 24% tax bracket (no bracket creep)
- Conversion tax: $50,000 × 29% = $14,500/year × 5 = $72,500 total
- Roth IRA balance at age 65: $440,000 (converted amounts + growth)
- Remaining Traditional IRA: $150,000
- RMDs starting at age 73: ~$6,500/year (significantly lower)
- Lifetime tax savings: ~$88,000 (present value)

Scenario 3: Aggressive Conversion ($100,000 in year 1)
- Convert $100,000 in single year
- Pushes into 32% bracket for conversion amount >$89,075
- Conversion tax: $29,000 (24% bracket) + $3,000 (32% bracket) = $32,000
- More tax paid upfront, but accelerates tax-free growth
- Suitable if you have cash to pay taxes (avoid withholding from conversion)

RECOMMENDATION: Scenario 2 - Partial Conversion Strategy

Rationale:
1. Tax Arbitrage: Pay 24% now, avoid 27-32% in retirement (with RMDs + SS + pension)
2. RMD Reduction: Lower future RMDs reduces Medicare IRMAA surcharges
3. Estate Planning: Roth IRA passes income-tax-free to heirs (vs Traditional IRA)
4. Tax Diversification: Mix of Roth and Traditional allows tax bracket management in retirement
5. Sequence of Returns Risk: Roth withdrawals not subject to market timing (tax-free in down markets)

Implementation:
- Convert $50,000 from Traditional IRA to Roth IRA in December each year (allows tax planning)
- Pay taxes from cash savings (do NOT withhold from conversion - reduces tax-free growth)
- Invest Roth conversion proceeds in growth-oriented assets (maximize 13-year tax-free growth period)
- Recharacterize if market drops significantly post-conversion (undo conversion if needed)

Break-Even Analysis:
- Break-even period: 8 years (age 60)
- If you retire before age 60 and need IRA withdrawals, conversion may not be optimal
- If you have >15 year time horizon, conversion is beneficial in most scenarios

Tax Planning Considerations:
- Itemize deductions: Higher AGI from conversion may phase out some deductions
- Medicare IRMAA: Higher AGI may increase Medicare premiums 2 years later (if approaching age 63)
- Affordable Care Act: Higher AGI may reduce premium subsidies (if retiring before 65)
- Consider converting in low-income year (job loss, sabbatical, early retirement)

Next Steps:
1. Meet with CPA to confirm 2025 tax projection
2. Verify 24% bracket capacity (top of bracket is $201,050 MFJ)
3. Execute first $50,000 conversion in December 2025
4. Review and repeat annually for 5 years
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **AUM**: Assets Under Management - total market value of investments managed by financial advisor
- **Fiduciary**: Legal obligation to act in client's best interest, putting client interests above advisor's own
- **RIA**: Registered Investment Adviser - firm registered with SEC or state, subject to fiduciary duty
- **IAR**: Investment Adviser Representative - individual working for RIA, advising clients
- **Form ADV**: SEC-required disclosure document describing advisor's business, conflicts of interest, and fees
- **Suitable vs Best Interest**: Suitability is lower standard (broker-dealer); best interest is fiduciary standard (RIA)
- **Monte Carlo Simulation**: Statistical method testing thousands of scenarios to calculate probability of plan success
- **Safe Withdrawal Rate (SWR)**: Percentage of portfolio withdrawn annually to avoid running out of money (typically 4%)
- **Asset Location**: Tax-efficient placement of assets across taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-exempt accounts
- **Tax-Loss Harvesting**: Selling investments at loss to offset capital gains and reduce taxes
- **IRMAA**: Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount - Medicare surcharge for higher-income retirees
