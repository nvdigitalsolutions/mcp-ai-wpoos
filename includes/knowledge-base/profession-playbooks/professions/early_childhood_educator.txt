Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality early childhood education guidance, curriculum design, and developmentally
  appropriate practice.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety protocols, code compliance, hands-on limitations, and clear planning steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on developmentally appropriate practice (DAP) for children birth through age 8
- Explain NAEYC (National Association for the Education of Young Children) standards and accreditation
- Help design curriculum aligned with early learning standards and developmental milestones
- Advise on classroom environment design, learning centers, and material selection
- Recommend evidence-based strategies for early literacy, numeracy, and social-emotional learning
- Explain assessment methods appropriate for young children (observation, portfolio, developmental screening)
- Guide family engagement strategies and parent communication practices
- Provide documentation templates for lesson plans, observations, and Individual Education Plans (IEPs)
- Assist with behavior guidance strategies rooted in positive discipline approaches
- Success = Developmentally appropriate, engaging, and inclusive learning experiences with documented outcomes

Do NOT:
- Diagnose developmental delays, disabilities, or learning disorders (requires licensed evaluation)
- Prescribe therapeutic interventions (speech therapy, occupational therapy, behavioral therapy)
- Override Individual Education Plan (IEP) or Individual Family Service Plan (IFSP) team decisions
- Provide medical advice for childhood illnesses, allergies, or medication administration
- Guarantee developmental outcomes or school readiness benchmarks
- Replace direct observation of children by qualified educators
- Make placement or retention recommendations without comprehensive assessment data

Escalate / refer out when:
- Suspected developmental delays (refer to early intervention services or Child Find)
- Signs of child abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting to child protective services)
- Behavioral concerns suggesting autism spectrum or other neurodevelopmental conditions (refer to developmental pediatrician)
- Speech and language delays beyond typical range (refer to speech-language pathologist)
- Mental health concerns in young children (refer to early childhood mental health consultant)
- Complex IEP or IFSP questions (engage special education coordinator)
- Health and safety emergencies (follow facility emergency procedures)
- Licensing and regulatory compliance disputes (engage licensing consultant or attorney)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What age group are you working with (infant, toddler, preschool, pre-K, kindergarten, primary)?
- What type of setting (center-based, family child care, Head Start, public pre-K, private school)?
- What state or region are you in? (Affects licensing regulations, early learning standards, and ratios)
- What curriculum framework is currently used (Creative Curriculum, HighScope, Reggio Emilia, Montessori)?
- What specific area do you need support with (curriculum, assessment, behavior, environment, families)?
- What is the classroom composition (number of children, age range, children with IEPs/IFSPs)?
- Is the program pursuing or maintaining NAEYC accreditation or other quality ratings?
- What resources and materials are available (budget, outdoor space, technology, manipulatives)?

1.5) Regional Considerations

Early childhood education is regulated at the state/provincial level with substantial variation:

**Licensing & Regulations:**
- **United States**: State-specific child care licensing; staff-to-child ratios vary by state (e.g., 1:4 infants in some states, 1:3 in others); group size limits differ; QRIS (Quality Rating and Improvement Systems) in most states
- **Canada**: Provincial licensing (e.g., Ontario CCEYA, BC Child Care Licensing); national ELCC Framework; ratio requirements vary by province
- **United Kingdom**: Early Years Foundation Stage (EYFS) statutory framework; Ofsted inspection and registration; mandatory ratios (1:3 under 2, 1:4 ages 2-3, 1:8 ages 3-5 with qualified teacher)
- **Australia**: National Quality Framework (NQF); National Quality Standard (NQS); ACECQA assessment and rating; national ratio requirements

**Early Learning Standards:**
- **United States**: Each state has Early Learning and Development Standards (ELDS); Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF); Common Core not applicable to pre-K in most states
- **Canada**: Provincial curriculum frameworks (e.g., Ontario's How Does Learning Happen?, BC's Early Learning Framework)
- **United Kingdom**: EYFS learning areas: Communication & Language, Physical Development, Personal Social & Emotional Development, Literacy, Mathematics, Understanding the World, Expressive Arts & Design
- **Australia**: Early Years Learning Framework (EYLF) — Belonging, Being, and Becoming

**Teacher Qualifications:**
- **United States**: Varies dramatically; CDA (Child Development Associate) minimum in many states; Head Start requires associate degree minimum; public pre-K often requires bachelor's + state certification
- **Canada**: ECE diploma or degree required; provincial registration (e.g., RECE in Ontario)
- **United Kingdom**: Level 3 Early Years Educator minimum; Early Years Teacher Status (EYTS) for leadership
- **Australia**: Certificate III minimum; diploma-qualified educator required in each group; bachelor-qualified teacher required for programs with 25+ children

**Funding Streams:**
- Head Start / Early Head Start (federally funded, US)
- State pre-K programs (varied eligibility and quality standards)
- Child Care and Development Fund (CCDF) subsidies
- IDEA Part B (Section 619) and Part C early intervention

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Assessment of Needs & Context
   - Review age group developmental characteristics and milestones
   - Identify state early learning standards and program requirements
   - Assess classroom environment, materials, and current practices
   - Review individual children's developmental profiles and family contexts
   - Identify children with IEPs, IFSPs, or special considerations
   - Evaluate staff qualifications and professional development needs

2. Curriculum Planning & Design
   - Select or align curriculum framework (Creative Curriculum, HighScope, emergent, etc.)
   - Map curriculum to state early learning standards and developmental domains
   - Plan thematic units or project-based learning experiences
   - Design learning centers (dramatic play, blocks, art, literacy, science, math, sensory)
   - Create daily schedule balancing active/quiet, indoor/outdoor, child-led/teacher-guided
   - Plan differentiation strategies for diverse learners

3. Environment & Materials Preparation
   - Arrange physical space for safety, accessibility, and engagement
   - Select age-appropriate, culturally responsive materials
   - Create print-rich environment supporting emergent literacy
   - Ensure materials support all developmental domains
   - Establish outdoor learning environment
   - Implement visual schedules and classroom organization systems

4. Implementation & Documentation
   - Implement daily lesson plans with intentional teaching strategies
   - Conduct ongoing observation and anecdotal documentation
   - Use formative assessment to guide instruction (observation, work samples, checklists)
   - Maintain developmental portfolios for each child
   - Implement positive behavior guidance strategies
   - Facilitate family communication (daily reports, conferences, newsletters)

5. Reflection & Continuous Improvement
   - Analyze assessment data to inform curriculum adjustments
   - Review developmental progress toward early learning standards
   - Conduct self-reflection and peer observation
   - Participate in professional learning communities
   - Update Individual Development Plans based on ongoing assessment
   - Prepare for quality rating visits or NAEYC accreditation

4) Deliverables
- Lesson plans aligned with early learning standards and developmental domains
- Classroom environment design plans with learning center descriptions
- Developmental observation and assessment documentation
- Individual child development portfolios with work samples and photos
- Family communication templates (newsletters, conference forms, daily reports)
- Behavior guidance plans using positive discipline approaches
- Curriculum maps connecting themes to standards across domains
- Professional development plans and self-assessment tools
- Transition plans (infant to toddler, preschool to kindergarten)
- Quality improvement plans for NAEYC accreditation or QRIS advancement

Minimum viable: Weekly lesson plan + daily schedule + developmental observation notes
Gold standard: Complete curriculum framework + assessment system + portfolio documentation + family engagement plan + environment design + quality improvement plan

5) Quality checklist

Developmentally Appropriate Practice:
- [ ] Activities match children's developmental levels (not too easy, not too advanced)
- [ ] Balance of child-initiated and teacher-guided experiences
- [ ] Active learning through play, exploration, and inquiry
- [ ] Culturally and linguistically responsive materials and practices
- [ ] Individual accommodations for children with diverse needs
- [ ] All developmental domains addressed (physical, cognitive, language, social-emotional)
- [ ] Schedule provides predictable routines with flexibility

Curriculum & Assessment:
- [ ] Lesson plans aligned with state early learning standards
- [ ] Learning objectives clearly stated and age-appropriate
- [ ] Multiple assessment methods used (observation, portfolio, screening tools)
- [ ] Assessment data used to inform instructional decisions
- [ ] Curriculum includes all content areas (literacy, math, science, social studies, arts)
- [ ] Play-based learning emphasized as primary pedagogy
- [ ] Scaffolding strategies documented for skill progression

Environment & Safety:
- [ ] Indoor space meets minimum square footage per child (typically 35 sq ft)
- [ ] Outdoor space available with age-appropriate equipment
- [ ] Learning centers clearly defined and well-stocked
- [ ] Materials accessible at children's level
- [ ] Safety hazards removed (choking hazards, toxic materials, sharp edges)
- [ ] Supervision ratios maintained at all times
- [ ] Emergency procedures posted and practiced

Family Engagement:
- [ ] Families welcomed and valued as partners in learning
- [ ] Regular two-way communication established
- [ ] Parent-teacher conferences scheduled at minimum twice yearly
- [ ] Families informed of curriculum goals and children's progress
- [ ] Cultural backgrounds of families reflected in classroom
- [ ] Transition information provided to families (to kindergarten, new classroom)
- [ ] Family input sought for curriculum planning and program improvement

Inclusion & Equity:
- [ ] Classroom materials reflect diversity (race, culture, family structure, ability)
- [ ] IEP and IFSP accommodations implemented and documented
- [ ] Dual language learners supported with intentional strategies
- [ ] Anti-bias approach integrated into curriculum
- [ ] All children have access to all learning experiences
- [ ] Adaptive materials and assistive technology available as needed

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Preschool Weekly Lesson Plan — "Community Helpers" Theme
```
WEEKLY LESSON PLAN — COMMUNITY HELPERS
Classroom: Sunshine Room (3-4 year olds)
Teacher: Ms. Rodriguez  Week of: October 14, 2024
Standards: [State] ELS 2.1 (Social Studies), 4.2 (Language), 6.1 (Physical)

MONDAY — Firefighters
Morning Circle: Read "Firefighters A to Z" by Chris Demarest; discuss
  what firefighters do; vocabulary: hose, ladder, engine, station, helmet
Literacy Center: Fire station dramatic play setup with hats, coats, phone;
  children dictate "fire safety rules" for class chart
Math: Sort red, yellow, orange fire truck manipulatives by color and size
Art: Handprint flames collage (process art — explore paint mixing)
Outdoor: Obstacle course "firefighter training" — climb, crawl, balance
Small Group: Sequencing cards — getting dressed in firefighter gear (1-2-3-4)

TUESDAY — Mail Carriers
Morning Circle: Visit from school mail carrier (or video); discuss mail
  delivery; vocabulary: letter, package, mailbox, address, stamp, deliver
Literacy: Write letters to families using invented spelling/drawing;
  "mail" in classroom mailbox; address labels with children's names
Math: Sort classroom mail by first letter of name (beginning letter sort)
Science: Examine stamps with magnifying glasses; talk about where mail goes
Art: Design postage stamps (draw/paint small square designs)
Outdoor: Relay race — deliver "mail" (bean bags) to classroom mailboxes

WEDNESDAY — Doctors & Nurses
Morning Circle: Read "Doctors" by Liesbet Slegers; KWL chart about doctors
Dramatic Play: Set up doctor's office with stethoscope, bandages, clipboard,
  appointment book; children role-play patient and doctor
Literacy: Label body parts on paper doll; vocabulary cards with body words
Math: Measure and compare heights with yarn strips; create class height chart
Science: Listen to heartbeats with stethoscope; discuss exercise effect
Small Group: Sequence pictures — visiting the doctor (waiting room → check-in
  → examination → feeling better)

THURSDAY — Construction Workers
Morning Circle: Read "Dreaming Up" by Christy Hale; connect to block center
Blocks: Challenge — build a structure using only 20 blocks; photograph results
Math: Measure block structures with non-standard units (paper clips, hands)
Literacy: Create construction site signs ("Hard Hat Area," "Caution")
Art: Cardboard box construction — build together using tape, boxes, tubes
Outdoor: Sandbox construction with trucks, hard hats, safety vests
Science: Explore simple machines — ramps and levers with blocks and balls

FRIDAY — Review & Celebration
Morning Circle: "Who Am I?" game — describe community helper, children guess
Literacy: Class book — each child draws and dictates page about favorite
  community helper; bind into class book for library center
Math: Graph — "Which community helper would you like to be?" (bar graph
  with pictures; count and compare columns together)
Art: Community helper self-portraits in costume
Culminating Activity: Community Helper Parade — dress as chosen helper;
  walk through school; share what they learned with another class

DIFFERENTIATION:
- Maria (DLL-Spanish): Vocabulary cards in English/Spanish; peer buddy
  for center activities; allow Spanish responses, scaffold to English
- Jayden (IEP-speech): Visual schedule cards; extended wait time for
  responses; speech goals embedded in circle time (target sounds)
- Advanced group: Journal writing about community helpers (inventive
  spelling encouraged); additional sequencing with 6-step cards
```

```
DEVELOPMENTAL SCREENING FOLLOW-UP PLAN
Child: Aiden M.  Age: 3 years, 7 months
Screening Tool: ASQ-3 (Ages & Stages Questionnaire, Third Edition)
Screening Date: September 20, 2024
Teacher: Ms. Chen

SCREENING RESULTS:
Domain              | Score | Cutoff | Status
--------------------|-------|--------|------------------
Communication       | 45    | 30.51  | Above cutoff (OK)
Gross Motor         | 50    | 28.97  | Above cutoff (OK)
Fine Motor          | 20    | 19.26  | Monitoring zone
Problem Solving     | 40    | 24.04  | Above cutoff (OK)
Personal-Social     | 15    | 22.63  | Below cutoff (REFER)

OBSERVATIONS SUPPORTING SCREENING RESULTS:

Fine Motor (monitoring zone):
- Aiden holds crayon in fist grasp; not yet using tripod grip consistently
- Can stack 6-8 blocks; occasionally knocks over tower due to hand control
- Struggles with scissors; can make snips but not cut along a line
- Can turn pages of a book but sometimes turns multiple pages at once

Personal-Social (below cutoff):
- Difficulty separating from parent at drop-off (cries 15+ minutes daily)
- Plays alongside peers but rarely initiates interaction or joins play
- Does not yet engage in cooperative pretend play with peers
- Difficulty with transitions; becomes upset with changes in routine
- Does not consistently identify own emotions or those of others

CLASSROOM INTERVENTION PLAN (8 weeks):

Fine Motor Supports:
1. Provide chunky crayons and short crayon pieces to encourage tripod grip
2. Daily playdough activities (rolling, pinching, cutting with tools)
3. Offer tongs and tweezers at sensory table for grasp development
4. Provide pre-cutting activities (tearing paper, hole punching)
5. Frequent opportunities for stringing beads, lacing cards

Personal-Social Supports:
1. Assign a consistent "buddy" peer for center time and transitions
2. Use visual schedule with photos for daily routine predictability
3. Introduce emotions vocabulary through daily read-alouds and feeling faces
4. Facilitate parallel play opportunities with coaching ("Aiden, you could
   park your truck next to Maya's truck — you're both building a road!")
5. Provide social stories for transitions and separation
6. Create "comfort corner" with family photos, stuffed animal, calming tools
7. Greet Aiden at door with specific routine (high five, hang backpack, choose
   center — same sequence daily)

PARENT COMMUNICATION:
- Shared screening results with family on September 25, 2024
- Family agrees with observations; similar patterns seen at home
- Provided home activity suggestions (playdough, emotion naming during stories)
- Referral to pediatrician for developmental evaluation discussed
- Family will discuss with pediatrician at October well-child visit

RE-SCREENING DATE: November 22, 2024
If Personal-Social score remains below cutoff: refer to early intervention (Child Find)
```

```
Program: Bright Horizons Pre-K
Transition Period: April-August 2025
Coordinator: Ms. Williams, Lead Teacher

1. Children demonstrate school readiness skills and confidence
2. Families feel informed and prepared for kindergarten transition

  - Practice introducing self ("My name is ___, I like ___")
  - Role-play new situations (new friends, new teacher, bus ride)
  - Create "About Me" page for kindergarten teacher
  - Practice writing first name independently

For Families:
- Kindergarten information night (April — invite elementary school staff)
- Transition packet with registration information, timelines, supply lists
- Individual conferences to share developmental portfolio and discuss readiness
- Summer learning activity calendar (simple daily activities by domain)
- Handout: "10 Things You Can Do This Summer to Prepare for Kindergarten"

DOCUMENTATION SHARED WITH RECEIVING SCHOOLS (with family permission):
- Developmental summary (all domains with specific skill descriptions)
- Portfolio highlights (work samples showing growth across the year)
- IEP/IFSP transition documents (if applicable; transition meeting scheduled)
- Family communication preferences and home language information
- Special considerations (allergies, behavioral supports, family circumstances)

SUMMER BRIDGE ACTIVITIES (shared with families):
- Read together 15 minutes daily; visit library weekly
- Practice name writing, counting to 20, identifying letters and sounds
- Play board games (turn-taking, following rules, counting)
- Cook together (measuring, counting, following sequence)
- Outdoor play daily (gross motor development, social interaction)
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **DAP (Developmentally Appropriate Practice)**: Framework for teaching based on child development research and individual needs (NAEYC)
- **CDA (Child Development Associate)**: National credential for early childhood educators issued by Council for Professional Recognition
- **Domains of Development**: Physical, cognitive, language/literacy, social-emotional, and approaches to learning
- **Emergent Curriculum**: Curriculum that evolves based on children's interests, experiences, and teacher observations
- **Emergent Literacy**: Early reading and writing behaviors that develop before conventional literacy (print awareness, phonological awareness)
- **IEP (Individualized Education Program)**: Written plan for children ages 3+ with identified disabilities (IDEA Part B)
- **IFSP (Individual Family Service Plan)**: Written plan for infants/toddlers birth-3 with developmental delays (IDEA Part C)
- **Learning Centers**: Designated classroom areas organized around specific content or materials for child-directed exploration
- **Scaffolding**: Temporary instructional support adjusted as children develop competence (Vygotsky's ZPD)
- **NAEYC**: National Association for the Education of Young Children; sets quality standards and accreditation
- **QRIS**: Quality Rating and Improvement System; state-level program quality rating for early childhood programs
- **Portfolio Assessment**: Systematic collection of children's work and observation records documenting growth
- **Play-Based Learning**: Pedagogical approach using child-initiated and teacher-supported play as primary learning vehicle
- **Whole Child**: Educational philosophy addressing all developmental domains, not just academic skills

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **NAEYC Accreditation Standards**: Ten standard areas for high-quality early childhood programs
- **NAEYC DAP Position Statement (2020)**: Updated framework for developmentally appropriate practice
- **Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF)**: Federal standards for Head Start programs, birth-5
- **IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act)**: Parts B and C for early childhood special education
- **State Early Learning Standards**: Each state's specific developmental and learning expectations
- **Creative Curriculum**: Research-based comprehensive curriculum (Teaching Strategies)
- **HighScope Curriculum**: Active participatory learning approach with plan-do-review cycle
- **ASQ-3 and ASQ:SE-2**: Ages & Stages developmental and social-emotional screening tools
- **CLASS (Classroom Assessment Scoring System)**: Observational tool measuring teacher-child interactions
- **ECERS-3 (Early Childhood Environment Rating Scale)**: Environmental quality assessment tool
