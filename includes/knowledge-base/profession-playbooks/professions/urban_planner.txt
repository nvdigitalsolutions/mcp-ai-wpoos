Profession Playbook — Urban Planner
Slug: urban_planner
Category: other
Includes: global.txt + categories/other.txt + this file

Intent
- This playbook provides actionable guidance for urban planners developing comprehensive plans, zoning codes, and land use policies following APA (American Planning Association) best practices, state planning laws, and local ordinances.
- Focus on sustainable development, community engagement, regulatory compliance, environmental considerations, transportation planning, and balancing economic development with livability goals.

Category guidance
- Use a practical, general workflow with checklists and templates.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Develop comprehensive plans, general plans, and specific/area plans per state planning enabling legislation
- Create and update zoning ordinances, subdivision regulations, and development standards
- Review development applications (site plans, subdivisions, conditional uses, variances, annexations)
- Conduct community engagement (public meetings, workshops, surveys, focus groups)
- Perform zoning compliance reviews and issue staff reports to planning commissions
- Prepare environmental assessments and NEPA documentation for federal projects
- Coordinate with transportation agencies on land use-transportation integration
- Analyze demographic, economic, and housing trends using GIS and statistical tools
- Facilitate stakeholder collaboration (developers, neighborhood groups, agencies, elected officials)
- Success = Adopted plans that balance growth with livability; development projects approved following clear standards; engaged community with transparent process

Do NOT:
- Guarantee specific development approval outcomes (planning commission/city council decisions discretionary)
- Provide legal advice on property rights, takings claims, or zoning litigation (requires land use attorney)
- Conduct engineering design of infrastructure (streets, utilities) without licensed engineer involvement
- Make decisions on development applications (planners make recommendations; commissions/councils decide)
- Recommend zoning changes violating state law or constitutional property rights
- Override environmental regulations (NEPA, CEQA, ESA) without proper review and mitigation
- Guarantee property values or economic outcomes from planning decisions
- Practice in jurisdictions requiring AICP certification without proper credentials (varies by jurisdiction)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal challenges to zoning decisions or takings claims (refer to land use attorney or city attorney)
- Complex environmental impact analysis (EIS/EIR) requiring specialized expertise (refer to environmental consultant)
- Transportation modeling and traffic impact studies (refer to transportation engineer or traffic consultant)
- Economic development incentives and tax increment financing (refer to economic development specialist)
- Historic preservation and Section 106 compliance (refer to preservation specialist or SHPO)
- Wetlands delineation and Section 404 permitting (refer to wetlands consultant or USACE)
- Affordable housing financial analysis and subsidy programs (refer to housing consultant)
- Public health impact assessment (refer to public health specialist or epidemiologist)
- Complex stakeholder conflicts requiring professional mediation (refer to professional mediator)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 6-10 questions that materially affect your planning approach:
- What is the planning project type (comprehensive plan, zoning update, specific plan, development review)?
- What is the jurisdiction size, character, and growth context (urban, suburban, rural; growing, stable, declining)?
- What are the key planning issues or challenges (housing affordability, traffic congestion, sprawl, revitalization)?
- What is the regulatory framework (state planning law, local ordinances, overlay districts)?
- What is the community demographic and economic profile?
- What stakeholders need to be engaged (residents, business owners, developers, agencies)?
- What is the project timeline and budget?
- Are there existing plans or studies to build upon?
- What environmental or infrastructure constraints exist?
- What are the elected officials' priorities and community values?

3) Default workflow
Use APA-informed planning process:

1. Project Initiation & Research
   - Define project scope, objectives, and deliverables with client/jurisdiction
   - Review existing plans (comprehensive plan, functional plans, previous studies)
   - Compile baseline data: demographics, housing, employment, land use, transportation
   - Analyze GIS data layers (parcels, zoning, environmental constraints, infrastructure)
   - Review regulatory framework (state enabling legislation, local ordinances, case law)
   - Identify stakeholders and develop public engagement strategy
   - Establish project timeline, milestones, and resource allocation
   - Form steering committee or task force with diverse representation
   - Deliverable: Project work plan, baseline conditions report, engagement strategy

2. Community Visioning & Goal Setting
   - Conduct public kick-off meeting explaining process and timeline
   - Facilitate visioning workshops using techniques (scenario planning, charrettes, design games)
   - Administer community surveys (mail, online, intercept) to reach diverse demographics
   - Hold focus groups with key stakeholders (neighborhood associations, business groups, agencies)
   - Analyze community input and identify common themes, priorities, and trade-offs
   - Draft vision statement and goals reflecting community values
   - Present draft vision to steering committee and public for feedback
   - Finalize goals and objectives guiding plan development
   - Deliverable: Community engagement summary, vision statement, goals and objectives

3. Alternatives Development & Analysis
   - Develop planning alternatives (land use scenarios, policy options, growth strategies)
   - Model alternatives using GIS and land use/transportation models (UrbanSim, CommunityViz)
   - Analyze impacts: housing capacity, employment, VMT, infrastructure needs, fiscal impacts
   - Evaluate alternatives against goals using multi-criteria evaluation matrix
   - Conduct environmental review (NEPA/CEQA) screening for significant impacts
   - Present alternatives to public and steering committee using visualizations (maps, 3D renderings)
   - Gather feedback on preferred alternative and modifications
   - Select preferred alternative incorporating community feedback and technical analysis
   - Deliverable: Alternatives analysis report, preferred alternative, environmental documentation

4. Plan/Code Development & Refinement
   - Draft plan elements or code sections: land use, circulation, housing, economic development, etc.
   - Develop policies and implementation actions for each element
   - Create land use maps, zoning maps, and regulatory graphics
   - Write zoning code amendments or new form-based code provisions
   - Prepare implementation chapter with timeframes, responsible parties, and funding sources
   - Circulate draft plan/code to steering committee, agencies, and key stakeholders
   - Revise based on technical review comments and policy direction
   - Conduct public workshops on draft plan/code with opportunity for written comments
   - Finalize plan/code incorporating public and agency feedback
   - Deliverable: Draft and final plan/code documents, maps, implementation matrix

5. Adoption Process & Implementation
   - Prepare planning commission staff report with findings and recommendations
   - Present to planning commission in public hearing (notice per legal requirements)
   - Respond to planning commission questions and public testimony
   - Revise plan/code based on planning commission recommendations
   - Prepare city council staff report and present in public hearing
   - Respond to city council questions and facilitate deliberation
   - Finalize plan/code following council adoption with any amendments
   - Develop implementation workplan with near-term priorities (1-2 years)
   - Monitor implementation through annual progress reports
   - Update plan/code periodically (5-10 year cycles typical) as conditions change
   - Deliverable: Adopted plan/code, staff reports, implementation workplan, monitoring reports

4) Deliverables
Common urban planning artifacts:
- Comprehensive Plan / General Plan with required elements (land use, circulation, housing, etc.)
- Zoning Ordinance with district regulations, use tables, development standards
- Specific Plan / Area Plan for targeted geographic area
- Form-Based Code with regulating plans, building form standards, and public space standards
- Subdivision Regulations governing land division and infrastructure standards
- Staff Reports to planning commission and city council with analysis and recommendations
- Community Engagement Summary documenting public input process
- Environmental Assessment (EA) or Environmental Impact Statement/Report (EIS/EIR)
- GIS Maps (existing conditions, future land use, zoning, constraints, opportunities)
- Development Review Checklists and Application Forms
- Capital Improvement Program (CIP) identifying infrastructure priorities and funding
- Affordable Housing Strategy or Housing Element (required in many states)
- Transportation Plan / Complete Streets Policy
- Urban Design Guidelines for specific districts or corridors

Minimum viable: Land use plan with policies, zoning map, basic community engagement
Gold standard: Comprehensive plan with extensive analysis, robust community engagement, detailed implementation program, adopted by unanimous vote

5) Quality checklist
Before finalizing planning deliverables:

Regulatory Compliance:
- [ ] State planning enabling legislation requirements met (required elements, procedures)
- [ ] Environmental review completed per NEPA/CEQA/state law
- [ ] Public notice requirements met (newspaper, website, mail) per local ordinance
- [ ] Consistency with regional plans (transportation, air quality, water quality)
- [ ] Housing element certified by state (if applicable)
- [ ] ADA accessibility considered in public facilities and rights-of-way

Technical Quality:
- [ ] Data sources credible and current (US Census, state data, local surveys)
- [ ] GIS analysis accurate (parcel data, constraints mapping, build-out analysis)
- [ ] Land use-transportation integration addressed (VMT reduction strategies)
- [ ] Infrastructure capacity adequate (water, sewer, stormwater, schools, parks)
- [ ] Fiscal impact analysis conducted (revenues vs. service costs by land use)
- [ ] Climate action considerations incorporated (GHG reduction, resilience)

Community Engagement:
- [ ] Diverse stakeholders engaged (not just usual participants)
- [ ] Multiple engagement methods used (meetings, surveys, online, focus groups)
- [ ] Input documented and demonstrably incorporated into plan
- [ ] Feedback loop closed (show how input influenced decisions)
- [ ] Materials accessible (plain language, translations, ADA-compliant)
- [ ] Transparency maintained (documents posted online, process explained)

Implementation:
- [ ] Policies specific and actionable (not vague aspirations)
- [ ] Responsible parties identified for each action
- [ ] Timeframes realistic (near-term, mid-term, long-term)
- [ ] Funding sources identified or strategies proposed
- [ ] Monitoring indicators established (track progress over time)
- [ ] Adoption resolutions prepared with necessary findings

6) Templates (paste-ready)

=== Comprehensive Plan Policy Template ===
Element: [Land Use / Housing / Circulation / Economic Development]
Policy Number: [LU-1.1]

Goal: [High-level aspirational statement]
Example: Promote sustainable, mixed-use development that supports walkability and transit use.

Policy: [Specific direction guiding decisions]
Example: LU-1.1 - Encourage mixed-use development in designated Mixed-Use Districts along transit corridors with ground-floor retail and upper-floor residential or office uses.

Implementation Actions:
- Action 1: Adopt form-based code for Mixed-Use Districts (Timeframe: Year 1, Responsibility: Planning Dept)
- Action 2: Offer density bonus for projects including affordable housing (Timeframe: Year 1, Responsibility: Planning & Housing)
- Action 3: Partner with transit agency to improve pedestrian access to stations (Timeframe: Years 2-3, Responsibility: Public Works & Transit Agency)

Performance Measures:
- Percent of new development in Mixed-Use Districts that includes ground-floor commercial (Target: >75%)
- Number of housing units within 1/2 mile of transit stations (Target: 5,000 by 2035)

=== Community Workshop Facilitation Agenda ===
Workshop: [Comprehensive Plan Update - Land Use Alternatives]
Date/Time: [Date, 6:00-8:00 PM]
Location: [Community Center, 123 Main St]

6:00-6:10 Welcome & Introductions
- Welcome by [City Planner]
- Purpose of workshop and agenda overview
- Ground rules for respectful participation

6:10-6:30 Project Background Presentation
- Recap of project timeline and previous engagement
- Review of community vision and goals
- Baseline conditions summary (demographics, housing, jobs, transportation)

6:30-7:00 Alternatives Presentation
- Alternative 1: [Status Quo / Current Trends]
- Alternative 2: [Moderate Growth / Balanced Development]
- Alternative 3: [Transit-Oriented Development / Urban Villages]
- Comparison matrix: housing, jobs, VMT, infrastructure costs

7:00-7:45 Small Group Discussions
- Facilitated table discussions (8-10 people per table, assigned facilitator)
- Discussion questions:
  1. Which alternative best reflects community values? Why?
  2. What do you like about each alternative?
  3. What concerns do you have?
  4. What modifications would improve the alternatives?
- Document feedback on flip charts

7:45-7:55 Report Out
- Each table reports key themes (2 minutes per table)
- Planner captures feedback on master flip chart

7:55-8:00 Next Steps & Closing
- Next engagement opportunities (online survey, follow-up workshop)
- How input will be used (refine preferred alternative)
- Thank participants, distribute evaluation forms

Materials Provided:
- Workshop agenda
- Alternatives summary handout with maps
- Comment cards for written feedback
- Refreshments and childcare (if provided)

```
Project: Riverside Mixed-Use Development (retail + 80 residential units)
General Plan Designation: Commercial Mixed-Use (CMU)
Zoning: C-2 (General Commercial)

Consistency Analysis:

Land Use Element:
✓ Policy LU-2.1 (Encourage mixed-use in downtown): Project provides ground-floor retail with residential above, implementing this policy.
✓ Policy LU-2.5 (Min. residential density 20 du/ac in CMU areas): Project density = 32 du/ac (CONSISTENT)
✓ Policy LU-3.2 (Active ground floor uses): 12,000 sf retail with storefront windows and pedestrian entries (CONSISTENT)

Housing Element:
✓ Policy H-1.2 (Support diverse housing types): Project includes studios, 1BR, 2BR units (CONSISTENT)
✓ Policy H-2.3 (Affordable housing requirement): 8 units (10%) at 80% AMI provided (CONSISTENT)

Circulation Element:
✓ Policy C-1.5 (Reduce vehicle trips): Project located 0.2 mi from transit station, includes bike parking (CONSISTENT)
⚠ Policy C-2.1 (Complete streets): Streetscape improvements needed on Main St frontage (CONDITION REQUIRED)

Environmental Quality:
✓ Policy EQ-3.2 (LEED Silver for new construction): Project committed to LEED Silver certification (CONSISTENT)

Economic Development:
✓ Policy ED-1.1 (Strengthen downtown): Project adds ground-floor retail and residential to support downtown vitality (CONSISTENT)

CONCLUSION: Project is consistent with General Plan. Recommend condition requiring streetscape improvements per Circulation Element Policy C-2.1 (wider sidewalk, street trees, bike lane connection).
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Comprehensive Plan / General Plan**: Long-range policy document guiding land use, growth, and development (20-30 year horizon typical)
- **Zoning Ordinance**: Regulatory code implementing comprehensive plan through district regulations and development standards
- **Conditional Use Permit (CUP)**: Discretionary approval for uses requiring case-by-case review due to potential impacts
- **Variance**: Relief from strict application of zoning standards due to practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship
- **Specific Plan / Area Plan**: Detailed plan for defined geographic area with more specificity than general plan
- **Form-Based Code**: Zoning approach regulating physical form (building type, placement, frontage) rather than use
- **Transit-Oriented Development (TOD)**: Compact, mixed-use development within walking distance of transit
- **Complete Streets**: Streets designed for all users (pedestrians, bicyclists, transit, vehicles) safely
- **NEPA**: National Environmental Policy Act requiring environmental review for federal actions

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **APA (American Planning Association)**: Professional association with policy guides, PAS reports, and AICP certification
- **Planners' Code of Ethics**: AICP code governing professional conduct (adopted 1981, amended 2016)
- **Smart Growth Principles**: Compact development, mixed uses, walkability, transit access, preservation (APA/EPA)
- **LEED-ND (Neighborhood Development)**: Green building rating system for sustainable neighborhoods (USGBC)
- **ITE (Institute of Transportation Engineers)**: Trip generation rates, parking standards, complete streets guidance
- **State Planning Enabling Acts**: State laws authorizing local planning (varies by state - CA Gov't Code §65000+, NY Town Law, etc.)
- **Standard City Planning Enabling Act (1928)**: Model legislation influencing state planning laws nationwide

Common Tools:
- GIS Software: ArcGIS, QGIS (spatial analysis, mapping, land use modeling)
- Land Use Modeling: UrbanSim, CommunityViz, MetroQuest (scenario planning, build-out analysis)
- Survey Platforms: SurveyMonkey, Polco, Balancing Act (community input, budget prioritization)
- Engagement: MindMixer (now Zencity), Neighborland, Social Pinpoint (online engagement platforms)
- Transportation: VISUM, TransCAD, Cube (travel demand modeling)
- Plan Software: Adobe InDesign, Microsoft Word (document layout and graphics)
- Code Software: Municode, General Code (codification and online publication)
- 3D Visualization: SketchUp, Rhino, Lumion (urban design visualizations)
