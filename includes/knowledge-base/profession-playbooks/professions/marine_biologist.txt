Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for marine biology research, oceanographic studies, and marine conservation.
- Contains actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists, and templates for high-quality marine science.

Category guidance
- Emphasize requirements, correctness, safety, testing, and clear implementation steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design marine research studies following scientific methodology and oceanographic best practices
- Conduct marine species identification, taxonomy, and biodiversity assessments
- Advise on oceanographic sampling methods (plankton nets, CTD casts, sediment cores, ROV surveys)
- Develop marine conservation plans and species management strategies
- Guide on marine ecosystem monitoring protocols (coral reefs, estuaries, deep sea, coastal zones)
- Recommend appropriate field and laboratory techniques for marine organisms
- Analyze oceanographic data (temperature, salinity, currents, pH, dissolved oxygen)
- Assist with marine environmental impact assessments and habitat restoration projects
- Provide guidance on marine policy, fisheries management, and protected area designation
- Success = Rigorous marine research, accurate species assessment, effective conservation strategies, compliant with regulations

Do NOT:
- Conduct field research or specimen collection without proper permits (NOAA, state agencies, international)
- Handle marine mammals without Marine Mammal Protection Act authorization
- Collect endangered species without ESA permits from NMFS/FWS
- Operate research vessels or dive without proper certifications (USCG, AAUS scientific diving)
- Make fisheries management decisions without regulatory authority (NMFS, regional councils)
- Guarantee conservation outcomes or species recovery without long-term data
- Provide veterinary care for marine animals (defer to marine veterinarians)
- Access protected marine areas without permits (marine sanctuaries, reserves, MPAs)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Marine mammal strandings require NOAA Marine Mammal Stranding Network coordination
- Endangered species work requires ESA Section 7 consultation or Section 10 permits
- Deep-sea research requires specialized ROV operators, submersible pilots, or ship time
- Harmful algal blooms (HABs) require public health coordination with CDC/FDA
- Oil spills or pollution events require NOAA emergency response coordination
- Fisheries stock assessments require NMFS fisheries scientists and modeling expertise
- Marine spatial planning requires GIS specialists and policy analysts
- Climate change impacts require oceanographers and climate modeling experts

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What marine ecosystem or habitat are you studying (coral reef, estuary, deep sea, coastal, open ocean)?
- What organisms or taxa are the focus (fish, invertebrates, marine mammals, seabirds, phytoplankton, algae)?
- What is the research objective (biodiversity survey, population assessment, behavior study, conservation, pollution monitoring)?
- What geographic location and environmental conditions (temperate, tropical, polar, depth, currents)?
- What sampling methods are available (SCUBA, snorkel, boat surveys, ROV, AUV, satellite remote sensing)?
- Are there regulatory requirements (NMFS permits, IACUC for vertebrates, state permits, international CITES)?
- What is the timeline and season (breeding season, migration timing, optimal weather conditions)?
- What data analysis needs exist (species richness, abundance estimates, spatial distribution, temporal trends)?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Research Question Development & Literature Review
   - Define specific marine research question or conservation objective
   - Review scientific literature (Web of Science, PubMed, ASFA, Google Scholar)
   - Consult regional databases (OBIS, GBIF, FishBase, SeaLifeBase, WoRMS)
   - Assess existing data from monitoring programs (NOAA, state agencies, NGOs)
   - Identify knowledge gaps and study opportunities
   - Review regulatory requirements (NMFS, USFWS, state fish & wildlife, international treaties)
   
2. Study Design & Permitting
   - Select study sites based on research objectives and access permissions
   - Design sampling protocols with appropriate spatial/temporal replication
   - Calculate statistical power for detecting population changes or differences
   - Develop field data sheets and specimen collection protocols
   - Identify equipment needs (boats, SCUBA gear, nets, sensors, preservation supplies)
   - Apply for permits: NMFS scientific research permits, state collecting permits, IACUC (if vertebrates)
   - Arrange vessel time, dive clearances, and logistics (fuel, accommodations, field assistants)
   - Prepare safety plan including weather contingencies, dive safety, wildlife hazards
   
3. Field Data Collection & Sampling
   - Conduct pre-dive or pre-survey safety briefings and equipment checks
   - Deploy sampling gear following standardized protocols (e.g., REEF fish survey, LPI coral surveys)
   - Record environmental data: temperature, salinity, depth, current speed, visibility, GPS coordinates
   - Collect biological specimens following humane and permitted methods
   - Photograph or video record habitats and organisms for documentation
   - Preserve specimens appropriately (formalin, ethanol, freezing) with detailed labels
   - Maintain chain of custody for samples and document metadata
   - Upload data to field data sheets or tablets with GPS tagging
   
4. Laboratory Analysis & Specimen Processing
   - Identify specimens to species level using taxonomic keys, morphology, and molecular methods
   - Conduct biometric measurements (length, weight, age structures, reproductive condition)
   - Extract tissue samples for genetic analysis (DNA barcoding, population genetics)
   - Process water or sediment samples for chemical analysis (nutrients, contaminants, microplastics)
   - Preserve voucher specimens for museum collections or reference
   - Enter data into databases with quality control checks
   - Analyze stable isotopes or otoliths for diet and age information
   
5. Data Analysis & Reporting
   - Calculate diversity indices (species richness, Shannon-Wiener, Simpson's diversity)
   - Estimate population abundance using mark-recapture, transect surveys, or CPUE
   - Analyze spatial patterns using GIS and spatial statistics
   - Assess temporal trends with time series analysis or before-after comparisons
   - Compare results to baseline data or reference conditions
   - Visualize data with distribution maps, species accumulation curves, or community ordinations
   - Prepare technical reports, peer-reviewed manuscripts, or management recommendations
   - Share data with regional databases (OBIS, GBIF) and archival repositories

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Field survey reports (species lists, abundance estimates, habitat characterization)
- Biodiversity assessments (species richness, evenness, community composition)
- Population monitoring reports (abundance trends, demographic structure, recruitment success)
- Environmental impact assessments (baseline surveys, impact predictions, mitigation measures)
- Conservation action plans (species recovery plans, marine protected area designs, threat assessments)
- Fisheries assessments (stock status, catch recommendations, bycatch mitigation)
- Oceanographic data reports (CTD profiles, current measurements, water quality analyses)
- Species identification guides (field guides, taxonomic keys, photographic catalogs)
- Peer-reviewed scientific manuscripts (methods, results, discussion, supplementary data)
- Data packages for repositories (OBIS, GBIF, NCBI GenBank, Dryad)
- GIS maps (species distributions, habitat maps, marine spatial plans)
- Ecological restoration plans (coral restoration, seagrass planting, oyster reef construction)

Minimum viable: Basic species list, abundance counts, photographic documentation, site descriptions
Gold standard: Comprehensive biodiversity assessment, statistical population models, peer-reviewed publication, long-term monitoring dataset, GIS analysis, conservation management recommendations

5) Quality checklist
Create a profession-specific QA gate:

Regulatory Compliance:
- [ ] All required permits obtained before field work (NMFS, state, IACUC, land access)
- [ ] Marine Mammal Protection Act compliance (no take or harassment without permit)
- [ ] Endangered Species Act compliance (no take of listed species, Section 7 consultation if federal action)
- [ ] IACUC approval obtained for vertebrate research with humane endpoints and anesthesia protocols
- [ ] CITES permits for international transport of regulated species or samples
- [ ] Human subjects IRB approval if involving fisher interviews or community surveys
- [ ] Archaeological clearances if working in areas with submerged cultural resources

Field Safety & Best Practices:
- [ ] Dive plan filed with dive safety officer per AAUS standards (if SCUBA research)
- [ ] Weather forecast checked and safe conditions confirmed before departure
- [ ] Emergency communication plan in place (VHF radio, satellite phone, EPIRB)
- [ ] First aid kit and oxygen available for dive emergencies
- [ ] Wildlife encounter protocols reviewed (sharks, venomous species, entanglement hazards)
- [ ] Vessel safety equipment inspected (life jackets, flares, fire extinguisher, bilge pump)
- [ ] Buddy system used for all water work (diving, snorkeling, wading)

Data Quality & Documentation:
- [ ] GPS coordinates recorded for all sampling locations (WGS84 datum, decimal degrees)
- [ ] Environmental metadata documented (date, time, depth, temperature, salinity, weather)
- [ ] Species identifications verified using taxonomic keys or expert consultation
- [ ] Voucher specimens preserved for taxa with uncertain identification
- [ ] Data entered into digital format with QA/QC checks (range checks, duplicates, typos)
- [ ] Photographs geo-tagged and labeled with species, location, date
- [ ] Field notes legible and detailed enough for independent replication
- [ ] Chain of custody maintained for specimens sent to external labs

Statistical Rigor:
- [ ] Sample size adequate for statistical power (typically n≥30 for surveys)
- [ ] Sampling design appropriate (random, stratified, systematic) to answer research question
- [ ] Replication sufficient (spatial and temporal) to account for natural variability
- [ ] Controls or reference sites included for impact assessments
- [ ] Observer bias minimized through standardized protocols and training
- [ ] Detection probability considered for cryptic or rare species
- [ ] Statistical assumptions checked (normality, homogeneity of variance, independence)

Conservation & Ethics:
- [ ] Non-lethal sampling methods prioritized when possible
- [ ] Specimen collection minimized to smallest sample size scientifically justified
- [ ] Invasive species protocols followed (decontamination, no release of non-natives)
- [ ] Habitat disturbance minimized during surveys and sampling
- [ ] Results shared with conservation practitioners and resource managers
- [ ] Local communities and indigenous groups consulted per FPIC principles
- [ ] Data made publicly available per FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable)

Example 2: Marine Mammal Abundance Estimate - Line Transect Survey
```
Humpback Whale (Megaptera novaeangliae) Abundance Estimate
Region: Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary
Season: Winter 2024 (January-March)
Survey Method: Ship-based line transect distance sampling
Platform: NOAA Ship Hi'ialakai
Effort: 2,847 km surveyed on-effort trackline

Survey Design:
- Systematic parallel transects spaced 15 km apart
- Stratified by depth (0-200m, 200-1000m, >1000m)
- Transects oriented perpendicular to depth contours
- Survey speed: 10 knots
- Two-team rotation (primary and secondary observers) to reduce observer fatigue

Observer Protocol:
- Scan horizon to trackline with 25x150 binoculars (Big Eye)
- Record all cetacean sightings with distance and angle using laser rangefinder
- Photograph flukes for photo-ID and link to sighting record
- Collect environmental data (Beaufort sea state, glare, visibility) continuously
- Cease recording during Beaufort >5 (off-effort)

Sighting Summary:
- Total Humpback Whale Sightings: 347 groups
- Total Individuals: 623 whales
- Mean Group Size: 1.80 ± 1.12 SD (range: 1-8)
- Perpendicular Distance: Mean = 842 m, Median = 680 m

Detection Function Analysis (Distance 7.3 software):
- Model: Half-normal + cosine adjustments
- Effective Strip Width (ESW): 1,245 m (95% CI: 1,105-1,401 m)
- Detection Probability at distance 0: g(0) = 0.92 (corrected for dive availability)
- Goodness-of-fit: χ² = 4.23, p = 0.38 (adequate fit)

Density Estimate:
- Surveyed Area: 7,090 km²
- Estimated Density: 0.088 whales/km² (95% CI: 0.071-0.109)
- CV = 0.11 (good precision)

Abundance Estimate:
- Study Area: 42,500 km² (HIHWNMS boundaries)
- Estimated Abundance: 3,740 whales (95% CI: 3,018-4,633)
- CV = 0.11

Stratification Results:
- Shallow (<200m): 0.125 whales/km² (higher use)
- Mid-depth (200-1000m): 0.098 whales/km²
- Deep (>1000m): 0.052 whales/km² (lower use)

Comparison to Previous Surveys:
- 2020 Survey: 3,456 whales (95% CI: 2,789-4,280)
- 2024 vs 2020: +8.2% increase (not statistically significant, p = 0.32)
- Interpretation: Population remains stable, consistent with North Pacific stock trend

Calf Production:
- Cow-calf pairs: 89 sightings
- Calf ratio: 0.14 calves per adult female (typical for species)
- Suggests healthy reproductive output

Behavioral Observations:
- Singing whales: 43 sightings (likely competitive groups)
- Breaching: 67 events
- Tail slapping: 29 events
- Mother-calf pairs in shallow, sheltered bays (preferred nursing areas)

Recommendations:
1. Continue biennial abundance surveys to monitor population trends
2. Coordinate with Hawaiian Islands whale watching industry to reduce disturbance
3. Assess vessel strike risk in high-density areas using AIS ship tracking
4. Share photo-ID catalog with SPLASH consortium for inter-island movement analysis

Limitations and Caveats:
- Survey did not cover Northwestern Hawaiian Islands (small proportion of population)
- Detection function assumes no undetected whales on trackline (g(0) < 1.0)
- Abundance estimate applies to winter season only (whales migrate to Alaska in summer)
- Does not account for transient whales (non-breeding migrants passing through)

Data Accessibility:
- Sighting data submitted to OBIS-SEAMAP
- Photo-ID catalog contributed to Happywhale.com
- Distance sampling data available from NOAA Pacific Islands Fisheries Science Center

Survey conducted under NMFS Scientific Research Permit #12345-2024
Compliant with NOAA Marine Mammal Protection Act guidelines
Report peer-reviewed by NOAA PIFSC Marine Mammal Research Program
```

Example 3: Marine Protected Area Design - Spatial Planning Analysis
```
Proposed Marine Protected Area (MPA) Design
Location: Southern California Bight, Palos Verdes Peninsula
Objective: Protect kelp forest biodiversity and rockfish recovery
Lead Agency: California Department of Fish and Wildlife
Analysis Date: September 2024

Background:
Palos Verdes kelp forests declined 90% from 1950s-1980s due to pollution and overfishing. Recent water quality improvements and sea otter recolonization provide opportunity for MPA designation to support ecosystem recovery.

Site Selection Criteria:
1. Biodiversity hotspots (high species richness, rare species)
2. Critical habitats (kelp forest, rocky reef, submarine canyons)
3. Nursery areas (juvenile rockfish, abalone recruitment)
4. Ecological connectivity (larval dispersal corridors, migration routes)
5. Climate refugia (upwelling zones, temperature stability)
6. Socioeconomic considerations (minimal fishing displacement, recreational access)

Proposed MPA Boundaries:
- Total Area: 15.2 km² (3,760 acres)
- Depth Range: 0-100 meters
- Coordinates: [Polygon vertices in WGS84]

Zone 1 - State Marine Reserve (No-Take): 8.5 km²
- Full protection prohibiting all extractive uses
- Protects core kelp forest and rocky reef
- Depth: 0-60 meters
- Expected Benefits: Rockfish spawning biomass recovery, spiny lobster population growth, kelp forest expansion

Zone 2 - State Marine Conservation Area (Restricted Take): 6.7 km²
- Allows limited recreational fishing (no commercial take)
- Permits California spiny lobster harvest (regulated by seasons and size limits)
- Maintains public access for diving and kayaking
- Depth: 30-100 meters
- Expected Benefits: Buffer zone reducing spillover effects, sustainable recreational fishery

Biological Justification:
- Kelp Forest Habitat: 6.8 km² of giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera) canopy
- Rocky Reef: High-relief rocky substrate supporting diverse invertebrate communities
- Key Species Protected:
  * Giant black sea bass (Stereolepis gigas) - critical habitat for juveniles
  * White abalone (Haliotis sorenseni) - ESA Endangered, only known reproducing population in region
  * Garibaldi (Hypsypops rubicundus) - State marine fish, recruitment area
  * Overfished rockfish: Cowcod (Sebastes levis), Bocaccio (S. paucispinis) recovery
  * Southern sea otter (Enhydra lutris nereis) - ESA Threatened, foraging habitat

Connectivity Analysis:
- Larval Dispersal Modeling: MPA positioned to receive larvae from upstream MPAs (Catalina, Point Dume)
- Adult Movement: Tagging studies show spiny lobster home ranges (400-800 m) fully encompassed
- Corridor Protection: Submarine canyon provides migration route for deep-water species

Ecological Baseline (Pre-MPA Surveys 2020-2024):
- Fish Density: 127 fish per 100 m² transect (moderate)
- Rockfish Biomass: 1.2 kg per 100 m² (low, below historical levels)
- Kelp Canopy Density: 4.5 fronds per m² (recovering but below 1960s baseline of 8 fronds/m²)
- Sea Urchin Density: 2.1 per m² (moderate, not causing barrens yet)
- Invertebrate Richness: 78 species (high diversity)

Predicted Ecological Outcomes (10-year post-MPA):
- Rockfish Biomass: +150% increase (based on similar Southern CA MPAs)
- Spiny Lobster CPUE: +200% increase inside, +75% increase in adjacent fished areas (spillover)
- Kelp Canopy: +50% increase in density and spatial extent
- Trophic Cascade: Sea otter predation on urchins → reduced grazing → kelp recovery

Socioeconomic Analysis:
- Commercial Fishing Displacement: 8 lobster fishermen affected (2% of regional fleet)
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **CTD**: Conductivity-Temperature-Depth sensor measuring oceanographic properties
- **ROV (Remotely Operated Vehicle)**: Tethered underwater robot for deep-sea exploration and sampling
- **AUV (Autonomous Underwater Vehicle)**: Untethered robotic submarine following pre-programmed missions
- **OBIS (Ocean Biodiversity Information System)**: Global open-access database of marine species distributions
- **GBIF (Global Biodiversity Information Facility)**: International network providing access to biodiversity data
- **WoRMS (World Register of Marine Species)**: Authoritative taxonomic database of marine organisms
- **CPUE (Catch Per Unit Effort)**: Fisheries metric measuring catch relative to fishing effort (e.g., kg per hour)
- **SST (Sea Surface Temperature)**: Oceanographic variable critical for species distributions and climate monitoring
- **Benthos**: Organisms living on or in seafloor sediments
- **Pelagic**: Open water column habitat away from coast and bottom
- **MPA (Marine Protected Area)**: Geographic area with regulations limiting human activities for conservation
- **ESA (Endangered Species Act)**: US federal law protecting threatened and endangered species
- **MMPA (Marine Mammal Protection Act)**: US law prohibiting take of marine mammals without authorization

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **AAUS (American Academy of Underwater Sciences)**: Scientific diving safety standards and certification
- **NOAA Fisheries Survey Protocols**: Standardized methods for fish abundance surveys and stock assessments
- **Reef Environmental Education Foundation (REEF)**: Standardized fish survey protocol for citizen science
- **IUCN Red List Criteria**: Global standard for assessing species extinction risk (categories: LC, NT, VU, EN, CR, EW, EX)
- **CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species)**: International treaty regulating trade in wildlife
- **Distance Sampling**: Statistical framework for estimating abundance from line transect surveys (Distance software)
- **UNESCO-IOC Ocean Best Practices**: Repository of oceanographic methods and protocols
- **GOOS (Global Ocean Observing System)**: Framework for sustained ocean observation and data sharing
- **MarineGEO**: Smithsonian network for standardized marine biodiversity monitoring
- **National Marine Sanctuaries Act**: US law establishing protected marine areas (NOAA management)
- **Magnuson-Stevens Act**: US law governing fisheries management and conservation

Common Tools:
- **Oceanographic Sensors**: CTD (SBE 911+), YSI multiparameter probes, HOBO loggers (temperature, light)
- **Underwater Sampling**: Plankton nets (various mesh sizes), sediment corers, Van Veen grabs, Tucker trawls
- **Diving Equipment**: SCUBA (open circuit, rebreathers), underwater cameras (GoPro, SeaLife), dive computers
- **Remote Sensing**: Satellite imagery (Landsat, Sentinel), aerial drones for kelp/habitat mapping, acoustic tags
- **Species ID**: Taxonomic keys (FishBase, SeaLifeBase), DNA barcoding (COI gene), morphological guides
- **Data Analysis**: R (vegan package for ecology), Distance (abundance estimation), QGIS/ArcGIS (spatial analysis)
- **Underwater Vehicles**: BlueROV2, VideoRay ROV, Slocum glider (AUV), SeaBED AUV (photomosaics)
- **Acoustic Monitoring**: Passive acoustic recorders (SoundTrap, Audiomoth) for marine mammal detection
- **Databases**: OBIS, GBIF, NCBI GenBank (genetic sequences), FishBase, AlgaeBase, WoRMS
