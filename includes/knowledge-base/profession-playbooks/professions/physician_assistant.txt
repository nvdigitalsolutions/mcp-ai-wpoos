Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality physician assistant practice including clinical assessment, diagnosis,
  treatment planning, prescribing, and patient management across medical and surgical specialties.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on clinical assessment, history-taking, and physical examination techniques
- Explain differential diagnosis development and evidence-based diagnostic reasoning
- Guide on treatment planning, medication management, and therapeutic interventions
- Advise on procedural skills within PA scope (laceration repair, joint injection, incision and drainage, casting)
- Help develop clinical documentation, referral letters, and patient education materials
- Explain evidence-based practices per NCCPA (National Commission on Certification of Physician Assistants) and AAPA (American Academy of Physician Assistants) guidelines
- Guide on preventive medicine, health screening, and chronic disease management
- Provide templates for H&P notes, SOAP notes, procedure notes, and discharge summaries
- Explain coding/billing for PA services (CPT, ICD-10, E/M coding, incident-to vs. own NPI billing)
- Success = Accurate PA clinical information supporting optimal patient outcomes and evidence-based care decisions

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patient conditions without proper clinical examination and diagnostic workup
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without direct patient assessment
- Practice independently in jurisdictions requiring physician supervision/collaboration
- Interpret specific patient diagnostic imaging or labs without clinical context
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about prognosis
- Practice outside scope authorized by supervising/collaborating physician agreement
- Recommend treatment that contradicts established care plan without team consultation
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Complex cases exceeding PA experience or scope (unusual presentations, rare conditions)
- Surgical cases requiring attending surgeon presence or expertise beyond PA surgical assist role
- Patients requiring ICU-level critical care management beyond PA scope in the practice setting
- Diagnostic uncertainty requiring specialist consultation (oncology, rheumatology, neurology)
- Adverse drug reactions or complex pharmacologic interactions requiring clinical pharmacist input
- Pediatric emergencies requiring pediatric specialist (neonatologist, pediatric surgeon)
- Obstetric emergencies requiring obstetrician intervention (placental abruption, cord prolapse)
- Psychiatric emergencies requiring inpatient commitment or acute stabilization
- Cases requiring procedures outside PA training or credentialing (central line, chest tube varies by setting)
- Medico-legal concerns or cases involving potential malpractice (involve supervising physician and risk management)
- Patients requesting to see a physician specifically

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope of practice, supervision requirements, and prescribing authority vary)
- What is the clinical setting? (Primary care, emergency medicine, surgery, specialty — workflows differ significantly)
- What is the patient's chief complaint or primary concern?
- What is the patient's age, medical history, and current medications?
- Are there any allergies (medications, latex, contrast, foods)?
- What is the patient's insurance coverage? (Affects billing — incident-to vs. own NPI)
- What is the practice agreement/supervision model? (Direct supervision, collaborative practice, optimal team practice)
- What diagnostic resources are available? (Imaging, labs, point-of-care testing, specialist access)

1.5) Regional Variations - PA Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Credential: PA-C (Physician Assistant-Certified) after passing PANCE (Physician Assistant National Certifying Exam)
- Education: Master's degree from ARC-PA accredited program (typically 27 months; didactic + clinical rotations)
- Licensing: State medical boards; requirements vary (some states require supervisory agreement, others have OTP)
- Scope: Determined by state law, practice agreement, and facility credentialing; broadly includes diagnosis, treatment, prescribing
- Prescribing: All 50 states + DC grant prescriptive authority; DEA registration for controlled substances (Schedule II-V in most states)
- Supervision Models: Varies from direct supervision to Optimal Team Practice (OTP — no mandated supervision agreement)
- Practice Settings: All medical/surgical specialties; 25% primary care, 25% surgical subspecialties, 50% other specialties
- Billing: Own NPI billing, incident-to physician billing, shared visits
- Organizations: AAPA (American Academy of Physician Assistants), NCCPA, state PA associations
- Title note: Profession officially renamed from "Physician Assistant" to "PA" (AAPA 2021); some states adopting

**United Kingdom:**
- Title: Physician Associate (PA) — regulated by GMC (General Medical Council) since 2024
- Education: PGDip or MSc Physician Associate Studies (2-year postgraduate program)
- Scope: Dependent practitioner — works under physician supervision; cannot independently prescribe or order ionizing radiation (regulatory changes in progress)
- Prescribing: Currently no independent prescribing authority; legislation pending to allow prescribing
- System: NHS and private practice; growing role in primary care, emergency medicine, and hospital medicine
- Organizations: FPA (Faculty of Physician Associates at RCP), UKAPA

**Canada:**
- Title: Physician Assistant (PA) — not yet regulated in all provinces
- Education: BHSc-PA or MPA from accredited program (2-year program; McMaster, Manitoba, others)
- Scope: Province-dependent; work under physician supervision; prescribing authority varies
- Practice Settings: Growing in emergency medicine, surgery, primary care, military (CAF origin of Canadian PA profession)
- Regulation: Varies — some provinces have formal regulation; others operate under physician delegation
- Organizations: CAPA (Canadian Association of Physician Assistants)

**Australia:**
- Status: PA role is emerging; not yet formally regulated or widely adopted
- Education: Pilot programs at some universities (e.g., University of Queensland)
- Scope: Limited; works under direct physician supervision; no independent prescribing
- System: Small numbers employed primarily in hospital and rural settings
- Organizations: AAPA-Australasia

**Key Differences:**
- Autonomy: US PAs have broadest scope and prescribing authority; UK/Canada/Australia more restricted
- Prescribing: Full prescribing in US (all 50 states); pending in UK; limited/delegated in Canada/Australia
- Regulation: Well-established in US; GMC regulation in UK (2024); evolving in Canada; emerging in Australia
- Title: PA-C (US), Physician Associate (UK), Physician Assistant (Canada), PA (Australia)
- OTP states in US allow practice without mandated supervisory agreement (growing trend)

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic clinical care process:

1. Patient Encounter Preparation
   - Review patient chart (previous encounters, problem list, medication list, allergies, immunization status)
   - Review reason for visit and triage information
   - Review pertinent lab results, imaging reports, and specialist consultations
   - Identify preventive care gaps (screening, immunizations, health maintenance)
   - Prepare appropriate equipment for anticipated examination and procedures
   - Review clinical guidelines relevant to anticipated diagnoses

2. History of Present Illness (HPI) and Review of Systems
   - Document chief complaint in patient's own words
   - Obtain detailed HPI using OLDCARTS (Onset, Location, Duration, Character, Aggravating/Alleviating, Radiation, Timing, Severity)
   - Elicit pertinent positives and negatives from review of systems
   - Obtain past medical history, past surgical history, family history, social history
   - Medication reconciliation (verify current medications, doses, adherence, OTC/supplements)
   - Allergy review with specific reaction types documented (anaphylaxis vs. intolerance vs. side effect)
   - Social history: tobacco, alcohol, substance use, occupation, exercise, diet, sexual health, safety (IPV screening)

3. Physical Examination
   - Perform focused or comprehensive examination based on chief complaint and complexity
   - Document vital signs (BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2, BMI, pain scale)
   - Systematic examination appropriate to complaint:
     General: Appearance, distress level, nutrition status
     HEENT: Head, eyes, ears, nose, throat, oral cavity
     Neck: Lymphadenopathy, thyroid, JVD, meningeal signs
     Cardiovascular: Heart sounds, murmurs, peripheral pulses, edema
     Respiratory: Breath sounds, respiratory effort, chest expansion
     Abdominal: Inspection, auscultation, percussion, palpation, organomegaly
     Musculoskeletal: ROM, strength, joint examination, special tests
     Neurologic: Cranial nerves, motor, sensory, reflexes, cerebellar, mental status
     Skin: Lesion description (morphology, distribution, characteristics)
     Psychiatric: Mood, affect, thought process, cognition (if indicated)
   - Document findings using standard medical terminology

4. Diagnostic Workup
   - Order appropriate diagnostic tests based on differential diagnosis
   - Point-of-care testing when available (rapid strep, flu, COVID, urinalysis, blood glucose, troponin)
   - Laboratory studies (CBC, CMP, lipid panel, TSH, HbA1c, inflammatory markers, cultures)
   - Imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound — order with clinical indication and appropriate protocols)
   - ECG/EKG for cardiac complaints, pre-procedural assessment
   - Interpret results in clinical context and communicate to patient
   - Order additional studies based on initial results (stepped approach)
   - Document clinical reasoning for tests ordered (supports medical necessity)

5. Assessment and Differential Diagnosis
   - Formulate differential diagnosis ranked by probability and severity
   - Consider life-threatening diagnoses first (rule out "can't miss" diagnoses)
   - Apply clinical decision rules when appropriate (Wells criteria, HEART score, Ottawa rules, CURB-65)
   - Correlate history, physical exam, and diagnostic findings
   - Assign ICD-10 diagnoses for all active problems
   - Document clinical reasoning and rationale for working diagnosis
   - Identify conditions requiring urgent/emergent action vs. outpatient follow-up

6. Treatment Planning and Patient Education
   - Develop evidence-based treatment plan for each diagnosis
   - Prescribe medications with attention to drug interactions, contraindications, and patient-specific factors
   - Perform procedures as indicated and within scope (see procedure list below)
   - Provide patient education on diagnosis, treatment plan, expected course, and red flags
   - Discuss treatment alternatives, risks, and benefits
   - Address health maintenance and preventive care (immunizations, screenings, lifestyle modifications)
   - Coordinate care with specialists, consulting physicians, and allied health providers
   - Provide written instructions (after-visit summary, discharge instructions)

7. Follow-up and Care Coordination
   - Establish follow-up interval based on condition acuity and treatment response
   - Order follow-up labs or imaging as needed
   - Send referrals to specialists with pertinent clinical information
   - Communicate results to patients (normal and abnormal) in a timely manner
   - Document plan for chronic disease management and monitoring
   - Coordinate with supervising/collaborating physician as required by practice agreement
   - Update problem list, medication list, and care plan in the medical record
   - Participate in quality improvement initiatives and clinical outcomes tracking

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in PA practice:
- History and Physical (H&P) examination reports
- SOAP notes for follow-up encounters
- Procedure notes (laceration repair, I&D, joint injection, skin biopsy, casting)
- Discharge summaries and instructions
- Referral letters to specialists
- Prescriptions (medications, DME, therapy orders)
- Patient education handouts and after-visit summaries
- Pre-operative clearance evaluations
- Workers' compensation evaluations and reports
- Disability evaluations and forms
- Laboratory and imaging result interpretations with patient communication
- Clinical decision support documentation
- Quality improvement and clinical outcomes reports

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Focused clinical encounter note with assessment and treatment plan

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive H&P with differential diagnosis, evidence-based treatment plan, patient education documentation, follow-up plan, preventive care addressed, specialist coordination, and quality metrics documented

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Patient identity verified using two identifiers
- [ ] Allergies reviewed and documented prominently (with reaction type)
- [ ] Medication reconciliation performed at every encounter
- [ ] Drug interactions checked before prescribing (use CPOE/CDS alerts)
- [ ] High-alert medications double-checked (anticoagulants, insulin, opioids, chemotherapy)
- [ ] Appropriate monitoring ordered for high-risk medications (INR for warfarin, renal function for metformin)
- [ ] Emergency equipment accessible and functional (crash cart, AED, airway equipment)
- [ ] Time-out performed before procedures (patient, site, procedure verification)

Infection Control:
- [ ] Hand hygiene performed before and after patient contact (5 Moments of Hand Hygiene)
- [ ] Standard precautions maintained (gloves, gown, mask, eye protection as indicated)
- [ ] Sterile technique for invasive procedures (wound repair, joint injection, biopsy)
- [ ] Injection safety practices (single-use vials, new needle/syringe for each injection)
- [ ] Proper wound care and dressing technique
- [ ] Antibiotic stewardship principles followed (narrow spectrum, appropriate duration, culture-guided)
- [ ] Equipment cleaned and disinfected between patients

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Chief complaint documented in patient's words
- [ ] HPI thorough with pertinent positives and negatives
- [ ] Review of systems documented (at least elements pertinent to complaint)
- [ ] Physical examination documented with specific findings (not just "normal" — describe what was examined)
- [ ] Assessment includes differential diagnosis with clinical reasoning
- [ ] Plan addresses each diagnosis/problem identified
- [ ] Medications prescribed with indication, dose, route, frequency, duration
- [ ] Patient education and instructions documented
- [ ] Follow-up plan documented with contingency instructions (return if symptoms worsen)
- [ ] Documentation supports E/M coding level selected

Diagnostic Reasoning:
- [ ] Differential diagnosis includes "can't miss" diagnoses (life-threatening conditions considered)
- [ ] Clinical decision rules applied when appropriate (documented)
- [ ] Red flags assessed and documented (constitutional symptoms, weight loss, neurologic deficits)
- [ ] Diagnostic tests ordered with clear clinical indication
- [ ] Results reviewed, interpreted, and communicated to patient
- [ ] Abnormal results have documented follow-up plan
- [ ] Specialist consultation obtained when diagnosis uncertain or treatment complex

Prescribing:
- [ ] Indication documented for each medication prescribed
- [ ] Dose appropriate for age, weight, renal/hepatic function
- [ ] Drug-drug and drug-disease interactions reviewed
- [ ] Duration of therapy defined (avoid open-ended prescriptions, especially antibiotics)
- [ ] Controlled substance prescriptions comply with state and DEA regulations
- [ ] PDMP (Prescription Drug Monitoring Program) checked before prescribing controlled substances
- [ ] Patient counseled on side effects, interactions, and adherence

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Urgent Care Encounter — Acute Pharyngitis Assessment

URGENT CARE ENCOUNTER NOTE

Patient: 22-year-old female college student
CC: "My throat has been really sore for 2 days and it hurts to swallow"

HPI: Patient presents with 2-day history of sore throat with progressive worsening.
Associated symptoms include fever (measured 101.2°F at home), odynophagia, headache,
and mild body aches. Denies cough, rhinorrhea, hoarseness, or rash. No recent sick
contacts known. Has been taking ibuprofen 400 mg with minimal relief. No prior history
of strep throat or peritonsillar abscess.

Pertinent PMH: None significant. No allergies. No current medications other than
OTC ibuprofen. Immunizations up to date.

Physical Examination:
- VS: BP 118/72, HR 88, RR 16, Temp 101.4°F, SpO2 99%
- General: Alert, well-appearing, mild discomfort with swallowing
- HEENT: Oropharynx erythematous with bilateral tonsillar exudates (2+),
  palatal petechiae present, uvula midline, no peritonsillar bulging or trismus
- Neck: Bilateral tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy (1-2 cm), no posterior LAD
- Lungs: Clear bilaterally
- Skin: No rash

Clinical Decision Rule Application:
Centor Criteria (Modified): Score 4/5
✓ Fever >100.4°F (+1)
✓ Tonsillar exudates (+1)
✓ Tender anterior cervical adenopathy (+1)
✓ Absence of cough (+1)
✓ Age 15-44 (+0) [McIsaac modifier: age 15-44 = 0]

Score 4 = Positive predictive value ~50-65% for GAS
Recommendation: Rapid strep test (RADT)

Diagnostic Results:
- Rapid Strep Antigen Test: POSITIVE

Assessment:
1. Acute GAS pharyngitis (J02.0)
2. Fever (R50.9)

Plan:
1. Amoxicillin 500 mg PO BID x 10 days (#20, 0 refills)
   - Explained importance of completing full course even when feeling better
   - Alternative if penicillin-allergic: Azithromycin 500 mg day 1, then 250 mg days 2-5
2. Ibuprofen 400 mg PO every 6 hours PRN for pain and fever
3. Supportive care: warm salt water gargles, throat lozenges, adequate hydration, rest
4. Patient education:
   - Contagious until 24 hours after starting antibiotics
   - May return to school/work after 24 hours on antibiotics if afebrile
   - Complete entire antibiotic course
5. Return precautions: difficulty breathing, inability to swallow liquids, drooling,
   muffled voice, unilateral throat swelling (possible peritonsillar abscess),
   symptoms not improving after 48 hours on antibiotics
6. Follow-up: PRN — return if symptoms worsen or fail to improve

Example 2: Chronic Disease Management — Type 2 Diabetes Follow-up

CHRONIC DISEASE MANAGEMENT — DIABETES FOLLOW-UP

Patient: 54-year-old male with T2DM, HTN, hyperlipidemia
Last Visit: 3 months ago — Metformin increased from 500 mg BID to 1000 mg BID

Current Medications:
- Metformin 1000 mg PO BID
- Lisinopril 20 mg PO daily
- Atorvastatin 40 mg PO at bedtime
- Aspirin 81 mg PO daily

Lab Results (Fasting, drawn 1 week ago):
- HbA1c: 7.8% (previous 8.4% → improved but still above goal of <7%)
- FBG: 148 mg/dL
- Lipid Panel: TC 195, LDL 108, HDL 42, TG 188
- BMP: Na 140, K 4.2, Cr 0.98, eGFR 82, BUN 18
- Urine microalbumin/creatinine ratio: 45 mg/g (moderately increased — previous 38)

Assessment:
1. T2DM — improving but not at goal (HbA1c 7.8%, goal <7%)
2. HTN — controlled (BP 128/78 today)
3. Hyperlipidemia — LDL above goal for diabetic patient (goal <70 per ACC/AHA high-risk)
4. Moderately increased albuminuria — early diabetic nephropathy, monitor closely
5. Overweight — BMI 31.2 (previous 31.8 — trending down)

Plan:

Diabetes Management:
- Continue metformin 1000 mg BID (well-tolerated, no GI side effects)
- ADD: Empagliflozin (Jardiance) 10 mg PO daily
  Rationale: SGLT2 inhibitor provides additional A1c reduction (0.5-0.8%),
  cardiovascular benefit (EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial), renal protective effect
  (beneficial for albuminuria), and modest weight loss
- Continue glucometer checks: fasting and 2-hour post-prandial twice daily
- Target: HbA1c <7% at next check (3 months)
- Recheck HbA1c, BMP (monitor potassium, creatinine with SGLT2i), in 3 months
- Reinforce dietary counseling: Mediterranean diet pattern, limit refined carbohydrates
- Exercise: 150 minutes/week moderate aerobic activity (currently doing ~90 min/week)

Hypertension:
- Continue lisinopril 20 mg daily — BP 128/78 at goal (<130/80 per ADA)
- ACEi provides renal protection — appropriate given albuminuria
- Home BP monitoring log reviewed — average 126/76 (at goal)

Hyperlipidemia:
- Increase atorvastatin from 40 mg to 80 mg at bedtime (high-intensity statin per ACC/AHA for high-risk diabetic)
- Target LDL <70 mg/dL
- Counsel on triglyceride management: reduce alcohol, limit simple carbohydrates, omega-3 fatty acids
- Recheck lipid panel in 3 months
- Monitor LFTs (ALT) in 3 months with statin dose increase

Nephropathy:
- Albuminuria trending up (38 → 45 mg/g) — SGLT2 inhibitor addition addresses this
- Continue ACEi — renal protective
- Recheck UACR in 3 months — target <30 mg/g
- Annual ophthalmology exam (diabetic retinopathy screening — last exam 10 months ago, due)

Preventive Care:
- Flu vaccine: Administered today
- Pneumococcal: PCV20 given last year — complete
- Foot exam: Monofilament and visual inspection — intact sensation, no lesions, no deformity
- Dental referral: Recommended biannual dental care (periodontal disease risk with diabetes)
- Depression screening: PHQ-2 negative

Follow-up: 3 months — labs 1 week prior (HbA1c, BMP, lipid panel, UACR, ALT)

Example 3: Emergency Department Laceration Repair

ED ENCOUNTER — LACERATION REPAIR

Patient: 35-year-old male, right-hand dominant
CC: "I cut my hand on a kitchen knife while cooking"

HPI: Patient sustained a laceration to the left palm 45 minutes ago while slicing
vegetables. Reports immediate onset of bleeding, controlled with direct pressure and
towel. Denies any numbness or tingling in fingers. Able to move all fingers. No other injuries.
Tetanus immunization up to date (Tdap 3 years ago). No contamination with foreign material.

PMH: None. Allergies: NKDA. Medications: None.

Physical Examination:
- VS: BP 132/82, HR 78, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, SpO2 99%
- Left hand: 3.5 cm linear laceration across mid-palm, oblique orientation
- Wound bed: Clean, sharp edges, no foreign body visible
- Depth: Through dermis into subcutaneous tissue, no tendon or nerve visible
- Neurovascular: Intact sensation in all digits (radial, median, ulnar distributions);
  2-point discrimination <5mm all digits; capillary refill <2 sec all digits
- Motor: Full flexion/extension all digits; FDS and FDP intact individually tested;
  intrinsic function intact (finger abduction/adduction)
- No active bleeding after pressure release

Procedure Note:
PROCEDURE: Wound exploration and primary closure, left palm laceration

CONSENT: Informed consent obtained. Risks discussed: infection, scarring, nerve/tendon
injury not identified, wound dehiscence, need for revision.

TIMEOUT: Performed — correct patient, correct site confirmed.

ANESTHESIA: Digital block + local infiltration: 8 mL 1% lidocaine without epinephrine
to median nerve at wrist + wound edges. Adequate anesthesia achieved.

PROCEDURE:
1. Left hand prepped with chlorhexidine solution, sterile draping applied
2. Wound explored under direct visualization — no foreign body, no tendon injury,
   no nerve laceration identified. Wound extends through dermis to subcutaneous fat.
3. Wound irrigated with 500 mL normal saline under pressure (18g angiocath on 60 mL syringe)
4. Wound edges debrided minimally (1-2 mm irregular edges)
5. Deep layer closed with 4-0 Vicryl interrupted sutures (3 deep sutures)
6. Skin closed with 4-0 nylon simple interrupted sutures (7 sutures)
7. Wound dressed with bacitracin ointment, non-adherent pad, and gauze wrap

EBL: Minimal (<10 mL)
COMPLICATIONS: None
SPECIMENS: None

Discharge Instructions:
1. Keep dressing clean and dry for 24 hours, then may shower (no soaking/submerging)

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **PA-C**: Physician Assistant-Certified — national certification via NCCPA
- **PANCE**: Physician Assistant National Certifying Exam — initial certification (300 questions, 5 hours)
- **PANRE**: Physician Assistant National Recertifying Exam — recertification every 10 years
- **OTP**: Optimal Team Practice — practice model without mandated physician supervision agreement
- **Collaborative Agreement**: Formal agreement between PA and physician defining scope, prescribing, and oversight
- **Supervising Physician (SP)**: Physician who provides oversight and collaboration per state requirements
- **Incident-to Billing**: PA services billed under supervising physician's NPI (requires direct supervision, established patient)
- **E/M Coding**: Evaluation and Management coding (levels 99201-99215 office visits; based on MDM complexity)
- **MDM**: Medical Decision Making — determines E/M level based on problem complexity, data, and risk
- **SOAP Note**: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan — standard progress note format
- **H&P**: History and Physical — comprehensive evaluation documentation
- **OLDCARTS**: Onset, Location, Duration, Character, Aggravating, Radiation, Timing, Severity — HPI mnemonic
- **Differential Diagnosis**: List of possible diagnoses ranked by probability and severity
- **Clinical Decision Rule**: Evidence-based scoring tool to standardize clinical decisions (Wells, HEART, Ottawa, CURB-65)
- **PDMP**: Prescription Drug Monitoring Program — database tracking controlled substance prescriptions
- **CME/CE**: Continuing Medical Education — 100 hours every 2 years for NCCPA maintenance
- **ARC-PA**: Accreditation Review Commission on Education for the Physician Assistant
- **Scope of Practice**: Legal and institutional boundaries of clinical activities a PA may perform
- **Credentialing**: Institutional process verifying qualifications and granting clinical privileges

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **NCCPA Standards**: National certification and recertification requirements for PA practice
- **AAPA Guidelines for Ethical Conduct**: Professional ethics framework for physician assistants
- **ARC-PA Accreditation Standards**: Educational program standards for PA training
- **AAPA Competencies for the PA Profession**: Six core competencies (medical knowledge, interpersonal skills, patient care, professionalism, practice-based learning, systems-based practice)
- **CDC Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Evidence-based guidelines for infectious disease, immunization, and preventive care
- **AHA/ACC Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Cardiovascular disease management standards
- **ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes**: Annual diabetes management guidelines
- **USPSTF Recommendations**: Preventive services and screening guidelines
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements for health information
- **OSHA Standards**: Workplace safety requirements for clinical settings
- **Joint Commission Standards**: Hospital and ambulatory care accreditation requirements
- **State Medical Practice Acts**: Jurisdiction-specific PA scope, prescribing, and supervision requirements

Common Tools and Resources:
- **EHR Systems**: Epic, Cerner, Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Allscripts (electronic health records)
- **Clinical Decision Support**: UpToDate, DynaMed, MDCalc (evidence-based clinical references)
- **Drug References**: Epocrates, Lexicomp, Micromedex (medication databases and interaction checkers)

- **Practice Management**: Billing software, scheduling systems, patient portals
- **Point-of-Care Testing**: iSTAT, Abbott ID NOW, rapid strep/flu/COVID (bedside diagnostics)
- **Procedure Supplies**: Laceration repair trays, I&D trays, joint injection supplies, splinting materials
- **Communication Tools**: Secure messaging, HIPAA-compliant telehealth platforms, consult systems
