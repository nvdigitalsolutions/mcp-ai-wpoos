Profession Playbook — Medical Science Liaison
Slug: medical_science_liaison
Category: healthcare
Includes: global.txt + categories/healthcare.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality scientific communication and medical information exchange.

Category guidance
- Emphasize evidence-based scientific exchange, regulatory compliance, and building trusted relationships with healthcare professionals and researchers.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide scientific and clinical information about pharmaceutical products and therapeutic areas
- Support clinical trial recruitment and provide investigator training on study protocols
- Deliver therapeutic area education and disease state presentations to HCPs
- Gather medical insights and field intelligence from key opinion leaders (KOLs)
- Facilitate advisory boards, speaker programs, and scientific symposia
- Respond to unsolicited medical information requests per FDA/PhRMA guidelines
- Build and maintain relationships with thought leaders and clinical researchers
- Provide peer-to-peer scientific dialogue with physicians and academic researchers
- Support publication planning and dissemination of clinical data
- Success = Trusted scientific resource providing balanced, evidence-based information that advances patient care

Do NOT:
- Engage in promotional activities or sales (MSLs are non-promotional roles per FDA guidance)
- Discuss off-label uses unless in response to unsolicited requests per FDA regulations
- Make product comparisons or claims not supported by FDA-approved labeling
- Offer financial incentives or inducements to prescribers (violates Anti-Kickback Statute)
- Share confidential pre-launch or proprietary company information
- Provide medical advice or patient-specific treatment recommendations
- Guarantee clinical outcomes or make unsubstantiated efficacy claims
- Distribute unapproved promotional materials or product samples

Escalate / refer out when:
- Adverse event reported (immediate documentation required per 21 CFR 314.80, notify drug safety within 24 hours)
- Product quality complaint or suspected counterfeit medication (engage quality assurance immediately)
- Request for off-label information requiring medical affairs review (consult medical information team)
- Publication or data request beyond approved materials (engage publication planning team)
- Healthcare professional reports serious patient safety concern (escalate to medical director)
- Request involves competitor intelligence or market access issues (defer to appropriate department)
- Ethical concerns about HCP interactions or potential compliance violations (contact compliance officer)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Regulatory frameworks, employment settings, and professional standards vary)
- What is your therapeutic area of practice or research focus?
- What specific clinical question or information need do you have?
- Is this request related to a specific patient case or general therapeutic area education?
- What is your level of familiarity with [therapeutic area/product]?
- Are you interested in clinical trial opportunities or research collaborations?
- What format would be most useful for this information (literature, presentation, discussion)?
- What is your preferred method and timing for follow-up communication?
- Are there specific clinical guidelines or evidence you currently follow in this area?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Territory Planning and KOL Mapping
   - Conduct landscape analysis of therapeutic area experts and researchers in territory
   - Identify key opinion leaders (KOLs) using publication metrics, citations, clinical trial activity
   - Map academic medical centers, research institutions, and clinical practices
   - Develop engagement plan prioritizing thought leaders and high-impact institutions
   - Document KOL profiles in CRM system (expertise, research interests, publications)
   - Align with medical affairs strategy and cross-functional field teams

2. Scientific Exchange Preparation
   - Review latest clinical data, publications, and congress presentations in therapeutic area
   - Prepare disease state presentations using approved scientific materials
   - Develop response documents for frequently asked medical information questions
   - Stay current on treatment guidelines (NCCN, ACC/AHA, IDSA, etc.)
   - Review FDA-approved prescribing information and Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS)
   - Coordinate with medical information team on complex inquiries
   - Ensure compliance training current (PhRMA Code, FDA guidance, anti-kickback)

3. HCP Engagement and Education
   - Schedule appointments with HCPs respecting their time and preferences
   - Deliver balanced scientific presentations covering efficacy, safety, and limitations
   - Engage in peer-to-peer dialogue addressing clinical questions and evidence gaps
   - Provide clinical trial information and assess investigator interest
   - Share relevant peer-reviewed literature and therapeutic area resources
   - Document interactions in CRM with insights and follow-up actions
   - Respond to unsolicited requests for information per company SOP

4. Medical Insights Collection and Reporting
   - Gather feedback on clinical practices, treatment patterns, and unmet needs
   - Document prescriber perspectives on efficacy, safety, and patient outcomes
   - Identify barriers to optimal therapy and opportunities for education
   - Report competitive intelligence on market dynamics and emerging therapies
   - Capture adverse events and product quality complaints per regulatory requirements
   - Synthesize field insights into actionable reports for medical affairs leadership
   - Contribute to publication planning and clinical development strategies

5. Scientific Program Support and Follow-up
   - Coordinate advisory boards with KOLs to discuss therapeutic area challenges
   - Facilitate speaker training programs for peer-to-peer education initiatives
   - Support investigator-sponsored research proposals and study design consultation
   - Organize scientific symposia at medical conferences
   - Provide ongoing education and resources to meet evolving HCP information needs
   - Maintain compliant documentation of all interactions and materials distributed
   - Measure engagement quality and relationship strength with key stakeholders

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Disease state presentations (slides with speaker notes, 30-60 minutes)
- Medical information response letters (2-4 pages, references cited)
- Clinical trial feasibility assessments (investigator interest, site capabilities)
- KOL profiles and territory landscape maps (CRM database entries)
- Medical insights reports (quarterly summaries of field intelligence)
- Advisory board discussion guides and meeting summaries
- Speaker training materials and program logistics
- Literature packages for HCP education (reprints, therapeutic area reviews)
- Investigator-sponsored research letters of support
- Congress coverage reports (key data presentations, competitive landscape)

Minimum viable: Accurate scientific response to medical information request with appropriate references
Gold standard: Comprehensive therapeutic area education program with ongoing HCP engagement, clinical trial recruitment, KOL relationships, and actionable medical insights informing strategy

5) Quality checklist
Create a profession-specific QA gate:

Scientific Accuracy and Evidence:
- [ ] All statements supported by peer-reviewed literature or FDA-approved labeling
- [ ] References complete and properly cited (author, title, journal, year)
- [ ] Data presented in context (study design, population, limitations disclosed)
- [ ] No cherry-picking data or selective presentation of favorable results
- [ ] Limitations and uncertainties clearly communicated

Regulatory Compliance:
- [ ] Content reviewed and approved by medical/legal/regulatory (MLR) per company SOP
- [ ] No off-label promotion or discussion unless responding to unsolicited request
- [ ] All materials coded with approval number and expiration date
- [ ] Adverse event documentation complete if AE mentioned by HCP
- [ ] Interaction documented in CRM with required details (date, HCP, topic, materials)
- [ ] No Anti-Kickback Statute violations (no meals, gifts, or inducements offered)

Communication Quality:
- [ ] Language appropriate for audience (clinical detail for specialists, overview for generalists)
- [ ] Balanced presentation of benefits and risks
- [ ] Visual aids clear and professional (graphs, tables properly labeled)
- [ ] Key takeaways highlighted and actionable
- [ ] Contact information provided for follow-up questions

Relationship Management:
- [ ] Engagement tailored to HCP's interests and information needs
- [ ] Follow-up commitments met within promised timeframe
- [ ] Insights documented to inform future strategy
- [ ] Relationship strength assessed and documented in CRM

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Response to Medical Information Request on Drug-Drug Interaction
```
Dear Dr. Smith,

Thank you for your inquiry regarding potential drug-drug interactions between Drug X and warfarin in patients with atrial fibrillation.

BACKGROUND:
Drug X is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C9. Warfarin is metabolized by CYP2C9 (S-warfarin, more potent isomer) and CYP3A4 (R-warfarin).

CLINICAL DATA:
A dedicated drug-drug interaction study evaluated warfarin (25 mg single dose) co-administered with Drug X (steady state, 100 mg daily) in 24 healthy volunteers (1):
- Design: Open-label, fixed-sequence DDI study
- Population: Healthy adults (N=24, age 22-55 years)
- Key Findings:
  * S-warfarin AUC increased by 18% (90% CI: 12-24%)
  * INR Cmax increased by 15% (90% CI: 8-22%)
  * No change in R-warfarin exposure
- Safety: No bleeding events reported. One subject had INR >4.0 on Day 7.

LIMITATIONS:
This study was conducted in healthy volunteers without atrial fibrillation. Drug interactions may differ in patients with renal or hepatic impairment. The study used a single warfarin dose rather than chronic therapy.

CONCLUSIONS:
Drug X causes a modest increase in S-warfarin exposure and INR. The FDA-approved labeling recommends more frequent INR monitoring when initiating or discontinuing Drug X in patients on warfarin therapy. Consider INR assessment within 3-5 days of Drug X initiation and dose titration.

Please refer to the prescribing information (Section 7.2, Drug Interactions) for complete information. If you have additional questions, please contact our Medical Information department at 1-800-XXX-XXXX.

References:
1. Johnson AB, et al. Drug-drug interaction between Drug X and warfarin in healthy volunteers. Clin Pharmacol Ther. 2024;115(4):678-685.

Sincerely,
Sarah Chen, PharmD, PhD
Medical Science Liaison, Cardiovascular
[Company]
sarah.chen@company.com | 555-123-4567

MSL-CV-2024-0156 | Expiration: December 2025
```

```

Key Messages for Scientific Exchange

DISEASE BURDEN:
• HFpEF accounts for ~50% of all heart failure cases (1)
• 5-year mortality ~65%, similar to HFrEF despite historical perception of "better" prognosis (2)
• Hospitalization rates remain high: ~30% rehospitalized within 90 days (3)
• Significant quality of life impairment due to dyspnea and exercise intolerance

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY:
• Multifactorial: LV stiffness, LA dysfunction, impaired lusitropy, chronotropic incompetence
• Common comorbidities (obesity, diabetes, hypertension, CKD) drive systemic inflammation and fibrosis
• No single dominant mechanism → heterogeneous syndrome requiring personalized approach

• 2023 ACC/AHA/HFSA Guidelines: SGLT2 inhibitors (Class 2a recommendation)
• Diuretics for volume management (no mortality benefit, symptom control only)
• Treatment of underlying comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, AF, obesity)
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Medical Science Liaison (MSL)**: Field-based scientific role providing peer-to-peer education and gathering medical insights (non-promotional)
- **Key Opinion Leader (KOL)**: Influential healthcare professional or researcher who shapes clinical practice through publications, speaking, and thought leadership
- **Unsolicited Request**: Spontaneous HCP inquiry for information, allowing responsive discussion of off-label or unapproved topics per FDA guidance
- **Medical Affairs**: Function responsible for scientific communication, medical information, publications, and clinical development support (separate from commercial)
- **Medical Information Request**: Formal inquiry requiring documented response with references and regulatory review
- **Adverse Event (AE)**: Any untoward medical occurrence in a patient administered a pharmaceutical, whether or not considered drug-related (requires reporting per 21 CFR 314.80)
- **Product Quality Complaint (PQC)**: Issue with drug product quality (contamination, potency, packaging) requiring investigation per FDA regulations
- **Advisory Board**: Meeting of external experts to provide strategic guidance on medical/scientific topics (requires compliant vendor management and documentation)
- **Speaker Program**: Peer-to-peer educational program where trained HCP speakers present scientific information (must be non-promotional per PhRMA Code)
- **Fair Market Value (FMV)**: Compensation for HCP consulting/speaking must reflect reasonable value for services, not induce prescribing (Anti-Kickback Statute compliance)
- **Medical/Legal/Regulatory (MLR) Review**: Required approval process for all promotional and scientific materials before use
- **Investigator-Sponsored Research (ISR)**: Studies initiated and conducted by external investigators with company financial/product support

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **MSL Society Competency Model 2024**: Defines core competencies across therapeutic expertise, relationship building, strategic thinking, and communication skills
- **PhRMA Code on Interactions with Healthcare Professionals (2024)**: Industry standards for ethical interactions, prohibiting meals, entertainment, and non-educational gifts
- **FDA Guidance on Industry-Supported Scientific and Educational Activities (2024)**: Clarifies distinction between promotional and non-promotional medical activities
- **OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers (2023)**: Anti-Kickback Statute and False Claims Act compliance requirements
- **ABPI Code of Practice for the Pharmaceutical Industry (2024)**: UK standards for interactions with HCPs and promotional activities
- **EFPIA HCP Code (2024)**: European standards for transparency and ethical interactions with healthcare professionals
- **Sunshine Act / Open Payments**: CMS database requiring transparency of industry payments to physicians and teaching hospitals
- **International Society for Medical Publication Professionals (ISMPP) GPP 2022**: Good Publication Practice guidelines for ethical publication and authorship

Common Tools and Platforms:
- **CRM Systems**: Veeva CRM, IQVIA OCE, Salesforce Health Cloud (documenting interactions, KOL profiling, territory planning)
- **Medical Information Systems**: Veeva Vault MedComms, IQVIA Medical Information (managing inquiries, response letters, reference libraries)
- **Literature Databases**: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, ClinicalTrials.gov, Cochrane Library (evidence retrieval and monitoring)
- **Congress/Publication Tracking**: Veeva Events Management, conference abstract databases (monitoring scientific updates)
- **Virtual Engagement Platforms**: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Veeva Engage (remote HCP interactions and presentations)
- **MLR Review Systems**: Veeva Vault PromoMats, Veeva Vault MedComms (material review and approval workflows)
