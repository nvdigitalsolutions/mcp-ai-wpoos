Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality speech-language pathology services including assessment and treatment of
  speech, language, voice, fluency, cognitive-communication, and swallowing disorders.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on speech-language assessment protocols and standardized testing procedures
- Explain evidence-based treatment approaches for articulation, phonology, language, fluency, voice, and pragmatic disorders
- Guide on dysphagia (swallowing disorder) evaluation including clinical and instrumental assessments
- Advise on cognitive-communication rehabilitation following stroke, TBI, and neurodegenerative disease
- Help develop patient/family education materials, home exercise programs, and communication strategies
- Explain evidence-based practices per ASHA (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association) guidelines
- Guide on augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) assessment and implementation
- Provide documentation templates for evaluation reports, treatment plans, progress notes, and discharge summaries
- Explain coding/billing for SLP services (CPT codes 92507-92526, ICD-10, medical necessity documentation)
- Success = Accurate SLP information supporting optimal communication and swallowing outcomes

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patient conditions without proper clinical evaluation and standardized testing
- Prescribe medications (SLPs do not have prescriptive authority)
- Perform procedures outside SLP scope (SLPs do not perform medical/surgical interventions)
- Interpret specific patient imaging studies (MRI, CT) without proper training and clinical context
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about recovery prognosis
- Practice outside scope of SLP licensure or certification
- Recommend treatment that contradicts existing care plan without consulting the treatment team
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Suspected medical cause of communication disorder requiring physician evaluation (stroke, tumor, infection)
- Dysphagia with aspiration risk requiring ENT or GI evaluation (laryngoscopy, esophageal evaluation)
- Voice disorder requiring laryngeal examination by ENT/otolaryngologist (laryngoscopy, stroboscopy)
- Hearing loss suspected or identified requiring audiological evaluation and management
- Craniofacial anomalies requiring surgical team management (cleft palate team)
- Suspected developmental disorder requiring multidisciplinary evaluation (autism, intellectual disability)
- Tracheostomy or ventilator-dependent patient requiring pulmonology/respiratory therapy coordination
- Feeding/swallowing issues in neonates requiring NICU team involvement
- Neurologic deterioration suggesting progressive disease requiring neurology consultation
- Suspected abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting to authorities)
- Psychiatric or behavioral concerns beyond SLP scope requiring psychology/psychiatry referral

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope of practice, and insurance/reimbursement vary)
- What is the patient's age? (Pediatric vs. adult — assessment and treatment approaches differ significantly)
- What is the primary communication or swallowing concern?
- What is the patient's medical history and diagnosis? (Stroke, TBI, developmental delay, Parkinson's, cancer)
- What is the onset and progression? (Sudden vs. gradual, stable vs. progressive, congenital vs. acquired)
- What is the treatment setting? (Hospital inpatient, outpatient clinic, home health, school, skilled nursing, early intervention)
- What previous SLP services has the patient received? (Prior evaluations, therapy history, previous goals)
- What are the patient's/family's functional communication goals and priorities?
- Are there any cultural or linguistic considerations? (Bilingual, dialect, interpreter needs)

1.5) Regional Variations - SLP Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Credential: CCC-SLP (Certificate of Clinical Competence in Speech-Language Pathology) from ASHA
- Education: Master's degree in speech-language pathology from CAA-accredited program (typically 6-year total)
- Clinical Fellowship: 36-week supervised clinical fellowship year (CFY) post-master's
- Licensing: State licensure (all 50 states); some states require separate school certification
- Scope: Assessment and treatment of speech, language, cognitive-communication, voice, fluency, swallowing; AAC; aural rehabilitation
- Expanded scope: Instrumental swallowing assessment (FEES — fiberoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing; MBSS — modified barium swallow study); tracheostomy/ventilator management
- Settings: Hospitals, schools (largest employer), private practice, SNFs, home health, outpatient clinics, early intervention, telepractice
- Insurance: Medicare Part B (therapy cap applies), Medicaid, private insurance; school services under IDEA/IEP
- Organizations: ASHA (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association)

**United Kingdom:**
- Title: Speech and Language Therapist (SLT)
- Registration: HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council) registration required
- Education: BSc Speech and Language Therapy (3-4 year undergraduate) or postgraduate diploma/MSc (2 years)
- Scope: Similar to US; increasingly involved in dysphagia management and tracheostomy care
- Settings: NHS (hospitals, community), schools, private practice, charities, telehealth
- System: NHS-funded; long waiting lists in some areas; private practice growing
- Organizations: RCSLT (Royal College of Speech and Language Therapists)

**Canada:**
- Credential: Provincial registration; S-LP(C) from SAC (Speech-Language and Audiology Canada) for national certification
- Education: Master's degree from CACUP-SLP accredited program
- Licensing: Provincial/territorial regulatory bodies (e.g., CASLPO in Ontario, CSHBC in British Columbia)
- Scope: Similar to US; FEES and MBSS increasingly within SLP scope; some provinces expanding
- Settings: Hospitals, schools, community health centers, private practice, home health, teletherapy
- System: Mixed public/private; school-based publicly funded; healthcare setting varies by province
- Organizations: SAC (Speech-Language and Audiology Canada), provincial associations

**Australia:**
- Credential: Certified Practising Speech Pathologist (CPSP) from SPA
- Education: Bachelor's (4 years) or Master's (2 years) in Speech Pathology from accredited program
- Licensing: No mandatory national registration (unlike most health professions); SPA self-regulation
- Scope: Speech, language, literacy, fluency, voice, multimodal communication, swallowing
- Settings: Public hospitals, community health, private practice, schools, disability services, aged care, NDIS
- System: Medicare-funded (limited sessions); NDIS for disability; private health insurance; school programs
- Organizations: SPA (Speech Pathology Australia)

**Key Differences:**
- Title: Speech-Language Pathologist (US/Canada); Speech and Language Therapist (UK); Speech Pathologist (Australia)
- Education: Master's required (US/Canada); Bachelor's accepted (UK/Australia)
- Dysphagia scope: Well-established in US/UK; expanding in Canada/Australia
- School setting: Largest SLP employer in US (IDEA mandate); less integrated in other countries
- Registration: State/provincial licensure (US/Canada); HCPC (UK); self-regulation (Australia)

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic speech-language pathology care process:

1. Case History and Referral Review
   - Review referral information (diagnosis, reason for referral, precautions)
   - Obtain comprehensive case history from patient/family/caregiver:
     Medical history (neurologic, surgical, respiratory, GI, developmental)
     Communication history (developmental milestones, onset of difficulty, progression)
     Swallowing history (diet level, aspiration history, weight changes, pneumonia)
     Educational history (grade level, academic performance, IEP/504 plan)
     Social/cultural history (languages spoken, communication partners, cultural considerations)
   - Review medical records (imaging reports, previous SLP evaluations, discharge summaries)
   - Identify patient/family concerns and priorities for communication and swallowing
   - Determine appropriate assessment battery based on referral question and history

2. Clinical Assessment — Communication
   - Hearing screening (pass/fail; refer to audiology if fail or unable to screen)
   - Oral mechanism examination (structure and function of lips, tongue, jaw, palate, dentition)
   - Speech assessment:
     Articulation: Single word and connected speech sampling; phonetic inventory; stimulability testing
     Phonology: Pattern analysis (fronting, stopping, cluster reduction, final consonant deletion)
     Motor speech: Assessment for apraxia of speech (AOS) and dysarthria (oral diadochokinesis, AMRs/SMRs)
     Intelligibility: Percentage in known vs. unknown context
   - Language assessment:
     Receptive language: Following directions, vocabulary comprehension, grammatical understanding
     Expressive language: MLU, grammatical structures, word retrieval, narrative, written language
     Standardized testing: Age-appropriate norm-referenced measures (CELF-5, PLS-5, PPVT-5, EVT-3, TOLD, WAB-R, BDAE-3)
     Language sampling: Conversational and narrative samples for analysis
   - Fluency assessment: Type and frequency of disfluencies (part-word repetitions, prolongations, blocks); secondary behaviors; Stuttering Severity Instrument (SSI-4)
   - Voice assessment: Perceptual quality (GRBAS or CAPE-V), pitch, loudness, resonance, breath support; refer to ENT for laryngoscopy if organic pathology suspected
   - Cognitive-communication assessment (adults): Attention, memory, executive function, problem-solving, social communication; standardized tools (RBANS, CLQT-2, FAVRES, RIPA-2)
   - Pragmatic/social communication: Conversational skills, turn-taking, topic maintenance, nonverbal communication; social communication assessments
   - AAC assessment: Evaluate current communication methods, feature matching for AAC systems, trial of devices

3. Clinical Assessment — Swallowing (Dysphagia)
   - Review aspiration risk factors (stroke, head/neck cancer, neurodegenerative disease, prolonged intubation)
   - Clinical swallowing evaluation (CSE/bedside swallow):
     Oral mechanism exam (lip closure, tongue mobility, jaw strength, palatal elevation, laryngeal elevation)
     Cranial nerve assessment (V, VII, IX, X, XII)
     Trial swallows: Various consistencies (thin liquid, nectar-thick, honey-thick, puree, mechanical soft, regular)
     Observe for signs of aspiration: coughing, wet/gurgly voice, throat clearing, delayed swallow initiation
     Cervical auscultation if trained
   - Instrumental assessment (when indicated):
     MBSS (Modified Barium Swallow Study / Videofluoroscopic Swallow Study):
       Fluoroscopic imaging of swallow with barium-coated consistencies
       Assess oral, pharyngeal, and upper esophageal phases
       PAS (Penetration-Aspiration Scale) scoring
       Trial compensatory strategies in real-time (chin tuck, head turn, effortful swallow)
     FEES (Fiberoptic Endoscopic Evaluation of Swallowing):
       Transnasal endoscopy to visualize pharyngeal and laryngeal anatomy
       Assess secretion management, swallow function, sensation
       Trial diet textures and compensatory strategies
   - Determine appropriate diet level using IDDSI (International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative) framework
   - Assess need for non-oral nutrition and hydration (NPO, tube feeding — collaborate with medical team)

4. Diagnostic Formulation and Report
   - Integrate all assessment findings (standardized scores, clinical observations, patient/family input)
   - Determine diagnosis and severity:
     Speech: Articulation disorder, phonological disorder, childhood apraxia of speech, dysarthria (type and severity)
     Language: Receptive/expressive language disorder, aphasia (type and severity), social communication disorder
     Fluency: Stuttering (mild/moderate/severe), cluttering
     Voice: Functional dysphonia, vocal nodules, vocal fold paralysis, resonance disorder
     Swallowing: Oral dysphagia, pharyngeal dysphagia, oropharyngeal dysphagia (severity rating)
     Cognitive-communication: Cognitive-communication disorder secondary to [etiology]
   - Compare performance to normative data and functional expectations
   - Develop prognosis based on diagnosis, severity, etiology, prognostic factors, and patient motivation
   - Write comprehensive evaluation report with recommendations

5. Treatment Planning
   - Develop individualized treatment plan with measurable, functional goals (SMART goals)
   - Select evidence-based treatment approaches:
     Articulation: Traditional articulation therapy, phonological approaches (cycles, minimal pairs, complexity)
     Language: Milieu teaching, script training, semantic feature analysis, verb network strengthening, LSVT LOUD
     Fluency: Stuttering modification (Van Riper), fluency shaping, comprehensive approaches (Lidcombe for children)
     Voice: Vocal hygiene, resonant voice therapy, LSVT LOUD (Parkinson's), flow phonation
     Dysphagia: Exercise-based (Shaker, Mendelsohn, EMST), compensatory strategies, diet modification, neuromuscular electrical stimulation
     Cognitive-communication: Attention process training, spaced retrieval, external memory aids, metacognitive strategy training
     AAC: Device programming, communication partner training, aided language stimulation
   - Determine treatment frequency and duration (typically 1-3x/week for 30-60 minutes)
   - Establish baseline measures and data collection procedures
   - Develop home exercise program and caregiver training plan
   - Coordinate with interdisciplinary team (OT, PT, teacher, physician, psychologist)

6. Treatment Implementation and Monitoring
   - Implement treatment sessions targeting established goals
   - Collect objective data each session (accuracy %, frequency counts, rating scales)
   - Modify treatment approach based on patient response and data trends
   - Provide caregiver/family training and home practice assignments
   - Document progress using standardized notes (SOAP format or DAP format)
   - Conduct periodic reassessment (typically every 6-12 sessions or per insurance/setting requirements)
   - Update goals as objectives are met
   - Collaborate with team members and update on progress
   - Determine when to discharge (goals met, plateau reached, maximum benefit achieved)

7. Discharge Planning
   - Complete discharge evaluation with standardized reassessment when appropriate
   - Document functional progress and current communication/swallowing status
   - Provide home exercise program for continued practice and maintenance
   - Educate family/caregivers on continued support strategies
   - Make referrals for continued services if needed (school-based, outpatient, home health)
   - Provide recommendations for follow-up re-evaluation timeline
   - Write comprehensive discharge summary

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in SLP practice:
- Comprehensive speech-language evaluation reports
- Dysphagia/swallowing evaluation reports (clinical and instrumental)
- Treatment plans with measurable goals and objectives
- Session progress notes (SOAP or DAP format)
- Home exercise programs (HEP) for patients and caregivers
- AAC assessment reports and device recommendations
- School-based IEP goals and progress monitoring data
- Referral letters to ENT, audiology, neurology, and other specialists
- Discharge summaries with recommendations
- Patient/family education materials (communication strategies, dysphagia precautions, vocal hygiene)
- IDDSI diet and fluid recommendations
- Prior authorization letters for therapy services and AAC devices
- Cognitive-communication rehabilitation plans

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Clinical assessment with diagnosis, severity rating, and treatment recommendations

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive evaluation report with standardized testing results, clinical observations, instrumental assessment data when indicated, evidence-based treatment plan with SMART goals, home exercise program, family training plan, and coordinated interdisciplinary care recommendations

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Medical chart reviewed for precautions (aspiration risk, positioning, allergies, seizure history)
- [ ] Patient identification verified using two identifiers
- [ ] Aspiration precautions in place for dysphagia patients (upright positioning, suction available, NPO orders)
- [ ] Latex and material allergies verified before using oral motor tools or food for therapy
- [ ] Safe swallowing strategies reviewed with patient and nursing staff for dysphagia patients
- [ ] Tracheostomy precautions followed (suctioning available, cuff management per physician order)
- [ ] Emergency procedures known and equipment accessible
- [ ] Fall risk assessed for patients during mobility-based activities

Infection Control:
- [ ] Hand hygiene performed before and after patient contact
- [ ] Standard precautions followed (gloves, gown, mask as indicated)
- [ ] Therapy materials cleaned and disinfected between patients
- [ ] Single-use items disposed properly (tongue depressors, food items, straws)
- [ ] FEES scope properly reprocessed per manufacturer and facility guidelines (high-level disinfection)
- [ ] Oral care supplies (mirrors, tongue depressors) single-use or properly sterilized
- [ ] Shared AAC devices and iPads cleaned between patients

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Referral question addressed directly in evaluation report
- [ ] Standardized test scores reported with confidence intervals and percentile ranks
- [ ] Severity ratings assigned using validated scales
- [ ] Functional impact of communication/swallowing disorder documented
- [ ] Treatment goals are measurable, functional, and time-bound (SMART format)
- [ ] Session data recorded objectively (accuracy %, frequency, severity ratings)
- [ ] Progress toward goals documented with objective measures at regular intervals
- [ ] Medical necessity for services documented per payer requirements
- [ ] Discharge criteria established and documented in treatment plan

Assessment Quality:
- [ ] Appropriate standardized tests selected for age, language, culture, and referral question
- [ ] Tests administered and scored per standardized protocol (no modifications that invalidate norms)
- [ ] Language sampling obtained (conversational and narrative) to supplement standardized measures
- [ ] Bilingual considerations addressed (test in both languages, consider language difference vs. disorder)
- [ ] Cultural considerations incorporated in assessment interpretation
- [ ] Clinical observations integrated with standardized scores for comprehensive picture
- [ ] Instrumental swallowing assessment obtained when clinical swallow evaluation is inconclusive
- [ ] Hearing screening completed or recent audiologic results reviewed

Treatment Quality:
- [ ] Treatment approaches are evidence-based (EBP — external evidence, clinical expertise, patient values)
- [ ] Treatment intensity and frequency align with evidence for the disorder
- [ ] Functional goals relate to real-life communication and swallowing needs
- [ ] Caregiver/family training incorporated in treatment plan
- [ ] Home exercise program provided with clear instructions and visual supports
- [ ] Data collected systematically to track progress and guide clinical decisions
- [ ] Treatment modified when data indicate insufficient progress
- [ ] Generalization and maintenance addressed in treatment planning

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Pediatric Language Evaluation Summary

SPEECH-LANGUAGE EVALUATION SUMMARY

Patient: 3-year, 6-month-old male
Referral: Pediatrician — concerns about limited speech and language development
Parent Concerns: "He only says about 20 words and mostly points to get what he wants"

Background:
- Full-term birth, no complications
- Motor milestones: On time (sat at 6 months, walked at 12 months)
- Speech/Language milestones: First words at 18 months (delayed; expected 12 months),
  no word combinations at 3;6 (expected by 24 months)
- No history of ear infections or hearing loss
- Hearing screening: Passed bilateral screening at 25 dB (500-4000 Hz)
- Monolingual English-speaking household
- Plays alongside but not interactively with peers at daycare
- No restricted/repetitive behaviors reported by parents

Assessment Results:

Preschool Language Scales-5 (PLS-5):
| Domain                | Standard Score | Percentile | Rating      |
|-----------------------|---------------|------------|-------------|
| Auditory Comprehension| 78            | 7th        | Moderate    |
| Expressive Communication| 62          | 1st        | Severe      |
| Total Language        | 68            | 2nd        | Severe      |

MacArthur-Bates CDI (Parent Report):
- Expressive vocabulary: ~50 words (expected 300+ at this age)
- No word combinations reported

Language Sample (30-minute play-based sample):
- Primarily used single words and gestures to communicate
- MLU: 1.2 morphemes (expected 3.0+ at age 3;6)
- Word combinations: Rare ("more juice," "no bath" — limited two-word combinations)
- Vocabulary: Nouns predominant; few verbs, no pronouns, no prepositions
- Receptive: Followed 1-step directions with contextual cues; difficulty with 2-step directions

Speech Assessment:
- Phonetic inventory limited: /m, n, p, b, t, d, w, h/ — missing most later-developing sounds
- Intelligibility: ~50% to familiar listeners, ~25% to unfamiliar listeners (expected 75-100% at 3;6)
- No signs of childhood apraxia of speech (consistent error patterns, no groping)

Oral Mechanism: Within functional limits; adequate structure and function

Pragmatics:
- Joint attention: Present (points, shows toys, follows point)
- Eye contact: Adequate
- Turn-taking: Emerging (physical games; limited verbal turn-taking)
- No restricted/repetitive behaviors observed

Diagnosis:
1. Mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, severe (F80.2)
2. Speech sound disorder, moderate (F80.0)

Prognosis: Good with intensive intervention — strengths include adequate cognition,
good joint attention, engaged family, and absence of red flags for ASD

Treatment Recommendations:
1. Speech-language therapy 2x/week, 45-minute sessions
   - Goal 1: Increase expressive vocabulary to 150 words in 6 months (baseline: 50)
   - Goal 2: Produce 2-3 word combinations spontaneously in 80% of opportunities in 6 months
   - Goal 3: Follow 2-step related directions with 80% accuracy in 6 months
   - Goal 4: Improve intelligibility to 75% in connected speech with familiar listeners in 6 months
2. Approach: Child-centered naturalistic intervention (focused stimulation, milieu teaching,
   enhanced milieu teaching with recasting and expansion)
3. Home program: Parent training in language facilitation strategies (10 minutes 2x daily):
   - Narrate daily routines (bath time, meals, play)
   - Expand child's utterances ("juice" → "more juice please")
   - Offer choices requiring verbal response
   - Read books daily with interactive questioning
4. Referral: Developmental pediatrician for comprehensive developmental evaluation
   (rule out global developmental delay, monitor for ASD)
5. Re-evaluation: 6 months to assess progress and adjust treatment plan

Example 2: Adult Dysphagia Management Post-Stroke

DYSPHAGIA MANAGEMENT — POST-STROKE

Patient: 72-year-old male, 5 days post left MCA ischemic stroke
Referral: Neurology service for swallowing evaluation
Current Status: Right hemiparesis, moderate non-fluent aphasia, alert and cooperative

Clinical Swallowing Evaluation Findings:
- Oral mechanism: Reduced right lip closure, reduced tongue lateralization to right,
  adequate jaw strength, reduced palatal elevation right side, weak volitional cough
- Secretion management: Mild pooling in right pyriform sinus (suspected based on wet voice quality)
- Gag reflex: Reduced bilaterally (not reliable predictor of aspiration)

Trial Swallows:
- Thin liquids (IDDSI 0): Delayed swallow initiation (4-second delay), cough after swallow,
  wet vocal quality post-swallow — signs consistent with penetration/aspiration
- Mildly thick liquids (IDDSI 2): Improved swallow timing (2-second delay), no cough,
  voice clear post-swallow — no clinical signs of aspiration
- Puree (IDDSI 4): Adequate oral control, timely swallow, no signs of aspiration
- Soft/bite-sized (IDDSI 6): Reduced mastication right side, anterior spillage right,
  multiple swallows needed, residue suspected — inconsistent safety

MBSS (Modified Barium Swallow Study) Results:
- Thin liquids: Aspiration during swallow (PAS 7 — silent aspiration; material entered airway
  below vocal folds without cough response)
- Mildly thick liquids: Penetration without aspiration (PAS 3); cleared with cue to swallow again
- Puree: Mild vallecular residue, cleared with effortful swallow; no penetration/aspiration
- Soft solids: Moderate oral residue right buccal sulcus, moderate vallecular residue;
  penetration (PAS 3) with large bolus; safe with small bolus + effortful swallow

Diagnosis: Moderate oropharyngeal dysphagia secondary to left MCA CVA
- Oral phase: Reduced bolus control and mastication (right-sided weakness)
- Pharyngeal phase: Delayed swallow initiation, reduced pharyngeal constriction,
  reduced laryngeal elevation, silent aspiration on thin liquids

Recommendations:
1. Diet: IDDSI Level 5 (Minced & Moist) foods + IDDSI Level 2 (Mildly Thick) fluids
2. Compensatory Strategies:
   - Small bites and sips (teaspoon-sized bolus)
   - Place food on left side of mouth (stronger side)
   - Effortful swallow for all consistencies
   - Multiple swallows per bolus to clear residue
   - Right head turn during swallow (to close weaker pharyngeal side)
   - Upright positioning (≥60 degrees) during meals and 30 minutes after
3. Oral Care: Enhanced oral hygiene protocol (brushing teeth 3x daily, oral sponge care)
   — critical to reduce bacterial load and aspiration pneumonia risk
4. Swallowing Therapy (daily during inpatient stay):
   - Mendelsohn maneuver (to improve laryngeal elevation and UES opening)
   - Effortful swallow exercises
   - Masako maneuver (tongue hold swallow for pharyngeal strengthening)
   - EMST (expiratory muscle strength training) when medically cleared
   - Shaker/head-lift exercise (for UES opening — when tolerated)
5. Nursing Education:
   - Aspiration precautions posted at bedside
   - Supervised meals with cueing for strategies
   - Monitor for signs of aspiration: coughing during/after meals, wet voice, fever, desaturation
   - Oral care protocol training
6. Follow-up: Reassess in 2 weeks or sooner if clinical status changes
   Repeat MBSS prior to diet advancement
7. Nutrition: Dietitian consulted for caloric needs (patient losing weight on modified diet)

Example 3: Voice Therapy Program — Vocal Nodules

VOICE THERAPY PROGRAM — BILATERAL VOCAL NODULES

Patient: 34-year-old female elementary school teacher
Diagnosis: Bilateral vocal fold nodules confirmed on videostroboscopy by ENT
Referral: Otolaryngologist for voice therapy

Voice Assessment:
- Perceptual evaluation (CAPE-V):
  Overall severity: Moderate (52/100)
  Roughness: Moderate (48/100)
  Breathiness: Mild-Moderate (38/100)
  Strain: Mild (25/100)
  Pitch: Low for gender/age
  Loudness: Normal
- Acoustic measures:
  Fundamental frequency (F0): 185 Hz (low-normal for adult female; expected ~220 Hz)
  Jitter: 1.8% (elevated; normal <1.04%)
  Shimmer: 4.2% (elevated; normal <3.81%)
  HNR: 16 dB (reduced; normal >20 dB)
- Aerodynamic measures:
  MPT: 12 seconds (reduced; expected ≥15 seconds for females)
  s/z ratio: 1.6 (elevated >1.4 suggests glottal insufficiency)
- VHI-10: 28/40 (moderate handicap)
- Patient goals: Reduce hoarseness, eliminate voice fatigue, maintain voice for full teaching day

Treatment Plan (12 sessions, 2x/week for 6 weeks):

Phase 1 — Vocal Hygiene Education (Sessions 1-2):
- Identify and reduce vocal abuse behaviors: yelling in classroom, throat clearing,
  talking over noise, excessive talking outside work
- Hydration: Increase water intake to 64 oz/day; reduce caffeine
- Environmental modifications: Use microphone/amplification system in classroom
- Eliminate throat clearing (replace with hard swallow or small sip of water)
- Reduce caffeine and alcohol intake
- Avoid whispering (more traumatic to folds than easy voice)
- Voice rest: 15 minutes of complete voice rest after school before any social talking

Phase 2 — Resonant Voice Therapy (Sessions 3-8):
- Teach forward focus/resonant voice production (Lessac-Madsen approach)
- Hierarchy: Humming → nasal consonants → words → phrases → sentences → conversation → classroom use
- Target: Anterior oral resonance with easy phonation
- Exercises: "mmmm" humming with lip buzz sensation, MVPT chants, resonant voice readings
- Semi-occluded vocal tract exercises: Straw phonation, lip trills, humming
- Transfer: Practice resonant voice during grading, reading aloud, parent phone calls

Phase 3 — Generalization and Carryover (Sessions 9-12):
- Use resonant voice in increasingly challenging situations (classroom, meetings, recess duty)
- Develop "voice budget" — plan daily voice use and build in rest periods
- Classroom voice strategies: attention signals (clapping pattern, bell), proximity to students,
  visual cues instead of verbal redirections
- Self-monitoring program: Rate voice quality 3x daily (morning, midday, end of day)
- Discharge criteria: CAPE-V overall severity <20/100, VHI-10 <15/40, MPT >15 seconds,
  consistent use of resonant voice in classroom

Follow-up: ENT re-evaluation with stroboscopy 6 weeks post-therapy completion
Prognosis: Good — nodules typically respond well to behavioral voice therapy when
compliance is high; surgery only if therapy fails after adequate trial

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ASHA**: American Speech-Language-Hearing Association — national professional organization and certifying body
- **CCC-SLP**: Certificate of Clinical Competence in Speech-Language Pathology — ASHA national credential
- **Articulation Disorder**: Difficulty producing specific speech sounds (substitutions, omissions, distortions, additions)
- **Phonological Disorder**: Pattern-based speech sound errors reflecting phonological rule difficulties
- **CAS (Childhood Apraxia of Speech)**: Motor speech disorder characterized by difficulty planning and programming speech movements
- **Dysarthria**: Group of motor speech disorders resulting from weakness, paralysis, or incoordination of speech musculature
- **Aphasia**: Acquired language disorder due to brain damage (typically stroke); types include Broca's, Wernicke's, global, anomic, conduction
- **Dysphagia**: Swallowing disorder affecting oral, pharyngeal, or esophageal phases of swallowing
- **IDDSI**: International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative — global framework for modified diet textures and fluid thickness (Levels 0-7)
- **PAS**: Penetration-Aspiration Scale — 8-point scale for rating airway invasion during instrumental swallow studies
- **MBSS/VFSS**: Modified Barium Swallow Study / Videofluoroscopic Swallow Study — radiographic swallow assessment
- **FEES**: Fiberoptic Endoscopic Evaluation of Swallowing — endoscopic visualization of pharyngeal swallow
- **AAC**: Augmentative and Alternative Communication — communication methods/devices supplementing or replacing speech
- **MLU**: Mean Length of Utterance — measure of expressive language complexity in morphemes or words
- **CAPE-V**: Consensus Auditory-Perceptual Evaluation of Voice — standardized perceptual voice assessment
- **VHI**: Voice Handicap Index — patient-reported outcome measure for voice disorders
- **LSVT LOUD**: Lee Silverman Voice Treatment — evidence-based voice therapy for Parkinson's disease
- **Stuttering Modification**: Therapy approach focusing on reducing severity of stuttering moments (Van Riper)
- **Fluency Shaping**: Therapy approach teaching new fluent speech patterns
- **EMST**: Expiratory Muscle Strength Training — respiratory strengthening for swallowing and cough
- **Spaced Retrieval**: Evidence-based cognitive-communication technique for memory impairment

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ASHA Practice Portal**: Evidence-based practice guidelines for all SLP practice areas
- **ASHA Code of Ethics (2023)**: Professional and ethical conduct standards for SLPs
- **ASHA Scope of Practice (2016)**: Defines the breadth of SLP practice activities
- **IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act)**: Federal law mandating free appropriate public education (FAPE) including SLP services for eligible children
- **IDDSI Framework (2019)**: International standardized diet texture and fluid thickness classification (Levels 0-7)
- **WHO ICF (International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health)**: Framework for describing health and health-related states guiding SLP assessment and goal-setting
- **AAN/ASHA Dysphagia Guidelines**: Evidence-based guidelines for dysphagia assessment and management
- **ASHA EBP Compendium**: Systematic reviews and evidence maps for SLP treatment approaches
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements
- **FERPA**: Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (for school-based SLP records)
- **Medicare Benefit Policy Manual**: Coverage requirements for SLP services (medical necessity, functional goals, skilled care)

Common Tools and Equipment:
- **Standardized Assessments**: CELF-5, PLS-5, PPVT-5, EVT-3, GFTA-3, CAAP-2, SSI-4, WAB-R, BDAE-3, CLQT-2, RIPA-2, ASHA-FACS, FOIS
