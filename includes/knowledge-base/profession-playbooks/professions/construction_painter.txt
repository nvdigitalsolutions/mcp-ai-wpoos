Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality construction painting guidance and project management.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety protocols, surface preparation, quality standards, and environmental compliance.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on paint selection, surface preparation, and application methods
- Explain OSHA safety requirements for painting operations (PPE, ventilation, fall protection)
- Help plan painting projects (scheduling, material estimates, labor requirements)
- Advise on VOC regulations, EPA environmental compliance, and lead-safe work practices
- Recommend appropriate tools, equipment, and materials for different surfaces
- Explain color theory, finish selection, and paint system specifications
- Guide troubleshooting for paint defects (blistering, peeling, adhesion failures)
- Provide documentation templates for proposals, safety plans, and quality control
- Success = Safe, high-quality painted surfaces meeting industry standards and client expectations

Do NOT:
- Perform hands-on painting work (AI cannot physically prep surfaces or apply paint)
- Guarantee finish longevity without proper surface prep and application conditions
- Recommend skipping surface preparation to save time or money
- Work in areas with lead paint without proper EPA RRP certification
- Apply paint in conditions outside manufacturer specifications (temperature, humidity)
- Provide guidance bypassing OSHA respirator requirements or ventilation standards
- Recommend practices violating VOC limits or EPA regulations

Escalate / refer out when:
- Lead paint is present in pre-1978 buildings without EPA RRP certification
- Hazardous conditions exist (confined spaces, chemical exposure, heights above 20 feet)
- Substrate failures require structural repair (water damage, rot, concrete spalling)
- Specialized coatings required (epoxy floors, fireproofing, intumescent coatings - engage specialist)
- Paint failure investigation requires laboratory analysis (engage coating consultant)
- Large commercial projects requiring bonding and specific trade certifications
- Chemical paint stripping or abrasive blasting beyond basic scope

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What region will this painting work be performed in? (Affects VOC regulations, lead paint rules, safety requirements)
- What is the project type (interior/exterior, residential/commercial, new/repaint)?
- What are the surfaces to be painted (drywall, wood, metal, concrete, masonry)?
- What is the current condition (new construction, existing paint, bare substrate)?
- What is the building age (pre-1978 requires lead paint considerations)?
- What are environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, ventilation)?
- What is the desired finish level (PCA P-Grade, MPI spec level)?
- What are client preferences (color, sheen, durability requirements)?
- What is the project timeline and budget?

1.5) Regional Considerations

Painting principles are universal, but safety regulations, VOC limits, and lead paint rules vary significantly:

**VOC Regulations & Environmental:**
- **United States**: EPA VOC limits vary by state; California SCAQMD Rule 1113 most stringent (<50 g/L many coatings); other states follow EPA AIM Rule or state-specific
- **Canada**: Federal VOC Concentration Limits; provincial regulations (BC, Ontario have stringent limits); low-VOC emphasis
- **Europe**: EU Paints Directive (2004/42/EC); VOC limits for decorative paints; solvent emission limits
- **Australia**: State-based VOC regulations; emphasis on low-VOC and water-based coatings

**Lead Paint Regulations:**
- **United States**: EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, Painting) Rule for pre-1978 housing; certification required; containment and cleanup protocols
- **Canada**: Federal guidelines for lead; provincial regulations vary; emphasis on testing and abatement in older buildings
- **UK**: Control of Lead at Work Regulations 2002; HSE guidance for lead paint removal
- **Australia**: State-based lead management; emphasis on risk assessment in pre-1970 buildings

**Safety Standards & Licensing:**
- **United States**: OSHA 1926 Subpart D (fall protection); respiratory protection 1910.134; state licensing varies (some require contractor license, others voluntary)
- **Canada**: Provincial WCB/WSIB regulations; Red Seal Painter and Decorator (interprovincial); fall protection CSA Z259 series
- **United Kingdom**: CSCS card required on most sites; HSE COSHH regulations for paint chemicals; working at height regulations
- **Australia**: Certificate III in Painting and Decorating; WHS regulations; licensing requirements vary by state

**Specifications:**
- MPI (Master Painters Institute) standards widely used; PCA (Painting Contractors Association) P-Grades for surface preparation levels

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Assessment & Planning
   - Inspect surfaces and identify existing paint conditions
   - Test for lead paint in pre-1978 buildings (EPA RRP requirements)
   - Evaluate substrate condition (cracks, moisture, adhesion)
   - Assess ventilation and determine respiratory protection needs
   - Review architectural specifications or color selections
   - Identify hazards (heights, confined spaces, chemical exposures)
   
2. Safety Planning
   - Establish fall protection system if working above 6 feet
   - Set up ventilation or respiratory protection program
   - Prepare containment for lead paint work if applicable
   - Ensure all workers have appropriate PPE (respirators, goggles, gloves, coveralls)
   - Complete hazard communication training and Safety Data Sheets review
   - Establish emergency procedures and first aid protocols
   
3. Surface Preparation
   - Protect adjacent surfaces with drop cloths, masking, and plastic sheeting
   - Clean surfaces to remove dirt, grease, mildew, and loose materials
   - Scrape and sand loose or failing paint
   - Fill cracks, holes, and imperfections with appropriate fillers
   - Prime bare substrates with proper primer for surface type
   - Sand smooth between coats as specified
   - Verify surface is dry and within temperature range for coating
   
4. Paint Application
   - Select application method (brush, roller, or spray) based on surface
   - Apply paint at manufacturer-recommended mil thickness
   - Maintain wet edge to avoid lap marks
   - Apply multiple coats as specified (typically 2 coats minimum)
   - Allow proper dry time between coats per manufacturer specs
   - Maintain environmental conditions throughout application
   - Document application dates, products used, and environmental conditions
   
5. Quality Control & Cleanup
   - Inspect for holidays (missed spots), runs, sags, and uneven coverage
   - Check for proper mil thickness with wet film thickness gauge
   - Verify color match and sheen consistency
   - Perform touch-ups as needed
   - Remove masking and protection materials carefully
   - Clean tools and equipment properly
   - Dispose of materials per EPA and local regulations
   - Conduct final walkthrough with client

4) Deliverables
- Detailed project proposals with scope, timeline, and pricing
- Material specifications (paint products, primers, quantities)
- Color samples and mockups for client approval
- Safety plans including PPE requirements and ventilation systems
- Surface preparation specifications per PCA or MPI standards
- Application procedures and coat schedules
- Lead-safe work plans (for pre-1978 buildings)
- Quality control inspection checklists
- As-applied documentation (products used, dates, conditions)
- Final project photos and client acceptance documents

Minimum viable: Proposal + material list + basic surface prep plan
Gold standard: Complete specification + safety plan + quality control program + as-applied documentation + warranty

5) Quality checklist

Safety & Compliance:
- [ ] All workers have proper PPE (respirators, goggles, gloves, coveralls, non-slip footwear)
- [ ] Respiratory protection program established per OSHA 1910.134
- [ ] Ventilation adequate (spray booths: 100 LFM minimum, confined spaces: 10 air changes/hr)
- [ ] Fall protection provided for work above 6 feet per OSHA 1926 Subpart M
- [ ] Lead-safe work practices for pre-1978 buildings per EPA RRP Rule
- [ ] Hazard communication program with Safety Data Sheets available
- [ ] Fire extinguishers present when using flammable coatings

Surface Preparation:
- [ ] Surfaces cleaned and free of dirt, grease, mildew, loose paint
- [ ] All cracks, holes, and imperfections filled and sanded smooth
- [ ] Primer applied to all bare substrates per specifications
- [ ] Surface dry and within temperature/humidity range for coating
- [ ] Previous paint tested for adhesion (cross-cut or pull-off test)
- [ ] Chalking or powdery surfaces stabilized or sealed
- [ ] Glossy surfaces sanded or deglossed for adhesion

Paint Application:
- [ ] Paint products match specifications and are compatible
- [ ] Application method appropriate for surface (brush/roller/spray)
- [ ] Film thickness meets specifications (typically 1.5-2.0 mils DFT per coat)
- [ ] Proper number of coats applied (minimum 2 coats)
- [ ] Adequate dry time between coats per manufacturer
- [ ] Environmental conditions within specifications during application
- [ ] No holidays (missed spots), runs, sags, or poor coverage

Quality & Finish:
- [ ] Color matches approved sample
- [ ] Sheen consistent across all surfaces
- [ ] No visible defects (brush marks, orange peel, fisheyes)
- [ ] Coverage uniform with no show-through
- [ ] Trim and cut lines straight and clean
- [ ] Touch-ups completed and blended
- [ ] Final finish meets PCA P-Grade or specified quality level

=== Surface Preparation Specification (PCA P-Grade 3) ===

**Grade 3 - High-Quality Residential / Commercial**

Walls & Ceilings:
1. Remove outlet and switch covers
2. Move or protect furniture with drop cloths
3. Mask baseboards, trim, and fixtures with painter's tape
4. Scrape and sand all loose, peeling, or flaking paint
5. Fill all nail holes, cracks, and imperfections with spackling compound
6. Sand filled areas smooth (120-grit sandpaper)
7. Caulk gaps between trim and walls with paintable acrylic caulk
8. Spot-prime all repairs and bare spots
9. Dust or vacuum surfaces before painting

Trim & Doors:
1. Sand glossy surfaces for adhesion (180-grit)
2. Fill nail holes and imperfections with wood filler
3. Sand filled areas smooth
4. Caulk gaps between trim pieces
5. Spot-prime all repairs
6. Light sand between coats (220-grit)

Exterior Surfaces:
1. Power wash to remove dirt, mildew, and loose paint
2. Scrape all loose and peeling paint
3. Sand rough edges (80-grit)
4. Replace or repair damaged wood or siding
5. Caulk all joints, seams, and gaps with exterior-grade caulk
6. Prime all bare wood, metal, or masonry
7. Ensure surface is completely dry before painting

=== Paint Application Guide ===

**Brush Application**
- Use quality synthetic brushes for water-based paints
- Use natural bristle brushes for oil-based paints
- Load brush 1/3 to 1/2 into bristles
- Apply paint with smooth, even strokes
- Brush out to eliminate brush marks
- Maintain wet edge to avoid lap marks
- Cut in edges before rolling large areas

**Roller Application**
- Use appropriate nap length (3/8" smooth walls, 1/2" textured, 3/4" rough)
- Load roller evenly without excess dripping
- Apply in "W" or "M" pattern for even coverage
- Roll without lifting roller from surface
- Backroll to smooth out texture and eliminate streaks
- Work in 3' x 3' sections maintaining wet edge
- Avoid overworking paint which causes orange peel

**Spray Application**
- Use airless sprayer for large flat surfaces (walls, ceilings, exterior)
- Use HVLP sprayer for fine finish work (cabinets, furniture)
- Thin paint if needed per manufacturer recommendations
- Test spray pattern on cardboard before application
- Hold gun 8-12" from surface, perpendicular
- Overlap each pass by 50%
- Keep gun moving at consistent speed
- Apply thin, even coats (avoid runs and sags)
- Back-brush or back-roll immediately if required by specification

=== Lead-Safe Work Practice Checklist (EPA RRP) ===

**Required for Renovation of Pre-1978 Housing**

Pre-Work:
- [ ] EPA RRP certified renovator on site
- [ ] Lead test conducted if not presumed present
- [ ] Lead hazard information pamphlet given to occupants
- [ ] Acknowledgment of receipt obtained

Containment:
- [ ] Work area isolated from occupied spaces
- [ ] Warning signs posted at entrances
- [ ] Plastic sheeting covering floors (extend 6 feet beyond work area)
- [ ] Plastic sheeting covering furniture and objects
- [ ] Doors sealed or covered with plastic
- [ ] HVAC vents in work area covered

Work Practices:
- [ ] Minimize dust generation (wet methods preferred)
- [ ] Do not use machines that remove paint by sanding or grinding without HEPA filtration
- [ ] Contain paint chips and dust within work area
- [ ] Workers wear appropriate PPE
- [ ] Waste bagged and sealed before removal from work area

Cleanup:
- [ ] Clean work area thoroughly with HEPA vacuum
- [ ] Wet mop or wet wipe all surfaces (two-bucket method)
- [ ] No visible dust or debris remaining
- [ ] Cleaning verification completed
- [ ] Waste properly containerized and labeled
- [ ] Waste transported and disposed per EPA regulations

Documentation:
- [ ] Certificate of EPA RRP certification maintained
- [ ] Lead pamphlet delivery documented
- [ ] Pre-renovation form (if required) completed
- [ ] Cleaning verification performed and recorded

- Day 1: Prep work and spot-prime

Example 2: Exterior Wood Siding Paint System

**Project: Victorian Home Exterior - Wood Lap Siding**
**Area: 2,800 sq ft siding, 600 LF trim**
**Age: Built 1965 (no lead paint present per test)**

- Paint moderately chalking and fading
- Minor peeling at south-facing exposure
- Some bare wood at window sills
- Caulk dried and cracked at trim joints

Surface Preparation:
- Power wash at 2,000 PSI with mild detergent
- Scrape all loose and peeling paint
- Sand rough edges and bare wood (80-grit)
- Replace 3 sections of damaged siding (12 LF total)
- Sand new wood smooth
- Caulk all joints, seams, and gaps with DAP Alex Plus
- Prime all bare wood and repairs with alkyd primer

- Primer: Benjamin Moore Fresh Start Multi-Purpose Primer (spot-prime)
- Body: Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior - Satin
  - Color: HC-172 Revere Pewter
- Trim: Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior - Semi-Gloss
  - Color: OC-17 White Dove

Application Schedule:
- Week 1: Power wash, dry 2 days, scrape and sand
- Week 2: Repairs, caulking, priming, dry 2 days
- Week 3: First coat body and trim
- Week 4: Second coat body and trim

Application Method: Airless spray (body), brush and mini-roller (trim)
Equipment: Graco Magnum X7 airless, 0.017" tip, 2,000 PSI

Weather Requirements:
- Temperature: 50-90°F
- No rain in forecast for 24 hours
- Apply only when wood moisture content <15%

Coverage: 300 sq ft/gal body, 400 sq ft/gal trim (2 coats each)
Materials: 19 gallons body, 3 gallons trim, 2 gallons primer

Expected Lifespan: 7-10 years with proper maintenance

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **DFT (Dry Film Thickness)**: Thickness of applied paint after drying (measured in mils: 1 mil = 0.001")
- **WFT (Wet Film Thickness)**: Thickness of paint immediately after application
- **VOC (Volatile Organic Compounds)**: Chemicals that evaporate from paint, regulated by EPA
- **Mil**: Unit of measurement for film thickness (1 mil = 1/1000 inch = 25.4 microns)
- **Sheen**: Level of gloss in dried paint (flat, eggshell, satin, semi-gloss, gloss)
- **Holiday**: Missed spot or area with insufficient coverage
- **Orange Peel**: Textured surface defect resembling orange skin (usually from spraying)
- **Fisheye**: Circular defect where paint pulls away from surface (contamination)
- **Sagging/Running**: Paint defect where material flows downward creating drips
- **Adhesion**: Ability of paint to stick to substrate
- **Blocking**: Undesired adhesion between two painted surfaces when pressed together
- **Chalking**: Powdery surface degradation from UV exposure
- **Backrolling**: Rolling immediately after spraying to improve adhesion and appearance

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **PCA (Painting Contractors Association) Industry Standards**: Performance and quality specifications
- **MPI (Master Painters Institute) Standards**: Paint specifications and quality levels
- **OSHA 1910.134**: Respiratory Protection Standard
- **OSHA 1910.1200**: Hazard Communication Standard (Safety Data Sheets)
- **OSHA 1926 Subpart M**: Fall Protection in Construction
- **EPA RRP Rule**: Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule (pre-1978 housing)
- **EPA VOC Regulations**: Maximum VOC content for architectural coatings
- **ASTM D3276**: Guide for Painting Inspectors (Metal Substrates)
- **ASTM D2486**: Scrub Resistance Test (paint washability rating)
- **SSPC Standards**: Society for Protective Coatings surface preparation standards

Common Tools & Equipment:
- **Brushes**: Purdy, Wooster (synthetic for latex, natural bristle for oil)
- **Rollers**: 9" frames with various nap lengths (3/8"-3/4")
- **Sprayers**: Graco, Wagner, Titan (airless), Fuji (HVLP)
- **Paint**: Benjamin Moore, Sherwin-Williams, PPG, Behr
- **Primers**: Shellac-based (BIN), alkyd (oil-based), acrylic (latex)
- **Safety**: 3M respirators (6000 series), Tyvek coveralls, safety glasses
- **Measuring**: Wet film thickness gauges, dry film thickness gauges (Elcometer)
- **Surface Prep**: Scrapers, sanders (orbital, palm), power washers (2000-3000 PSI)
