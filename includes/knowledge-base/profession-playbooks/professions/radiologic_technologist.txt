Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality diagnostic imaging and radiation safety protocols.

Category guidance
- Emphasize ALARA principles, radiation safety, accurate positioning, image quality, and patient care during imaging procedures.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on radiographic positioning, imaging protocols, and technical factors
- Explain radiation safety principles (ALARA), shielding, and dose optimization per ARRT/ASRT standards
- Guide on image quality assessment, artifact identification, and troubleshooting
- Advise on patient preparation, positioning, and communication strategies
- Help develop procedure protocols and technical imaging techniques
- Explain equipment operation, quality control, and maintenance procedures
- Guide on documentation of imaging procedures and radiation exposure
- Provide patient education materials for various imaging procedures
- Success = Diagnostic-quality images obtained safely with minimal radiation exposure and optimal patient care

Do NOT:
- Interpret diagnostic images or provide radiologic diagnoses (radiologists interpret)
- Perform imaging procedures without valid physician orders
- Exceed radiation dose limits or compromise ALARA principles
- Modify physician orders or examination protocols without authorization
- Guarantee diagnostic outcomes or treatment recommendations
- Perform procedures outside scope of ARRT certification and state licensure
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization
- Operate equipment without proper training and competency validation

Escalate / refer out when:
- Patient has acute trauma or medical emergency requiring immediate physician assessment
- Image quality issues cannot be resolved and repeat exposure would exceed safe dose limits
- Equipment malfunction poses safety risk to patient or staff
- Patient refuses essential imaging after explanation and cannot provide informed consent
- Contrast reaction or adverse event requiring emergency medical intervention (call code team)
- Suspected pregnancy requires protocol modification (consult radiologist, use alternative imaging)
- Patient condition deteriorates during procedure (altered mental status, respiratory distress, cardiac event)
- Incidental findings on images suggest urgent pathology (radiologist immediate notification)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/state/country? (Licensing, scope, and radiography title vary by region)
- What is the examination ordered and clinical indication (reason for imaging)?
- What is the patient's age, pregnancy status, and relevant medical history?
- Are there any contraindications to the procedure (implants, allergies, pregnancy)?
- What is the patient's mobility status and ability to cooperate with positioning?
- Is contrast media required, and has the patient been screened for renal function/allergies?
- What is the care setting and equipment available (portable, fixed, fluoroscopy, CT)?
- Are there any prior images available for comparison or technique reference?
- What is the required turnaround time and urgency level (routine, stat, emergency)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Radiologic Technology Practice

**United States:**
- Certification: ARRT (American Registry of Radiologic Technologists) - RT(R) credential
- Licensing: State licensing in most states (some states mandatory, others voluntary)
- Education: Associate or bachelor's degree in radiologic technology
- Scope: Radiography, fluoroscopy, CT, MRI (with additional certifications)
- Specialties: Bone densitometry, mammography, CT, MRI, vascular-interventional, radiation therapy
- Radiation safety: State regulations; medical physicist oversight
- Organizations: ASRT (American Society of Radiologic Technologists), state societies

**United Kingdom:**
- Title: Radiographer (not "technologist")
- Regulation: HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council)
- Education: BSc in Diagnostic Radiography; 3-year program
- Scope: Diagnostic radiography (X-ray, fluoroscopy, CT); therapeutic radiography separate
- Advanced practice: Radiographer reporting, CT/MRI advanced practitioners
- System: NHS Band 5-7; some private practice
- Organizations: SCoR (Society and College of Radiographers)

**Canada:**
- Title: Medical Radiation Technologist (MRT)
- Certification: CAMRT (Canadian Association of Medical Radiation Technologists)
- Licensing: Provincial regulation (e.g., CMRTO in Ontario)
- Education: College diploma or bachelor's degree
- Scope: Radiography, CT, MRI, nuclear medicine, radiation therapy (separate streams)
- Organizations: CAMRT, provincial regulatory bodies

**Australia:**
- Title: Radiographer or Medical Imaging Technologist
- Licensing: AHPRA via Medical Radiation Practice Board of Australia
- Education: Bachelor's degree in Medical Radiation Science
- Scope: Diagnostic radiography, CT, MRI, nuclear medicine; radiation therapy separate
- Advanced practice: Radiographer-led clinics, preliminary reporting
- Organizations: AIR (Australian Institute of Radiography), ASMIRT

**Key Differences:**
- Title: Radiologic Technologist (US); Radiographer (UK/Australia); MRT (Canada)
- Licensing: State-based (US); national HCPC (UK); provincial (Canada); national AHPRA (Australia)
- Specialization: Multiple ARRT certifications (US); diagnostic vs therapeutic streams (UK/Canada/Australia)
- Advanced practice: Growing scope internationally (reporting, advanced practitioners)

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic imaging procedure process per ASRT Practice Standards:

1. Pre-Procedure Preparation & Verification
   - Verify physician order matches patient information and clinical indication
   - Confirm correct patient using two identifiers (name, DOB) per Joint Commission protocol
   - Review patient history, allergies, and contraindications in electronic health record
   - Screen for pregnancy (last menstrual period, pregnancy test if indicated)
   - Assess patient's ability to follow instructions and cooperate with positioning
   - Explain procedure to patient, obtain informed consent if required
   - Review prior images if available for comparison and technique reference
   - Verify equipment functionality and radiation safety devices (collimation, shielding, dosimeters)
   - Document pre-procedure assessment and patient education

2. Patient Positioning & Technical Setup
   - Position patient according to ACR/ASRT protocol for examination type
   - Ensure proper alignment of anatomical part to image receptor
   - Apply appropriate immobilization devices for pediatric or uncooperative patients
   - Use proper collimation to limit radiation field to area of interest (ALARA)
   - Place gonad shielding when indicated and when it won't obscure anatomy of interest
   - Select appropriate technical factors (kVp, mAs, SID, grid) for patient size and anatomy
   - Verify patient positioning using anatomical landmarks and positioning aids
   - Communicate clearly with patient about breath-holding, remaining still during exposure
   - Ensure patient comfort and safety (fall prevention, oxygen/IV line management)

3. Image Acquisition & Radiation Safety
   - Apply ALARA principles: minimum exposure necessary for diagnostic image
   - Use appropriate beam restriction (collimation) to reduce scatter radiation
   - Select optimal kVp (penetrating power) and mAs (quantity) for examination
   - Utilize automatic exposure control (AEC) when appropriate for consistent image density
   - Monitor radiation dose indicators (dose area product, entrance skin dose, CTDI for CT)
   - Verify proper distance from radiation source during exposure (6-foot rule for mobile radiography)
   - Ensure all personnel and family members are properly shielded or behind barrier
   - Document radiation exposure parameters (technique factors, dose) in examination record
   - Use digital radiography systems efficiently to minimize repeat exposures

4. Image Quality Assessment & Troubleshooting
   - Evaluate image for diagnostic quality before releasing patient
   - Check for proper positioning: anatomy fully demonstrated, proper alignment and rotation
   - Assess image density/brightness and contrast (digital processing may need adjustment)
   - Identify artifacts (motion, positioning, equipment, patient-related) and determine cause
   - Evaluate for anatomical markers (R/L, upright, time markers) are correctly placed
   - Ensure collimation borders visible to demonstrate proper beam restriction
   - Assess for necessary anatomy inclusion per protocol (joints, specific structures)
   - Determine if repeat image is necessary or if existing image is diagnostic
   - If repeat required, adjust technique factors to correct issue (positioning, exposure)
   - Document reason for repeat exposures and corrective actions taken

5. Post-Procedure & Documentation
   - Remove immobilization devices and assist patient from table safely
   - Provide post-procedure instructions and address patient questions
   - Transmit images to PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System) with proper identifiers
   - Document procedure completion, technical factors, patient tolerance, and any complications
   - Alert radiologist to stat/urgent examinations requiring immediate interpretation
   - Clean and disinfect equipment per infection control protocol
   - Dispose of contaminated supplies and used shielding devices properly
   - Report equipment malfunctions or quality control issues to supervisor/biomed
   - Monitor for delayed contrast reactions if contrast was administered (observe for 30 minutes)

4) Deliverables
Common work products and documentation:
- Diagnostic-quality radiographic images transmitted to PACS with proper labeling
- Procedure documentation in RIS (Radiology Information System) including technical factors
- Radiation dose reports for procedures (required for CT, fluoroscopy, interventional)
- Patient communication sheets explaining procedure, preparation, and follow-up
- Quality control logs for equipment testing (daily, weekly, monthly per state regulations)
- Incident reports for adverse events, equipment failures, or radiation safety concerns
- Patient screening forms (pregnancy, contrast allergy, renal function, implant compatibility)
- Positioning reference guides and protocol cards for specific examinations
- Radiation safety audits and dose optimization reports
- Continuing education documentation and competency assessments

Minimum viable: Single diagnostic-quality image with proper identification and documentation of technique factors
Gold standard: Complete examination series with optimal image quality, minimal radiation dose, comprehensive documentation, patient education materials, and quality assurance verification

5) Quality checklist

Radiation Safety & ALARA:
- [ ] Radiation exposure justified based on clinical indication
- [ ] Minimum radiation dose used to obtain diagnostic image
- [ ] Proper collimation applied to limit field size
- [ ] Gonad shielding used when appropriate and not obscuring anatomy
- [ ] Two-identifier patient verification performed before exposure
- [ ] Pregnancy screening completed for females of childbearing age
- [ ] Radiation dose parameters documented (kVp, mAs, dose indicators)
- [ ] Personnel shielding or barrier protection used during exposure

Image Quality & Positioning:
- [ ] Anatomical part properly positioned according to protocol
- [ ] All required anatomy demonstrated on image
- [ ] Proper alignment and no rotation evident
- [ ] Anatomical markers (R/L, upright, time) correctly placed and visible
- [ ] Image density/brightness appropriate for visualization of anatomy
- [ ] Contrast resolution adequate for diagnostic interpretation
- [ ] No motion artifact or patient movement
- [ ] Collimation borders visible demonstrating beam restriction
- [ ] Artifacts identified and minimized (equipment, positioning, patient)

Patient Care & Safety:
- [ ] Informed consent obtained if required for procedure
- [ ] Patient allergies and contraindications reviewed and documented
- [ ] Procedure explained to patient in understandable language
- [ ] Patient comfort and safety ensured (fall prevention, monitoring)
- [ ] Patient modesty and dignity maintained throughout procedure
- [ ] Contrast screening completed if contrast media required
- [ ] Post-procedure instructions provided to patient
- [ ] Patient questions answered and concerns addressed

Documentation & Compliance:
- [ ] Physician order verified and matches patient information
- [ ] Clinical indication documented for examination
- [ ] Technical factors (kVp, mAs, SID, grid) documented
- [ ] Radiation dose metrics recorded per regulatory requirements
- [ ] Images transmitted to PACS with correct patient identifiers
- [ ] Procedure documentation completed in RIS
- [ ] Stat/urgent examinations flagged for radiologist
- [ ] Equipment quality control performed per protocol
- [ ] HIPAA compliance maintained throughout procedure

=== Patient Communication: Radiographic Examination ===
Your doctor has ordered: [EXAMINATION NAME]

What to expect:
- The examination will take approximately [TIME] minutes.
- You will be positioned on an X-ray table or standing against an upright image receptor.
- The technologist will position you and may ask you to hold your breath or remain still.
- You will hear a brief buzzing sound when the X-ray is taken.
- The X-ray itself is painless and takes only a second or two.

Preparation:
- Remove jewelry, glasses, and any metal objects from the area being imaged.
- You may be asked to change into a hospital gown.
- Inform the technologist if you are pregnant or think you might be pregnant.

Safety:
- X-rays use a small amount of radiation to create images.
- We follow ALARA principles: "As Low As Reasonably Achievable."
- Shielding will be used to protect sensitive areas when appropriate.
- The benefits of the examination outweigh the small radiation risk.

Results:
- A radiologist (doctor specialized in reading X-rays) will interpret your images.
- Results are typically available within [TIMEFRAME].
- Your ordering physician will discuss the results with you.

Questions? Please ask your technologist. We are here to help you.

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Chest X-ray PA and Lateral - Technical Approach
```
Clinical Indication: Evaluation for pneumonia

PA Chest Radiograph:
- Patient positioned upright (72" SID) with arms abducted, hands on hips, shoulders rotated forward
- Central ray perpendicular to image receptor at level of T7
- Technical factors: 120 kVp, 3-5 mAs (based on AEC), grid
- Collimation to lung fields, include apices to costophrenic angles
- Exposure on full inspiration (patient coached to take deep breath and hold)
- Image criteria: Scapulae projected lateral to lung fields, equidistant clavicles (no rotation), 9-10 posterior ribs visible above diaphragm

Lateral Chest Radiograph:
- Patient positioned with left side against image receptor
- Arms raised overhead, chin elevated
- Central ray perpendicular to image receptor at level of T7, midcoronal plane
- Technical factors: 120 kVp, 10-15 mAs, grid
- Exposure on full inspiration
- Image criteria: Superimposition of ribs posteriorly, no rotation, hemidiaphragms visible

Radiation Dose: DAP approximately 0.15-0.30 Gy·cm² for two views combined
Image Quality: Excellent visualization of lung parenchyma, mediastinum, and bony thorax
Patient Tolerance: Good, patient cooperative
```

Example 2: Pediatric Forearm Radiography - Age-Appropriate Approach
```
Patient: 6-year-old with suspected distal radius fracture after fall from monkey bars

Pre-Procedure:
- Explain procedure using child-friendly language: "We're going to take pictures of your arm"
- Allow parent/guardian to stay in room with lead apron for emotional support
- Screen parent for pregnancy before allowing in room
- Assess child's ability to hold still (use immobilization if needed)

AP Forearm:
- Child seated or supine with arm fully extended on image receptor
- Palm facing up (supination)
- Use sandbag or Velcro strap for immobilization if needed
- Include wrist and elbow joints (both joints on long bone)
- Technical factors: 55-60 kVp, 2-4 mAs (pediatric technique chart for 6yo)
- Gonad shielding placed (age-appropriate size)
- Collimate tightly to forearm
- Parent holds opposite arm/comforts child during exposure

Lateral Forearm:
- Rotate arm 90 degrees, thumb up position
- Elbow flexed 90 degrees for comfort
- Maintain immobilization
- Include both joints
- Same technical factors as AP

Radiation Safety:
- Pediatric technique (reduced kVp/mAs compared to adult)
- Tight collimation to area of interest
- Gonad shielding used
- No repeat exposures (get it right the first time)

Total Radiation Dose: <0.01 mGy entrance skin dose per view
Result: Diagnostic images demonstrating distal radius buckle fracture
Parent satisfaction: Parent thanked for patience and cooperation, child given sticker
```

Example 3: Portable ICU Chest Radiograph - Critical Care Environment
```
Patient: 72yo male, intubated and mechanically ventilated, suspected pneumothorax

Pre-Procedure Coordination:
- Coordinate with ICU nurse for optimal timing (not during medication administration)
- Verify patient identification using wristband (two identifiers)
- Assess patient stability and monitor placement (EKG leads, IV lines, ventilator tubing)
- Notify radiologist of stat priority
- Ensure respiratory therapist aware to coordinate with ventilator

Portable AP Chest Setup:
- Position image receptor (CR cassette) behind patient's back
- Remove monitoring leads from chest if possible, reposition if over lung fields
- Elevate head of bed 30-45 degrees if patient tolerates (simulate upright)
- Center to T7, ensure midline alignment
- SID: 40-44 inches (standard portable distance)
- Technical factors: 90-100 kVp, 1.5-3 mAs, no grid
- Collimate to lung fields
- Use "upright" marker if head of bed elevated

Radiation Safety in ICU:
- Request all non-essential personnel leave room or stand 6 feet away
- ICU nurse may stay if needed for patient stability (provide lead apron)
- Announce "X-ray" before exposure
- Verify no pregnant staff in room

Image Acquisition:
- Instruct respiratory therapist to deliver breath via ventilator at moment of exposure (optimizes inspiration)
- Expose image at peak inspiration
- Verify image quality on portable workstation before leaving room

Post-Procedure:
- Assist nurse with repositioning monitors and leads
- Transmit image to PACS immediately (stat priority)
- Notify radiologist directly (phone call) for urgent interpretation
- Document ETT (endotracheal tube) position, central line placement on image

Result: Image demonstrates appropriately positioned ETT (2-3 cm above carina), correctly placed central venous catheter, and rules out pneumothorax. Stat radiologist read confirms findings within 10 minutes.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ALARA**: As Low As Reasonably Achievable—radiation safety principle minimizing exposure
- **kVp (kilovoltage peak)**: Controls penetrating power of X-ray beam and image contrast
- **mAs (milliampere-seconds)**: Controls quantity of X-rays produced and image density
- **SID (Source-to-Image Distance)**: Distance from X-ray tube to image receptor (typically 40" or 72")
- **Collimation**: Restriction of X-ray beam to area of interest, reduces scatter and patient dose
- **AEC (Automatic Exposure Control)**: System that terminates exposure when optimal image density reached
- **Grid**: Device that absorbs scatter radiation, improves image contrast
- **PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System)**: Digital system for storing and viewing images
- **RIS (Radiology Information System)**: Database for managing radiology workflow and orders
- **CTDIvol (CT Dose Index volume)**: Standardized measure of radiation dose in CT examinations
- **DLP (Dose Length Product)**: Total radiation dose for entire CT examination
- **Entrance Skin Dose (ESD)**: Radiation dose at skin surface where beam enters patient
- **Artifact**: Unwanted density or appearance on image not representing actual anatomy

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ARRT (American Registry of Radiologic Technologists) Standards**: National certification and continuing education requirements, updated exam specifications 2025 with emphasis on advanced modalities and ethics
- **ASRT (American Society of Radiologic Technologists) Practice Standards 2024**: Clinical performance standards for medical imaging and radiation therapy, defining scope of practice
- **ACR (American College of Radiology) Practice Parameters**: Evidence-based guidelines for imaging protocols and quality assurance
- **ACR-SPR Practice Parameter for Diagnostic Reference Levels**: Dose optimization benchmarks for common examinations
- **Joint Commission Patient Safety Goals**: Two-identifier verification, preventing wrong-site/wrong-patient procedures
- **CDC Radiation Safety Guidelines**: ALARA principles, time-distance-shielding strategies
- **FDA 21 CFR Part 1020**: Federal performance standards for diagnostic X-ray equipment
- **State Radiation Control Regulations**: Vary by state, cover licensure, equipment standards, quality control
- **HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules**: Protecting patient health information in imaging
- **Image Gently Campaign**: Pediatric radiation dose reduction initiative
- **Image Wisely Campaign**: Adult radiation dose optimization and appropriateness criteria

Common Equipment & Technologies:
- **Digital Radiography (DR)**: Direct digital image capture, immediate image availability
- **Computed Radiography (CR)**: Cassette-based digital imaging using photostimulable phosphor plates
- **Fluoroscopy**: Real-time X-ray imaging for dynamic studies and interventional procedures
- **Computed Tomography (CT)**: Cross-sectional imaging using rotating X-ray tube
- **Mobile/Portable Units**: Radiographic equipment brought to bedside for ICU, ED, surgical cases
- **C-arm**: Mobile fluoroscopy unit for surgical and interventional procedures
- **PACS/RIS**: Enterprise imaging and radiology information systems
- **Dosimeters**: Personal radiation monitoring badges (ring badges for hands, body badges)
- **Phantom**: Test object for quality control imaging and equipment calibration
- **Lead Shielding**: Aprons, thyroid shields, gonad shields for radiation protection

Quality Control & Safety:
- **Daily QC**: Visual inspection, collimator alignment, laser accuracy
- **Weekly QC**: Phantom imaging, image quality assessment
- **Monthly QC**: Repeat/reject analysis, dose audits
- **Annual QC**: Physicist inspection, equipment calibration
- **State inspection**: Typically every 1-3 years depending on state regulations
