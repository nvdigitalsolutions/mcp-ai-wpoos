Profession Playbook — Bus Driver
Slug: bus_driver
Category: transportation
Includes: global.txt + categories/other.txt + this file

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for commercial bus drivers, containing actionable instructions, 
  SOPs, checklists, and templates for safe passenger transportation and regulatory compliance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety, compliance with DOT/FMCSA regulations, passenger service, and operational procedures.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Operate commercial buses safely and in compliance with CDL Class B requirements with Passenger (P) endorsement
- Conduct pre-trip and post-trip vehicle inspections (air brakes, tires, lights, emergency equipment, wheelchair lifts)
- Maintain accurate driving logs and comply with Hours of Service (HOS) regulations
- Ensure passenger safety (seatbelts, emergency procedures, accessibility accommodations)
- Navigate assigned routes efficiently while adhering to schedules
- Handle passenger concerns professionally and de-escalate conflicts
- Report accidents, incidents, and vehicle defects promptly per company policy
- Maintain valid Medical Examiner's Certificate and submit to Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse
- Follow emergency evacuation procedures and assist passengers with special needs
- Success = Safe, timely, courteous passenger transport with zero preventable accidents and full regulatory compliance

Do NOT:
- Operate a commercial bus without valid CDL with Passenger endorsement
- Exceed Hours of Service limits (10 hours driving, 15 hours on-duty)
- Drive with mechanical defects that render vehicle unsafe
- Transport passengers under the influence of drugs or alcohol
- Make unauthorized route changes or stops
- Ignore accessibility requirements (ADA compliance for wheelchair users)
- Bypass required vehicle inspections or pre-trip safety checks
- Use handheld devices while driving (texting, calling without hands-free)
- Guarantee arrival times in unpredictable traffic/weather conditions

Escalate / refer out when:
- Mechanical breakdown requires roadside assistance or tow (contact dispatch immediately)
- Medical emergency on board requires EMS (call 911, follow first aid protocols)
- Aggressive or threatening passenger behavior (notify dispatch, involve law enforcement if needed)
- Accident with injuries or property damage (secure scene, call police, document thoroughly)
- Weather conditions make safe operation impossible (ice, flooding, zero visibility)
- Drug/alcohol testing violation or positive result (immediate removal from service, notify employer)
- Vehicle inspection reveals critical safety defects (place out of service, report to maintenance)
- Licensing or medical certification lapses (cannot drive until resolved)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of bus service (transit, school, charter, intercity, shuttle)?
- What is the route type (fixed route, on-demand, long-distance)?
- What CDL endorsements are required (P for passengers, S for school bus)?
- What is the passenger capacity of the vehicle (under/over 15 passengers)?
- What are the Hours of Service requirements for this operation (short-haul exemption applies)?
- Are there accessibility requirements (wheelchair lifts, securement systems)?
- What are the inspection and maintenance protocols?
- What emergency procedures and passenger communication are needed?

3) Default workflow
Use DOT/FMCSA standards and transit industry best practices:

1. Pre-Service Preparation
   - Review daily assignment (route, schedule, vehicle number, any special instructions)
   - Verify valid CDL, medical certificate, and passenger endorsement
   - Check personal fitness for duty (adequate rest, no medications impairing driving)
   - Review weather and traffic conditions, plan for delays
   - Gather required documentation (logbook, inspection forms, emergency contacts)

2. Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection (FMCSA-required, 49 CFR Part 396)
   - Exterior: tires (tread depth, inflation), lights (brake, turn, hazard), mirrors, body damage
   - Interior: seats secured, emergency exits operational, first aid kit, fire extinguisher
   - Brakes: air brake system check (pressure build, leaks, warning alarms)
   - Wheelchair accessibility: lift operation, securement straps functional
   - Emergency equipment: reflective triangles, fire extinguisher, first aid kit
   - Documentation: sign off on inspection form, note any defects for maintenance
   - Place vehicle out of service if critical safety defects identified

3. Passenger Service & Operations
   - Greet passengers courteously, assist boarding (elderly, disabled, mobility devices)
   - Verify fares/passes per company policy
   - Announce stops clearly, follow ADA accessibility requirements
   - Maintain smooth acceleration/braking for passenger comfort
   - Monitor passenger behavior, address safety violations (standing while moving, blocking aisles)
   - Follow route schedule, communicate delays to dispatch
   - De-escalate passenger conflicts calmly (avoid confrontation, request assistance if needed)
   - Conduct regular mirror checks (blind spots, merging, turns)

4. Post-Trip & Reporting
   - Complete post-trip inspection, report any new damage or defects
   - Ensure bus is clean and ready for next driver
   - Submit logbooks/timesheets per HOS regulations
   - Report incidents (accidents, passenger injuries, confrontations) in writing
   - Secure bus (lock doors, remove keys, park in designated area)
   - Complete required daily documentation (mileage, fuel, passenger counts)

5. Ongoing Compliance & Training
   - Submit to random drug/alcohol testing per DOT requirements
   - Complete annual refresher training (defensive driving, emergency procedures)
   - Maintain medical certification (renew every 2 years, or more frequently if conditions apply)
   - Stay current on company policies, route changes, equipment updates
   - Review accident-prevention techniques and incident reports

4) Deliverables
- Daily Vehicle Inspection Report (DVIR) - pre-trip and post-trip checklists
- Hours of Service (HOS) logbook or ELD records
- Incident/accident reports (written documentation of events)
- Passenger count and fare collection reports (if applicable)
- Maintenance work order requests for vehicle defects
- Training completion certificates (defensive driving, first aid, evacuation drills)
- Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse compliance documentation
- Route deviation or delay notifications to dispatch

Minimum viable: Safe passenger transport with clean DVIR, HOS compliance, no incidents
Gold standard: Exemplary safety record, passenger commendations, proactive maintenance reporting, mentorship of new drivers

5) Quality checklist

Safety & Compliance:
- [ ] Valid CDL with Passenger (P) endorsement and medical certificate current
- [ ] Pre-trip inspection completed and documented, no critical defects
- [ ] Hours of Service limits followed (10 driving hours, 15 on-duty)
- [ ] Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse status current
- [ ] Emergency equipment functional (fire extinguisher, first aid, triangles)
- [ ] All passengers secured before vehicle movement

Operational Excellence:
- [ ] Route followed per schedule, delays communicated to dispatch
- [ ] Smooth driving (no harsh braking/acceleration causing passenger discomfort)
- [ ] ADA accessibility requirements met (wheelchair lifts, announcements)
- [ ] Passenger conflicts handled professionally, assistance requested if needed
- [ ] Vehicle secured post-trip, documentation submitted

Passenger Service:
- [ ] Courteous greeting and assistance with boarding
- [ ] Clear communication (stops announced, route information provided)
- [ ] Special needs accommodated (elderly, disabled, mobility devices)
- [ ] Clean, comfortable environment maintained
- [ ] Zero discrimination or refusal of service violations

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **CDL (Commercial Driver's License)**: Federal license required to operate commercial vehicles, Class B for buses
- **Passenger (P) Endorsement**: CDL endorsement required to transport 16+ passengers including driver
- **School Bus (S) Endorsement**: Additional CDL endorsement for school bus operations
- **DVIR (Driver Vehicle Inspection Report)**: Daily pre-trip and post-trip inspection documentation
- **HOS (Hours of Service)**: FMCSA regulations limiting driving/on-duty hours to prevent fatigue
- **ELD (Electronic Logging Device)**: Device for automatically recording HOS compliance
- **Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse**: FMCSA database tracking CDL driver violations
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)**: Federal law requiring accessibility accommodations
- **Critical Safety Defect**: Vehicle condition requiring immediate repair before operation
- **Out of Service**: Vehicle status when critical defects prevent safe operation
- **Medical Examiner's Certificate**: DOT physical examination required for CDL drivers
- **Air Brake System**: Compressed air braking system requiring CDL air brake endorsement
- **Defensive Driving**: Proactive driving techniques to prevent accidents

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **49 CFR Part 383**: Commercial Driver's License Standards (FMCSA)
- **49 CFR Part 391**: Qualifications of Drivers (medical certification, drug/alcohol testing)
- **49 CFR Part 382**: Controlled Substances and Alcohol Use and Testing
- **49 CFR Part 396**: Inspection, Repair, and Maintenance (DVIR requirements)
- **49 CFR Part 392**: Driving of Commercial Motor Vehicles (operational safety rules)
- **49 CFR Part 395**: Hours of Service of Drivers (HOS limits)
- **ADA Title II & III (1990, amended 2010)**: Public transportation accessibility requirements
- **FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse (2020, updated 2024)**: Driver violation tracking
- **NHTSA FMVSS Standards**: Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards for bus construction

Common Tools:
- ELD devices: Omnitracs, KeepTruckin, Samsara, Garmin eLog
- Inspection tools: Tire pressure gauge, tread depth gauge, flashlight, inspection forms
- Communication: Two-way radio, mobile data terminal (MDT), dispatch software
- Accessibility equipment: Wheelchair lifts (Braun, Ricon), Q'Straint securement systems
- Safety equipment: Fire extinguisher (BC or ABC rated), first aid kit, warning triangles
