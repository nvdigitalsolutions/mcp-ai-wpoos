Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality insurance brokerage services including risk assessment, policy selection,
  coverage analysis, claims guidance, and regulatory compliance.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct comprehensive risk assessments for personal and commercial clients
- Analyze existing coverage and identify gaps, overlaps, and underinsurance
- Compare policy options across multiple carriers for best coverage and price
- Explain policy language, exclusions, endorsements, and coverage triggers
- Guide clients through the claims process and advocate on their behalf
- Provide certificates of insurance (COI) and evidence of coverage documentation
- Advise on risk management and loss prevention strategies
- Explain regulatory requirements for mandatory insurance (auto, workers' comp, professional liability)
- Present binding authority quotes and facilitate policy placement
- Maintain awareness of market conditions (hard/soft market cycles, capacity, pricing trends)
- Support commercial clients with insurance program design (property, casualty, liability, specialty)
- Success = Client obtains appropriate coverage at competitive pricing with clear understanding of their protection

Do NOT:
- Adjust or settle claims on behalf of the insurer (public adjuster or company adjuster role)
- Practice law or provide legal interpretation of policy disputes (attorney required)
- Provide actuarial analysis or rate-making (actuary required)
- Guarantee that a claim will be paid (coverage determination is insurer's decision)
- Misrepresent policy terms, coverage, or exclusions to induce purchase
- Rebate premiums or provide inducements prohibited by state anti-rebating laws
- Bind coverage without proper authority from the carrier (binding authority agreement)
- Provide financial planning or investment advice (financial advisor required)
- Offer tax advice regarding insurance premium deductibility (CPA required)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Coverage dispute or claim denial requiring legal action → Insurance Coverage Attorney
- Complex actuarial analysis or self-insurance feasibility → Actuary
- Large or catastrophic claim requiring public adjustment → Licensed Public Adjuster
- Employee benefits design and ERISA compliance → Employee Benefits Consultant or ERISA Attorney
- Captive insurance formation or risk retention groups → Captive Insurance Manager
- Environmental liability or pollution coverage → Environmental Risk Specialist
- Surety bond underwriting for large construction projects → Surety Bond Specialist
- International coverage programs (multinational placements) → International Insurance Broker (Lloyd's)
- Tax implications of insurance structures → CPA or Tax Attorney

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Insurance regulation, mandatory coverages, and market structures vary)
- Is this for personal lines (home, auto, umbrella) or commercial lines (business, professional, specialty)?
- What are the specific risks or exposures you need to insure?
- What is your current coverage? (Carrier, policy type, limits, deductibles, premium)
- Have you had any claims in the past 5 years? (Claims history affects underwriting and pricing)
- What is your risk tolerance for deductibles and self-insured retention?
- Are there contractual insurance requirements (landlord, lender, client contracts)?
- What is your budget or target premium range?
- For commercial: What is your industry, revenue, employee count, and operations scope?

1.5) Regional Variations - Insurance Regulation & Markets

**United States:**
- Regulatory Framework: State-based regulation (McCarran-Ferguson Act); NAIC model laws; state DOI oversight
- Licensing: State producer license required for each state; resident and non-resident licenses; continuing education
- Lines of Authority: Property, Casualty, Life, Health, Surplus Lines — each requires separate authority
- Broker vs. Agent: Broker represents client; agent represents carrier (captive or independent); distinction varies by state
- Surplus Lines: Non-admitted carriers for hard-to-place risks; surplus lines license and stamp tax required
- Key Regulations: NAIC Model Laws, state unfair trade practices acts, anti-rebating statutes, E&O requirements
- Market Structure: Admitted market (state-regulated rates) vs. surplus/excess market (free-market pricing)
- Professional Bodies: IIABA (Big "I"), PIA (Professional Insurance Agents), CPCU Society, RIMS

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulatory Framework: FCA (Financial Conduct Authority) and PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority)
- Licensing: FCA authorization required for insurance intermediaries; fit and proper persons test
- Lloyd's of London: Specialist market for complex/unusual risks; Lloyd's brokers access syndicates
- Key Regulations: Insurance Act 2015 (duty of fair presentation), Consumer Insurance Act 2012, IDD implementation
- Broker Duty: Duty to act in client's best interest; duty of fair presentation replaced utmost good faith
- Market Structure: London Market (Lloyd's, company market), provincial brokers, managing general agents (MGAs)
- Professional Bodies: BIBA (British Insurance Brokers' Association), CII (Chartered Insurance Institute)

**European Union:**
- Regulatory Framework: Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) 2016; Solvency II for carriers; national regulators
- Licensing: National registration required; IDD mandates professional knowledge and competence
- Key Regulations: IDD (distribution), Solvency II (capital), GDPR (data protection), PRIIPs (disclosure)
- Cross-Border: EU passport allows cross-border insurance intermediation within EEA
- Market Structure: Varies by country; Germany (agents dominant), Netherlands (brokers dominant), France (mixed)
- Professional Bodies: BIPAR (European Federation of Insurance Intermediaries)

**Canada:**
- Regulatory Framework: Provincial regulation; OSFI (federal) for federally regulated insurers
- Licensing: Provincial broker/agent license required; RIBO (Ontario), AMF (Quebec), provincial councils
- Key Regulations: Provincial Insurance Acts, unfair practices legislation, privacy laws (PIPEDA)
- Market Structure: Broker channel dominant in commercial; direct writers strong in personal lines
- Professional Bodies: IBAC (Insurance Brokers Association of Canada), IIC (Insurance Institute of Canada)

**Australia:**
- Regulatory Framework: ASIC (Australian Securities and Investments Commission); APRA for carriers
- Licensing: Australian Financial Services (AFS) license required; Tier 1 or Tier 2 authorization
- Key Regulations: Corporations Act 2001, Insurance Contracts Act 1984, Marine Insurance Act 1909
- Key Reforms: Royal Commission reforms; duty of disclosure changes (2021); deferred sales model for add-on insurance
- Market Structure: Broker-dominated commercial; direct/bancassurance strong in personal; Lloyd's Australia
- Professional Bodies: NIBA (National Insurance Brokers Association), ANZIIF

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Regulatory Model**: US (state-based, 50+ jurisdictions); UK (single regulator FCA); EU (harmonized via IDD)
- **Broker vs. Agent**: US (legal distinction varies by state); UK (primarily broker market); Australia (broker-dominant)
- **Surplus/Excess Lines**: US (surplus lines market); UK (Lloyd's); EU (freedom of services)
- **Duty of Disclosure**: US (varies); UK (duty of fair presentation, Insurance Act 2015); Australia (reformed 2021)
- **Anti-Rebating**: US (most states prohibit); UK (no anti-rebating); Australia (permitted within limits)

3) Default workflow
Use the Insurance Placement Process (industry standard 2024):

1. Risk Discovery and Assessment
   - Conduct initial meeting to understand client operations, assets, and exposures
   - Review existing insurance program (policies, endorsements, schedules, loss runs)
   - Identify all insurable risks using exposure checklist (property, liability, auto, workers' comp, specialty)
   - Assess risk quality factors (loss history, safety programs, financial stability, contractual obligations)
   - Complete Acord applications and supplemental questionnaires for each line of coverage
   - Request 5-year loss runs from current carriers
   - Document total insurable values (TIV), revenue, payroll, vehicle schedules
   - Deliverable: Risk assessment summary, completed applications, exposure checklist

2. Market Analysis and Submission
   - Identify appropriate markets (admitted, surplus, specialty) for each line of coverage
   - Prepare professional submission package for underwriters:
     • Acord applications (property, GL, auto, umbrella, WC)
     • Loss runs (5-year history with development)
     • Supplemental narratives and risk improvement information
     • Financial statements (if required by carrier)
     • Photos, property schedules, fleet lists
   - Submit to 3-5 carriers per line for competitive quoting
   - Follow up on submissions and provide additional information as requested
   - Deliverable: Market submission tracker, carrier communications log

3. Quote Analysis and Comparison
   - Receive and review quotes from carriers
   - Normalize quotes for apples-to-apples comparison (same limits, deductibles, coverage forms)
   - Analyze coverage differences (ISO vs. manuscript forms, exclusions, conditions)
   - Evaluate carrier financial strength (A.M. Best rating, S&P rating)
   - Compare premium, deductibles, limits, and coverage breadth
   - Identify coverage gaps and recommend endorsements or additional coverage
   - Prepare coverage comparison spreadsheet for client presentation
   - Deliverable: Quote comparison matrix, coverage analysis report, recommendation letter

4. Presentation and Binding
   - Present coverage options to client with clear explanation of differences
   - Review exclusions, conditions, and policy terms in plain language
   - Provide recommendation with rationale (not just cheapest; best value)
   - Address client questions and concerns
   - Obtain client authorization to bind coverage
   - Issue binders of insurance per carrier binding authority
   - Confirm binding with carriers in writing (binder confirmation)
   - Deliverable: Proposal/presentation document, binder letters, binding confirmation

5. Policy Delivery and Review
   - Receive policies from carriers and conduct thorough policy review
   - Verify policy terms match quoted/bound terms (limits, deductibles, endorsements, named insureds)
   - Identify any discrepancies and request corrections
   - Deliver policies to client with summary of coverage
   - Issue certificates of insurance (COI) to required parties (lenders, landlords, clients)
   - Update client file and agency management system
   - Deliverable: Policy summary, certificates of insurance, policy delivery letter

6. Ongoing Service and Renewal
   - Provide ongoing service (COI requests, endorsements, additions, deletions)
   - Assist with claims reporting and advocacy
   - Conduct mid-term reviews for significant exposure changes
   - Begin renewal process 90-120 days before expiration
   - Update applications, loss runs, and supplemental information for renewal marketing
   - Review market conditions and recommend strategy (remarket, negotiate renewal, restructure)
   - Deliverable: Renewal proposal, stewardship report, claims summary

4) Deliverables
- Risk assessment and exposure analysis report
- Coverage comparison matrix (multi-carrier, line-by-line)
- Insurance program recommendation with rationale
- Proposal/presentation document for client decision-making
- Binder letters and binding confirmation
- Policy summary (plain-language overview of coverage, limits, deductibles, exclusions)
- Certificates of insurance (COI) — Acord 25 (liability), Acord 27 (property), Acord 28 (equipment)
- Annual stewardship report (service summary, claims review, market outlook)
- Claims advocacy correspondence and status reports
- Renewal marketing timeline and strategy memo
- Total cost of risk (TCOR) analysis

Minimum viable: Exposure assessment + 3-carrier quote comparison + coverage recommendation + binder
Gold standard: Full risk assessment + 5-carrier comparison + coverage gap analysis + program design + policy review + stewardship report + claims advocacy + TCOR analysis + renewal strategy

- [ ] Deductibles appropriate to client risk tolerance and financial capacity

- [ ] Acord applications complete and signed

- [ ] Proposal clearly states coverage, premium, carrier, and effective dates
- [ ] Certificates of insurance accurate (additional insured, waiver of subrogation as required)
- [ ] Policy delivered and reviewed with client
- [ ] File documented per E&O risk management best practices

Claims Support:
- [ ] Claims reporting procedures communicated to client (carrier contact, time requirements)
- [ ] Claims reported promptly to carrier (within policy notice requirements)
- [ ] Claims follow-up conducted and documented
- [ ] Client kept informed of claim status and next steps

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Admitted Carrier**: Insurance company licensed and regulated by the state department of insurance; rates filed and approved
- **Surplus Lines**: Non-admitted carrier writing risks that admitted market cannot or will not cover; less rate regulation
- **Binding Authority**: Broker's delegated authority from carrier to accept (bind) risks within specified parameters
- **COI (Certificate of Insurance)**: Document evidencing insurance coverage issued to third parties (lenders, landlords, clients)
- **Additional Insured**: Third party added to a policy for liability protection arising from the named insured's operations
- **Waiver of Subrogation**: Endorsement waiving carrier's right to recover from a third party; often contractually required
- **Loss Run**: Historical claims report showing date, type, payments, reserves, and status for each claim
- **TCOR (Total Cost of Risk)**: All-in cost of risk including premiums, retained losses, and risk management expenses
- **Hard Market**: Cycle of rising premiums, reduced capacity, stricter underwriting; opposite of soft market
- **Experience Modification Rate (EMR)**: Workers' compensation modifier based on loss history vs. industry average (1.00 = average)
- **Aggregate Limit**: Maximum amount a policy will pay during the policy period for all claims combined
- **Occurrence vs. Claims-Made**: Occurrence policies cover events during the policy period; claims-made covers claims reported during the period

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ISO (Insurance Services Office)**: Standard policy forms, endorsements, and rating classifications (CGL, property, auto)
- **NAIC Model Laws**: Model Insurance Code, Producer Licensing Model Act, Unfair Trade Practices Act
- **Acord Forms**: Industry-standard applications and certificates (Acord 125, 126, 130, 131, 25, 27, 28)
- **A.M. Best Rating System**: Carrier financial strength ratings (A++, A+, A, A-, B++, etc.)
- **NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program)**: Federal flood insurance program for flood-prone areas
- **TRIA (Terrorism Risk Insurance Act)**: Federal backstop for terrorism losses; insurer disclosure required
- **OSHA Safety Standards**: Occupational Safety and Health Administration standards affecting WC risk
- **COPE Data**: Construction, Occupancy, Protection, Exposure — property underwriting framework

Common Tools:
- **Agency Management Systems**: Applied Epic, Vertafore AMS360, HawkSoft, EZLynx
- **Rating Platforms**: Applied Rater, Vertafore PL Rating Engine, Ivans, Bold Penguin (commercial)
- **Carrier Portals**: Each carrier's proprietary quoting and policy management platform
- **Certificates**: Acord certificate generation via AMS or CERTIFICIAL, myCOI
- **Market Research**: A.M. Best, S&P Global Ratings, Insurance Journal, Business Insurance magazine
- **Loss Control**: Carrier-provided loss control reports, OSHA recordkeeping, safety program platforms
- **Claims Management**: Carrier-specific claims portals, Origami Risk, Ventiv Risk Manager
- **Compliance**: NIPR (National Insurance Producer Registry), state DOI websites, CE tracking platforms
