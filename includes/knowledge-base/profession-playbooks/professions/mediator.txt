Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality mediation and dispute resolution assistance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize clear legal context, risk identification, and procedural compliance.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on mediation processes, facilitative and evaluative techniques, and ADR frameworks
- Explain pre-mediation preparation, session management, and impasse-breaking strategies
- Assist with drafting mediation agreements, settlement term sheets, and memoranda of understanding
- Advise on mediator ethics, impartiality standards, and confidentiality obligations
- Guide on court-ordered mediation compliance and ADR program administration
- Help design conflict management systems and organizational dispute resolution policies
- Explain specialized mediation contexts (family, commercial, employment, construction, international)
- Success = Durable, voluntary agreement reached by informed parties with preserved relationships

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice to either party (mediator is a neutral facilitator, not an advocate)
- Make binding decisions or impose outcomes (that is arbitration or adjudication)
- Coerce, pressure, or manipulate parties toward a specific settlement
- Draft final legal documents without parties obtaining independent legal review
- Continue mediation when there is evidence of domestic violence, duress, or incapacity without safeguards
- Disclose confidential mediation communications without consent or legal exception
- Serve as mediator when a conflict of interest exists

Escalate / refer out when:
- Parties need independent legal advice on rights and obligations (refer to respective counsel)
- Power imbalance or safety concerns cannot be addressed in mediation (refer to court or protective services)
- Issues of fraud, criminal conduct, or child endangerment arise (mandatory reporting obligations)
- Complex tax, valuation, or forensic accounting issues require expert input (engage specialists)
- Parties request binding arbitration or adjudication (refer to arbitrator or court)
- Mental health or substance abuse issues impair a party's capacity (refer to appropriate professionals)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the nature of the dispute (commercial, employment, family, construction, personal injury, community)?
- How many parties are involved, and are they represented by counsel?
- Is this mediation court-ordered, contractually required, or voluntary?
- What is the jurisdiction and applicable ADR rules or program requirements?
- Have the parties attempted prior negotiation or other dispute resolution methods?
- What are the key issues in dispute (monetary, relational, contractual, custody)?
- Are there time constraints, pending litigation deadlines, or statute of limitations concerns?
- What is the emotional temperature — are parties willing to engage in good faith?

1.5) Regional Variations - Mediation Standards & Regulations

**United States — Federal:**
- Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of 1998 (28 U.S.C. §§ 651-658) — requires federal courts to authorize ADR
- Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) — labor-management disputes
- Uniform Mediation Act (UMA) — adopted by 12+ states; governs privilege and confidentiality
- Federal court local rules — each district has specific ADR/mediation procedures
- EEOC Mediation Program — employment discrimination mediation

**United States — State Level:**
- State mediation confidentiality statutes vary significantly
- Court-connected mediation programs with specific qualification standards
- Mandatory mediation in some states for certain case types (family, foreclosure, construction)
- Mediator certification/registration requirements (e.g., Florida Rule 10, Texas ADR Act)
- Community mediation centers — often volunteer mediators with basic training requirements
- State ethical standards (e.g., Florida Rules for Certified and Court-Appointed Mediators)

**European Union:**
- EU Mediation Directive 2008/52/EC — cross-border mediation in civil and commercial matters
- National implementation varies (some mandate mediation before litigation)
- Italy: mandatory mediation for certain civil/commercial disputes (Legislative Decree 28/2010)
- Germany: Mediation Act (MediationsG, 2012) — defines mediation, confidentiality, and mediator duties
- France: court-ordered mediation provisions in Code of Civil Procedure

**United Kingdom:**
- Civil Procedure Rules (CPR) — courts can stay proceedings for ADR
- Pre-Action Protocols encourage ADR before litigation
- CEDR (Centre for Effective Dispute Resolution) — leading mediation provider and training body
- Civil Mediation Council (CMC) — sets registration and practice standards
- Halsey v. Milton Keynes (2004) — landmark case on unreasonable refusal to mediate and cost consequences
- Churchill v. Merthyr Tydfil (2023) — courts can compel ADR participation

**Canada:**
- Provincial mediation statutes (e.g., Ontario Mandatory Mediation Program, Rule 24.1)
- ADR Institute of Canada — national standards and mediator designations (C.Med, Q.Med)
- Roster-based court mediation programs in most provinces
- Bilingual mediation capacity in federal and Quebec proceedings

**Australia:**
- National Mediator Accreditation System (NMAS) — voluntary national standards
- Federal Court of Australia ADR programs
- State/territory mediation legislation (e.g., Civil Dispute Resolution Act 2011)
- Family Dispute Resolution (FDR) — mandatory before family court in most cases
- LEADR/IAMA (now Resolution Institute) — professional body

**International:**
- Singapore Convention on Mediation (2019) — enforcement of international mediated settlements
- UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Mediation (2018)
- ICC Mediation Rules (International Chamber of Commerce)
- ICSID Conciliation Rules — investor-state disputes
- IMI (International Mediation Institute) — global certification and standards

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Case Intake & Conflict Check
   - Review referral source and case information
   - Conduct conflict of interest check against all parties and counsel
   - Assess suitability for mediation (power balance, safety, voluntariness)
   - Confirm authority to settle for all participating parties
   - Determine mediation model (facilitative, evaluative, transformative, or hybrid)

2. Pre-Mediation Preparation
   - Execute Agreement to Mediate with all parties (confidentiality, fees, ground rules)
   - Request and review mediation briefs or position statements from each party
   - Conduct pre-mediation calls with each party (and counsel) separately
   - Identify core interests, underlying needs, and potential settlement zones
   - Prepare room logistics (joint session space, separate caucus rooms) or virtual platform
   - Develop session agenda and opening statement framework

3. Mediation Session — Opening Phase
   - Welcome parties, introduce the process, and review ground rules
   - Confirm confidentiality, voluntariness, and mediator neutrality
   - Each party presents opening statement (uninterrupted)
   - Mediator summarizes key issues and creates shared agenda
   - Establish communication norms (respect, good faith, no personal attacks)

4. Mediation Session — Exploration & Negotiation
   - Use joint sessions for information exchange and issue clarification
   - Conduct private caucuses to explore interests, priorities, and flexibility
   - Apply interest-based negotiation to identify mutual gains
   - Reality-test positions by exploring BATNA, WATNA, and litigation risks
   - Use reframing, active listening, and summarization to manage emotions
   - Generate and evaluate settlement options collaboratively
   - Address impasses using bracketing, conditional proposals, or mediator proposals

5. Agreement & Closure
   - Draft settlement terms or memorandum of understanding
   - Review all terms with parties (and counsel) for clarity and completeness
   - Confirm voluntary agreement and informed consent from each party
   - Recommend independent legal review before final execution
   - Execute settlement agreement or term sheet
   - Discuss implementation timeline, monitoring, and contingency provisions
   - Close session with acknowledgment of parties' effort and cooperation

6. Post-Mediation Follow-Up
   - Provide signed agreement copies to all parties and counsel
   - Follow up on compliance with settlement terms (if agreed in scope)
   - Address any post-mediation clarification requests
   - Complete case file and billing
   - Maintain confidential records per retention policy
   - Collect party feedback for quality improvement

4) Deliverables
- Agreement to Mediate (pre-signed by all parties)
- Mediation briefs / position statements (collected from parties)
- Mediator's session agenda and issue framework
- Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) or term sheet
- Final settlement agreement (for counsel to formalize)
- Mediator's closing summary (non-substantive, process only)
- Compliance monitoring reports (if retained post-mediation)
- Statistical reports for court-connected programs (case outcome, hours, satisfaction)
- Post-mediation evaluation forms

Minimum viable: Agreement to Mediate + session facilitation + signed MOU or term sheet
Gold standard: Full pre-mediation preparation + skilled facilitation + detailed settlement agreement + compliance follow-up + satisfaction surveys

5) Quality checklist
Process Integrity:
- [ ] Conflict of interest check completed and documented
- [ ] Agreement to Mediate executed before substantive discussions
- [ ] Confidentiality obligations explained and acknowledged
- [ ] Voluntariness confirmed — no party coerced into agreement
- [ ] Impartiality maintained throughout; no favoritism or bias exhibited
- [ ] Self-determination honored — parties made their own decisions
- [ ] Power imbalances identified and addressed appropriately

Preparation:
- [ ] Pre-mediation briefs reviewed from all parties
- [ ] Pre-mediation calls conducted with each party/counsel
- [ ] Settlement authority confirmed for all participants
- [ ] Special needs addressed (interpreter, accessibility, safety protocols)
- [ ] Relevant legal framework and precedent understood by mediator

Session Management:
- [ ] Ground rules established and enforced
- [ ] All parties had adequate opportunity to be heard
- [ ] Issues identified and prioritized effectively
- [ ] Interests explored beyond stated positions
- [ ] Impasse strategies employed when needed
- [ ] Emotional dynamics managed professionally
- [ ] Time managed effectively; breaks offered as needed

Settlement Quality:
- [ ] Terms are clear, specific, and enforceable
- [ ] All disputed issues addressed or expressly reserved
- [ ] Implementation timeline and responsibilities defined
- [ ] Contingency provisions included for foreseeable complications
- [ ] Parties advised to obtain independent legal review
- [ ] Agreement signed voluntarily with informed consent
- [ ] Copies distributed to all parties and counsel

Ethics & Compliance:
- [ ] Mediator ethics code followed (Model Standards, UMA, state rules)
- [ ] Confidentiality maintained; no unauthorized disclosures
- [ ] Fees disclosed and agreed upon in advance
- [ ] No unauthorized practice of law
- [ ] Mandatory reporting obligations fulfilled if triggered
- [ ] Court reporting requirements met (if court-ordered mediation)

6) Templates (paste-ready)

=== Agreement to Mediate ===
MEDIATION AGREEMENT

Case/Matter: [Description or Case Number]
Date: [MM/DD/YYYY]
Mediator: [Name, Credentials]

Parties:
1. [Party A Name] represented by [Counsel Name, Firm] (or pro se)
2. [Party B Name] represented by [Counsel Name, Firm] (or pro se)

The undersigned parties agree to the following terms for mediation:

1. Process: The mediator will facilitate negotiations but will not
   render a binding decision. Any settlement must be voluntarily
   agreed upon by all parties.

2. Confidentiality: All statements, documents, and communications
   made during mediation are confidential and inadmissible in any
   subsequent proceeding, except as required by law or to enforce
   a written settlement agreement.

3. Mediator Neutrality: The mediator is an impartial facilitator
   and does not represent or advocate for any party.

4. Good Faith: Parties agree to participate in good faith and with
   authority to settle.

5. Fees: Mediator's fee is $[Amount]/hour, split equally unless
   otherwise agreed. Cancellation requires [48/72] hours notice.

6. Termination: Any party or the mediator may terminate the
   mediation at any time.

Signatures:
Party A: _________________________ Date: __________
Party B: _________________________ Date: __________
Mediator: ________________________ Date: __________

=== Mediation Settlement Term Sheet ===
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING

Case/Matter: [Description or Case Number]
Mediation Date: [MM/DD/YYYY]
Mediator: [Name]

The parties have reached the following agreement through mediation:

1. Payment Terms:
   [Party A/B] shall pay [Party B/A] the sum of $[Amount] as follows:
   - $[Amount] within [X] days of execution
   - $[Amount] in [monthly/quarterly] installments of $[Amount] beginning [Date]

2. Non-Monetary Terms:
   [Describe specific actions, obligations, or commitments]

3. Release:
   Upon full performance, the parties shall exchange mutual releases
   of all claims arising from [describe scope of dispute].

4. Confidentiality:
   The terms of this agreement are confidential and shall not be
   disclosed except as necessary for enforcement or as required by law.

5. Enforcement:
   This agreement may be enforced as a contract. If litigation was
   pending, the parties shall [file stipulation of dismissal / submit
   consent order] within [X] days.

6. Contingency:
   If [Party] fails to perform, [describe remedy or consequence].

7. Legal Review:
   Each party has been advised to review this term sheet with
   independent legal counsel before execution.

This memorandum reflects the parties' agreement and is binding
upon execution.

Party A: _________________________ Date: __________
Party B: _________________________ Date: __________
Counsel A: _______________________ Date: __________
Counsel B: _______________________ Date: __________

=== Mediator Opening Statement Script ===
Good [morning/afternoon]. My name is [Name], and I will be serving as
your mediator today. Thank you all for being here.

Let me briefly explain the mediation process:

1. My role is to help you communicate and explore options for resolving
   this dispute. I am not a judge. I will not decide who is right or
   wrong, and I will not impose a solution.

2. Everything said in mediation is confidential. I cannot be called as
   a witness, and the discussions here cannot be used in court. The
   exception is any written settlement agreement you sign today.

3. I may meet with each of you privately in what we call a caucus.
   Anything you tell me in caucus is confidential unless you give me
   permission to share it.

4. This process is voluntary. You are free to leave at any time.
   However, I encourage you to give this process a genuine effort.

5. Ground rules: Please let each person speak without interruption.
   Treat each other with respect. Focus on solving the problem.

We'll start with each party giving a brief opening statement describing
the situation from your perspective. [Party A], would you like to begin?

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Pre-Mediation Preparation Memo
```
CONFIDENTIAL — MEDIATOR'S PREPARATION NOTES

Case: Riverdale LLC v. Summit Construction, Inc.
Case No: 2024-CV-03456
Mediation Date: February 28, 2025
Mediator: [Name], IMI Certified

Dispute Summary:
Commercial construction dispute. Riverdale (owner) claims $1.2M in
damages for defective HVAC installation and 4-month project delay.
Summit (contractor) counterclaims $380K for unpaid change orders and
alleges owner-caused delays.

Key Issues:
1. HVAC system defects — competing expert reports ($800K vs. $200K repair cost)
2. Project delay damages — liquidated damages clause vs. force majeure defense
3. Unpaid change orders — documentation dispute on 6 change orders
4. Warranty obligations — scope and duration under contract Section 12.3
5. Attorney fees — prevailing party clause in contract

Pre-Mediation Intelligence:
- Riverdale: Highly motivated to settle; building is operational but HVAC
  underperforming. CFO has $750K settlement authority (per counsel call).
- Summit: Concerned about reputation; has pending bids requiring clean
  litigation record. Willing to perform remedial work in lieu of cash.

BATNA/WATNA Analysis:
- Riverdale BATNA: Trial in 14 months; estimated legal costs $200K+
- Summit BATNA: Trial; risk of $1.2M judgment plus attorney fees
- Both parties' WATNA: Adverse judgment, appellate costs, 2+ year timeline

Potential Settlement Zone: $400K - $700K (cash + remedial work combination)

Strategy:
- Start in joint session to humanize the dispute
- Separate into caucuses for reality-testing
- Explore hybrid solution (partial cash + remedial HVAC work by Summit)
- Use bracketing if parties stall on numbers
- Keep focus on business relationship and future opportunities
```

Example 2: Impasse-Breaking Strategy Memo
```
MEDIATOR'S IMPASSE ANALYSIS — CAUCUS NOTES

Situation: Parties have been at $600K (claimant) and $250K (respondent)
for two rounds. Emotional tension high after respondent challenged
claimant's expert credibility.

Impasse-Breaking Strategies (in sequence):
1. Reframe: Shift from positional bargaining to interest-based.
   Ask each party: "What outcome would let you move forward?"

2. Risk Analysis: Walk each party through trial cost-benefit:
   - Legal fees remaining: $150K-$200K each
   - Trial duration: 5-7 days
   - Outcome uncertainty: 40-60% on liability for each side
   - Expected value calculation for each party

3. Bracket Proposal: Suggest conditional bracket.
   "If claimant would come to $500K, would respondent come to $350K?
    We're not committing — just testing whether a zone exists."

4. Non-Monetary Elements: Explore value beyond cash:
   - Extended payment terms (reduce cash flow impact)
   - Remedial work (Summit performs HVAC repairs at cost)
   - Mutual non-disparagement (protects both reputations)
   - Future contract consideration

5. Mediator's Proposal (last resort): If bracket fails, offer
   confidential mediator's proposal at $425K. Each party
   independently accepts or rejects. If both accept, deal is done.
   If either rejects, no one knows the other's answer.

Target: Resume joint session by 2:30 PM with renewed momentum.
```

Example 3: Post-Mediation Settlement Summary
```
CASE CLOSURE SUMMARY

Case: Riverdale LLC v. Summit Construction, Inc.
Mediation Date: February 28, 2025
Result: FULL SETTLEMENT REACHED

Settlement Terms (High-Level):
- Summit to pay Riverdale $325,000 in three installments over 6 months
- Summit to perform HVAC remedial work (valued at approx. $150K) within 90 days
- Summit provides extended 3-year warranty on remedial HVAC work
- Mutual release of all claims and counterclaims
- Mutual non-disparagement clause
- Confidentiality of settlement terms
- Stipulation of dismissal with prejudice to be filed within 14 days

Total Settlement Value: Approximately $475,000

Process Metrics:
- Duration: 8.5 hours (9:00 AM - 5:30 PM)
- Caucus sessions: 6 (3 per party)
- Key turning point: Respondent's willingness to perform remedial work
  reduced cash component to acceptable range
- Impasse at 2:00 PM resolved through bracket proposal technique

Follow-Up:
- Formal settlement agreement to be drafted by counsel within 10 days
- Mediator available for clarification questions for 30 days
- Compliance check scheduled at 90-day mark (HVAC work completion)

Party Satisfaction:
- Both parties expressed satisfaction with outcome
- Feedback forms collected; ratings: Process 4.5/5, Mediator 5/5
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ADR**: Alternative Dispute Resolution — umbrella term for mediation, arbitration, and other non-litigation methods
- **Mediation**: Facilitated negotiation where a neutral third party helps disputants reach voluntary agreement
- **Arbitration**: Process where a neutral renders a binding (or non-binding) decision
- **Facilitative Mediation**: Mediator guides process without evaluating merits or suggesting outcomes
- **Evaluative Mediation**: Mediator provides assessment of case strengths/weaknesses and likely outcomes
- **Transformative Mediation**: Focuses on empowerment and recognition; aims to transform party interaction
- **Caucus**: Private meeting between mediator and one party; confidential by default
- **Joint Session**: Meeting with all parties present
- **BATNA**: Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement — strongest option if mediation fails
- **WATNA**: Worst Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement — worst-case outcome without settlement
- **ZOPA**: Zone of Possible Agreement — overlap between parties' acceptable ranges
- **MOU**: Memorandum of Understanding — preliminary settlement document
- **Impasse**: Point where parties cannot move closer to agreement
- **Bracketing**: Negotiation technique using conditional "what if" ranges to narrow gaps
- **Mediator's Proposal**: Confidential settlement figure proposed by mediator; parties accept/reject independently
- **Self-Determination**: Core mediation principle that parties control the outcome
- **Shuttle Diplomacy**: Mediator moves between parties who remain in separate rooms
- **Med-Arb**: Hybrid process where unresolved issues after mediation proceed to binding arbitration

Standards & Ethics:
- **Model Standards of Conduct for Mediators (ABA/AAA/ACR)**: Primary U.S. ethics framework
- **Uniform Mediation Act (UMA)**: Model statute governing mediation privilege and confidentiality
- **EU Mediation Directive 2008/52/EC**: EU cross-border mediation framework
- **Singapore Convention on Mediation (2019)**: International enforcement of mediated settlement agreements
- **UNCITRAL Model Law on Mediation (2018)**: International commercial mediation framework
- **IMI Competency Standards**: International Mediation Institute certification criteria
- **Florida Rules for Certified and Court-Appointed Mediators**: Model state regulation
- **CPR Institute Mediation Procedure**: International Institute for Conflict Prevention & Resolution rules

Common Tools & Resources:
- **AAA (American Arbitration Association)**: Major ADR provider with mediation panels
- **JAMS**: Leading private ADR provider (mediation, arbitration)
- **CPR International**: ADR provider for complex commercial disputes
- **CEDR**: Centre for Effective Dispute Resolution (UK-based, global reach)
- **Resolution Institute**: Australia/NZ ADR professional body
- **ICC ADR/Mediation Rules**: International Chamber of Commerce procedures
- **ODR (Online Dispute Resolution)**: Digital platforms for remote mediation
- **Modria / Tyler Technologies**: ODR technology platforms
- **Zoom / WebEx / Teams**: Video conferencing for virtual mediation sessions
