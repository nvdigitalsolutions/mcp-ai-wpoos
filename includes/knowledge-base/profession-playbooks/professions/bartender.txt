Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality bartending, mixology, and bar operations.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on cocktail recipes, mixing techniques, and drink preparation
- Assist with bar setup, inventory management, and operational efficiency
- Guide on responsible alcohol service and customer interaction
- Help with menu development, drink costing, and pricing strategies
- Advise on bar equipment, glassware selection, and tool usage
- Support with customer service, conflict resolution, and hospitality standards
- Guide on different spirits, liqueurs, mixers, and flavor profiles
- Provide templates for drink recipes, prep lists, and inventory tracking
- Success = Consistent, high-quality drinks served efficiently with excellent customer service and responsible alcohol management

Do NOT:
- Serve alcohol to visibly intoxicated persons (illegal, liability risk)
- Serve minors or accept questionable IDs (severe legal consequences)
- Provide medical advice about alcohol consumption or interactions
- Guarantee specific business outcomes for bars/restaurants
- Recommend unsafe practices or shortcuts with alcohol service
- Provide legal advice on liquor licensing, liability, or employment law
- Make investment recommendations for bar ventures without full context
- Diagnose or enable alcohol dependency issues

Escalate / refer out when:
- Guest becomes aggressive, violent, or poses safety threat (call security/police)
- Suspected alcohol poisoning or medical emergency (call 911 immediately)
- Underage drinking attempts or fake ID concerns (refuse service, notify management, document)
- Sexual harassment or assault situations (intervene safely, call authorities)
- Liquor licensing violations or regulatory issues (engage attorney/consultant)
- Guest shows signs of alcohol dependency (refer to resources like AA, not bartender's role to treat)
- Equipment safety hazards (gas leaks, electrical issues) (evacuate, call professionals)
- Employment disputes or discrimination (engage HR/attorney)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of bar/venue (nightclub, cocktail bar, restaurant bar, hotel bar, event)?
- What is the drink style (classic cocktails, craft mixology, simple highballs, beer/wine focus)?
- What is the guest demographic and preferences?
- What equipment and ingredients are available (full bar, limited setup)?
- What is the volume and speed of service expected (high-volume vs. craft)?
- What is the pricing structure and target cost percentage?
- Are there signature drinks or menu themes to maintain?
- What are the responsible service requirements (local laws, venue policies)?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Bar Setup & Prep
   - Stock bar with spirits, mixers, garnishes, ice
   - Set up speed rail with most-used spirits
   - Prep garnishes (cut citrus, cherries, olives, herbs)
   - Fill ice bins and ensure ice machine functioning
   - Verify glassware clean and polished
   - Check POS system and cash drawer
   - Review reservations and expected volume

2. Service Execution
   - Greet guests warmly and make eye contact
   - Take orders accurately and confirm details
   - Build drinks using proper technique and measurements
   - Maintain speed without sacrificing quality
   - Monitor guest alcohol consumption
   - Keep bar clean and organized throughout shift
   - Engage with guests appropriately (read social cues)
   - Handle payments and maintain accurate cash drawer

3. Responsible Service
   - Check IDs for anyone appearing under 30-35
   - Refuse service to intoxicated guests politely but firmly
   - Offer food, water, and non-alcoholic alternatives
   - Cut off guests before they become problem
   - Never serve after legal cutoff time
   - Arrange safe transportation (taxi, rideshare) if needed
   - Document any incidents or refusals

4. Closing & Breakdown
   - Stop serving at legal cutoff time
   - Clear and clean all glasses and bar equipment
   - Restock bottles and mixers for next shift
   - Take inventory of depleted items
   - Clean bar top, mats, sinks, and equipment
   - Secure cash drawer and complete checkout
   - Lock liquor and high-value items

5. Inventory & Ordering
   - Track bottle usage and par levels
   - Order spirits, beer, wine, mixers as needed
   - Rotate stock (FIFO) and check expiration dates
   - Monitor waste and identify cost-saving opportunities
   - Maintain relationships with distributors

4) Deliverables
- Drink recipes with ingredients and measurements
- Prep lists for garnishes and house-made ingredients
- Opening and closing checklists
- Inventory tracking and par level sheets
- Signature cocktail menu with descriptions and pricing
- Training materials for new bartenders
- Responsible service documentation
- Daily sales and cash reports

Minimum viable: Accurate drinks served quickly with friendly service
Gold standard: Craft cocktails with presentation, engaging hospitality, efficient operations, zero waste

5) Quality checklist
Drink Quality:
- [ ] Proper measurements (use jiggers, not free pour for accuracy)
- [ ] Correct ice (type and amount per drink)
- [ ] Fresh ingredients (no oxidized citrus or wilted herbs)
- [ ] Proper dilution through shaking/stirring
- [ ] Garnishes fresh and properly prepared
- [ ] Glassware clean and appropriate for drink
- [ ] Temperature correct (chilled for cocktails, room temp for spirits neat)
- [ ] Consistent taste across same drink orders

Responsible Service:
- [ ] All IDs checked for under-30 appearance guests
- [ ] Guest monitored for intoxication signs (slurred speech, loss of coordination, aggression)
- [ ] No service to visibly intoxicated persons
- [ ] No over-serving (track number of drinks per guest)
- [ ] Food and water offered alongside alcohol
- [ ] Safe transportation arranged for impaired guests
- [ ] Incidents documented with date, time, details
- [ ] Cut-off time strictly enforced

Service Quality:
- [ ] Guests greeted within 30 seconds
- [ ] Orders taken accurately and confirmed
- [ ] Drinks delivered promptly (2-3 min for simple, 5-7 min for craft)
- [ ] Bar kept clean and organized
- [ ] Spills cleaned immediately
- [ ] Eye contact and friendly demeanor maintained
- [ ] Tab accuracy verified before payment
- [ ] Tips handled appropriately per venue policy

Operations:
- [ ] Bar fully stocked before service
- [ ] Mise en place complete (garnishes, ice, glassware)
- [ ] Equipment functioning properly
- [ ] Inventory tracked accurately
- [ ] Par levels maintained
- [ ] Waste minimized
- [ ] Cash drawer balanced
- [ ] Health and safety standards met

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Classic Cocktail Recipe
Old Fashioned - 2 oz whiskey, 0.25 oz simple syrup, 2 dashes bitters, orange peel. Build in rocks glass with large ice, stir 20-30 seconds. Spirit-forward classic.

Example 2: High-Volume Service Strategy
Friday night 200+ guests: Three bartenders (well, service, rail). Batch popular cocktails. Pre-cut citrus double. Extra ice backup. Well handles beer/wine/simple (70%). Service bar for servers. Rail for craft cocktails. Stay calm, track IDs strictly.

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- Well Spirits: House brands (vodka, gin, rum, tequila, whiskey)
- Call Spirits: Specific brand requested
- Top Shelf: Premium spirits
- Neat: No ice, room temperature
- On the Rocks: Over ice
- Up: Chilled, strained, no ice in glass
- Back: Side of water/mixer
- Muddle: Press ingredients to release oils
- Shake: Vigorous with ice (10-12 sec) for citrus/cream
- Stir: Gentle with ice (20-30 sec) for spirit-forward
- Float: Pour gently on top

Glassware:
- Rocks/Old Fashioned: Short tumbler
- Highball/Collins: Tall glass
- Coupe: Shallow stem for "up" cocktails
- Martini: V-shaped stem
- Pint: 16 oz beer

Ice Types:
- Cubed: Standard 1-inch
- Crushed: Small for tiki/juleps
- Large Cube/Sphere: Slow-melting for spirits

Tools:
- Jigger: Measuring tool (1 oz/2 oz)
- Boston Shaker: Two-piece shaker
- Hawthorne Strainer: Coil-spring strainer
- Bar Spoon: Long stirring spoon
- Muddler: Pressing tool

Responsible Service:
- BAC: Blood Alcohol Content (0.08% legal limit)
- Standard Drink: 1.5 oz spirits, 5 oz wine, 12 oz beer
- Intoxication Signs: Slurred speech, impaired coordination, aggression
- Dram Shop Law: Bar liability for harm caused by intoxicated patron
- TIPS: Training for Intervention Procedures certification
- 86: Refuse service/ban guest

Common Spirits:
- Vodka: Neutral (Tito's, Grey Goose)
- Gin: Juniper (Tanqueray, Hendrick's)
- Rum: Sugar cane - white, gold, dark (Bacardi, Mount Gay)
- Tequila: Agave - blanco, reposado, añejo (Patrón, Espolòn)
- Whiskey: Grain - bourbon, rye, scotch (Jack Daniel's, Jameson)
- Liqueurs: Sweet, flavored (Cointreau, Kahlúa, Baileys)
- Bitters: Concentrated flavoring (Angostura, Peychaud's)

Industry Standards:
- Pour Size: 1.5 oz spirits, 5 oz wine, 12 oz beer
- Beverage Cost %: 18-24% target
- Tip Standard: 15-20% table, $1-2 per drink at bar
- Last Call: 15-30 min before close
