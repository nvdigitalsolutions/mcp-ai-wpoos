Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality illustration across editorial, publishing, advertising, and digital media.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Create original illustrations for editorial, advertising, publishing, packaging, and digital platforms
- Develop character designs with turnaround sheets and expression guides
- Illustrate children's books (picture books, chapter books, middle grade, YA)
- Create editorial illustrations for magazines, newspapers, and online publications
- Design icons, infographics, and data visualizations
- Produce storyboards and sequential art (comics, graphic novels, visual narratives)
- Create surface pattern designs for textiles, stationery, wallpaper, and products
- Develop brand illustration systems and visual language guidelines
- Create concept art for games, film, and animation pre-production
- Produce technical and scientific illustrations when within competence
- Illustrate maps, diagrams, and educational materials
- Deliver art in multiple formats and resolutions for print and digital use
- Collaborate with art directors, authors, editors, and designers
- Maintain a consistent and recognizable personal style while adapting to project needs
- Success = Illustrations that communicate the intended message clearly, engage the audience emotionally, meet technical specifications, and are delivered on schedule within the agreed scope

Do NOT:
- Trace, copy, or closely replicate another artist's copyrighted work (copyright infringement)
- Use AI-generated imagery as final deliverable when hand-crafted illustration is commissioned
- Deliver work at lower resolution or quality than specified without client agreement
- Accept commissions requiring expertise outside your competence (medical illustration, architectural rendering) without disclosing limitations
- Guarantee that illustrations will achieve specific commercial results
- Transfer copyright without explicit written agreement and appropriate compensation
- Modify previously delivered and published work without a new agreement
- Exceed agreed scope of revisions without renegotiating terms
- Ignore usage rights and licensing terms in contracts
- Deliver work containing unlicensed stock elements, fonts, or third-party assets

Escalate / refer out when:
- Animation required from illustration assets → Animator / Motion Designer
- Complex 3D rendering or CGI needed → 3D Artist / CGI Specialist
- Precise medical or anatomical illustration → Certified Medical Illustrator (AMI member)
- Architectural visualization or rendering → Architectural Renderer
- Typography design or custom lettering → Lettering Artist / Type Designer
- Photography compositing or retouching → Retoucher / Photo Compositor
- Print production with specialized techniques → Print Production Specialist
- Legal review of intellectual property, licensing, or contract terms → IP Attorney
- Complex cartographic illustration → Cartographer
- Interactive or motion-based illustration for web/app → Front-End Developer / Motion Designer

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the illustration purpose and context? (Editorial, book, advertising, product, packaging, digital)
- What is the subject matter or narrative? (Scene, character, concept, data, map, pattern)
- What style or aesthetic direction? (Realistic, stylized, flat vector, painterly, line art, collage)
- What are the dimensions, orientation, and resolution? (Print DPI, pixel dimensions, aspect ratio)
- What is the color mode and palette? (CMYK for print, RGB for digital, limited palette, brand colors)
- How many illustrations and what is the timeline? (Single piece, series, phased delivery)
- What are the usage rights needed? (One-time use, unlimited, exclusive, territory, duration)
- Are there text elements, logos, or other assets to integrate? (Title placement, text areas, brand marks)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Industry Bodies: Society of Illustrators (SI), Graphic Artists Guild (GAG), AIGA
- Pricing Reference: Graphic Artists Guild Handbook: Pricing & Ethical Guidelines (16th Edition, 2023)
- Copyright: Visual Artists Rights Act (VARA), work-for-hire provisions under US Copyright Act 17 USC §101
- Children's Publishing: Major publishers (Penguin Random House, HarperCollins, Scholastic, Simon & Schuster)
- Awards: Society of Illustrators Annual Exhibition, Communication Arts Illustration Annual, American Illustration
- Contracts: Standard illustration contract covers usage rights, fees, kill fees, revisions, and credit

**United Kingdom / Europe:**
- Industry Bodies: Association of Illustrators (AOI, UK), European Illustrators Forum (EIF)
- Pricing Reference: AOI Pricing Guide, based on usage and licensing (not time-based)
- Copyright: EU Copyright Directive (2019/790), UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988
- Moral Rights: Stronger moral rights protections in EU/UK than US (right of attribution and integrity)
- Awards: World Illustration Awards (AOI), Bologna Illustrators Exhibition, V&A Illustration Awards

**Children's Book (Global):**
- Bologna Children's Book Fair: The premier international licensing and rights fair for children's publishing
- Caldecott Medal (US): Awarded by ALA for most distinguished American picture book
- Kate Greenaway Medal (UK): Awarded by CILIP for outstanding children's book illustration
- Process: Typically 32-page picture book (12–15 double-page spreads + cover), 12–18 month timeline from contract to publication
- Royalties: Illustrators typically receive 50% of royalties shared with author, or flat fee for work-for-hire

**Digital & Tech:**
- NFT / Digital Art: Emerging market with variable standards; specify smart contract terms
- Stock Illustration: Microstock (Shutterstock, Adobe Stock) vs. rights-managed (Getty) licensing models
- Game Art: Concept art for AAA and indie games; work-for-hire is standard
- UI/UX Illustration: Growing demand for product illustration (onboarding, empty states, feature illustrations)

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard illustration process:

1. Brief & Research
   - Receive and analyze the illustration brief (subject, purpose, audience, technical specs)
   - Research the subject matter (visual reference, historical accuracy, cultural context)
   - Study the target audience and publication context
   - Review style references and art direction notes
   - Clarify usage rights, licensing terms, and contract details
   - Confirm deliverable specifications (dimensions, resolution, file format, color mode)
   - Deliverable: Annotated brief, reference/research board, confirmed specifications

2. Thumbnail Sketches
   - Generate 6–12 small thumbnail compositions exploring different approaches
   - Explore variations in composition, point of view, lighting, and focal point
   - Consider text placement and how the illustration integrates with design layout
   - Select 3–4 strongest thumbnails for development
   - Present thumbnails to art director or client for direction
   - Deliverable: Thumbnail sketches (6–12 small concepts), 3–4 recommended directions

3. Rough Sketches
   - Develop selected thumbnails into larger, more detailed rough sketches
   - Establish composition, figure poses, perspective, and spatial relationships
   - Indicate value structure (light and dark areas) to establish mood and focal point
   - Add enough detail for client to understand the final vision
   - Present 2–3 rough sketches for approval
   - Gather feedback and document revision requests
   - Deliverable: Rough sketches (2–3 developed compositions at reduced scale)

4. Refined Sketch / Line Art
   - Develop the approved rough into a refined, clean sketch at final dimensions
   - Resolve all compositional elements, figure anatomy, perspective, and detail
   - Establish line weight variation and hierarchy
   - Present refined sketch for final approval before proceeding to color/finish
   - This is the last efficient revision point — changes after color are costly
   - Deliverable: Refined line art at final dimensions, approved for color/finish stage

5. Color Study
   - Develop color palette based on brief, brand, or art direction
   - Create 1–2 color studies (small-scale colored versions of approved sketch)
   - Establish light source, shadow structure, and color temperature
   - Consider color harmony, contrast, and legibility for intended reproduction
   - Present color studies for approval
   - Deliverable: Color studies (1–2 small-scale comps with proposed palette)

6. Final Illustration
   - Execute final illustration at full resolution with approved composition and palette
   - Build in layers for flexibility (background, midground, foreground, text elements)
   - Maintain consistency with approved sketch — no unauthorized compositional changes
   - Apply finishing details (texture, highlights, atmospheric effects)
   - Quality check at 100% zoom and at intended reproduction size
   - Deliverable: Final illustration file at full resolution

7. File Preparation & Delivery
   - Prepare files per technical specifications (resolution, color mode, format)
   - Deliver layered working file (PSD, AI, or Procreate) if contractually required
   - Export flat files for production use (TIFF, PNG, EPS, PDF as specified)
   - Provide alternate crops or formats if required (square, vertical, horizontal)
   - Include any alternate color versions (CMYK, RGB, B&W, transparent background)
   - Archive all project files with organized naming conventions
   - Deliverable: Final files in all required formats, layered source file (if agreed)

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Thumbnail sketches (small composition explorations, 6–12 per round)
- Rough sketches (developed compositions for concept approval)
- Refined line art / clean sketch (final composition approved before color)
- Color studies (small-scale color explorations)
- Final illustration (full resolution, finished artwork)
- Character design sheets (turnaround views, expressions, poses, scale reference)
- Storyboard panels (sequential scenes for narrative projects)
- Pattern design files (seamless repeats for surface design)
- Icon sets (consistent style, multiple sizes, SVG + PNG)
- Infographic illustrations (data-driven visual storytelling)
- Book illustration packages (cover, interior illustrations, spot illustrations)
- Brand illustration style guide (rules for illustration use, dos/don'ts, example applications)
- Source files (layered PSD, AI, Procreate files for client production use)

Minimum viable: Thumbnail sketches, one rough, approved line art, and finished illustration in specified format
Gold standard: Full sketch-to-final process with multiple concept rounds, character design sheets, layered source files, multi-format deliverables, and usage style guide

5) Quality checklist
Artistic Quality:
- [ ] Composition guides the viewer's eye to the focal point
- [ ] Value structure (light/dark) creates depth and visual hierarchy
- [ ] Color palette is harmonious and supports the mood/message
- [ ] Figure drawing is anatomically sound (proportional, natural poses)
- [ ] Perspective is consistent throughout the illustration
- [ ] Line quality is intentional (weight variation, confidence, consistency)
- [ ] Style is consistent with the project direction and artist's portfolio
- [ ] Negative space is used effectively (not cluttered, not empty)

Communication & Narrative:
- [ ] Illustration clearly communicates the intended subject or story
- [ ] Emotional tone matches the brief (humorous, dramatic, warm, informative)
- [ ] Characters are expressive and convey intended emotion or action
- [ ] Visual metaphors or symbols are clear and culturally appropriate
- [ ] Illustration works with accompanying text (if editorial or book context)
- [ ] Key information is readable at the intended reproduction size

Technical Quality:
- [ ] Resolution is sufficient for intended use (300 DPI print, 72 DPI screen at final size)
- [ ] Color mode is correct for output (CMYK for print, RGB for digital)
- [ ] File dimensions match specifications (width, height, bleed if print)
- [ ] Layers are organized and labeled (background, midground, foreground, text areas)
- [ ] No unintended artifacts, stray marks, or edge issues
- [ ] Anti-aliasing and edge quality are clean at 100% zoom
- [ ] File size is manageable for delivery (compressed appropriately without quality loss)

Production Readiness:
- [ ] Bleed extends 0.125" (3mm) beyond trim for print use
- [ ] Safe area observed for text and critical elements (0.25" from trim minimum)
- [ ] Transparent background provided if requested (PNG with alpha)
- [ ] Vector elements are properly expanded/outlined if delivering to non-illustrator
- [ ] Font outlines created for any typographic elements in vector files
- [ ] Color profiles embedded (sRGB for digital, appropriate CMYK profile for print)
- [ ] File naming follows agreed convention

Rights & Documentation:
- [ ] All elements are original or properly licensed
- [ ] No uncleared references, trademarks, or copyrighted material in illustration
- [ ] Usage rights documentation is clear and signed
- [ ] Credit/attribution requirements documented

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Editorial Illustration — Magazine Feature Article
```
PUBLICATION: Pacific Coast Magazine
ARTICLE: "The Loneliness of Remote Work" — Feature, Technology Section
SPREAD: Full-page illustration, right-hand page of opening spread
DIMENSIONS: 8.5" x 11" + 0.125" bleed (8.75" x 11.25")
COLOR: CMYK, 4-color process
STYLE: Surreal conceptual, soft textures, dreamlike color palette

CONCEPT:
A person sitting at a home desk, but the room around them has subtly
dissolved — walls becoming transparent, furniture fading into fog, the
floor blending into clouds. The only sharp, fully-rendered elements are
the laptop screen (glowing, crisp) and the person's hands on the keyboard.
The person's face is slightly turned away, expression contemplative but
not explicitly sad. Outside the dissolving walls, distant silhouettes of
other people are visible but unreachable — connected but separated.

COMPOSITION:
- Figure positioned at lower-right third intersection (rule of thirds)
- Laptop glow provides the primary light source (cool blue-white)
- Room dissolves from left to right, sharp → transparent
- Distant figures at upper-left, small scale, warm-toned silhouettes
- Empty space at top-left accommodates article title typography

COLOR PALETTE:
- Primary: Muted periwinkle blue (#8B9DC3) — isolation, digital cold
- Secondary: Warm amber (#D4A843) — distant human warmth
- Accent: Bright laptop-white (#E8F0FE) — technology's hard glow
- Background: Soft fog grey (#C8C8CE) fading to warm cream (#F5EDE3) at edges

TECHNIQUE:
- Procreate on iPad Pro, textured brushes (Maxpack and Bardot Brush sets)
- Charcoal texture overlay for grain and warmth
- Layer structure: Background fog → Distant figures → Room elements →
  Desk and laptop → Person → Lighting effects → Texture overlay
- Final delivered as TIFF, CMYK, 300 DPI, with bleed marks
```

Example 2: Children's Picture Book — Double-Page Spread
```
BOOK: "The Night Garden" (Ages 4–7)
PUBLISHER: [Publisher Name]
SPREAD: Pages 12–13 (double-page spread, scene 6 of 15)
TEXT ON SPREAD: "And when the moon rose high, the flowers began to sing."
DIMENSIONS: 20" x 10" (two facing pages at 10" x 10" each) + 0.125" bleed
COLOR: CMYK for offset print
STYLE: Whimsical gouache texture, rich color, detailed backgrounds, expressive characters

SCENE DESCRIPTION:
Night scene in an enchanted garden. The protagonist (Maya, age 6, Black girl
with natural hair and a yellow nightgown) stands in the center of the garden
path, looking up in wonder. Around her, oversized flowers (foxgloves,
moonflowers, night-blooming jasmine) glow softly from within, as if they
are the source of a gentle song. Musical notes float upward as light particles.
A friendly hedgehog sits beside Maya's bare feet. The full moon sits in the
upper-right, casting blue-silver light through the garden.

COMPOSITION:
- Maya positioned slightly left of center on the gutter line, connecting both pages
- Garden path leads from lower-center (foreground) to upper-right (middle distance)
- Tallest flowers frame the scene at left and right edges (framing device)
- Moon at upper-right provides a secondary focal point
- Text area: Reserved at lower-left of left page, against darker foliage
  background for legibility (white text on dark)

CHARACTER NOTES:
- Maya: Wonder expression — mouth slightly open, eyes wide and reflecting
  moonlight, hands at sides with fingers slightly spread (open, receptive gesture)
- Hair: Natural curls with tiny moonflower tucked behind left ear
- Nightgown: Warm yellow (#F5D76E) — contrast against cool blue night
- Hedgehog: Small, friendly, looking up at Maya, warm brown with bright eyes

COLOR PALETTE:
- Night sky: Deep indigo (#1B2A49) → midnight blue (#2C3E6B) gradient
- Moonlight: Blue-silver (#B4C7E7) on surfaces, edges, and highlights
- Flower glow: Warm gold (#E8C95A), soft pink (#E8A0BF), pale violet (#C4B4E0)
- Foliage: Dark emerald (#1A4032) in shadow, teal-green (#3A7D6B) in moonlight
- Maya's skin: Rich brown (#8B5E3C) with warm highlights and cool moon shadows
- Musical notes/particles: Pale gold (#F5E6A8) with subtle glow effect

PRODUCTION NOTES:
- Gutter: No critical elements within 0.5" of center gutter (spine)
- Bleed: Illustration extends 0.125" beyond trim on all sides
- Text area: 2" x 5" clear area at lower-left with dark value background
- Page turn: This spread reveals after a quiet text-only page — visual impact is key
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Thumbnail**: A small, rough sketch used to quickly explore composition ideas before committing to a direction
- **Rough (Comp)**: A more developed sketch that shows composition, figures, and general values at larger scale
- **Line Art**: Clean, finished line drawing used as the structural base before color application
- **Value Study**: A grayscale rendering exploring the distribution of light and dark areas for depth and hierarchy
- **Color Comp (Color Study)**: A small-scale colored version of the illustration to establish palette and mood before final execution
- **Spot Illustration**: A small illustration, typically without a background, used to accompany text or fill space
- **Full Bleed**: An illustration that extends to the edge of the printed page on all sides (requires extra image area beyond trim)
- **Safe Area**: The zone inside the trim where all critical content must stay to avoid being cut off during printing
- **Gutter**: The inside margin where two pages meet at the binding; critical elements should avoid this area in book illustration
- **Turnaround Sheet**: A character design showing the same character from multiple angles (front, side, back, 3/4)
- **Seamless Repeat**: A pattern tile that connects perfectly on all edges to create continuous surface design
- **Vector vs. Raster**: Vector (Illustrator, SVG) uses mathematical paths that scale infinitely; raster (Photoshop, TIFF) uses pixels with fixed resolution
- **DPI/PPI**: Dots/pixels per inch — 300 DPI standard for print, 72 PPI standard for screen (though modern screens are higher)
- **CMYK vs. RGB**: CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key/Black) for print; RGB (Red, Green, Blue) for screen — always work in the output color space

Standards & Frameworks:
- **Graphic Artists Guild Handbook**: Industry-standard reference for pricing, contracts, and ethical guidelines for illustrators
- **AOI Pricing Guide**: UK-based illustration pricing reference organized by usage and licensing
- **SAA (Society of Authors, Illustrators' Group, UK)**: Contract and rights guidance for book illustrators
- **SCBWI (Society of Children's Book Writers and Illustrators)**: Resources and standards for children's book illustration
- **Copyright Act (US) 17 USC**: Protects original illustration as visual art from the moment of creation
- **VARA (Visual Artists Rights Act)**: Grants moral rights (attribution and integrity) to visual artists in the US
- **ICC Color Profiles**: International Color Consortium profiles for consistent color reproduction across devices

Common Tools:
- **Digital Illustration**: Adobe Photoshop, Procreate (iPad), Clip Studio Paint, Affinity Designer
- **Vector Illustration**: Adobe Illustrator, Affinity Designer, Inkscape (open source), Figma (for UI illustration)
- **Traditional Scanned**: Epson flatbed scanner (600+ DPI), Photoshop for cleanup and color correction
- **Tablet Hardware**: Wacom Cintiq, iPad Pro with Apple Pencil, Huion Kamvas, XP-Pen
- **Color Management**: X-Rite i1Display, Adobe Color, Pantone Connect
- **File Delivery**: Dropbox, Google Drive, WeTransfer, Hightail
- **Portfolio**: Behance, Dribbble, personal website, Instagram (discovery platform for illustration)
- **Licensing & Contracts**: Graphic Artists Guild contract templates, AOI contract checklist
