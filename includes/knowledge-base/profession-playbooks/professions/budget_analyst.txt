Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality budget analysis assistance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize diagnosis, options with tradeoffs, recommendations, and execution plans.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on budget development, execution, monitoring, and reporting
- Explain budgeting frameworks (zero-based, incremental, performance-based, PPBS)
- Help develop budget proposals, justifications, and cost-benefit analyses
- Advise on variance analysis, forecasting, and fiscal management
- Explain government budgeting processes (federal, state, local) and appropriations
- Guide on corporate budgeting cycles, capital budgeting, and financial planning
- Assist with budget presentation, narrative writing, and stakeholder communication
- Success = Aligned resource allocation, fiscal discipline, transparent reporting, informed decisions

Do NOT:
- Make final budget allocation decisions (requires authorized budget authority)
- Commit government funds or approve expenditures (requires warranted authority)
- Provide investment advice or recommend specific financial products
- Substitute for policy decisions on program priorities
- Guarantee funding levels or legislative outcomes
- Override organizational budget approval chains

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legislative or regulatory interpretation required (engage legal counsel or legislative analyst)
- Complex procurement or contracting questions arise (engage contracting officer)
- Tax revenue forecasting requires econometric modeling (engage economist)
- Capital project cost estimation beyond budget scope (engage cost engineer)
- Pension or OPEB liability calculations needed (engage actuary)
- IT system implementations for budget management (engage IT project manager)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What sector are you in? (Federal, state/local government, nonprofit, corporate)
- What is the budget cycle timeline? (Fiscal year dates, submission deadlines)
- What budgeting methodology is used? (Zero-based, incremental, program-based, activity-based)
- What is the total budget size and major categories?
- What are the primary funding sources? (Appropriations, grants, revenue, fees)
- Are there specific compliance requirements? (OMB circulars, GFOA standards, bond covenants)
- What are the key budget drivers and known pressures?
- What reporting requirements exist? (Board, council, legislature, grantor)

1.5) Regional Variations - Budget Frameworks & Standards

**United States — Federal Government:**
- Budget Process: President's Budget → Congressional Budget Resolution → Appropriations
- Fiscal Year: October 1 – September 30
- Key Laws: Budget and Accounting Act (1921), Congressional Budget Act (1974), GPRA Modernization Act (2010)
- OMB Circulars: A-11 (budget preparation), A-123 (internal controls), A-136 (financial reporting)
- Appropriation Types: Annual, multi-year, no-year, revolving funds
- Budget Concepts: Budget Authority (BA), Obligations, Outlays, Offsetting Collections
- Sequestration: Automatic spending cuts under Budget Control Act
- Continuing Resolutions: Temporary funding when appropriations not enacted

**United States — State & Local Government:**
- Balanced Budget: Most states constitutionally required to balance operating budgets
- GFOA: Government Finance Officers Association best practices
- NACSLB: National Advisory Council on State and Local Budgeting — recommended practices
- Fund Accounting: General Fund, Special Revenue, Capital Projects, Enterprise, Trust
- GASB: Governmental Accounting Standards Board (financial reporting standards)
- Property Tax: Primary local revenue source; assessed value × mill rate
- Budget Calendar: Varies by jurisdiction (typically July 1 – June 30 or Jan 1 – Dec 31)
- Citizen Engagement: Public hearings, budget-in-brief documents, participatory budgeting

**United States — Corporate/Private Sector:**
- Budget Cycle: Typically annual with quarterly reforecasting
- Capital Budgeting: NPV, IRR, payback period analysis
- Rolling Forecasts: Continuous 12-18 month forward-looking projections
- Driver-Based Budgeting: Link budget to operational metrics (headcount, units, etc.)
- FP&A: Financial Planning & Analysis function
- ERP Integration: SAP, Oracle, Workday for budget management
- Board Reporting: Budget vs. actual, forecast updates, KPI dashboards

**European Union:**
- Stability and Growth Pact: Fiscal rules for EU member states
- Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF): EU's 7-year spending plan
- European Semester: Annual cycle of economic policy coordination
- Member State Budgets: National fiscal frameworks per EU Directive 2011/85/EU
- Accrual-Based Accounting: Trend toward EPSAS (European Public Sector Accounting Standards)

**United Kingdom:**
- HM Treasury: Spending Reviews and departmental budgets
- Fiscal Year: April 1 – March 31
- Resource Accounting and Budgeting (RAB): Accrual-based government accounting
- Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR): Independent fiscal forecasting
- CIPFA: Chartered Institute of Public Finance and Accountancy standards

**Canada:**
- Federal: Department of Finance budget; Treasury Board Secretariat expenditure management
- Fiscal Year: April 1 – March 31
- Estimates Process: Main Estimates → Supplementary Estimates (A, B, C)
- Provincial: Each province has its own budget process and fiscal year

**International Standards:**
- IPSAS: International Public Sector Accounting Standards
- IMF GFS: Government Finance Statistics Manual
- OECD: Best Practices for Budget Transparency
- PEFA: Public Expenditure and Financial Accountability framework

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Budget Preparation & Development
   - Review prior year actuals, current year trends, and strategic priorities
   - Issue budget instructions and templates to departments/cost centers
   - Collect budget requests with justifications
   - Analyze submissions against available resources and policy guidance
   - Develop revenue projections and expenditure estimates
   - Prepare budget recommendations with options and tradeoffs
   
2. Budget Review & Approval
   - Present budget proposals to management/leadership
   - Facilitate budget hearings and deliberations
   - Incorporate feedback and revise budget
   - Prepare budget document for governing body approval
   - Document assumptions, risks, and contingencies
   
3. Budget Execution & Monitoring
   - Establish allotments and spending plans
   - Monitor obligations and expenditures against appropriations
   - Track revenue collections against projections
   - Identify and communicate variances exceeding thresholds
   - Process budget amendments, transfers, and reprogramming actions
   - Maintain budget control ledger
   
4. Variance Analysis & Forecasting
   - Perform monthly/quarterly variance analysis (actual vs. budget)
   - Investigate significant variances (>5% or material dollar threshold)
   - Update year-end forecast based on current trends
   - Prepare variance explanation narratives
   - Recommend corrective actions for adverse variances
   
5. Reporting & Year-End Close
   - Prepare periodic budget status reports (monthly/quarterly)
   - Compile year-end budget performance report
   - Document lapsed and carryover balances
   - Prepare budget-to-actual comparison for financial statements
   - Conduct lessons learned for next budget cycle improvement

4) Deliverables
- Annual budget document (proposed and adopted)
- Budget instructions and templates for departments
- Revenue and expenditure projections with assumptions
- Budget justification narratives and decision packages
- Monthly/quarterly budget status reports
- Variance analysis reports with explanations
- Year-end forecast updates
- Capital improvement plan and budget
- Budget performance report (annual)
- Budget-in-brief (public-facing summary)
- Multi-year financial plan/forecast

Minimum viable: Budget proposal + monthly status reports + variance analysis
Gold standard: Comprehensive budget document + multi-year plan + rolling forecasts + performance metrics + citizen engagement materials

5) Quality checklist
Accuracy:
- [ ] Revenue projections based on documented assumptions and historical trends
- [ ] Expenditure estimates tied to specific cost drivers and justifications
- [ ] Calculations verified (formulas, totals, crossfoots)
- [ ] Budget data reconciled to financial system/ERP
- [ ] Prior year comparisons accurate

Compliance:
- [ ] Budget balanced per applicable requirements (balanced budget laws, policies)
- [ ] Appropriation limits not exceeded
- [ ] Fund restrictions observed (restricted, committed, assigned)
- [ ] Grant budget aligned with award terms and conditions
- [ ] Filing and approval deadlines met

Completeness:
- [ ] All funds and departments included
- [ ] Personnel costs include benefits, taxes, and projected increases
- [ ] Non-recurring items identified and excluded from base
- [ ] Contingency/reserve amounts adequate
- [ ] Capital and operating budgets linked

Analysis Quality:
- [ ] Variance explanations identify root causes, not just describe differences
- [ ] Forecasts use multiple scenarios (best case, base case, worst case)
- [ ] Tradeoffs and alternatives clearly presented for decision-makers
- [ ] Performance metrics linked to budget allocations
- [ ] Risks and uncertainties disclosed

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Appropriation**: Legal authority to incur obligations and make payments from the treasury
- **Budget Authority (BA)**: Authority to enter into financial obligations (federal)
- **Obligation**: Binding agreement that will result in expenditure
- **Outlay**: Actual payment/disbursement of funds
- **Allotment**: Subdivision of an appropriation for a specific time period or purpose
- **Encumbrance**: Commitment of funds for a purchase order or contract not yet paid
- **Fund Balance**: Residual of assets over liabilities in governmental fund
- **Mill Rate**: Property tax rate per $1,000 of assessed value
- **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)**: Building budget from zero; every expense must be justified
- **Incremental Budgeting**: Adjusting prior year budget by a percentage or fixed amount
- **Performance Budgeting**: Linking budget allocations to measurable outcomes
- **Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)**: Multi-year plan for capital asset investments
- **Operating Budget**: Budget for day-to-day expenditures (personnel, supplies, services)
- **Continuing Resolution (CR)**: Temporary funding measure (federal)
- **Variance**: Difference between budgeted and actual amounts

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **GFOA Best Practices**: Budgeting, financial planning, and reporting guidance
- **GFOA Distinguished Budget Presentation Award**: Criteria for excellence in budgeting
- **NACSLB Recommended Practices**: National standards for government budgeting
- **OMB Circular A-11**: Federal budget preparation instructions
- **GASB 54**: Fund balance reporting and governmental fund type definitions
- **GASB 87**: Leases — impact on budget and financial reporting
- **IPSAS**: International public sector standards for budget reporting
- **2 CFR Part 200**: Uniform Guidance for federal awards (cost principles, audit)
- **PPBS**: Planning, Programming, and Budgeting System (DOD and federal agencies)

Common Tools:
- **Questica / OpenGov**: Government budgeting and performance management
- **Workday Adaptive Planning**: Corporate planning and budgeting
- **Anaplan**: Connected planning platform
- **SAP BPC**: Business Planning and Consolidation
- **Oracle PBCS**: Planning and Budgeting Cloud Service
- **Excel**: Universal budget modeling and analysis tool
- **Tableau / Power BI**: Budget dashboards and visualization
- **Tyler Technologies / Munis**: Local government ERP and budgeting
- **CGI Advantage**: State and local government financial management
