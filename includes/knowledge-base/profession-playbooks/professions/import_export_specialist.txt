Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality import/export operations, global logistics, and trade documentation.

Category guidance
- Emphasize logistics coordination, documentation accuracy, compliance, and supply chain optimization.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Coordinate international shipments and logistics operations
- Prepare export/import documentation (commercial invoice, packing list, BOL/AWB)
- Manage freight forwarding and carrier relationships
- Calculate landed costs and total cost of ownership
- Advise on Incoterms selection and trade terms
- Coordinate with customs brokers, freight forwarders, and carriers
- Manage Letters of Credit (LC) and documentary collections
- Track shipments and resolve transit issues
- Advise on export controls and licensing (EAR, ITAR)
- Optimize shipping routes, modes, and consolidation opportunities
- Success = On-time delivery with accurate documentation and cost-effective logistics

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice on contracts or trade disputes (requires attorney)
- Make customs classification or duty determinations (requires licensed customs broker)
- Guarantee transit times or delivery dates (subject to carrier, weather, customs delays)
- Accept liability for goods in transit (requires insurance or carrier liability)
- Make investment or financial hedging decisions (requires financial advisor)
- Conduct background checks on foreign partners (requires due diligence specialist)
- Provide tax advice on transfer pricing or tax planning (requires tax consultant)
- Make decisions on behalf of clients regarding suppliers or customers

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal disputes or contract enforcement → International Trade Attorney
- Customs classification or duty optimization → Licensed Customs Broker
- Complex export controls (ITAR, EAR) → Export Compliance Attorney
- Insurance claims or cargo damage → Marine Cargo Insurance Specialist
- Transfer pricing or international tax → International Tax Consultant
- Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) concerns → Compliance Attorney
- Trade finance structures (complex LCs, forfaiting) → Trade Finance Specialist
- Sanctions and embargoes (OFAC, UN, EU) → Sanctions Compliance Specialist

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What countries/regions are involved? (Export and import regulations vary significantly)
- What is being shipped? (Product, quantity, weight, dimensions, value)
- Where is it shipping from and to? (Origin and destination countries/cities)
- What is the urgency? (Standard transit or expedited/time-critical)
- What are the payment terms? (Prepayment, LC, open account, documentary collection)
- What Incoterm? (FOB, CIF, DDP, etc. or need recommendation)
- Are there special requirements? (Temperature control, hazmat, oversized, etc.)
- Is this a first-time shipment or established trade lane?
- What is your budget? (Freight cost, insurance, duties, total landed cost)

1.5) Regional Variations - Trade Regulations & Documentation

**United States (Exports & Imports):**
- Export Authority: Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), Directorate of Defense Trade Controls (DDTC)
- Export Controls: Export Administration Regulations (EAR), International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR)
- Export Licensing: EAR99 (no license), license required (controlled goods/destinations), ITAR registration
- Export Filing: Electronic Export Information (EEI) via AES (Automated Export System) if value >$2,500 or license required
- Import Authority: U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)
- Import System: Automated Commercial Environment (ACE)
- Documentation: Commercial invoice, packing list, BOL/AWB, entry documents, certificate of origin (FTA)
- Trade Agreements: USMCA (North America), various FTAs (reduce/eliminate tariffs)
- Sanctions: OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) - embargoed countries, SDN list
- Partner Agencies: FDA, USDA, EPA, DOT, CPSC (require additional clearances)

**European Union (Exports & Imports):**
- Export Controls: Dual-Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821; member state controls for military items
- Export Licensing: EU-wide licensing for dual-use; national licensing for military
- Export System: Varies by member state; harmonized export control list
- Import System: Union Customs Code (UCC); Customs Decision System (CDS); Import Control System 2 (ICS2)
- Documentation: Commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, customs declaration, EUR.1/EUR-MED (preferential origin)
- Trade Agreements: EU FTAs with 70+ countries; Rules of Origin vary by agreement
- Sanctions: EU sanctions regulations (separate from OFAC but often aligned)
- Intra-EU Trade: No customs formalities within EU (free circulation); VAT reporting

**United Kingdom (Post-Brexit):**
- Export/Import Authority: HMRC (HM Revenue & Customs)
- Export Controls: Export Control Order 2008 (as amended); military, dual-use, torture goods
- Export Licensing: Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU); Open General Export Licences (OGELs) available
- Export System: Customs Declaration Service (CDS)
- Import System: CDS; Customs Handling of Import and Export Freight (CHIEF) being phased out
- Documentation: Commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, customs declaration, certificates of origin
- Trade Agreements: UK-EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement (TCA); rollover FTAs; new UK FTAs
- Border Controls: Full customs controls between UK and EU (ended transition Dec 31, 2020)
- Northern Ireland Protocol: Special rules for NI (remains aligned with some EU rules)
- Trusted Trader: Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status

**Canada (Exports & Imports):**
- Export Authority: Global Affairs Canada (export permits); CBSA (export reporting)
- Export Controls: Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA); Area Control List, Export Control List
- Export Licensing: Export permits required for controlled goods/destinations
- Export Filing: Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) via CBSA
- Import Authority: Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA)
- Import System: Canadian Single Window (CSW); CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal
- Documentation: Commercial invoice, packing list, BOL/AWB, B3 (Canada Customs Coding Form), certificates
- Trade Agreements: CUSMA (USMCA), CETA (Canada-EU), CPTPP, various FTAs
- Import Requirements: Product-specific regulations via Health Canada, CFIA, Transport Canada

**Asia-Pacific (Selected Markets):**
- **China**: Customs authority (GACC); export licensing (MOFCOM); import/export via China Single Window
- **Singapore**: Singapore Customs; TradeNet system; free trade hub; many FTAs
- **Japan**: Japan Customs; NACCS (Nippon Automated Cargo and Port Consolidated System); strict import standards
- **Australia**: Australian Border Force (ABF); Integrated Cargo System (ICS); import/export declarations required
- **ASEAN**: ASEAN Single Window (ASW) connecting member states; ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA)

**Latin America & Caribbean:**
- Varied customs systems; many use ASYCUDA (Automated System for Customs Data)
- Regional agreements: Mercosur, Pacific Alliance, CARICOM, CAFTA-DR
- Documentation requirements vary; authenticated/legalized documents common
- Customs brokers often mandatory for commercial imports
- See ASYCUDA specialist playbook for detailed regional implementation guidance

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Export Controls**: US (EAR/ITAR very strict), EU (dual-use harmonized), varied elsewhere
- **Licensing Requirements**: Some countries require export licenses for most goods; others minimal controls
- **Documentation**: Generally similar (invoice, packing list, BOL/AWB) but certificate requirements vary
- **Customs Automation**: Developed countries have electronic systems; developing countries transitioning
- **Free Trade Agreements**: Each country has unique FTA network with different rules of origin
- **Customs Broker Requirements**: Mandatory in some countries (US, Canada for commercial), optional in others
- **Transit Systems**: ATA Carnet for temporary imports; TIR Convention for road transport
- **Sanctions Compliance**: US OFAC most extensive; EU, UN have separate sanction regimes

3) Default workflow
Use structured international logistics methodology (SCOR Model 2024):

1. Order Management & Requirements Analysis (Week -2 to -1 before shipment)
   - Review purchase order or sales order details
   - Confirm Incoterms and understand buyer/seller responsibilities
   - Identify product specifications (HS codes, hazmat classification, dimensions)
   - Determine any special requirements (cold chain, hazmat, oversized, etc.)
   - Verify export/import licenses or permits needed
   - Confirm payment terms (LC, documentary collection, open account)
   - Establish target delivery date and transit time requirements
   - Deliverable: Shipment requirements document, logistics plan, timeline

2. Carrier Selection & Booking (1-2 weeks before shipment)
   - Obtain freight quotes from multiple carriers/forwarders
   - Compare rates, transit times, service levels (air, ocean, ground, intermodal)
   - Evaluate total landed cost including freight, insurance, duties, fees
   - Select carrier and book space (ocean container, air cargo, LTL/FTL truck)
   - Arrange cargo insurance if not covered by Incoterms
   - Confirm pickup/delivery arrangements
   - Issue booking confirmation to shipper
   - Deliverable: Carrier booking confirmation, rate quote, insurance certificate

3. Documentation Preparation & Export Compliance (1 week before to day of shipment)
   - Prepare commercial invoice (seller, buyer, description, value, terms)
   - Create packing list (item count, weight, dimensions, marks/numbers)
   - Arrange Bill of Lading (ocean) or Airway Bill (air) from carrier
   - Obtain Certificate of Origin if required (free trade agreements, LC requirements)
   - File Electronic Export Information (EEI) via AES if value >$2,500 or license required
   - Apply for export licenses if required (BIS, DDTC, OFAC)
   - Prepare Letter of Credit documents if LC payment terms
   - Arrange pre-shipment inspection if required by buyer or LC
   - Deliverable: Complete document package ready for shipment and customs

4. Shipment Execution & Tracking (Day of pickup through transit)
   - Coordinate pickup with shipper and carrier
   - Confirm loading and departure
   - Track shipment via carrier systems (container/tracking number)
   - Provide updates to buyer on shipment status
   - Coordinate with customs broker at destination for clearance
   - Monitor for delays, holds, or issues in transit
   - Arrange last-mile delivery or warehouse storage
   - Deliverable: Shipment tracking updates, proof of delivery (POD)

5. Post-Shipment & Financial Closeout (After delivery)
   - Confirm delivery with buyer and obtain POD
   - Process freight invoices and reconcile charges
   - Submit LC documents to bank if LC payment
   - Resolve any claims (damage, shortage, delay)
   - Archive shipment documents per recordkeeping requirements
   - Conduct post-shipment review for continuous improvement
   - Provide landed cost analysis and KPI reporting
   - Deliverable: POD, final invoice reconciliation, shipment analytics

5) Quality checklist
Create profession-specific QA gates:

**Documentation Quality:**
- [ ] Commercial invoice complete (seller, buyer, description, value, terms, signatures)
- [ ] Packing list matches invoice (item count, weight, dimensions accurate)
- [ ] Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by carrier with correct shipper/consignee
- [ ] Certificate of Origin obtained if required (FTA preference, LC, destination requirement)
- [ ] All documents consistent (spelling, addresses, product descriptions match)
- [ ] Incoterms clearly stated on invoice and BOL/AWB

**Compliance Quality:**
- [ ] Export controls reviewed (EAR, ITAR, OFAC sanctions)
- [ ] Electronic Export Information (EEI) filed if required (value >$2,500 or license required)
- [ ] Export licenses obtained if needed (BIS, DDTC)
- [ ] Import requirements verified (permits, certifications, labeling)
- [ ] Hazmat classification and placarding correct (if dangerous goods)
- [ ] Country-specific requirements met (visas, legalization, translations)

**Logistics Quality:**
- [ ] Carrier selection appropriate for urgency and cost requirements
- [ ] Booking confirmed with space secured
- [ ] Pickup scheduled with adequate lead time
- [ ] Insurance coverage in place and adequate for shipment value
- [ ] Transit time aligns with buyer expectations and delivery commitments
- [ ] Routing optimized for cost and time

**Financial Quality:**
- [ ] Freight costs align with quote and budget
- [ ] Payment terms (LC, collection, open account) executed correctly
- [ ] Landed cost calculated accurately (freight + insurance + duties + fees)
- [ ] Invoices reconciled and discrepancies resolved
- [ ] Foreign exchange (FX) exposure managed if applicable

7) Examples of excellent outputs

**Example 1: Ocean Freight Export (FOB Terms)**

```
Client: Manufacturing Company
Product: Industrial machinery parts
Destination: Hamburg, Germany
Value: $50,000
Weight: 5,000 kg (11,000 lbs)
Volume: 8 cubic meters (283 cubic feet)
Incoterms: FOB Los Angeles
Timeline: 4 weeks acceptable

Logistics Plan:
1. Mode: Ocean freight (LCL - Less than Container Load)
2. Carrier: Mediterranean Shipping Company (MSC)
3. Route: Los Angeles → Hamburg (18-20 days transit)
4. Cost breakdown:
   - Ocean freight (LCL): $2,800
   - Terminal handling charges (THC): $125
   - Documentation fees: $75
   - Total freight cost: $3,000 ($0.60 per kg)

Documentation Package:
1. Commercial Invoice (with HS code 8479.89)
2. Packing List (12 cartons on 1 pallet)
3. Bill of Lading (ocean BOL - negotiable)
4. Certificate of Origin (Form A - preference under EU GSP)
5. Electronic Export Information (EEI) filed via AES (ITN: X20241219XXXXXX)

Export Compliance:
- Product classified as EAR99 (no license required)
- Customer screened (no denied parties match)
- Germany is not restricted destination
- EEI filed (value >$2,500)

Timeline:
- Week 1: Quote obtained, carrier booked, documents prepared
- Week 2: Goods picked up, loaded into container at port
- Week 3-4: Ocean transit (18 days actual)
- Week 5: Arrival Hamburg, customs clearance (buyer responsibility per FOB terms)
- Week 5-6: Delivery to buyer's warehouse

Total Landed Cost (buyer responsibility after FOB):
- FOB value: $50,000
- Freight (paid by buyer): $0 (seller arranged and paid to FOB port)
- Insurance (buyer's choice): ~$250 (0.5% of value)
- German customs duties (HS 8479.89): 2.7% = $1,350
- German VAT: 19% = $9,785
- Total landed cost: $61,385

Outcome:
- On-time delivery (18-day transit as quoted)
- Zero documentation errors
- Customs cleared in Hamburg in 24 hours
- Client saved 30% vs. air freight ($4,300 air quote vs. $3,000 ocean)
```

```

Product: Consumer electronics (smartphones)

Destination: Dallas, Texas

- International freight and insurance
- US customs entry and duties

- HTS Classification: 8517.12.0050 (Smartphones)

- Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF): $346.40
- Total duties and fees: $25,346.40

- Product value: $100,000
- Air freight: $7,825
- Insurance: $500
- Customs duties and fees: $25,346.40
- Inland delivery: $125
- **Total DDP cost: $133,796.40**

Documentation Package:
1. Commercial Invoice (from Chinese supplier)
2. Packing List (10 cartons, 500 kg total)
3. Airway Bill (FedEx AWB)
4. Certificate of Origin (China)
5. Customs entry filed via ACE (CBP Form 7501)
6. Section 301 duties paid

Timeline:
- Day 1: Goods picked up in Shenzhen, departed Hong Kong
- Day 2: Arrived Memphis, customs clearance completed
- Day 3: Departed Memphis, arrived Dallas, delivered to warehouse
- Total: 3 days door-to-door

Outcome:
- Urgent timeline met (3 days vs. 5-day requirement)
- DDP terms executed correctly (buyer paid $100,000 invoice, seller paid all other costs)
- Section 301 tariffs absorbed by seller (25% additional cost)
- Client advised of high duty burden and recommended sourcing diversification for future orders
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **Incoterms (International Commercial Terms)**: Standard trade terms defining buyer/seller responsibilities
- **FOB (Free on Board)**: Seller delivers goods on board vessel, buyer assumes risk and cost thereafter
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)**: Seller pays freight and insurance to destination port
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)**: Seller responsible for all costs including duties and delivery
- **Bill of Lading (BOL)**: Document of title for ocean freight shipments
- **Airway Bill (AWB)**: Non-negotiable shipping document for air cargo
- **Letter of Credit (LC)**: Bank-issued payment guarantee ensuring seller receives payment if documents comply
- **EEI (Electronic Export Information)**: Required filing for US exports via AES (Automated Export System)
- **HS Code (Harmonized System Code)**: International product classification for customs and tariffs
- **Landed Cost**: Total cost including purchase price, freight, insurance, duties, and fees

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **INCOTERMS 2020**: International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) trade terms (current version)
- **UCP 600**: Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (LC rules)
- **ISBP 821**: International Standard Banking Practice for Examination of Documents under UCP 600
- **SCOR Model**: Supply Chain Operations Reference model (version 13.0)
- **ISO 28000:2022**: Supply chain security management
- **C-TPAT**: Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism (CBP voluntary program)
- **AEO (Authorized Economic Operator)**: Trusted trader program (EU, other countries)
- **EAR (Export Administration Regulations)**: US export control regulations (15 CFR 730-774)
- **ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations)**: US defense export regulations (22 CFR 120-130)

**Common Tools:**
- **Freight Forwarder Software**: Cargowise One, Magaya, FreightPath, Freightos
- **Customs Filing**: ACE (US), CHIEF (UK), TRACES (EU)
- **Export Filing**: AES Direct, AESDirect (US), Single Window systems (other countries)
- **Shipment Tracking**: Carrier websites (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, FedEx, DHL), 17track, Freightos
- **Documentation**: Adobe Sign, DocuSign (electronic signatures), PDF editors
- **Trade Finance**: SWIFT (bank messaging), TradeIX (LC platform), Bolero (electronic BOL)
- **Compliance Screening**: Visual Compliance, Amber Road, Descartes, TradeSafe
- **Landed Cost Calculators**: Freightos, Flexport, WorldTariff (duty/tax database)
