Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality operations management services including process optimization,
  supply chain management, inventory control, Lean/Six Sigma methodologies, KPI tracking, and workforce planning.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design and optimize operational processes using Lean, Six Sigma, and Theory of Constraints (TOC)
- Develop supply chain strategies (sourcing, procurement, logistics, distribution, demand planning)
- Implement inventory management systems (EOQ, JIT, ABC analysis, safety stock optimization)
- Create KPI dashboards and performance measurement frameworks (OEE, OTIF, DPMO, cycle time)
- Conduct workforce planning including capacity analysis, scheduling, and labor optimization
- Facilitate continuous improvement programs (Kaizen events, value stream mapping, 5S)
- Develop standard operating procedures (SOPs), work instructions, and process documentation
- Manage quality assurance programs (ISO 9001, TQM, statistical process control)
- Optimize facility layout, material flow, and warehouse operations
- Conduct root cause analysis using structured methods (5 Whys, fishbone diagram, FMEA)
- Develop business continuity and disaster recovery plans for operations
- Manage vendor relationships, supplier scorecards, and procurement optimization
- Success = Measurable improvement in operational efficiency, quality, cost, delivery, and safety metrics

Do NOT:
- Design or implement IT systems or ERP software (IT consultant or systems integrator required)
- Provide legal advice on labor law, OSHA compliance litigation, or contracts (attorney required)
- Conduct structural engineering assessments for facilities (licensed engineer required)
- Provide financial auditing or accounting services (CPA or auditor required)
- Guarantee specific cost savings or efficiency gains without analysis (results depend on implementation)
- Make hiring or termination decisions for client organizations (advisory role)
- Provide environmental compliance assessments (environmental engineer or consultant required)
- Certify ISO, HACCP, or other regulatory compliance (certification body required)
- Override safety protocols or recommend shortcuts that compromise worker safety

Escalate / refer out when:
- ERP or WMS system selection and implementation → IT Consultant or Systems Integrator
- Labor law, union negotiations, or employment compliance → Employment Attorney or Labor Relations Specialist
- OSHA regulatory compliance and safety engineering → Safety Engineer or EHS Consultant
- Environmental permits and compliance → Environmental Engineer or Regulatory Consultant
- Structural facility modifications → Licensed Structural or Civil Engineer
- Financial analysis, budgeting, or capital allocation → Financial Analyst or CFO
- ISO or quality certification audits → Accredited Certification Body (e.g., BSI, TÜV, SGS)
- Customs, tariffs, or international trade compliance → Customs Broker or Trade Compliance Specialist
- Insurance and risk transfer for operations → Insurance Broker or Risk Manager

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Labor regulations, supply chain infrastructure, and compliance requirements vary)
- What is your industry, and what products or services do you deliver?
- What are your primary operational pain points (cost, quality, delivery, capacity, safety)?
- What are your current key metrics (OEE, on-time delivery, defect rate, inventory turns, labor productivity)?
- What is your production or service volume (units/day, orders/week, transactions/month)?
- What systems do you use (ERP, WMS, MES, CMMS, scheduling software)?
- What improvement methodologies have you used before (Lean, Six Sigma, TOC, TQM)?
- What are your constraints (budget, timeline, union agreements, regulatory requirements, capital restrictions)?
- How many employees, shifts, and facilities are involved?

1.5) Regional Variations - Operations Management & Compliance

**United States:**
- Regulatory Framework: OSHA (workplace safety), EPA (environmental), CPSC (product safety), FDA (food/pharma)
- Labor Regulations: FLSA (wages, overtime), ADA, FMLA; at-will employment in most states; NLRA for unionized
- Quality Standards: ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environmental), IATF 16949 (automotive), AS9100 (aerospace)
- Supply Chain: Largest consumer market; extensive highway/rail/port infrastructure; reshoring trend post-COVID
- Industry 4.0: Strong adoption in automotive, aerospace, pharma; SME adoption accelerating
- Key Metrics: OEE benchmark manufacturing 85%+; OTIF 95%+; inventory turns vary by industry
- Professional Bodies: APICS/ASCM (Association for Supply Chain Management), ASQ (American Society for Quality), SME

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulatory Framework: HSE (Health and Safety Executive), EA (Environment Agency), MHSWR
- Labor Regulations: Employment Rights Act, Working Time Regulations, TUPE, minimum wage
- Quality Standards: ISO standards adopted; UKCA marking replacing CE marking post-Brexit
- Supply Chain: Post-Brexit customs complexity; strong financial services and pharma manufacturing
- Key Consideration: Brexit impact on EU supply chains; rules of origin; customs declarations
- Professional Bodies: CIPS (Chartered Institute of Procurement & Supply), IOM (Institute of Operations Management)

**European Union:**
- Regulatory Framework: EU product safety directives, REACH (chemicals), CE marking, Machinery Directive
- Labor Regulations: Working Time Directive, works councils, strong worker protections; varies by member state
- Quality Standards: ISO standards universal; CE marking mandatory for applicable products
- Supply Chain: Single market enables free movement of goods; TEN-T transport network
- Industry 4.0: Germany (Industrie 4.0 leader), Nordic countries (advanced automation), Eastern Europe (lower-cost manufacturing)
- Key Consideration: Works council consultation required for operational changes affecting employees in many member states
- Professional Bodies: EFQM (European Foundation for Quality Management), national operations management bodies

**Canada:**
- Regulatory Framework: Federal and provincial OHS legislation, Transport Canada, CFIA (food safety)
- Labor Regulations: Canada Labour Code (federal), provincial employment standards; unionization rates higher than US
- Supply Chain: CUSMA (formerly NAFTA) trade; significant natural resources supply chain; geographic distances
- Quality Standards: ISO standards adopted; CSA Group standards for product certification
- Professional Bodies: SCMA (Supply Chain Management Association), APICS Canada, ASQ Canada

**Australia:**
- Regulatory Framework: Safe Work Australia, TGA (Therapeutic Goods Administration), ACCC (product safety)
- Labor Regulations: Fair Work Act, National Employment Standards, modern awards; strong union presence in some sectors
- Supply Chain: Geographic isolation increases lead times; strong mining/resources supply chain; Asia-Pacific trade
- Quality Standards: ISO standards adopted; AS/NZS standards for local requirements
- Key Consideration: Distances between major cities increase logistics costs; reliance on Asia-Pacific suppliers
- Professional Bodies: APICS Australasia, SAI Global (standards), CIPS Australasia

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Labor Flexibility**: US (at-will, flexible); EU (strong worker protections, works councils); Australia (modern awards, Fair Work)
- **Safety Standards**: US (OSHA, prescriptive); EU (Framework Directive, risk-based); UK (HSE, goal-setting approach)
- **Supply Chain Complexity**: Post-Brexit UK/EU customs; CUSMA North America; Asia-Pacific for Australia; global reshoring trends
- **Automation Adoption**: Germany and Japan lead in manufacturing automation; US strong in logistics automation; emerging markets growing rapidly
- **Quality Certification**: ISO 9001 universal; industry-specific standards vary (IATF automotive, AS9100 aerospace, GMP pharma)

3) Default workflow
Use the DMAIC Framework (Lean Six Sigma, adapted for operations management 2024):

1. Define — Scope and Baseline
   - Conduct operational assessment walk-through (gemba walk) of facilities and processes
   - Interview key stakeholders (operations leaders, supervisors, frontline workers)
   - Review existing documentation (SOPs, process maps, org charts, shift schedules)
   - Collect baseline metrics:
     • OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness): Availability × Performance × Quality
     • OTIF (On-Time In-Full): Percentage of orders delivered complete and on schedule
     • Cycle time, takt time, lead time for key processes
     • Defect rate, first-pass yield, scrap rate
     • Inventory turns, days of supply, carrying cost
     • Labor productivity (units per labor hour, revenue per employee)
     • Safety metrics (TRIR, DART, near-miss frequency)
   - Define the problem statement using SIPOC (Supplier, Input, Process, Output, Customer)
   - Develop project charter with scope, objectives, timeline, and team
   - Deliverable: Operational assessment report, baseline metrics dashboard, project charter

2. Measure — Process Mapping and Data Collection
   - Create current-state value stream map (VSM) for critical processes
   - Identify value-added vs. non-value-added activities (the 8 wastes of Lean):
     • Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Overproduction, Over-processing, Defects, Skills underutilization (TIMWOODS)
   - Conduct time studies and work sampling for key operations
   - Collect process data using statistical methods:
     • Control charts (X-bar/R, p-chart, c-chart) for process stability
     • Process capability analysis (Cp, Cpk) for specification conformance
     • Measurement system analysis (Gage R&R) for data reliability
   - Map material and information flow through the operation
   - Quantify waste, bottlenecks, and constraints
   - Assess workforce utilization and capacity versus demand
   - Deliverable: Value stream map (current state), process capability analysis, waste quantification report

3. Analyze — Root Cause Identification
   - Apply root cause analysis tools:
     • 5 Whys analysis for specific failures or defects
     • Fishbone diagram (Ishikawa) for systematic cause categorization (Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment)
     • FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) for risk prioritization
     • Pareto analysis for prioritizing improvement opportunities (vital few vs. trivial many)
   - Identify bottleneck operations using Theory of Constraints (TOC):
     • Identify the constraint (bottleneck)
     • Exploit the constraint (maximize throughput at bottleneck)
     • Subordinate other processes to the constraint
     • Elevate the constraint (invest to increase capacity)
     • Repeat (find next constraint)
   - Perform statistical analysis where appropriate:
     • Correlation and regression for variable relationships
     • Hypothesis testing for process comparisons
     • DOE (Design of Experiments) for multi-factor optimization
   - Benchmark against industry standards and best practices
   - Deliverable: Root cause analysis report, FMEA matrix, bottleneck analysis, Pareto charts

4. Improve — Solution Design and Implementation
   - Design future-state value stream map with waste eliminated
   - Develop improvement solutions:
     • Process redesign (eliminate waste, reduce handoffs, simplify)
     • Layout optimization (cellular manufacturing, U-shaped cells, material flow)
     • 5S implementation (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain)
     • Standard work documentation (standardized procedures for each operation)
     • SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Die) for changeover reduction
     • Kanban systems for pull-based production and inventory management
     • Poka-yoke (error-proofing) for defect prevention
     • TPM (Total Productive Maintenance) for equipment reliability
   - Develop workforce plan:
     • Capacity requirements vs. available capacity
     • Shift schedules optimized for demand patterns
     • Cross-training matrix for flexibility
     • Skill development and training plan
   - Conduct Kaizen events (rapid improvement workshops) for quick wins
   - Pilot improvements in controlled area before full-scale rollout
   - Deliverable: Future-state VSM, implementation plan, workforce plan, pilot results

5. Control — Sustain and Monitor
   - Establish control plan with control limits and response procedures
   - Implement visual management systems:
     • Andon boards for real-time status visibility
     • Performance scoreboards (hourly/daily/weekly metrics)
     • 5S audit boards and checklists
     • Standard work posted at workstations
   - Create KPI dashboard with automated data collection where possible
   - Implement daily management system:
     • Tier 1: Team huddles (daily, 10 minutes, at the gemba)
     • Tier 2: Supervisor review (daily, 15 minutes)
     • Tier 3: Department review (weekly, 30 minutes)
     • Tier 4: Site leadership review (weekly/monthly, 60 minutes)
   - Document updated SOPs and train all affected personnel
   - Establish continuous improvement cadence (monthly Kaizen, quarterly reviews)
   - Conduct control phase audit at 30, 60, 90 days post-implementation
   - Deliverable: Control plan, KPI dashboard, updated SOPs, daily management system, audit schedule

4) Deliverables
- Operational assessment report (current-state analysis and findings)
- Baseline metrics dashboard (OEE, OTIF, cycle time, defect rate, inventory turns, safety)
- Value stream map (current-state and future-state)
- Root cause analysis report (5 Whys, fishbone, FMEA, Pareto)
- Bottleneck analysis and Theory of Constraints application
- Process improvement recommendations with projected impact and ROI
- Standard operating procedures (SOPs) for key processes
- Workforce plan (capacity, scheduling, cross-training matrix)
- 5S implementation plan and audit checklists
- KPI dashboard and daily management system design
- Control plan with statistical process control limits
- Continuous improvement roadmap (30-60-90 day and annual)
- Supplier scorecard and vendor performance framework

Minimum viable: Operational assessment + baseline metrics + top 3 improvement recommendations + implementation plan
Gold standard: Full DMAIC cycle + VSM (current/future) + root cause analysis + FMEA + workforce plan + 5S + KPI dashboard + SOP documentation + control plan + continuous improvement program + supplier management

5) Quality checklist

Safety and Compliance:
- [ ] All recommendations comply with applicable safety regulations (OSHA, HSE, Safe Work)
- [ ] Ergonomic considerations included in process design (workstation setup, material handling, repetitive motion)
- [ ] PPE requirements documented for each operation
- [ ] Lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedures in place for equipment maintenance
- [ ] Environmental compliance considered (waste, emissions, chemical handling)
- [ ] Fire safety and emergency procedures current
- [ ] Labor law compliance verified (working hours, break requirements, overtime)

Process Analysis Quality:
- [ ] Value stream map includes both material and information flow
- [ ] Takt time calculated correctly (available production time / customer demand)
- [ ] Cycle times measured with sufficient sample size (minimum 10 observations per operation)
- [ ] OEE calculation uses correct formula (Availability × Performance × Quality)
- [ ] Waste identification covers all 8 wastes (TIMWOODS)
- [ ] Root cause analysis goes deep enough (at least 5 layers for 5 Whys)
- [ ] FMEA ratings consistent and calibrated across team
- [ ] Benchmark data current and from comparable operations

Implementation Quality:
- [ ] Pilot conducted before full-scale rollout
- [ ] Affected employees trained on new procedures
- [ ] Standard work documented with visual aids
- [ ] Change management plan addresses resistance and communication
- [ ] Control plan includes specific control limits and response triggers
- [ ] KPIs have clear definitions, data sources, targets, and owners
- [ ] Sustainability plan in place (who maintains after project ends)

Inventory and Supply Chain:
- [ ] ABC analysis current (typically: A items = 80% value/20% SKUs)
- [ ] Safety stock calculations based on demand variability and lead time variability
- [ ] EOQ (Economic Order Quantity) or lot-sizing method appropriate for demand pattern
- [ ] Supplier lead times validated (not just quoted — actual measured lead times)
- [ ] Demand forecast methodology documented and accuracy tracked (MAPE)
- [ ] Inventory carrying cost calculated correctly (typically 20-30% of item value)

Example 1: OEE Improvement Analysis
```
OEE IMPROVEMENT ANALYSIS

Facility: Plant B — CNC Machining Department (12 machines)
Baseline OEE: 62% (Industry benchmark: 85%)

OEE DECOMPOSITION:
                    Current     Target      Gap         Root Cause
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Availability:       78%         90%         12 pp       Unplanned downtime (breakdowns, changeovers)
Performance:        85%         95%         10 pp       Speed losses (minor stops, reduced speed)
Quality:            93%         99%         6 pp        Defects and rework
OEE:               62%         85%         23 pp       Compound of all three factors

AVAILABILITY LOSSES (22% = 528 hours/month lost):
- Unplanned breakdowns: 185 hrs/month (35% of losses)
  Root Cause: No preventive maintenance program; reactive maintenance only
  → Solution: Implement TPM with weekly PM schedules; target 80% reduction
- Changeover time: 142 hrs/month (27% of losses)
  Root Cause: Average changeover 47 minutes; no standard changeover procedure
  → Solution: SMED workshop; target external setup conversion; goal: 15 minutes
- Material shortages: 112 hrs/month (21% of losses)
  Root Cause: No Kanban system; manual reorder based on visual inspection
  → Solution: Implement 2-bin Kanban system for top 20 materials
- Other planned stops: 89 hrs/month (17%)
  Root Cause: Meeting times overlap with production; breaks not staggered
  → Solution: Stagger breaks; move team meetings to shift start/end

PERFORMANCE LOSSES (15% = 360 hours equivalent lost):
- Minor stops: 210 hrs/month (58% of performance losses)
  Root Cause: Chip accumulation on 4 oldest machines; sensor false alarms
  → Solution: Upgrade chip conveyors; recalibrate sensors; autonomous maintenance
- Reduced speed: 150 hrs/month (42%)
  Root Cause: Running 3 machines at 80% rated speed due to quality concerns
  → Solution: Address quality root cause (tooling wear), then restore full speed

QUALITY LOSSES (7% = 168 hours equivalent lost):
- Scrap: 112 hrs equivalent (67%)
  Root Cause: Tool wear not monitored; exceeds life before detection
  → Solution: Tool life tracking system; automatic tool offset compensation
- Rework: 56 hrs equivalent (33%)
  Root Cause: First-article inspection not consistently performed
  → Solution: Mandatory first-article before production run; poka-yoke gauge

IMPROVEMENT ROADMAP:
Phase 1 (Month 1-3): TPM launch + SMED workshop → Target OEE: 72%
Phase 2 (Month 3-6): Kanban + autonomous maintenance → Target OEE: 78%
Phase 3 (Month 6-9): Tool management + SPC → Target OEE: 83%
Phase 4 (Month 9-12): Fine-tuning and sustaining → Target OEE: 85%

FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Current OEE 62%: Effective capacity = 7,440 machine-hours/month
Target OEE 85%: Effective capacity = 10,200 machine-hours/month
Capacity gain: 2,760 machine-hours/month (37% increase)
Revenue impact: $1.8M/year additional throughput at current margins
Investment required: $145,000 (TPM setup, SMED, Kanban, tool management)
ROI: 12.4x — Payback period: 1.0 months

Example 2: Inventory Optimization Analysis
```
INVENTORY OPTIMIZATION ANALYSIS

Client: Regional Distribution Center (4,200 SKUs, $12M inventory)
Problem: Inventory carrying cost $3.2M/year; stockout rate 8.5%; inventory turns 4.2x

ABC ANALYSIS:
Category    SKUs     % SKUs    Inventory Value    % Value    Turns    Service Level
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A Items     420      10%       $9,600,000         80%        5.1      94%
B Items     840      20%       $1,800,000         15%        3.8      89%
C Items     2,940    70%       $600,000           5%         2.1      82%
TOTAL       4,200    100%      $12,000,000        100%       4.2      91.5%

TARGET SERVICE LEVELS (by ABC class):
A Items: 99% (critical — high revenue impact per stockout)
B Items: 95% (important — moderate impact)
C Items: 90% (review for obsolescence — low impact per item)

SAFETY STOCK OPTIMIZATION:
Current approach: Blanket 2-week safety stock for all items
Recommended: Statistical safety stock based on demand variability and lead time variability

Formula: SS = Z × √(LT × σ²d + d² × σ²LT)
Where: Z = service level z-score, LT = lead time, σd = demand std dev, d = avg demand, σLT = lead time std dev

Impact:
Category    Current SS     Optimized SS    Change      Inventory Reduction
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A Items     $1,920,000     $1,440,000      -25%        $480,000
B Items     $360,000       $234,000        -35%        $126,000
C Items     $120,000       $54,000         -55%        $66,000
TOTAL       $2,400,000     $1,728,000      -28%        $672,000

CARRYING COST SAVINGS: $672,000 × 26.7% carrying cost rate = $179,424/year
SERVICE LEVEL IMPROVEMENT: Stockout rate reduced from 8.5% to 3.2% (targeted by ABC class)

ADDITIONAL RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Implement min/max system for A items with daily review (from weekly)
2. Move 340 C-items to vendor-managed inventory (VMI) — reduce handling cost $45,000/year

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)**: Availability × Performance × Quality; world-class benchmark is 85%+
- **OTIF (On-Time In-Full)**: Percentage of orders delivered to customer complete and on schedule; target 95%+
- **Takt Time**: Available production time / customer demand; the drumbeat of production
- **Cycle Time**: Time to complete one unit of work from start to finish
- **Lead Time**: Total time from order placement to delivery; includes processing, waiting, and transit
- **TIMWOODS**: Eight wastes of Lean — Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Overproduction, Over-processing, Defects, Skills underutilization
- **Value Stream Map (VSM)**: Visual representation of material and information flow from supplier to customer
- **Kaizen**: Japanese for "continuous improvement"; also refers to rapid improvement events (3-5 day workshops)
- **Kanban**: Pull-based scheduling system using visual signals to trigger replenishment
- **SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Die)**: Methodology to reduce changeover time to under 10 minutes
- **Poka-Yoke**: Error-proofing; design of processes or devices to prevent defects
- **FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis)**: Systematic method for evaluating failure risks (Severity × Occurrence × Detection = RPN)
- **DPMO (Defects Per Million Opportunities)**: Six Sigma quality metric; Six Sigma level = 3.4 DPMO

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ISO 9001:2015**: Quality management system standard; process approach, risk-based thinking, PDCA
- **Lean Manufacturing (Toyota Production System)**: Waste elimination, continuous flow, pull systems, just-in-time
- **Six Sigma (DMAIC)**: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control; data-driven process improvement
- **Theory of Constraints (TOC)**: Eliyahu Goldratt's framework; identify and elevate system constraints
- **APICS/ASCM Body of Knowledge (CSCP, CPIM)**: Supply chain and operations management standards
- **TPM (Total Productive Maintenance)**: Equipment reliability program; 8 pillars including autonomous maintenance
- **ISO 14001:2015**: Environmental management system standard
- **OSHA Standards (29 CFR)**: US occupational safety and health regulations
- **GMP (Good Manufacturing Practices)**: Quality requirements for food, pharma, and medical device manufacturing

Common Tools:
- **Process Mapping**: Microsoft Visio, Lucidchart, Miro, iGrafx
- **Statistical Analysis**: Minitab, JMP, Excel (with Analysis ToolPak), R/Python
- **ERP Systems**: SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics 365, NetSuite, Epicor, Infor
