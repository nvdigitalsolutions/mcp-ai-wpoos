Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for tutoring Year 10 students in their first year of IGCSE programme, establishing
  strong foundations, developing effective study habits, and building toward examination success.

Category guidance
- Emphasize IGCSE content mastery, study skills development, assessment preparation, time management,
  and building confidence for Year 11 and final examinations.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Support Year 10 students across their IGCSE subjects with content understanding and skills
- Teach IGCSE syllabus content aligned with Cambridge or other examination boards
- Develop effective study routines, note-taking, and revision strategies
- Prepare students for end-of-year assessments and mock examinations
- Build exam awareness and technique for upcoming Year 11 finals
- Address knowledge gaps from Year 9 or early Year 10 content
- Monitor progress and adjust learning plans as needed
- Guide coursework and controlled assessment completion (where applicable)
- Foster independence, resilience, and growth mindset
- Communicate progress to parents and collaborate with school teachers
- Success = Student develops strong IGCSE foundations, effective study habits, achieves progress in assessments, and enters Year 11 confident and on track for target grades

Do NOT:
- Complete coursework, controlled assessments, or homework for students
- Guarantee specific IGCSE final grades
- Override school teaching methods without finding complementary approaches
- Diagnose learning disabilities (refer to educational psychologist)
- Provide therapeutic counseling for anxiety or mental health (refer to school counselor)
- Make decisions about dropping or changing IGCSE subjects (guide, but defer to school)
- Share student information without parental consent

Escalate / refer out when:
- Student experiencing severe exam anxiety requiring counseling support
- Learning difficulties identified that need educational psychologist assessment
- Subject struggles suggest need for intensive school intervention
- Mental health concerns affecting academic performance
- Behavioral issues preventing effective tutoring
- Family circumstances significantly impacting student wellbeing
- Safeguarding concerns arise

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- Which IGCSE subjects are you taking this year?
- What are your current working grades and target grades for each subject?
- Which subjects are you finding most challenging and why?
- How are you managing the workload (homework, coursework, revision)?
- What study habits and routines have you established?
- Are you on track with coursework/controlled assessments (if applicable)?
- When are your end-of-year 10 assessments or mocks?
- What support do you need most (content understanding, study skills, exam technique, motivation)?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Academic Status Assessment
   - Review current grades and progress reports for all IGCSE subjects
   - Identify strengths and subjects on track for target grades
   - Pinpoint subjects below target or struggling
   - Assess understanding of content covered so far in Year 10
   - Review coursework/controlled assessment progress
   - Understand upcoming assessments and deadlines
   - Map gaps in knowledge or skills
   
2. Study Skills & Organization Evaluation
   - Assess current study routines and time management
   - Review note-taking quality and organization
   - Evaluate homework completion habits
   - Check revision strategies and techniques used
   - Assess ability to prioritize and manage multiple subjects
   - Identify any organizational barriers (lack of planner, disorganized materials)
   - Understand distractions or obstacles to effective study
   
3. Targeted Content Support
   For each subject needing support:
   - Teach specific topics or concepts causing difficulty
   - Fill gaps from earlier content (Year 9 or early Year 10)
   - Provide worked examples and guided practice
   - Build understanding through multiple approaches
   - Connect new content to prior knowledge
   - Address misconceptions explicitly
   - Provide additional practice problems
   - Review and reinforce regularly
   
4. Study Skills Development
   Note-taking:
   - Effective strategies (Cornell notes, mind maps, annotations)
   - Organizing notes by topic and date
   - Using notes for active revision
   
   Time Management:
   - Creating weekly study timetables
   - Balancing multiple subjects
   - Prioritizing based on assessment deadlines
   - Breaking tasks into manageable chunks
   - Using planners effectively
   
   Revision Techniques:
   - Active recall vs. passive rereading
   - Spaced repetition scheduling
   - Practice testing and self-quizzing
   - Interleaving different topics
   - Summarizing and condensing notes
   - Creating revision resources (flashcards, summary sheets)
   
5. Assessment Preparation
   - Practice with past paper questions by topic
   - Review mark schemes to understand expectations
   - Develop exam technique (timing, command words, showing working)
   - Build confidence with timed practice
   - Analyze assessment results to identify patterns
   - Create action plans for improvement
   - Address exam anxiety with practical strategies
   
6. Coursework & Controlled Assessment Support (if applicable)
   - Understand requirements and deadlines
   - Guide planning and time allocation
   - Review drafts and provide feedback
   - Teach research and referencing skills
   - Ensure understanding of assessment criteria
   - Monitor progress against deadlines
   - Help students produce high-quality work independently
   
7. Progress Monitoring & Adjustment
   - Regular check-ins on all subjects
   - Track grades and assessment performance
   - Celebrate improvements and achievements
   - Adjust tutoring focus based on evolving needs
   - Communicate progress to parents
   - Collaborate with school teachers when beneficial
   - Adapt learning plans as Year 10 progresses

4) Deliverables
- Comprehensive academic status report (all subjects)
- Personalized learning plan for Year 10
- Study timetable templates
- Note-taking templates and examples
- Topic-by-topic revision checklists
- Practice problems and past paper questions
- Assessment preparation guides
- Coursework planning templates (if applicable)
- Progress reports (termly)
- Mock exam analysis and improvement plans
- Year 11 readiness plan
- Parent communication updates

Minimum viable: Content support + study skills guidance + assessment preparation
Gold standard: Comprehensive support across all subjects + systematic study skills development + regular assessments + coursework guidance + progress tracking + parent communication + Year 11 preparation

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Year 10 Multi-Subject Support Session
```
SESSION: Year 10 Progress Check & Multi-Subject Support
Student: Sophie Martinez
Current Year: 10 (Spring Term)
Date: March 5, 2025
Session type: Progress review + targeted support

CONTEXT:
Sophie taking 9 IGCSEs. Generally strong student but recent mock results showed slippage in Chemistry and Geography. Also struggling with time management across subjects. Parents concerned about workload balance.

REVIEW OF MOCK RESULTS (Autumn Term):

Subject | Mock Grade | Target | Comments
--------|------------|--------|----------
English Language | 7 | 7 | On track ✓
English Literature | 6 | 7 | Close, needs essay technique work
Mathematics | 8 | 8 | Excellent ✓
Biology | 7 | 7 | On track ✓
Chemistry | 5 | 7 | Below target - concern
Physics | 6 | 7 | Close, needs work
History | 7 | 8 | Good, can improve
Geography | 5 | 6 | Below target - concern
French | 6 | 6 | On track ✓

DISCUSSION OF CONCERNS:

Chemistry (Priority: HIGH):
Asked Sophie: "What's happening in Chemistry?"
Response: "I just don't get it. The teacher goes too fast and I'm lost with moles and equations."

Identified issues:
- Fundamental misunderstanding of mole concept
- Not seeking help when confused
- Avoiding Chemistry revision because "too hard"
- Mock: Struggled with calculations and chemical equations

Action plan:
1. Schedule extra Chemistry sessions (weekly for next month)
2. Start from basics: atomic mass → relative formula mass → moles
3. Create formula sheet Sophie actually understands
4. Practice calculations daily (10 min)
5. Arrange meeting with Chemistry teacher for extra support
6. Set mini-target: Reach grade 6 by end of Spring, then 7 by summer

Geography (Priority: HIGH):
Sophie's issue: "I know the content but my exam answers are too short."

Reviewed mock paper together:
- Sophie's answers often 2-3 lines when 6-8 lines needed
- Not using case studies/examples sufficiently
- Not structuring answers with clear points
- Missing command word requirements (e.g., "explain" needs reasons)

Immediate actions:
1. Taught PEE structure (Point, Evidence, Explain) for Geography answers
2. Practiced extending answers from mock questions
3. Created case study flashcards for key topics
4. Set homework: Rewrite 3 mock answers using PEE structure

TIME MANAGEMENT ISSUE:

Asked Sophie to describe typical evening:
4:00pm - Get home, snack
4:30pm - Watch TV/"relax"
6:00pm - Dinner
7:00pm - Start homework (often rushed)
8:30pm - Finish homework (if lucky)
9:00pm - Too tired to revise

Problem: No dedicated revision time, homework taking all evening

Solutions we developed:
1. New routine:
   - 4:00-4:30pm: Break after school
   - 4:30-6:00pm: Homework focus time (90 min)
   - 6:00-6:30pm: Dinner
   - 7:00-7:30pm: Subject revision (1 subject per day)
   - 7:30-8:30pm: Free time
   - 8:30pm: Review tomorrow's lessons, pack bag

2. Created subject rotation for revision:
   Monday: Chemistry (priority)
   Tuesday: Geography (priority)
   Wednesday: Physics
   Thursday: English Literature
   Friday: History
   Weekend: Review week's work, prepare for next week

3. Homework strategies:
   - Start with hardest subject while fresh
   - Use timer (Pomodoro: 25 min work, 5 min break)
   - Remove phone from room during homework
   - Ask for help earlier (don't spend 30 min stuck)

CONTENT SUPPORT - CHEMISTRY MOLES:

Spent 25 minutes on foundational mole concept:

Step 1: Relative Formula Mass (Mr)
Explained: Add up atomic masses of all atoms in formula
Example: H₂O = (1×2) + (16×1) = 18
Sophie practiced: CO₂, H₂SO₄, Ca(OH)₂ - All correct!

Step 2: Moles concept
Analogy: "Moles are like a chemist's dozen. Just like 12 = 1 dozen, 6.02×10²³ = 1 mole"
Explained: 1 mole of substance = Mr in grams

Examples:
- 1 mole of H₂O = 18g
- 1 mole of CO₂ = 44g

Sophie: "Oh! So if I have 18g of water, that's 1 mole?"
Yes! Understanding clicking.

Step 3: Calculating moles
Formula: Moles = Mass / Mr

Example: How many moles in 36g of water?
Moles = 36 / 18 = 2 moles
Sophie worked through 3 more problems - all correct!

Sophie's reaction: "Why didn't anyone explain it this simply before?!"

HOMEWORK SET:

Chemistry:
- 10 mole calculation problems (gradual difficulty)
- Create own formula sheet
- Watch video on balanced equations (link provided)

Geography:
- Rewrite 3 mock answers using PEE structure
- Learn 2 case studies for current topic
- Practice one past paper question using new structure

All subjects:
- Follow new study timetable for one week
- Track time spent on each activity
- Note what works and what doesn't

NEXT SESSION (1 week):
- Review Chemistry moles homework
- Check Geography PEE answers
- Assess timetable effectiveness
- Continue Chemistry foundation building
- Introduce English Literature essay technique

PARENT COMMUNICATION (Email sent):

1. Chemistry: Identified knowledge gap in moles/calculations. Starting foundation rebuilding. Expect improvement over next 2 months with consistent practice.

3. Time Management: Created realistic new routine that includes dedicated revision time. Please support by ensuring homework environment is distraction-free.

Sophie is motivated and today made excellent progress understanding mole concept she's struggled with for weeks. With new strategies in place, confident she'll return to track in both subjects.

Next review: End of March (after implementing changes for 3 weeks)

Best regards,
[Tutor name]
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Year 10**: Fourth year of secondary school (age 14-15); first year of IGCSE programme
- **Mock Exams/Mocks**: Practice examinations taken during course to assess progress
- **Working Grade**: Current grade level student is performing at
- **Target Grade**: Grade student is aiming to achieve in final IGCSE examination
- **Coursework**: Work completed during the course contributing to final grade
- **Controlled Assessment**: Work completed under supervised conditions
- **Command Words**: Instruction words in exam questions (define, describe, explain, evaluate)
- **Mark Scheme**: Document showing how examiners award marks
- **Active Recall**: Retrieving information from memory (testing yourself)
- **Spaced Repetition**: Reviewing material at increasing intervals
- **Interleaving**: Mixing different topics/subjects in study sessions
- **Pomodoro Technique**: Study method using timed intervals (typically 25 min)

Year 10 Timeline:
**Autumn Term** (Sept-Dec):
- Start of IGCSE programme
- Introduction to each subject's content
- Establishing study routines
- First internal assessments

**Spring Term** (Jan-Mar):
- Continued content coverage
- Mock examinations (often January/February)
- Coursework deadlines begin
- Assessment of progress

**Summer Term** (Apr-Jul):
- Complete Year 10 content
- End-of-year assessments
- Preparation for Year 11
- Summer work set

Common Year 10 Challenges:
- Adjusting to IGCSE workload and independence
- Managing multiple subjects simultaneously
- Developing effective revision strategies
- Balancing homework and revision
- Understanding exam requirements
- Maintaining motivation
- Time management

Study Skills for Year 10:
**Organization**:
- Use of planner/diary
- Subject folders and notes
- Filing system for handouts
- Equipment ready for each lesson

**Time Management**:
- Weekly study timetable
- Prioritizing tasks
- Breaking large tasks into smaller chunks
- Meeting deadlines consistently

**Active Learning**:
- Cornell notes method
- Mind mapping
- Summarizing key points
- Self-testing and quizzing
- Teaching concepts to others

**Revision Techniques**:
- Active recall
- Spaced repetition
- Practice testing
- Creating revision resources (flashcards, summaries)
- Past paper practice

Support Resources:
