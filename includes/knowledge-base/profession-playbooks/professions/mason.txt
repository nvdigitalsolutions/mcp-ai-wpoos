Profession Playbook — Mason
Slug: mason
Category: other

Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality masonry guidance and planning.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety protocols, code compliance, hands-on limitations, and clear planning steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on masonry project design, planning, and specifications
- Explain IBC (International Building Code) Chapter 21 and TMS 402/602-22 requirements
- Help plan brick, stone, and concrete block construction projects
- Advise on material selection, mortar mixing ratios, and bonding patterns
- Recommend appropriate tools, equipment, and safety measures
- Explain OSHA construction safety standards for masonry work
- Guide layout techniques and structural considerations for masonry walls
- Provide documentation templates for material lists, specifications, and project plans
- Advise on pointing, tuckpointing, and finishing techniques
- Success = Safe, code-compliant masonry plans with accurate specifications and proper structural design

Do NOT:
- Perform hands-on masonry work (AI cannot physically lay brick, stone, or block)
- Guarantee code approval or pass/fail inspection outcomes
- Override local jurisdiction requirements or building official decisions
- Provide structural engineering calculations without proper engineering license
- Recommend work that violates IBC, TMS standards, OSHA, or manufacturer specifications
- Make decisions about existing structures without proper inspection
- Advise on demolition without addressing structural concerns and hazardous materials
- Guarantee specific load-bearing capacities without engineering analysis

Escalate / refer out when:
- Hazardous conditions exist (structural instability, unsafe scaffolding, fall hazards, silica dust exposure)
- Structural modifications affecting load-bearing walls or foundations (engage structural engineer)
- Complex arches, vaults, or buttresses requiring specialized engineering (engage structural engineer)
- Suspected structural damage, settlement, or deterioration requiring professional assessment
- Retaining walls exceeding 4 feet in height or with surcharge loads (engage structural engineer)
- Work requires special permits or engineering stamps
- Historic masonry restoration requiring specialized expertise (engage preservation specialist)
- Chimney structural issues or code violations (life safety issue)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What region or jurisdiction will this masonry work be performed in? (Affects building codes, standards, measurement systems)
- What is the project type (new construction, repair, restoration, veneer, structural masonry)?
- What masonry units are preferred (brick, stone, concrete block, combination)?
- What is the building type and use (residential, commercial, load-bearing, non-load-bearing)?
- What are the local building codes and jurisdiction requirements (IBC, amendments, local codes)?
- What are the structural requirements (load-bearing walls, retaining walls, veneer only)?
- What is the existing condition of the structure (if repair/restoration work)?
- What are the exposure conditions (interior, exterior, ground contact, freeze-thaw cycles)?
- What aesthetic preferences exist (bond pattern, joint finish, color matching)?

1.5) Regional Considerations

Masonry construction follows universal structural principles, but codes, materials, and standards vary:

**Building Codes & Standards:**
- **United States**: IBC Chapter 21 (Masonry); TMS 402/602 (Building Code Requirements for Masonry Structures); ACI 530; state/local amendments
- **Canada**: CSA A371 (Masonry Construction); National Building Code masonry provisions; metric units standard
- **United Kingdom**: BS EN 1996 (Eurocode 6 - Design of Masonry Structures); BS 5628 (older UK code); emphasis on sustainability
- **Australia**: AS 3700 (Masonry Structures); AS 4773 (Masonry in Small Buildings)

**Measurement Systems:**
- **United States**: Imperial measurements (8" x 4" x 2⅜" modular brick); mortar joint 3/8" typical
- **Canada/International**: Metric measurements (190mm x 90mm x 57mm brick); 10mm joint typical

**Materials & Standards:**
- **North America**: ASTM C55 (Concrete Block), C62/C216 (Clay Brick), C270 (Mortar); Type N/S/M mortar designations
- **Europe/UK**: EN standards for masonry units; mortar designation by strength (M4, M6, M12); different brick/block sizing
- **Australia**: AS/NZS 4456 (Masonry Units); AS 3700 mortar designation by compressive strength

**Licensing & Apprenticeship:**
- **United States**: State licensing varies (contractor license common, trade certification voluntary); NCCER, BAC/IMI training programs
- **Canada**: Red Seal Bricklayer (interprovincial); provincial apprenticeship programs (typically 3 years)
- **UK**: NVQ Level 2-3 in Trowel Occupations; CSCS card for construction sites
- **Australia**: Certificate III in Bricklaying/Blocklaying; state licensing for contractors

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Assessment & Planning
   - Review site conditions and verify foundation adequacy for masonry loads
   - Measure dimensions accurately and check for level, plumb, and square
   - Identify code requirements for project type and jurisdiction (IBC Chapter 21, TMS 402/602-22)
   - Assess existing masonry condition if repair work (cracks, deterioration, moisture issues)
   - Verify soil conditions for retaining walls or foundation work
   - Determine exposure category for freeze-thaw and weather resistance requirements
   
2. Design & Specification
   - Select appropriate masonry units per ASTM standards (C55, C62, C90, C216)
   - Specify mortar type per ASTM C270 (Type N, S, M based on application)
   - Design bond pattern (running bond, common bond, Flemish bond, English bond)
   - Calculate wall thickness and reinforcement requirements per IBC and TMS 402
   - Plan joint spacing and control joints per TMS 402 Section 5.2
   - Specify flashing, weep holes, and drainage details per building science principles
   - Determine joint finish (concave, flush, weathered, raked) for aesthetics and water resistance
   
3. Documentation & Material Planning
   - Prepare masonry elevations and construction drawings
   - Create material quantity takeoffs (units, mortar, reinforcement, anchors)
   - Calculate mortar quantities (typically 7-8 cubic feet per 100 square feet of wall)
   - Account for waste factors (typically 5% for units, 15-25% for mortar)
   - Prepare detailed specifications including ASTM standards references
   - Document safety procedures including fall protection and silica dust control
   - Create project schedule accounting for mortar curing time and weather constraints
   
4. Installation Guidance (for skilled masons)
   - Verify all materials meet specifications and ASTM standards (check manufacturer certs)
   - Follow OSHA requirements for scaffolding (1926.451) and fall protection (1926.501)
   - Mix mortar per ASTM C270 specifications and use within 2.5 hours
   - Lay corner leads first and use string lines to maintain level, plumb, and alignment
   - Maintain consistent joint thickness (typically 3/8" for brick, 1/2" for block)
   - Ensure full bedding and proper bond strength by buttering all contact surfaces
   - Strike joints at proper time (when mortar thumbprint hard) for weather resistance
   - Install reinforcement, anchors, and flashing per engineered design
   - Control silica dust exposure using water suppression or vacuum systems when cutting
   - Protect work from freezing and excessive heat during curing
   
5. Quality Control & Inspection
   - Verify walls are plumb (within 1/4" per 10 feet) and level
   - Check joint quality (full mortar coverage, proper tooling, no voids)
   - Ensure proper bond pattern maintained and units properly aligned
   - Verify reinforcement, flashing, and weep holes installed per specifications
   - Inspect for cracking, spalling, or efflorescence
   - Conduct structural special inspections per IBC Chapter 17 and TMS 402
   - Document work with photos before concealment (reinforcement, flashing, grouting)
   - Address punch list items and deficiencies
   - Obtain inspection approvals before proceeding to next phase

4) Deliverables
- Material lists with masonry unit specifications, mortar type, and quantities
- Masonry elevations and construction drawings
- Bond pattern layouts and details
- Material takeoff spreadsheets
- Mortar mixing specifications and batch sheets
- Reinforcement and anchor schedules
- Flashing and drainage detail drawings

Minimum viable: Accurate material list, basic masonry elevation, mortar specifications, and safety requirements
Gold standard: Complete construction drawings, detailed bond patterns, reinforcement schedules, 3D renderings, comprehensive safety plan, special inspection protocols, and quality assurance documentation

5) Quality checklist

Structural Integrity:
- [ ] Load-bearing walls designed per TMS 402 with adequate thickness and reinforcement
- [ ] Wall thickness appropriate for height and exposure conditions (IBC Table 2109.3.4.1)
- [ ] Reinforcement properly placed and sized per engineered design
- [ ] Proper bearing provided at beam and joist supports (minimum 4" for masonry)
- [ ] Control joints located to control cracking (maximum 25 feet spacing for concrete block)
- [ ] Proper anchorage to structural framing with approved connectors
- [ ] Lintels properly sized for openings with adequate bearing length

Code Compliance:
- [ ] All work complies with IBC Chapter 21 Masonry requirements
- [ ] Masonry units meet ASTM standards (C55, C62, C90, C216 as applicable)
- [ ] Mortar type appropriate for application per ASTM C270 (Type N, S, M, or O)
- [ ] Special inspection requirements met per IBC Chapter 17
- [ ] Fire resistance ratings achieved if required for occupancy type
- [ ] Proper clearances from combustible materials maintained
- [ ] Seismic detailing per TMS 402 Chapter 9 if in seismic design category C or higher

Measurement & Layout:
- [ ] Foundation level and adequate for masonry loads
- [ ] Layout marks clearly established with corner leads and string lines
- [ ] Walls plumb within 1/4" per 10 feet
- [ ] Courses level within 1/4" per 10 feet
- [ ] Openings properly dimensioned with square corners (diagonals equal within 1/4")
- [ ] Bond pattern maintained consistently throughout project
- [ ] Joint thickness consistent (3/8" for brick, 1/2" for concrete block typical)

Materials & Workmanship:
- [ ] Masonry units free from cracks, chips, and excessive variation
- [ ] Units properly wetted before laying (brick) or kept dry (concrete block)
- [ ] Mortar mixed to proper consistency and workability
- [ ] Full mortar bedding on all horizontal and vertical joints
- [ ] Head joints completely filled (no voids)
- [ ] Proper bond between mortar and units achieved
- [ ] Joints tooled at proper time for water resistance and durability
- [ ] Units laid with shiner, stretcher, header, or soldier orientation as specified
- [ ] Masonry ties and anchors installed per specifications

Safety:
- [ ] Fall protection in place for work above 6 feet per OSHA 1926.501
- [ ] Scaffolding properly erected per OSHA 1926.451 with proper load capacity
- [ ] Personal protective equipment worn (hard hat, safety glasses, gloves, steel-toed boots)
- [ ] Silica dust control measures implemented (water suppression, wet cutting, ventilation)
- [ ] Respirators provided and used when silica dust exposure exceeds OSHA PEL
- [ ] Masonry saw guards and safety features functional
- [ ] Work area clear of trip hazards and materials properly stacked
- [ ] Proper lifting techniques used for heavy units (team lifts, mechanical aids)
- [ ] Extension cords and power tools inspected and grounded

Moisture Protection:
- [ ] Proper flashing installed at foundation, shelf angles, and openings
- [ ] Weep holes provided at 24" on center maximum spacing
- [ ] Drainage space maintained behind veneer (1" minimum air space)
- [ ] Through-wall flashing properly lapped and sealed
- [ ] Proper slope provided for water drainage away from structure
- [ ] Coping and caps installed on parapet walls for weather protection
- [ ] Expansion joints and sealants installed per manufacturer specifications

FALL PROTECTION METHODS:

- [ ] Mid-rails and toe boards installed
- [ ] Access gates or removable sections properly secured when not in use
- [ ] Ladders properly secured and extending 3 feet above landing
- [ ] Hole covers secured and marked

SCAFFOLDING REQUIREMENTS (OSHA 1926.451):
- [ ] Scaffolding erected by competent person
- [ ] Load capacity adequate for masonry materials (typically 75 psf)
- [ ] Platforms fully planked with no gaps greater than 1"
- [ ] Platforms minimum 18" wide
- [ ] Mud sills or base plates on firm, level footing
- [ ] Proper bracing and anchoring to structure
- [ ] Daily inspection before use
- [ ] Access ladders secured and proper angle

PPE REQUIREMENTS:
Required for all workers:
- [ ] Hard hats (ANSI Z89.1) at all times
- [ ] Safety glasses (ANSI Z87.1) at all times
- [ ] Work gloves for material handling (leather or reinforced)
- [ ] Steel-toed boots
- [ ] Long sleeves for cement/lime contact protection

Task-specific:
- [ ] Dust masks/respirators (N95 minimum) required when cutting masonry or silica dust present
- [ ] Hearing protection when noise levels exceed 85 dBA (power saws)
- [ ] Knee pads for ground-level work
- [ ] Chemical-resistant gloves when handling mortar materials

SILICA DUST CONTROL (OSHA 1926.1153):
Crystalline silica exposure is a serious hazard in masonry work. Control measures:
- [ ] Use water suppression when cutting brick, block, or stone (wet cutting)
- [ ] Use vacuum dust collection systems on power saws
- [ ] Use proper ventilation in enclosed spaces
- [ ] Limit dry cutting to emergencies only
- [ ] Clean work area with HEPA vacuum or wet methods (no dry sweeping or air hoses)
- [ ] Provide respirators (half-face with P100 filters minimum) when engineering controls insufficient
- [ ] Monitor exposure levels if extensive cutting operations

TOOL & EQUIPMENT SAFETY:
- [ ] Masonry saws equipped with blade guards
- [ ] Electrical tools inspected before use (no damaged cords)
- [ ] Extension cords inspected (GFCI protected)
- [ ] Mortar mixers properly grounded
- [ ] Lockout/tagout procedures for equipment maintenance
- [ ] Proper ergonomic techniques for lifting heavy units (team lifts for >50 lbs)

MATERIAL HANDLING:
- [ ] Masonry units stored on level surface, stacked securely (max 6-7 feet high)
- [ ] Units stored away from edges and traffic areas
- [ ] Brick cubes banded and not leaning
- [ ] Proper lifting techniques used (bend knees, not back)
- [ ] Mechanical aids used when feasible (forklifts, hoists, material carts)

CHEMICAL HAZARDS:
Portland cement and lime are alkaline and can cause burns:
- [ ] Safety data sheets (SDS) for all materials on-site
- [ ] Wash stations or water available for cement contact
- [ ] First aid procedures posted for cement burns
- [ ] Bags opened carefully to minimize dust
- [ ] Wet mortar contact with skin minimized, wash immediately if contact occurs

SITE SAFETY:
- [ ] First aid kit location: [Specify]
- [ ] Fire extinguisher location: [Specify]
- [ ] Emergency contact numbers posted
- [ ] Eyewash station location: [Specify]
- [ ] Material staging areas designated and marked
- [ ] Debris removal planned (dumpster location)
- [ ] Adequate lighting for work areas
- [ ] Traffic control if near roadways

WEATHER PRECAUTIONS:
- [ ] No masonry work when temperature below 40°F or forecast to freeze within 24 hours
- [ ] Hot weather (above 90°F): Shade materials, use cooler water, protect fresh work
- [ ] Wind: Secure tarps and loose materials, suspend work if high winds
- [ ] Rain: Protect work in progress, cover walls overnight

EMERGENCY PROCEDURES:
In case of emergency:
1. Secure the scene and call 911 if needed
2. Notify project supervisor: [Phone]
3. Administer first aid if trained
   - Cement burns: Flush with water for 15 minutes minimum, seek medical attention
   - Falls: Do not move injured person unless immediate danger, call 911
   - Silica exposure: Remove from area, provide fresh air, seek medical evaluation
4. Document incident and complete incident report

DAILY SAFETY BRIEFING TOPICS:
- Hazards specific to day's work
- Weather conditions affecting safety
- Scaffolding inspection results
- New workers or tasks requiring special attention
- Review of previous safety incidents/near misses

- Quantity: 7.0 units per SF = 2,100 bricks for 300 SF wall

MORTAR:
- Type: Type N mortar per ASTM C270
- Mix: 1 part Portland cement : 1 part hydrated lime : 6 parts sand
- Joint thickness: 3/8" horizontal and vertical
- Joint finish: Concave tooled for weather resistance
- Color: Natural gray

BOND PATTERN:
- Running bond (most common, economical, structurally sound)
- 1/2 lap pattern with vertical joints staggered

WALL CONSTRUCTION:

- Grout: Fine grout in cells with reinforcement

- Design load: 1,500 lbs per linear foot at top of wall
- Wall designed per TMS 402 Building Code Requirements for Masonry
- Control joints: Maximum 25 feet spacing (at 20' and 40' marks)
- Bond beam: Horizontal grouted bond beam at top of wall with (2) #5 bars

- Verify reinforcement placement before grouting
- Observe grout placement and consolidation
- Sample mortar for compression testing (3 samples minimum)
- Inspect completed wall for plumb and alignment

CONSTRUCTION NOTES:
- Cells to be grouted shall be kept clean and free of mortar droppings

Example 3: Brick Pointing and Restoration Specification
```
HISTORIC BRICK WALL REPOINTING

EXISTING CONDITIONS:
- Building: 1920s residential structure
- Brick type: Historic clay brick (hard-fired, low absorption)
- Existing mortar: Deteriorated lime-based mortar with 50% joint loss
- Wall exposure: North-facing exterior with freeze-thaw cycles

ASSESSMENT:
- Mortar deterioration due to age and water infiltration
- Some brick face spalling from moisture trapped behind hard cement pointing
- Original mortar tested: High lime content, soft and permeable

RESTORATION APPROACH - MATCH HISTORIC MORTAR:
- Remove deteriorated mortar to depth of 2-1/2 times joint width (minimum 1")
- Use hand tools and light mechanical methods (no aggressive grinding)
- Avoid damage to brick faces during mortar removal

REPLACEMENT MORTAR:
- Type: Historic lime-based mortar (Type O or weaker)
- Mix: 1 part natural cement : 2 parts lime putty : 9 parts sand
- Must be softer and more permeable than existing brick (critical for historic work)
- Color matched to original through sand selection and pigments if needed

REPOINTING PROCEDURE:
1. Clean joints thoroughly with brush and water rinse
2. Pre-wet joints before pointing (but no standing water)
3. Pack mortar in layers, filling from back to front
4. Tool joints to match historic profile when thumbprint hard
5. Keep newly pointed areas damp for 3-5 days (mist spray)
6. Protect from rain, direct sun, and freezing temperatures

QUALITY CRITERIA:
- New mortar shall be slightly recessed from brick faces (1/16"-1/8")
- Joints completely filled with no voids
- Joint profile matches historic tooling (likely concave or grapevine)
- Color and texture compatible with building's historic character
- No mortar smears or stains on brick faces

HISTORIC PRESERVATION STANDARDS:
- Work to comply with Secretary of Interior's Standards
- Maintain historic character and materials compatibility
- Document work with before, during, and after photos
- Consider consulting historic preservation specialist for significant structures
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Bed Joint**: Horizontal mortar joint between courses of masonry units
- **Head Joint**: Vertical mortar joint between ends of adjacent units
- **Course**: Horizontal layer of masonry units bonded with mortar
- **Wythe**: Each continuous vertical section of a masonry wall one unit in thickness
- **Bond Pattern**: Arrangement of masonry units (running bond, common bond, Flemish, English, stack)
- **Collar Joint**: Vertical longitudinal space between wythes of masonry
- **Stretcher**: Masonry unit laid flat with longest dimension parallel to wall face
- **Header**: Masonry unit laid flat with shortest dimension parallel to wall face (ties wythes together)
- **Soldier**: Unit set vertically with narrow face showing (often used above openings)
- **Shiner**: Unit laid flat with largest surface showing
- **Tuckpointing**: Process of removing deteriorated mortar and replacing with new mortar
- **Efflorescence**: White crystalline or powdery deposit on masonry surface from water-soluble salts
- **Weep Hole**: Opening in mortar joint to allow water drainage from masonry cavity
- **Flashing**: Impervious material used to direct water away from wall interior
- **Control Joint**: Continuous vertical joint allowing for planned cracking and movement
- **Expansion Joint**: Joint allowing for thermal expansion and contraction of masonry
- **Prism**: Test assembly of masonry units and mortar used to determine compressive strength
- **Grout**: Fluid mixture of cementitious materials used to fill cells in hollow masonry units

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **IBC Chapter 21**: International Building Code requirements for masonry construction (2024 edition)
- **TMS 402/602-22**: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures (The Masonry Society)
- **ASTM C270**: Standard Specification for Mortar for Unit Masonry (defines Types M, S, N, O)
- **ASTM C216**: Standard Specification for Facing Brick (solid units for exterior walls)
- **ASTM C90**: Standard Specification for Loadbearing Concrete Masonry Units
- **ASTM C55**: Standard Specification for Concrete Building Brick
- **ASTM C62**: Standard Specification for Building Brick (solid units)
- **ASTM C144**: Standard Specification for Aggregate for Masonry Mortar
- **OSHA 1926.451**: Scaffolding safety standards
- **OSHA 1926.501**: Fall protection requirements
- **OSHA 1926.1153**: Respirable Crystalline Silica standards

Common Tools & Equipment:
- **Hand Tools**: Brick trowels (10"-11" London pattern), pointing trowel, jointer/striking tool, mason's level (48" or longer), string line, line pins, brick hammer, chisel set
- **Measuring**: Tape measure, mason's rule, story pole, corner blocks, chalk line
- **Cutting Tools**: Masonry saw (gas or electric with diamond blade), brick set chisel, angle grinder with diamond blade
- **Mixing**: Mortar mixer (electric or gas-powered paddle mixer), wheelbarrow, mortar boards, mixing hoe
- **Safety Equipment**: Hard hat, safety glasses, dust mask/respirator, work gloves, steel-toe boots, knee pads
- **Scaffolding**: Adjustable scaffolding, scaffold planks, guardrails, outriggers
- **Testing**: Water absorption test equipment, mortar penetrometer, prism testing apparatus

Mortar Types Quick Reference:
- **Type M** (2,500 psi): High-strength applications, below grade, high compression, earth contact
- **Type S** (1,800 psi): General structural use, load-bearing walls, below grade, high lateral strength
- **Type N** (750 psi): General purpose, above-grade veneer, non-load-bearing walls, interior walls
- **Type O** (350 psi): Low-strength applications, interior non-load-bearing, repointing historic masonry

Bond Patterns:
- **Running Bond**: Most common, each course offset by 1/2 unit length
- **Common Bond**: Running bond with header course every 5th-7th course for structural tie
- **Flemish Bond**: Alternating headers and stretchers in same course, highly decorative
- **English Bond**: Alternating courses of headers and stretchers
- **Stack Bond**: Units aligned vertically, requires reinforcement (weak structurally)
