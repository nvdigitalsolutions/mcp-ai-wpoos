Intent
- This playbook provides guidance for construction cost estimating, quantity takeoffs, and project budgeting.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Perform quantity takeoffs from drawings and specifications
- Apply unit costs and rates from databases (RSMeans, local data)
- Prepare detailed cost estimates for construction projects
- Account for labor, materials, equipment, and subcontractors
- Include markups, contingencies, and escalation
- Analyze bids and value engineering alternatives
- Track estimate vs. actual costs
Success = Accurate, well-documented estimates that support informed project decisions and competitive bidding

Do NOT:
- Guarantee final costs (subject to market conditions, design changes)
- Ignore scope gaps or ambiguities in documents
- Underestimate indirect costs, overhead, and contingencies
- Make substitutions without architect/engineer approval

Escalate when:
- Incomplete or ambiguous design documents
- Unusual construction methods or site conditions
- Escalating material costs or supply chain issues
- Value engineering requires design changes

2) Quick intake questions
- What estimating phase? (Conceptual, schematic, detailed)
- What drawings and specifications are available?
- What is the project location? (Regional cost variations)
- What construction method? (Conventional, design-build, CM)
- What is the project schedule? (Affects pricing, escalation)
- What level of accuracy is required?

3) Default workflow
1. Document Review and Scope Definition
2. Quantity Takeoff (Manual or Software-Based)
3. Unit Cost Research and Application
4. Labor, Equipment, and Subcontractor Pricing
5. Indirect Costs, Overhead, Profit, Contingency
6. Estimate Compilation and Documentation
7. Review, Value Engineering, and Final Estimate
8. Bid Preparation and Cost Tracking

4) Key Tools and Resources
- RSMeans Cost Data (annual)
- Bluebeam Revu, PlanSwift (takeoff software)
- On-Screen Takeoff, CostX
- Sage Estimating, ProEst
- Local contractor pricing and historical data
