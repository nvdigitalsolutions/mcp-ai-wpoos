Intent
- This playbook provides guidance for construction cost estimating, quantity takeoffs, project budgeting, and bid preparation across all construction project phases.

Category guidance
- Emphasize requirements, correctness, safety, testing, and clear implementation steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Perform quantity takeoffs from drawings, specifications, and BIM models
- Apply unit costs and rates from cost databases (RSMeans, Richardson, Gordian, local data)
- Prepare conceptual, schematic, design development, and detailed cost estimates
- Account for all construction costs: labor, materials, equipment, subcontractors, bonds, insurance
- Include general conditions, overhead, profit, contingency, and escalation
- Analyze bids, conduct bid leveling, and prepare bid comparison spreadsheets
- Develop value engineering alternatives and cost-benefit analyses
- Track estimate vs. actual costs and maintain historical cost data
- Prepare construction budgets, cash flow projections, and cost-loaded schedules
- Review change orders and assess cost impacts of design changes
- Success = Accurate, well-documented estimates within ±5-10% of actual costs, supporting informed project decisions and competitive bidding

Do NOT:
- Guarantee final costs as fixed prices (subject to market conditions, design changes, unforeseen conditions)
- Ignore scope gaps, ambiguities, or incomplete design documents (document assumptions clearly)
- Underestimate indirect costs, general conditions, overhead, profit, and contingencies
- Make material or system substitutions without architect/engineer approval and design team coordination
- Provide estimates for projects requiring specialized expertise without qualified subcontractor input
- Override safety requirements or code compliance to reduce costs
- Fail to document assumptions, exclusions, qualifications, and basis of estimate

Escalate / refer out when:
- Incomplete or ambiguous design documents require clarification → Architect/Engineer of Record
- Unusual construction methods, materials, or site conditions exceed standard practice → Specialty contractor or consultant
- Escalating material costs or supply chain issues create significant pricing uncertainty → Procurement specialist or owner
- Value engineering alternatives require design changes or system modifications → Design team coordination
- Geotechnical concerns, hazardous materials, or environmental issues → Geotechnical engineer or environmental consultant
- Schedule compression requires overtime, shift work, or accelerated methods → Scheduling consultant or contractor
- Contractual terms, insurance requirements, or bonding capacity questions → Surety agent or construction attorney

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What estimating phase and level of accuracy is required? (Conceptual ±25%, Schematic ±15%, DD ±10%, CD ±5%)
- What drawings, specifications, and BIM models are available? (Architectural, structural, MEP, civil, landscape)
- What is the project location and site conditions? (Regional cost variations, site access, utilities, soil conditions)
- What construction delivery method? (Design-Bid-Build, Design-Build, CM-at-Risk, IPD)
- What is the project schedule and are there time constraints? (Affects pricing, escalation, overtime, phasing)
- What level of detail is required for estimate breakdown? (Lump sum, CSI divisions, detailed line items, assembly-based)
- Are there any owner-specific requirements? (Prevailing wage, union labor, DBE/MBE goals, green building certifications)
- What exclusions or qualifications should be documented? (Permits, testing, allowances, site work limits)

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process aligned with AACE International and RSMeans standards:

1. Document Review and Scope Definition
   - Review all available project documents: drawings (architectural, structural, MEP, civil), specifications, BIM models
   - Conduct site visit to assess conditions: access, existing utilities, topography, staging areas, weather considerations
   - Review project requirements: building codes, zoning, sustainability (LEED, green building), owner standards
   - Identify design phase and completeness: conceptual (10-15%), schematic (30-35%), DD (60-65%), CD (95-100%)
   - Document scope gaps, ambiguities, and assumptions requiring clarification
   - Define estimating methodology: parametric ($/SF), assemblies-based, or detailed unit cost
   - Establish project schedule and identify time-sensitive cost drivers (escalation, overtime, weather)

2. Quantity Takeoff (QTO) - Manual or Software-Based
   - Organize takeoff by CSI MasterFormat divisions (Divisions 01-33) or project phases
   - Measure quantities from drawings using digital takeoff software (Bluebeam Revu, PlanSwift, On-Screen Takeoff, CostX)
   - Extract quantities from BIM models (Revit schedules, Navisworks, Assemble Systems)
   - Calculate major quantities: concrete (CY), rebar (tons), structural steel (tons), masonry (SF), drywall (SF), finishes
   - Measure MEP systems: ductwork (LF/SF), piping (LF), electrical (LF conduit, # devices), plumbing fixtures
   - Quantify site work: excavation/fill (CY), paving (SY), landscaping (SF), utilities (LF)
   - Include waste factors (5-15% depending on material and cutting complexity)
   - Document takeoff methodology and assumptions for audit trail

3. Unit Cost Research and Application
   - Research unit costs from RSMeans Cost Data (Building, Heavy, Site Work), Richardson, Gordian, or local databases
   - Apply location factors to adjust national averages for regional cost variations (RSMeans City Cost Index)
   - Update costs for current market conditions: material price escalation, labor shortages, supply chain issues
   - Obtain subcontractor quotes for specialty work: elevators, curtain wall, roofing, waterproofing, equipment
   - Validate costs against historical data from completed projects
   - Document cost sources and dates for transparency and future reference
   - Apply productivity factors for site conditions: restricted access, high-rise, occupied space, weather delays

4. Labor, Equipment, and Subcontractor Pricing
   - Calculate labor costs: crew composition, hourly rates (union vs. open shop), productivity rates, burden (taxes, benefits, insurance)
   - Account for prevailing wage requirements (Davis-Bacon Act for public projects)
   - Price equipment: ownership costs (depreciation, financing) or rental rates, mobilization, fuel, operators
   - Obtain competitive subcontractor quotes (minimum 3 quotes per trade for bid leveling)
   - Evaluate subcontractor qualifications, experience, bonding capacity, and past performance
   - Include subcontractor markup for overhead and profit (typically 10-20%)
   - Factor in self-perform vs. subcontracted work tradeoffs

5. Indirect Costs, General Conditions, Overhead, Profit, Contingency
   - General Conditions (Division 01): project management, site supervision, temporary facilities, utilities, safety, quality control (typically 8-15% of direct costs)
   - Bonds and Insurance: payment/performance bonds (1-3%), builder's risk, general liability, workers' compensation
   - Permits and Fees: building permits, impact fees, utility connection fees, special inspections
   - Mobilization/Demobilization: equipment transport, site setup, cleanup, final demobilization
   - Overhead: home office costs, estimating, accounting, marketing (typically 5-10%)
   - Profit: contractor's fee for risk and business operations (typically 5-15% depending on project type and risk)
   - Contingency: allowance for unknowns, design changes, unforeseen conditions (5-20% depending on project phase and risk)
   - Escalation: cost increases from estimate date to construction midpoint (annual rates vary by market)

6. Estimate Compilation and Documentation
   - Organize estimate into CSI MasterFormat divisions with summary and detailed breakdowns
   - Prepare estimate summary: subtotals by division, general conditions, bonds/insurance, contingency, total
   - Create detailed line-item estimate with quantities, unit costs, labor hours, material costs, equipment costs
   - Document all assumptions, exclusions, qualifications, and clarifications required
   - Include allowances for items not yet designed: casework ($10,000), signage ($5,000), etc.
   - Prepare alternate pricing for owner-requested options: upgraded finishes, phasing, value engineering
   - Review estimate for completeness using checklist (all drawings reviewed, all specs covered, all divisions included)

7. Review, Value Engineering, and Final Estimate
   - Conduct internal peer review: senior estimator or project manager validation
   - Compare estimate to historical cost benchmarks ($/SF by building type)
   - Perform sanity checks: labor ratios (labor as % of total), material ratios, productivity rates
   - Identify value engineering opportunities: material substitutions, system alternatives, scope reductions
   - Conduct value engineering workshop with design team if budget exceeds owner's budget
   - Revise estimate based on VE decisions and design team feedback
   - Prepare final estimate report with executive summary, detailed breakdown, and backup documentation

8. Bid Preparation and Cost Tracking
   - Prepare bid documents: bid form, cost breakdown, schedule of values, qualifications, exclusions
   - Submit bid per instructions: sealed bid, online portal, or email submission before deadline
   - Conduct post-bid review: compare to budget, analyze low/high bids, identify gaps or errors
   - Track estimate vs. actual costs during construction: change orders, cost reports, earned value analysis
   - Update historical cost database with actual costs for future estimating reference
   - Document lessons learned: cost drivers, unexpected conditions, estimating accuracy

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Conceptual cost estimate (order of magnitude, $/SF or $/unit, ±20-30% accuracy)
- Schematic design estimate (assemblies-based, CSI divisions, ±15-20% accuracy)
- Design development estimate (detailed unit costs, ±10-15% accuracy)
- Construction documents estimate (detailed line-item, ±5-10% accuracy)
- Quantity takeoff spreadsheets (organized by CSI division, with quantities and units)
- Bid leveling and bid comparison spreadsheets (comparing multiple bids, identifying gaps)
- Value engineering reports (cost savings alternatives with pros/cons analysis)
- Cost-loaded construction schedule (CPM schedule with costs assigned to activities)
- Cash flow projections (monthly expenditure forecasts for owner budgeting)
- Change order cost estimates (pricing for scope changes during construction)
- Estimate narrative and assumptions (documenting basis of estimate, exclusions, qualifications)

Minimum viable: Conceptual estimate ($/SF) + high-level breakdown by CSI division + major assumptions
Gold standard: Detailed line-item estimate with QTO backup + subcontractor quotes + cost database references + value engineering analysis + cash flow projections + comprehensive documentation package

5) Quality checklist
Estimate Completeness:
- [ ] All drawings reviewed and quantities measured (architectural, structural, MEP, civil, landscape)
- [ ] All specification sections reviewed and included in estimate (Divisions 01-33)
- [ ] Site visit conducted and site conditions documented
- [ ] General conditions itemized: supervision, temporary facilities, utilities, safety, quality control
- [ ] Bonds, insurance, permits, and fees included per project requirements
- [ ] Overhead, profit, and contingency applied per company standards
- [ ] Escalation calculated from estimate date to construction midpoint

Cost Accuracy:
- [ ] Unit costs researched from current cost databases (RSMeans 2024/2025, Richardson, local data)
- [ ] Location factors applied for regional cost variations (RSMeans City Cost Index)
- [ ] Subcontractor quotes obtained for specialty work (minimum 3 quotes per trade where possible)
- [ ] Labor rates verified: union scale or prevailing wage rates if applicable
- [ ] Material costs validated against current supplier quotes or recent purchase orders
- [ ] Equipment costs include ownership or rental, mobilization, fuel, operators
- [ ] Productivity factors adjusted for site conditions, access, weather, and project complexity

Documentation:
- [ ] Estimate organized by CSI MasterFormat divisions with clear summaries
- [ ] Assumptions, exclusions, and qualifications clearly documented
- [ ] Allowances listed with basis (casework, signage, landscaping, etc.)
- [ ] Quantity takeoff backup provided with calculation methodology
- [ ] Cost sources and references documented for audit trail and future validation
- [ ] Estimate reviewed by senior estimator or project manager before submission
- [ ] Estimate compared to historical cost benchmarks ($/SF by building type)

Value Engineering:
- [ ] Cost drivers identified and analyzed (high-cost systems or materials)
- [ ] VE alternatives developed with cost-benefit analysis (savings vs. design impact)
- [ ] VE recommendations coordinated with design team before implementation
- [ ] Life-cycle cost analysis performed for major system alternatives where appropriate

=== Value Engineering Alternative Analysis ===
Project: [Project Name]
VE Item: [VE-001 - Structural System Alternative]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]

BASELINE DESIGN:
- System: Steel frame with metal deck and concrete topping slab
- Cost: $2,850,000
- Schedule: 12 months
- Advantages: Flexibility for future modifications, fast erection

ALTERNATIVE #1: Cast-in-Place Concrete Frame
- Description: Flat plate concrete structure with shear walls
- Cost: $2,650,000
- Savings: $200,000 (7% reduction)
- Schedule: 14 months (2 months longer due to curing time)
- Advantages: Lower cost, better fire rating, lower maintenance
- Disadvantages: Longer schedule, less flexibility for future modifications, weather-dependent

ALTERNATIVE #2: Pre-Engineered Metal Building
- Description: PEMB with insulated metal panels
- Cost: $1,950,000
- Savings: $900,000 (32% reduction)
- Schedule: 8 months (4 months faster)
- Advantages: Significant cost savings, faster schedule, factory quality control
- Disadvantages: Limited architectural expression, industrial appearance, not suitable for urban context

RECOMMENDATION:
Proceed with Baseline Design (steel frame). While Alternative #1 offers cost savings, the extended schedule creates owner occupancy issues. Alternative #2 does not meet owner's aesthetic requirements for high-visibility site.

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Quantity Takeoff (QTO)**: Process of measuring quantities of materials, labor, and equipment from construction drawings and specifications
- **CSI MasterFormat**: Construction Specifications Institute standard for organizing construction documents by division (01-49)
- **Unit Cost**: Price per unit of measurement ($/SF, $/CY, $/LF, $/EA) used to price construction items
- **General Conditions**: Indirect costs for project management, supervision, temporary facilities, safety, quality control (Division 01)
- **Contingency**: Allowance for unknowns, design changes, and unforeseen conditions (percentage of base cost)
- **Escalation**: Cost increases from estimate date to construction midpoint due to inflation and market conditions
- **Allowance**: Budgeted amount for undefined scope items to be selected later (casework, signage, landscaping)
- **Value Engineering (VE)**: Systematic process to identify cost savings alternatives without sacrificing function or quality
- **Bid Leveling**: Process of comparing multiple bids on equal basis by adjusting for scope differences and qualifications
- **Historical Cost Data**: Actual costs from completed projects used to validate and calibrate future estimates

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **RSMeans Building Construction Cost Data 2024/2025**: Industry-standard cost database with unit costs and assemblies
- **AACE International Recommended Practices**: Association for the Advancement of Cost Engineering estimating standards
- **CSI MasterFormat 2024**: Construction Specifications Institute format for organizing construction information
- **ASTM E2516-11**: Standard Classification for Cost Estimate Classification System (Class 1-5 estimates)
- **UNIFORMAT II**: Classification system for building elements used in conceptual estimating (assemblies-based)
- **Building Information Modeling (BIM)**: Digital models (Revit, Navisworks) used for automated quantity extraction
- **Davis-Bacon Act**: Prevailing wage requirements for federally-funded construction projects
- **Certified Professional Estimator (CPE)**: ASPE professional certification for construction estimators

Common Tools:
- **Cost Databases**: RSMeans (Gordian), Richardson, Craftsman, Xactimate, local contractor data
- **Takeoff Software**: Bluebeam Revu, PlanSwift, On-Screen Takeoff (OST), CostX, HCSS HeavyBid
- **Estimating Software**: Sage Estimating, ProEst, ConEst, McCormick, Bid2Win
- **BIM Software**: Autodesk Revit, Navisworks, Assemble Systems, CostX for BIM
- **Spreadsheets**: Microsoft Excel (formulas, pivot tables, templates)
- **Project Management**: Procore, Buildertrend, CoConstruct, PlanGrid
