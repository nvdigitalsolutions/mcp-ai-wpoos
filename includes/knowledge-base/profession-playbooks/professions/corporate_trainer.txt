Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality corporate training design, delivery, and evaluation.

Category guidance
- Use a practical, general workflow with checklists and templates focused on adult learners.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Conduct training needs analysis to identify skills gaps and learning objectives
- Design and develop training programs for corporate employees (onboarding, technical skills, soft skills, compliance)
- Deliver live training sessions (in-person, virtual, hybrid) using adult learning principles
- Create training materials (presentations, workbooks, job aids, e-learning modules)
- Facilitate workshops, role-plays, simulations, and hands-on practice
- Assess training effectiveness using Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Evaluation
- Provide coaching and feedback to learners during and after training
- Measure training ROI and report results to stakeholders
- Adapt training methods for different learning styles and levels
- Coordinate with SMEs (subject matter experts) to ensure content accuracy
- Success = Learners can apply new skills on the job, leading to measurable performance improvement

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice on employment law, compliance regulations, or HR policies (requires attorney)
- Make hiring, firing, or promotion decisions (HR responsibility)
- Conduct licensed psychological assessments or clinical counseling (requires psychologist)
- Promise specific business outcomes or ROI without proper evaluation framework
- Create training on topics requiring specialized certification without proper credentials
- Guarantee learner performance or behavior change (many factors affect outcomes)
- Conduct safety training for regulated industries without proper certification (e.g., OSHA)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal compliance training content requires attorney review → Employment Attorney
- Specialized technical training needs SME validation → Subject Matter Expert
- Learning management system (LMS) technical issues → IT or LMS Administrator
- Individual performance issues beyond training → Manager or HR Business Partner
- Accessibility accommodations for disabled learners → Accessibility Specialist
- Organizational change management beyond training → Change Management Consultant
- Budget approval or resource allocation → Training Manager or L&D Leader

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What region/countries and what regulatory training requirements apply (compliance, safety, industry-specific)?
- What is the performance gap or business problem driving this training need?
- Who is the target audience? (Role, experience level, prior knowledge, learning environment)
- What should learners be able to do after training? (Specific, observable behaviors)
- What is the delivery format? (In-person, virtual, self-paced, blended)
- What is the timeline and budget for design and delivery?
- What resources are available? (SMEs, existing materials, technology, space)
- How will training effectiveness be measured? (Tests, observations, business metrics)
- What constraints exist? (Time away from work, technology limitations, organizational culture)

1.5) Regional Considerations

Corporate training regulations and practices vary by region:

**Regulatory Training Requirements:**
- **United States**: OSHA safety training, anti-harassment (Title VII), securities (FINRA), healthcare (HIPAA), state-specific
- **Europe**: Working Time Directive, health & safety regulations, national employment law variations
- **United Kingdom**: Health and Safety at Work Act, mandatory training for certain industries
- **Industry-specific**: Financial services, healthcare, transportation regulations vary dramatically

**Professional Development:**
- **United States**: ATD certifications (CPTD, APTD), SHRM for HR-related training, industry associations
- **United Kingdom**: CIPD qualifications (Levels 3-7), Chartered status available
- **Global**: ASTD/ATD model prevalent in multinational corporations

**Work Culture:**
- **Training hours**: European countries generally mandate more paid training time, US more variable
- **Learning culture**: Varies by region (Scandinavian emphasis on development, Asia hierarchical training approaches)
- **Virtual training**: Timezone considerations for global teams, regional preferences for synchronous vs asynchronous

**Employment Law:**
- **Paid training time**: Regional variations (EU generally requires pay, US depends on circumstances)
- **Time off for learning**: Varies (UK Apprenticeship Levy, French CPF account, limited in US)
- **Discrimination/accessibility**: ADA (US), Equality Act (UK), various national laws affect training delivery

3) Default workflow
Use ADDIE instructional design model (ATD 2024 standards):

1. Analysis Phase
   - Conduct needs assessment (surveys, interviews, performance data)
   - Identify performance gaps (current vs. desired state)
   - Define target audience characteristics (role, experience, motivation)
   - Analyze root cause (skill gap, knowledge gap, motivation, or environmental factors)
   - Determine if training is appropriate solution or if other interventions needed
   - Define learning objectives using SMART criteria and Bloom's Taxonomy
   - Assess constraints (budget, timeline, technology, organizational support)
   - Deliverable: Training needs analysis report, learning objectives, project plan

2. Design Phase
   - Select instructional strategies aligned with adult learning principles (Knowles' Andragogy)
   - Choose delivery method (instructor-led, e-learning, blended, microlearning)
   - Design assessment strategy (pre-test, post-test, skills demonstration, observation)
   - Create course outline with modules, activities, and timing
   - Plan for practice and application opportunities
   - Design evaluation strategy (Kirkpatrick Level 1-4)
   - Identify required resources (materials, equipment, space, technology)
   - Deliverable: Training design document, course outline, evaluation plan

3. Development Phase
   - Create trainer guide with facilitation notes and timing
   - Develop participant materials (workbooks, job aids, quick reference guides)
   - Design presentations (PowerPoint, Google Slides) with engaging visuals
   - Develop activities (case studies, role-plays, simulations, group exercises)
   - Create assessments (quizzes, scenarios, skills demonstrations)
   - Develop e-learning modules if applicable (Articulate, Captivate, Rise)
   - Review content with SMEs for accuracy and relevance
   - Pilot test materials with small group and revise
   - Deliverable: Complete training package (slides, guides, activities, assessments)

4. Implementation (Delivery) Phase
   - Prepare training environment (room setup, technology check, materials)
   - Welcome participants and establish learning climate
   - Present content using varied methods (lecture, discussion, demonstration, practice)
   - Facilitate activities and provide feedback
   - Encourage participation and address questions
   - Manage group dynamics and keep training on track
   - Adapt in real-time based on learner needs and engagement
   - Administer assessments and provide feedback
   - Close with action planning and next steps
   - Deliverable: Conducted training session, learner assessments, participant feedback

5. Evaluation Phase
   - Level 1 (Reaction): Collect participant feedback via survey
   - Level 2 (Learning): Assess knowledge/skill gain via tests or observations
   - Level 3 (Behavior): Follow up 30-90 days to assess on-the-job application
   - Level 4 (Results): Measure business impact (productivity, quality, sales, retention)
   - Analyze evaluation data and identify improvement opportunities
   - Report results to stakeholders with recommendations
   - Revise training based on feedback and performance data
   - Deliverable: Evaluation report, training effectiveness metrics, improvement plan

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts commonly produced:

**Analysis Phase:**
- Training needs analysis report
- Learning objectives (measurable, aligned with business goals)
- Learner profile and audience analysis
- Project plan and timeline

**Design Phase:**
- Training design document
- Course outline with modules and timing
- Evaluation strategy and instruments

**Development Phase:**
- Trainer guide / facilitator guide
- Participant workbook or handouts
- PowerPoint / Google Slides presentation
- Activities and exercises (case studies, role-plays, simulations)
- Job aids and quick reference guides
- Assessments (pre-test, post-test, skills checklist)
- E-learning modules (if applicable)

**Implementation Phase:**
- Training attendance records
- Assessment results
- Participant feedback surveys (Level 1)
- Training completion certificates

**Evaluation Phase:**
- Evaluation report (Levels 1-4)
- Training effectiveness metrics
- Recommendations for improvement

**Minimum viable deliverable**: 
Training session delivered with basic materials and participant feedback collected.

**Gold standard deliverable**: 
Comprehensive training program with needs-aligned design, engaging materials, strong facilitation, and proven business impact measured through all four Kirkpatrick levels.

5) Quality checklist
Create profession-specific QA gates:

**Analysis Quality:**
- [ ] Performance gap clearly identified with data
- [ ] Root cause analyzed (training vs. non-training solution)
- [ ] Learning objectives are SMART and aligned with business needs
- [ ] Audience characteristics and constraints documented
- [ ] Stakeholder expectations aligned and documented

**Design Quality:**
- [ ] Instructional strategies aligned with adult learning principles
- [ ] Practice and application opportunities built in
- [ ] Delivery method appropriate for content and audience
- [ ] Assessment strategy measures actual performance, not just knowledge
- [ ] Evaluation plan includes Level 3 (behavior) or Level 4 (results)
- [ ] Accessibility considerations addressed

**Development Quality:**
- [ ] Content accurate and validated by SMEs
- [ ] Materials visually appealing and professional
- [ ] Activities engaging and relevant to learner context
- [ ] Trainer guide includes timing, facilitation notes, and tips
- [ ] Assessments valid (measure what they should) and reliable (consistent)
- [ ] Materials accessible (Section 508/WCAG compliance if digital)
- [ ] Pilot tested with representative learners

**Delivery Quality:**
- [ ] Training environment prepared (room, technology, materials)
- [ ] Positive learning climate established at start
- [ ] Content presented clearly with varied methods
- [ ] All learners engaged and participating
- [ ] Questions answered accurately and thoroughly
- [ ] Time managed effectively (not rushed or too slow)
- [ ] Feedback provided to learners during activities
- [ ] Learning objectives achieved per assessments

**Evaluation Quality:**
- [ ] Level 1 (reaction) surveys completed by all participants
- [ ] Level 2 (learning) assessments show knowledge/skill gain
- [ ] Level 3 (behavior) follow-up conducted 30-90 days post-training
- [ ] Level 4 (results) business metrics tracked where feasible
- [ ] Evaluation data analyzed for trends and insights
- [ ] Results reported to stakeholders with recommendations

=== Learning Objectives Template ===
**Training Title:** [Title]
**Target Audience:** [Job role, experience level]
**Training Duration:** [X hours/days]

**Learning Objectives (ABCD Format):**

1. **[Action Verb] + [Behavior] + [Condition] + [Degree]**
   - Example: "By the end of this training, participants will be able to **conduct** a performance review conversation **using the STAR feedback model**, **with at least 80% of required elements demonstrated** during a role-play."

2. **[Action Verb] + [Behavior] + [Condition] + [Degree]**
   - Example: "After completing this module, learners will be able to **create** a project plan **using Microsoft Project**, **including all required components** (tasks, timeline, resources, dependencies) **within 30 minutes**.

3. **[Action Verb] + [Behavior] + [Condition] + [Degree]**
   - Example: "At the conclusion of sales training, participants will be able to **identify** customer objections and **respond** with appropriate rebuttals, **achieving a score of 85% or higher** on the final assessment.

**Bloom's Taxonomy Levels:**
- Remember: List, recall, define, describe
- Understand: Explain, summarize, classify, compare
- Apply: Demonstrate, use, execute, implement
- Analyze: Differentiate, organize, attribute, deconstruct
- Evaluate: Judge, critique, assess, defend
- Create: Design, construct, produce, develop

=== Facilitator Guide Template ===
**Module: [Module Title]**
**Duration:** [X] minutes
**Learning Objectives:** [List relevant objectives for this module]

**Materials Needed:**
- Participant workbooks
- PowerPoint slide deck
- Flipchart and markers
- [Activity-specific materials]

**Room Setup:** [U-shape / Classroom / Tables / Theatre]

---

**[SLIDE 1]: Welcome and Introduction**
**Time:** 5 minutes

**SAY:** "Good morning everyone, and welcome to [Training Title]. My name is [Your Name], and I'll be facilitating today's session."

**DO:**
- Introduce yourself (relevant experience, role)
- Share learning objectives for today
- Review agenda and logistics (breaks, restrooms, phones/laptops policy)

**FACILITATOR TIPS:**
- Arrive 30 minutes early to set up room and test technology
- Greet participants as they arrive to build rapport
- Start on time even if some participants are late

---

**[SLIDE 2-3]: Icebreaker Activity**
**Time:** 10 minutes

**SAY:** "Before we dive in, let's get to know each other. Please share your name, role, and one thing you hope to learn today."

**DO:**
- Go first to model (keep it brief)
- Facilitate introductions around room
- Note themes in what participants want to learn
- Bridge to content: "Great, I heard several of you mention [theme], and we'll cover that in Module 2."

**FACILITATOR TIPS:**
- Keep introductions moving (30-45 seconds each)
- Use names throughout training to build connections
- If group is large (15+), do introductions in small groups

---

**[SLIDE 4-6]: Concept Overview**
**Time:** 15 minutes

**SAY:** "Let's start with the foundational concept of [Concept]. This is important because [relevance to job]."

**DO:**
- Present concept with visual (model, framework, process)
- Use concrete examples from learner's work context
- Check for understanding with questions: "What questions do you have so far?"

**KEY POINTS:**
- [Key point 1 with explanation]
- [Key point 2 with explanation]
- [Key point 3 with explanation]

**FACILITATOR TIPS:**
- Avoid jargon or define technical terms
- Pause frequently to check understanding
- Use "thumbs up/down/sideways" for quick comprehension check
- If concept is complex, break into smaller chunks

---

**[SLIDE 7]: Activity - [Activity Name]**
**Time:** 20 minutes (15 min activity + 5 min debrief)

**SAY:** "Now let's practice applying [Concept] with a real-world scenario."

**DO:**
1. Explain activity instructions clearly
2. Divide into groups of 3-4 (or pairs)
3. Distribute handouts with case study/scenario
4. Set timer and circulate to observe and provide feedback
5. Call time and bring group back together
6. Debrief with questions:
   - "What did your group discover?"
   - "What challenges did you face?"
   - "How does this relate to your work?"
7. Highlight key learning points and connect to objectives

**FACILITATOR TIPS:**
- Walk around during activity to listen and support groups
- Note interesting insights to share during debrief
- If activity finishes early, have extension questions ready
- If running behind, shorten debrief but don't skip it

---

**[SLIDE 8]: Module Summary**
**Time:** 5 minutes

**SAY:** "Let's recap the key points from this module."

**DO:**
- Review 3-4 main takeaways
- Ask participants: "What's one thing you'll use from this module?"
- Preview next module
- Take questions

**KEY TAKEAWAYS:**
- [Takeaway 1]
- [Takeaway 2]
- [Takeaway 3]

**Example 1: New Manager Training Program**

```
Program: First-Time Manager Essentials
Duration: 2 days (16 hours)
Audience: Individual contributors promoted to first-time manager role
Class Size: 12-16 participants

Business Need:
- New managers struggling with transition from IC to manager
- High direct report turnover (25%) on teams with new managers
- Managers spending time doing IC work instead of managing

Learning Objectives:
1. Conduct effective 1:1 meetings using a structured agenda
2. Deliver constructive feedback using the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model
3. Set SMART goals with direct reports and track progress
4. Delegate tasks appropriately while maintaining accountability
5. Build psychological safety on team through specific actions

Day 1 Agenda:

**Module 1: Transition to Manager** (2 hours)
- Mindset shift: IC vs. Manager responsibilities
- Activity: "What got you here won't get you there" reflection
- Your management philosophy and values
- Building trust with your team

**Module 2: One-on-One Meetings** (2 hours)
- Purpose and structure of effective 1:1s
- Creating a recurring 1:1 agenda template
- Active listening and asking powerful questions
- Practice: Mock 1:1 conversations in pairs

**Lunch Break** (1 hour)

**Module 3: Goal Setting** (2 hours)
- SMART goals framework
- Cascading team goals to individual goals
- Activity: Writing SMART goals for case study employees
- Tracking and adjusting goals

**Module 4: Feedback Fundamentals** (1 hour)
- Growth mindset and feedback culture
- SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model
- Practice: Writing SBI feedback scenarios

Day 2 Agenda:

**Module 5: Delivering Feedback** (2 hours)
- Positive and constructive feedback delivery
- Role-play: Feedback conversations with actors
- Handling defensive reactions
- Documenting feedback for performance reviews

**Module 6: Delegation** (2 hours)
- What to delegate and what to keep
- Delegation conversation framework
- Activity: Identifying delegation opportunities from your workload
- Monitoring without micromanaging

**Lunch Break** (1 hour)

**Module 7: Building Psychological Safety** (1.5 hours)
- Google's Project Aristotle findings
- Behaviors that build vs. undermine safety
- Leading inclusive meetings
- Responding to mistakes and failures

**Module 8: Action Planning** (30 minutes)
- 30-60-90 day manager action plan
- Peer coaching partnerships
- Resources and next steps
- Manager community of practice invitation

Materials:
- Participant workbook (80 pages)
- Manager toolkit (templates for 1:1s, goals, feedback)
- Book: "The Making of a Manager" by Julie Zhuo
- Access to online manager resources portal

Evaluation Results (After 3 Cohorts):

Level 1 (Reaction): 4.6/5.0 average
- "Highly relevant and immediately applicable"
- "Role-plays were challenging but incredibly valuable"
- "Wish I'd had this training when I first became a manager"

Level 2 (Learning): 87% pass rate on post-training assessment
- Pre-test average: 58%
- Post-test average: 87%
- Knowledge gain: +29 percentage points

Level 3 (Behavior): 90-day follow-up survey
- 82% report conducting regular 1:1s (up from 45% pre-training)
- 76% report using SBI feedback model at least monthly
- 71% have set SMART goals with all direct reports
- Managers feel more confident (3.2 → 4.1 on 5-point scale)

Level 4 (Results): 12-month impact
- Direct report engagement scores improved 12% for trained managers
- Turnover on teams with trained managers decreased from 25% to 15%
- 360 feedback scores for trained managers: 3.9/5.0 (vs. 3.4 for untrained)

ROI Calculation:
- Training cost per manager: $1,500 (design + delivery + materials)
- Cost savings from reduced turnover: $75,000 per manager (10% reduction × $15K replacement cost × 5 direct reports)
- ROI: 50:1 in Year 1 from turnover reduction alone
```

**Example 2: Sales Skills Training**

```
Program: Consultative Selling Skills
Duration: 1 day (8 hours) + 30-day reinforcement
Audience: B2B Account Executives
Class Size: 10-12 participants

- Win rate: 18% (target: 30%)

1. Conduct discovery calls using SPIN questioning technique

4. Handle objections by addressing root concerns
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **ADDIE Model**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation
- **Bloom's Taxonomy**: Hierarchical classification of learning objectives (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create)
- **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels**: Evaluation framework (Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results)
- **Andragogy**: Adult learning theory (Knowles' principles)
- **SMART Objectives**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- **SME (Subject Matter Expert)**: Person with deep expertise in specific content area
- **Facilitator vs. Trainer**: Facilitator guides learning, trainer delivers content
- **Blended Learning**: Combination of delivery methods (e-learning + in-person)
- **Microlearning**: Short, focused learning modules (5-10 minutes)
- **Transfer of Training**: Application of learned skills on the job

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **ATD (Association for Talent Development)**: Professional standards for trainers and L&D professionals
- **CPTD (Certified Professional in Talent Development)**: Industry certification for trainers
- **Section 508 / WCAG 2.1**: Accessibility standards for digital learning content
- **SCORM / xAPI (Tin Can)**: E-learning standards for LMS compatibility
- **70-20-10 Model**: Learning framework (70% on-the-job, 20% social, 10% formal training)
- **SAM (Successive Approximation Model)**: Agile instructional design alternative to ADDIE
- **Gagne's Nine Events of Instruction**: Instructional design framework
- **Kolb's Learning Cycle**: Experiential learning model (Experience → Reflect → Conceptualize → Experiment)

**Common Tools:**
