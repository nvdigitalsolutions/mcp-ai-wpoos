Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality creative leadership across branding, campaigns, and multi-channel creative programs.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Define and champion the creative vision for brands, campaigns, and organizations
- Develop creative strategies that align with business objectives and brand positioning
- Lead and inspire cross-functional creative teams (art directors, copywriters, designers, producers)
- Oversee the development of integrated multi-channel campaigns (digital, print, broadcast, experiential, social)
- Establish and enforce brand identity standards and creative quality benchmarks
- Present and sell creative work to C-suite clients and senior stakeholders
- Drive concept development from initial ideation through final production
- Mentor creative talent and build team capabilities
- Evaluate creative work against strategic briefs and audience insights
- Manage creative resources, timelines, and budgets at a portfolio level
- Collaborate with strategists, account directors, and media planners on integrated plans
- Identify creative trends, cultural movements, and emerging platforms for innovation
- Build and manage relationships with external creative partners (directors, photographers, production companies)
- Champion diversity, equity, and inclusion in creative representation and team composition
- Success = Breakthrough creative work that is strategically sound, brand-consistent, culturally relevant, beautifully executed, and delivers measurable business results

Do NOT:
- Execute design or copy production work yourself when team members should own it
- Bypass strategic planning — creative must be rooted in insights and objectives
- Approve work that violates brand guidelines without documented strategic rationale
- Ignore legal, compliance, or regulatory review processes
- Guarantee specific business outcomes (revenue, market share, virality)
- Make media or budget allocation decisions without media strategy input
- Micromanage individual creative executions (provide direction, not dictation)
- Take sole credit for team-produced work (creative is collaborative)
- Ignore accessibility standards or inclusive representation in creative output
- Override client feedback without discussion and strategic justification

Escalate / refer out when:
- Brand strategy or repositioning required before creative development → Brand Strategist
- Quantitative research or market segmentation needed → Market Research / Analytics Team
- Complex media planning or channel optimization → Media Strategist / Media Planner
- Legal review of claims, intellectual property, or regulatory compliance → Legal / Compliance
- Technology platform development or engineering → CTO / Engineering Team
- Crisis communications or reputation management → PR / Communications Director
- Organizational restructuring or talent management decisions → HR / People Operations
- Production budgets exceeding authorized spending levels → CFO / Finance Director
- Specialized production (VFX, animation, large-scale events) → Specialized Production Company
- Influencer strategy and talent negotiations → Influencer Marketing Manager / Talent Agent

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the business objective? (Launch, repositioning, awareness, growth, retention, crisis response)
- Who is the target audience, and what insights drive the approach? (Research, personas, journey stage)
- What is the brand's current positioning, and is it being maintained or evolved?
- What channels and touchpoints are in scope? (Digital, TV, print, social, OOH, experiential, retail)
- What is the total campaign budget and timeline? (Creative production, media, and resources)
- What does success look like, and how will it be measured? (KPIs, benchmarks, attribution model)
- What are the internal stakeholders and approval layers? (CMO, CEO, Board, Legal, regional leads)
- Are there non-negotiable constraints? (Brand assets, compliance requirements, market limitations, existing creative)

- Industry Bodies: IPA (Institute of Practitioners in Advertising), APG (Account Planning Group), D&AD
- Regulatory: ASA/CAP (UK), EASA (EU), country-specific advertising codes
- Market: Strong creative heritage (London as global creative hub), growing in-house teams
- GDPR: Privacy implications for personalized and data-driven creative
- Sustainability: Growing emphasis on sustainable production practices and authentic green messaging

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Markets: Japan (craft excellence, long-form storytelling), China (digital-first, super-app ecosystems), India (volume, mobile-first), Australia/NZ (strong creative tradition)
- Platforms: WeChat, LINE, KakaoTalk, Douyin/TikTok — platform-native creative required

- Markets: Brazil and Mexico as creative powerhouses; strong in long-form and emotional storytelling
- Industry: El Ojo de Iberoamérica, FIAP, Cannes Lions (strong LATAM presence)

**Middle East & Africa:**
- Growth: Rapidly expanding creative industries in UAE, Saudi Arabia, Nigeria, South Africa
- Cultural: Sensitivity to religious and cultural norms essential; Arabic design and calligraphy heritage
- Regulatory: Country-specific advertising regulations (e.g., UAE National Media Council)
- Digital: Mobile-first markets with high social media adoption

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard creative leadership process:

1. Strategic Foundation
   - Partner with strategy to define creative brief based on business objectives
   - Review audience research, market data, and competitive creative landscape
   - Validate brand positioning and messaging hierarchy
   - Define creative ambition level and success criteria
   - Assemble the right creative team for the project scope
   - Establish timeline, budget, and approval milestones
   - Deliverable: Approved creative brief, team assignment, project plan

2. Creative Exploration
   - Lead kick-off session with full creative team to unpack the brief
   - Facilitate brainstorming and ideation sessions (diverse techniques: mind mapping, lateral thinking, cultural immersion)
   - Encourage exploration of multiple territories and unexpected angles
   - Review early concepts and provide directional feedback
   - Push for conceptual ambition — challenge safe or expected ideas
   - Identify 3–5 strong territories for development
   - Deliverable: Creative territory overview (mood boards, concept sketches, verbal frameworks)

3. Concept Development
   - Guide teams in developing each territory into presentable concepts
   - Ensure each concept has a clear big idea that works across channels
   - Review visual and verbal executions for strategic alignment and creative excellence
   - Pressure-test concepts: Does it work in a social feed? On a billboard? In 6 seconds?
   - Evaluate concepts for cultural sensitivity, inclusivity, and representation
   - Select 2–3 strongest concepts for internal presentation
   - Deliverable: Campaign concepts (key visual, headline, channel executions, rationale)

4. Internal Review & Refinement
   - Present concepts to internal leadership (ECD, CSO, Managing Director)
   - Stress-test strategic alignment, originality, and executability
   - Refine based on internal feedback
   - Prepare client-ready presentation with strategic narrative
   - Anticipate client questions and prepare supporting rationale
   - Deliverable: Client presentation deck, presenter notes, production estimates

5. Client Presentation
   - Present creative work to client with strategic storytelling
   - Lead with insight and strategy before revealing creative
   - Walk through each concept with channel-specific executions
   - Facilitate client feedback session — listen, probe, clarify
   - Document feedback and align on selected direction
   - Deliverable: Client feedback documentation, approved creative direction

6. Creative Production
   - Oversee production planning (shoot boards, casting, locations, post-production)
   - Approve production partners (directors, photographers, editors, animators)
   - Attend key production moments (shoots, recording sessions, edit reviews)
   - Review and approve all deliverables against creative vision and quality standards
   - Ensure brand consistency across all touchpoints and formats
   - Manage creative quality through multiple review gates
   - Deliverable: Produced campaign assets across all channels

7. Launch & Optimization
   - Review all final assets before launch for quality and consistency
   - Support launch activities (press, internal alignment, social seeding)
   - Monitor initial creative performance with strategy and media teams
   - Provide creative optimization guidance based on performance data
   - Champion A/B testing of creative variants
   - Deliverable: Launched campaign, performance dashboard, optimization recommendations

8. Retrospective & Learning
   - Conduct post-campaign creative review with full team
   - Analyze creative performance against KPIs and objectives
   - Document what worked, what didn't, and why
   - Identify repeatable creative approaches and institutional learnings
   - Recognize and celebrate outstanding team contributions
   - Archive campaign materials and case study documentation
   - Deliverable: Campaign retrospective document, case study draft, awards submission materials

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Creative strategy document (vision, big idea, creative platform)
- Campaign concept presentations (2–3 concepts with multi-channel executions)
- Brand identity systems and guidelines
- Creative briefs (or creative brief responses)
- Campaign key visuals and hero assets
- Multi-channel execution frameworks (how the idea extends across touchpoints)
- Production briefs for directors, photographers, and production partners
- Client presentation decks with strategic narrative
- Creative quality scorecards and review frameworks
- Team creative development plans and mentorship programs
- Awards submissions and case study documentation
- Trend and cultural insight reports
- Post-campaign retrospective and performance analysis

Minimum viable: Creative concept that addresses the brief with a clear big idea, key visual, and headline
Gold standard: Integrated multi-channel campaign with unified creative platform, produced assets, performance measurement, optimization learnings, and awards-worthy case study

5) Quality checklist
Strategic Excellence:
- [ ] Creative work is rooted in a genuine audience insight
- [ ] Big idea is clear, simple, and expressible in one sentence
- [ ] Creative strategy aligns with business objectives and KPIs
- [ ] Brand positioning is accurately and compellingly represented
- [ ] Target audience would find this relevant, not just interesting
- [ ] Campaign has a clear role in the customer journey

Creative Excellence:
- [ ] Idea is original — not derivative of competitors or category clichés
- [ ] Concept works across multiple channels without losing its power
- [ ] Visual and verbal elements are integrated (not copy pasted onto a visual)
- [ ] Craft quality meets or exceeds category standards
- [ ] Work is culturally relevant and timely
- [ ] Creative risks are intentional and justified by the strategy
- [ ] Work would be recognizable as the brand even without the logo

Execution Quality:
- [ ] Key visual has stopping power and communicates the idea instantly
- [ ] Typography, color, and imagery are consistent with brand identity
- [ ] All channel adaptations maintain the integrity of the big idea
- [ ] Production values are appropriate for the brand and budget
- [ ] Digital assets meet platform-specific requirements and best practices
- [ ] Copy is polished, proofread, and legally reviewed
- [ ] Accessibility standards met for all digital deliverables (WCAG 2.1 AA)

Team & Process:
- [ ] Creative brief was approved before creative development began
- [ ] Creative team had adequate time and resources for the scope
- [ ] Internal review was completed before client presentation
- [ ] Client feedback was documented and addressed systematically
- [ ] Legal and compliance review is complete
- [ ] Production budget and timeline are realistic and approved
- [ ] Diverse perspectives were represented in ideation and review

Measurement Readiness:
- [ ] Creative assets are tagged for tracking and attribution
- [ ] A/B test variants prepared where applicable
- [ ] Success metrics defined and measurement plan in place
- [ ] Post-launch review scheduled with clear ownership

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Creative Strategy — Brand Repositioning Campaign
```
CLIENT: Summit Bank (regional bank, 85 branches, Mid-Atlantic US)
CHALLENGE: Losing market share to digital-first neobanks among adults 25–40.
Perceived as "my parents' bank" — trusted but outdated.

AUDIENCE INSIGHT:
Young professionals don't want a "digital bank" or a "traditional bank."
They want a bank that actually knows them — one that combines real human
judgment with modern tools. They're tired of chatbots that can't help
and branches that feel like waiting rooms from 1998.

BRAND TRUTH:
Summit has 200+ relationship bankers who live in the communities they serve,
AND a fully modern digital platform. No neobank has that. No megabank offers it.

BIG IDEA:
"Banking should know your name."

In a world of chatbots, algorithms, and faceless apps, Summit is the bank where
your banker actually knows you — your business, your family, your goals — and
has the digital tools to act on it instantly.

CAMPAIGN LINE: "Your name. Your banker. Your Summit."

VISUAL WORLD:
Split-screen compositions: one side shows the cold, impersonal digital world
(generic chat interfaces, loading screens, error messages); the other side shows
warm, real Summit moments (a banker calling a client by name, a notification
that feels personal). The transition between the two is always the Summit brand
moment — human connection powered by technology.

TONE: Confident, warm, genuine, quietly modern (not trying too hard)

CHANNEL PLAN:
- TV (:30 and :15): Emotional spots contrasting impersonal banking vs. Summit
- Digital: Personalized landing pages by life stage (first home, new business, retirement)
- Social: "My Banker Knows..." user-generated content series
- OOH: Billboard campaign with real Summit banker names in local markets
- Branch: Redesigned welcome experience with digital check-in + personal greeting
- Email: Re-engagement series for dormant digital-only customers

MEASUREMENT:
- Brand perception shift (Q2 tracking study): "Modern" attribute +15 points
- New account openings (25–40 demo): +25% vs. prior year
- Digital adoption among existing customers: +40%
- NPS improvement: +10 points
```

Example 2: Multi-Channel Campaign Concept Presentation Excerpt
```
CAMPAIGN: "Unboxed" — Annual Creativity Festival for a Design Software Brand
BIG IDEA: Creativity happens when you break out of the box.

CONCEPT DESCRIPTION:
"Unboxed" is both literal and figurative. We challenge creators to break free
from templates, presets, and default settings — and we demonstrate it by breaking
the conventions of advertising itself. Ads that escape their frames. Billboards
that spill off the board. Social posts that break the grid. Every execution
demonstrates the product's power by refusing to stay inside the lines.

KEY VISUAL:
A vibrant, colorful explosion of design elements (typography, illustration,
photography, 3D shapes) bursting out of a white rectangular "box" frame.
The box is visibly cracked and broken. The elements that escape are diverse
creative works made with the software.

CHANNEL EXECUTIONS:
TV (:60 anthem): Time-lapse of a creator staring at a blank canvas (the box).
They make one mark — and the creation literally explodes out of the screen,
filling the room, the street, the city with color and design.

Social (IG/TikTok): "Break the Grid" challenge — creators share work that
escapes standard formats. Template provided that literally breaks the
Instagram grid when posted as a carousel. 3-post series creates one broken frame.

OOH: 3D anamorphic billboards in NYC, London, Tokyo — the billboard
appears to shatter as design elements pour out into the street (forced
perspective illusion).

Digital: Interactive landing page — users "break" the page by clicking,
revealing hidden creator stories and product features behind the broken walls.

Experiential: "Unboxed Studios" pop-up in 5 cities — white-box spaces where
attendees create live. Their work is projected onto the exterior walls in
real-time, literally filling the box with creativity.
```

```
CAMPAIGN: "Midnight Kitchen" — Premium Cookware Brand Launch
PERIOD: September–November 2024
BUDGET: $2.4M creative production + $8.6M media

RESULTS vs. OBJECTIVES:
| Metric                      | Objective    | Actual       | Status      |
|-----------------------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Aided Brand Awareness       | 35%         | 41%         | ✅ Exceeded  |
| Purchase Intent (target)    | 12%         | 14.3%       | ✅ Exceeded  |
| Website Traffic (unique)    | 500K        | 623K        | ✅ Exceeded  |
| E-commerce Revenue          | $1.8M       | $2.1M       | ✅ Exceeded  |
| Social Engagement Rate      | 3.5%        | 2.8%        | ❌ Below     |
| Email Sign-ups              | 25K         | 31.2K       | ✅ Exceeded  |

CREATIVE LEARNINGS:
What Worked:
- Long-form video (:60) outperformed :15 cuts by 2.3x on completion rate
- "Chef Stories" UGC content drove 4x engagement vs. product-focused posts
- Dark, cinematic visual style differentiated strongly from category (bright, clean kitchens)
- Email sequence with recipe content had 47% open rate (industry avg: 21%)

What Didn't Work:
- Social carousel format underperformed Reels and Stories
- OOH static billboards had low recall vs. digital OOH
- Product-only hero shots performed 60% below lifestyle imagery in digital ads

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR NEXT PHASE:
1. Double down on long-form storytelling — launch a branded content series
2. Shift social budget from carousel to short-form video (Reels, TikTok, Shorts)
3. Replace static OOH with programmatic DOOH for dynamic creative
4. Develop always-on content program around "Chef Stories" UGC community
5. Test influencer collaborations with mid-tier food creators (10K–100K followers)

AWARDS SUBMITTED: Cannes Lions (Film Craft, Brand Experience), One Show, D&AD
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Big Idea**: The central creative concept that unifies all campaign elements and channels; should be expressible in one sentence
- **Creative Platform**: The strategic and creative framework that defines how a brand shows up across all touchpoints over time
- **Integrated Campaign**: A coordinated marketing effort using multiple channels and touchpoints unified by a single idea and strategy
- **Insight**: A deep understanding of human behavior or motivation that reveals an opportunity for creative connection
- **Brief**: The strategic document that defines the objectives, audience, message, and constraints for a creative project
- **Concepting**: The process of developing creative ideas and approaches before execution
- **Execution**: The specific manifestation of a creative concept in a particular format or channel
- **Creative Territory**: A conceptual space or thematic direction that defines the boundaries and possibilities for creative exploration
- **Brand Equity**: The cumulative value of a brand's identity, reputation, and associations in the minds of its audience
- **Channel-Agnostic**: An idea or concept that works across any medium or platform, not dependent on a specific channel
- **Performance Creative**: Creative work designed and optimized for measurable digital outcomes (clicks, conversions, ROAS)
- **Cultural Relevance**: The degree to which creative work connects meaningfully with current cultural conversations, values, and movements

Standards & Frameworks:
- **Cannes Lions**: The international festival of creativity; global benchmark for creative excellence
- **Effie Awards**: Awards recognizing marketing effectiveness; validates creative work that drives business results
- **D&AD**: Design and Art Direction awards; gold standard for creative craft and innovation
- **IPA Effectiveness Databank**: Evidence-based research on what makes advertising effective (Binet & Field)
- **Byron Sharp's "How Brands Grow"**: Evidence-based framework on brand growth through reach and distinctiveness
- **WARC Creative Effectiveness Framework**: Research linking creative quality to business outcomes
- **WCAG 2.1 AA**: Accessibility standards for all digital creative deliverables

Common Tools:
- **Presentation**: Keynote, PowerPoint, Google Slides, Pitch
- **Creative Review**: Frame.io, Ziflow, Filestage, Wipster (for video/asset review)
- **Project Management**: Monday.com, Asana, Basecamp, Wrike
- **Creative Suites**: Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma, Canva (for team enablement)
- **Research & Insight**: WARC, Think with Google, GWI, Mintel, cultural trend platforms
- **Performance Tracking**: Google Analytics, social platform analytics, brand tracking studies
- **Collaboration**: Slack, Microsoft Teams, Miro (for brainstorming), FigJam
