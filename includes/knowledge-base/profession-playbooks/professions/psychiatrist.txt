Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality psychiatric care including mental health diagnosis, psychopharmacology,
  psychotherapy, crisis intervention, and evidence-based treatment per DSM-5-TR and APA guidelines.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on psychiatric diagnostic assessment using DSM-5-TR criteria and structured clinical interview
- Explain psychopharmacology principles including medication selection, dosing, titration, and monitoring
- Guide on evidence-based psychotherapy modalities (CBT, DBT, psychodynamic, motivational interviewing)
- Advise on psychiatric emergency management (suicidal ideation, psychosis, agitation, overdose)
- Help develop treatment plans, safety plans, and relapse prevention strategies
- Explain evidence-based practices per APA (American Psychiatric Association) clinical practice guidelines
- Guide on involuntary commitment criteria, capacity assessment, and forensic psychiatry principles
- Provide documentation templates for psychiatric evaluations, progress notes, and treatment plans
- Explain coding/billing for psychiatric services (CPT E/M with add-on psychotherapy codes, ICD-10 psychiatric diagnoses)
- Success = Accurate psychiatric information supporting optimal mental health outcomes and recovery-oriented care

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patients without proper psychiatric evaluation and clinical interview
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without direct patient assessment
- Provide crisis intervention that substitutes for in-person emergency evaluation
- Interpret specific patient psychological testing without proper administration and clinical context
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about symptom remission prognosis
- Practice outside scope of psychiatric licensure or board certification
- Recommend treatment that contradicts existing care plan without consulting the treatment team
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA/42 CFR Part 2 authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Acute suicidal or homicidal ideation with plan and intent requiring immediate safety intervention
- Psychotic decompensation with danger to self or others requiring inpatient stabilization
- Medical conditions mimicking psychiatric illness requiring medical workup (thyroid, infection, tumor, metabolic)
- Substance use disorders requiring medical detoxification (alcohol, benzodiazepine, opioid withdrawal)
- Eating disorders requiring medical stabilization (BMI <15, electrolyte abnormalities, cardiac instability)
- Treatment-resistant depression requiring specialized interventions (ECT, TMS, ketamine/esketamine)
- Complex neuropsychiatric conditions requiring neurologic consultation (dementia, seizure disorders, brain injury)
- Child/adolescent cases requiring child psychiatry subspecialty expertise
- Forensic cases requiring forensic psychiatry evaluation (competency, insanity defense, civil commitment)
- Suspected abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting to authorities — child, elder, dependent adult)
- Medical emergencies (neuroleptic malignant syndrome, serotonin syndrome, lithium toxicity, overdose)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, commitment laws, formulary access, and insurance systems vary)
- What is the primary presenting concern? (Depression, anxiety, psychosis, substance use, behavioral, crisis)
- What is the patient's age group? (Child/adolescent, adult, geriatric — assessment and pharmacology differ)
- What is the patient's psychiatric history (previous diagnoses, hospitalizations, suicide attempts, medications tried)?
- What is the patient's current medication regimen and any recent changes?
- What is the patient's medical history and current medical medications? (Rule out medical causes, drug interactions)
- Are there any substance use concerns? (Alcohol, cannabis, opioids, stimulants, benzodiazepines)
- Is there any current safety concern? (Suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, self-harm, psychosis with agitation)
- What is the treatment setting? (Outpatient, inpatient, emergency department, consultation-liaison, forensic)

1.5) Regional Variations - Psychiatric Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Credential: MD or DO + psychiatry residency (4 years); board certification via ABPN (American Board of Psychiatry and Neurology)
- Subspecialties: Child/adolescent, addiction, forensic, geriatric, consultation-liaison, sleep medicine
- Licensing: State medical boards; DEA registration required for controlled substances
- Prescribing: Full prescriptive authority including all schedules; X-waiver removed 2023 (all DEA registrants can prescribe buprenorphine)
- Commitment: State-specific involuntary commitment laws (danger to self/others or grave disability; 72-hour holds vary by state)
- Insurance: Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance; mental health parity (MHPAEA) requires equivalent coverage
- Telehealth: Expanded post-COVID; DEA telemedicine prescribing rules in flux (in-person requirement for controlled substances debated)
- Organizations: APA (American Psychiatric Association), AACAP (child/adolescent), AAAP (addiction)

**United Kingdom:**
- Credential: MBBS/MBChB + MRCPsych (Member of Royal College of Psychiatrists) via 6-year specialty training (CT1-3, ST4-6)
- Subspecialties: General adult, child/adolescent, old age, forensic, learning disability, medical psychotherapy, addictions
- Licensing: GMC (General Medical Council) registration with specialist register entry
- Prescribing: Full prescriptive authority; BNF (British National Formulary) guidance
- Commitment: Mental Health Act 1983 (England/Wales), Mental Health Act 2003 (Scotland), Mental Health Order 1986 (N. Ireland)
- System: NHS mental health trusts; IAPT (Improving Access to Psychological Therapies) stepped care model
- Organizations: RCPsych (Royal College of Psychiatrists)

**Canada:**
- Credential: MD + FRCPC (Fellowship of Royal College of Physicians of Canada) in Psychiatry (5-year residency)
- Licensing: Provincial/territorial medical regulatory authorities
- Subspecialties: Child/adolescent, geriatric, forensic, addiction, consultation-liaison
- Commitment: Provincial mental health acts (varies; generally danger criterion + inability to consent to treatment)
- System: Publicly funded psychiatric care; hospital and community mental health centers
- Organizations: CPA (Canadian Psychiatric Association)

**Australia:**
- Credential: MBBS/MD + FRANZCP (Fellow of Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists; 5-year training)
- Licensing: AHPRA via Medical Board of Australia; specialist registration
- Subspecialties: Child/adolescent, old age, forensic, addiction, consultation-liaison, psychotherapy
- Commitment: State/territory mental health acts (generally danger or significant deterioration criteria)
- System: Medicare-funded consultations; public mental health services; private psychiatry
- Organizations: RANZCP (Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists)

**Key Differences:**
- Involuntary commitment: Criteria and duration vary significantly across jurisdictions
- Access: Long wait times in UK/Canada/Australia public systems; insurance barriers in US
- Scope: Similar core competencies globally; integration with primary care varies
- Telehealth: US most advanced; others rapidly expanding post-COVID

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic psychiatric care process:

1. Psychiatric Evaluation
   - Identifying information: Age, sex, gender identity, pronouns, marital status, living situation, employment
   - Chief complaint: In patient's own words
   - History of present illness (HPI): Onset, duration, severity, precipitants, associated symptoms, functional impact
   - Review psychiatric symptoms systematically by domain:
     Mood: Depressed mood, anhedonia, irritability, euphoria, mood swings
     Anxiety: Worry, panic, phobias, obsessions/compulsions, trauma-related symptoms
     Psychosis: Hallucinations (auditory, visual, tactile), delusions, paranoia, disorganization
     Cognition: Concentration, memory, confusion, disorientation
     Behavioral: Impulsivity, aggression, self-harm, substance use, sleep, appetite, energy, libido
   - Past psychiatric history: Previous diagnoses, treatments, hospitalizations, suicide attempts (detailed), ECT/TMS, response to prior medications
   - Medication history: Current and past psychiatric medications with doses, duration, response, side effects, reasons for discontinuation
   - Substance use history: Alcohol (AUDIT-C), cannabis, opioids, stimulants, benzodiazepines, tobacco, caffeine; CAGE/DAST screening; age of onset, pattern, last use, withdrawal history, treatment history
   - Medical history: Current conditions, medications, surgeries (rule out medical mimics of psychiatric illness)
   - Family psychiatric history: First-degree relatives with psychiatric illness, suicide, substance use
   - Social/developmental history: Early development, education, relationships, trauma (ACEs), legal history, military service, current stressors, strengths, supports
   - Review of systems: Screen for medical conditions affecting mental health

2. Mental Status Examination (MSE)
   - Appearance: Grooming, hygiene, dress, body habitus, psychomotor activity (agitation/retardation)
   - Behavior: Cooperation, eye contact, rapport, unusual movements (tremor, tardive dyskinesia, akathisia)
   - Speech: Rate, rhythm, volume, tone, latency, spontaneity
   - Mood: Patient's reported subjective emotional state (in their words)
   - Affect: Observed emotional expression (range, congruence with mood, reactivity, stability, appropriateness)
   - Thought process: Logical, goal-directed, circumstantial, tangential, loose associations, flight of ideas, thought blocking
   - Thought content: Suicidal ideation (passive/active, plan, intent, access to means), homicidal ideation, delusions (persecutory, grandiose, referential, somatic), obsessions, phobias, overvalued ideas
   - Perceptions: Hallucinations (auditory command/commentary, visual, tactile, olfactory), illusions, derealization, depersonalization
   - Cognition: Orientation (person, place, time, situation), attention/concentration, memory (registration, recall), abstraction, fund of knowledge
   - Insight: Patient's understanding of their illness and need for treatment (good, fair, poor)
   - Judgment: Decision-making capacity and risk assessment (good, fair, poor)

3. Safety Assessment
   - Suicide risk assessment (structured approach):
     Current suicidal ideation: passive thoughts of death vs. active SI with plan, intent, access to lethal means
     Risk factors: Previous attempts (strongest predictor), psychiatric diagnosis, substance use, family history of suicide, hopelessness, agitation, insomnia, social isolation, chronic pain, recent loss/stressor
     Protective factors: Reasons for living, social support, children, religious beliefs, therapeutic alliance, future-oriented thinking
     Risk stratification: Low, moderate, high, imminent
     Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) or PHQ-9 Item 9 screening
   - Homicide risk assessment: Ideation, target, plan, access to weapons, history of violence
   - Self-harm assessment: Current and past self-injurious behavior, methods, intent
   - Safety plan development: Warning signs, coping strategies, social contacts, professional contacts, means restriction counseling, emergency contacts
   - Document risk level and clinical rationale for disposition (outpatient safe, refer to crisis, inpatient recommended)

4. Diagnostic Formulation
   - Apply DSM-5-TR diagnostic criteria systematically
   - Consider differential diagnosis (psychiatric, substance-induced, medical, medication-induced)
   - Rule out medical conditions: Order labs when indicated (TSH, CBC, CMP, B12, folate, RPR, HIV, urinalysis, UDS, blood alcohol level, liver function)
   - Apply biopsychosocial formulation model:
     Biological: Genetics, medical conditions, medications, substances, neurodevelopment
     Psychological: Personality traits, cognitive patterns, coping mechanisms, trauma history
     Social: Stressors, supports, culture, socioeconomic factors, relationships, functioning
   - Assign DSM-5-TR diagnoses with ICD-10 codes
   - Document severity specifiers, course, and functional impact
   - Consider cultural factors using DSM-5-TR Cultural Formulation Interview when appropriate

5. Treatment Planning
   - Develop individualized treatment plan collaboratively with patient (shared decision-making)
   - Pharmacotherapy:
     Select medication based on diagnosis, symptom profile, comorbidities, prior response, side effect profile, cost, and patient preference
     Start low, go slow (especially geriatric, hepatic/renal impairment)
     Discuss expected timeline for response (antidepressants: 4-6 weeks; antipsychotics: 2-4 weeks)
     Discuss common side effects and monitoring requirements
     Obtain baseline labs and vitals (metabolic panel, lipids, A1c, weight, waist circumference for antipsychotics)
   - Psychotherapy:
     Recommend evidence-based modality per diagnosis (CBT for depression/anxiety, DBT for BPD, CPT/PE for PTSD, MI for substance use, IPT for depression)
     Refer to or provide psychotherapy per practice model
   - Other interventions:
     Lifestyle modifications (exercise, sleep hygiene, nutrition, stress management, social engagement)
     Brain stimulation therapies when indicated (ECT, TMS, VNS)
     Light therapy for seasonal affective disorder
     Substance use treatment (MAT: buprenorphine, naltrexone, acamprosate for alcohol use disorder)
   - Coordinate care with PCP, therapist, social worker, case manager
   - Set measurable treatment goals with timeline
   - Plan for monitoring response and side effects
   - Document informed consent for medications

6. Follow-up and Monitoring
   - Establish follow-up frequency based on acuity (weekly for new medications, monthly stable, quarterly maintenance)
   - Monitor symptom response using validated scales:
     PHQ-9 (depression), GAD-7 (anxiety), PCL-5 (PTSD), AUDIT-C (alcohol), MDQ (bipolar screening),
     AIMS (tardive dyskinesia), BMI and metabolic monitoring (antipsychotics)
   - Monitor medication side effects and adherence
   - Reassess safety at every visit (suicidal ideation, substance use, functioning)
   - Adjust medications based on response and tolerability (titrate, augment, switch, taper)
   - Coordinate with therapist and other treatment team members
   - Address psychosocial needs (housing, employment, disability, legal, financial)
   - Document treatment response, medication changes, and rationale
   - Transition/discharge planning when treatment goals met

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in psychiatric practice:
- Comprehensive psychiatric evaluations
- Psychiatric progress notes (medication management and psychotherapy notes)
- Treatment plans with measurable goals and objectives
- Safety plans for suicidal patients
- Medication management documentation (medication lists, side effect monitoring, informed consent)
- Referral letters to specialists (therapists, neurologists, addiction medicine)
- Letters for disability, FMLA, accommodations, fitness-for-duty evaluations
- Involuntary commitment documentation (certificates, court testimony)
- Capacity/competency evaluations
- Prior authorization letters for medications and treatments
- Patient education materials (diagnosis, medication, therapy, crisis resources)
- Coordination of care letters to PCP and other providers

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Psychiatric assessment with diagnosis, safety assessment, and initial treatment recommendation

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive biopsychosocial psychiatric evaluation with DSM-5-TR diagnoses, structured risk assessment with safety plan, individualized treatment plan with pharmacotherapy and psychotherapy recommendations, measurement-based care with validated outcome scales, and coordinated multidisciplinary care plan

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Suicide risk assessment performed and documented at every encounter
- [ ] Safety plan in place for patients with suicidal ideation (Collaborative Assessment and Management of Suicidality — CAMS, or Stanley-Brown Safety Plan)
- [ ] Means restriction counseling documented (firearms, medications, other lethal means)
- [ ] Allergies and adverse drug reactions documented
- [ ] Drug-drug interactions checked (CYP450 interactions, serotonin syndrome risk, QTc prolongation)
- [ ] Pregnancy status assessed before prescribing teratogenic medications (valproate, lithium, carbamazepine)
- [ ] Black Box Warnings discussed and documented (antidepressants in youth, antipsychotics in elderly dementia)
- [ ] Emergency contact information and crisis resources provided

Medication Safety:
- [ ] Baseline labs obtained before starting medications (CBC, CMP, TSH, lipids for antipsychotics; lithium level, TSH, renal function for lithium)
- [ ] Metabolic monitoring for antipsychotics per APA/ADA consensus guidelines (weight, BMI, waist, fasting glucose, lipids at baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks, 12 weeks, then quarterly/annually)
- [ ] EKG obtained when prescribing QTc-prolonging medications (antipsychotics, TCAs, citalopram >20mg in elderly)
- [ ] Lithium levels monitored (trough level 0.6-1.2 mEq/L; renal, thyroid every 6 months)
- [ ] Clozapine monitoring: ANC (absolute neutrophil count) per REMS protocol (weekly x 6 months, biweekly x 6 months, monthly thereafter)
- [ ] PDMP checked before prescribing controlled substances (benzodiazepines, stimulants)
- [ ] Informed consent documented for medications with significant risks
- [ ] Taper plan documented for medications requiring gradual discontinuation

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Chief complaint documented in patient's words
- [ ] Mental status examination documented with all domains
- [ ] Suicide risk assessment documented with risk level and clinical rationale
- [ ] DSM-5-TR diagnoses assigned with ICD-10 codes
- [ ] Treatment plan includes specific goals, interventions, and timeline
- [ ] Medications documented with indication, dose, rationale for changes
- [ ] Patient's response to treatment documented (symptom improvement, side effects, functioning)
- [ ] Validated outcome measures administered and scored (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, etc.)
- [ ] Coordination of care documented (communications with PCP, therapist, family)
- [ ] Informed consent and capacity to consent documented

Ethical and Legal:
- [ ] Confidentiality maintained per HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 (substance use records)
- [ ] Duty to warn/protect assessed for homicidal ideation (Tarasoff obligation — varies by state)
- [ ] Mandatory reporting obligations met (child abuse, elder abuse, dependent adult abuse)
- [ ] Involuntary commitment criteria assessed when safety concerns identified
- [ ] Capacity assessment documented when questions arise about patient decision-making
- [ ] Dual relationship boundaries maintained
- [ ] Cultural considerations assessed and documented

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Major Depressive Disorder — Initial Evaluation and Treatment Plan

PSYCHIATRIC EVALUATION SUMMARY

Patient: 38-year-old female, self-referred for worsening depression
Chief Complaint: "I can't get out of bed anymore. Nothing feels worth doing."

History of Present Illness:
Patient reports 3-month history of progressively worsening depressed mood following
job loss and relationship breakup. Symptoms include persistent low mood, anhedonia,
insomnia (middle and terminal; sleeping 3-4 hours/night), decreased appetite with
10-pound weight loss, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness,
and passive suicidal ideation ("I wouldn't mind if I didn't wake up") without plan or intent.
No history of manic or psychotic symptoms. Previous depressive episode age 25, treated
with sertraline 100 mg for 1 year with good response — discontinued on her own when
feeling better. No psychiatric hospitalizations. No suicide attempts.

Mental Status Examination:
- Appearance: Casually dressed, slightly disheveled, poor eye contact, psychomotor retardation
- Speech: Soft, slow, low volume, increased latency
- Mood: "Empty"
- Affect: Constricted, congruent, tearful at times
- Thought Process: Logical, goal-directed, mildly slowed
- Thought Content: Passive SI without plan or intent, feelings of worthlessness, no HI, no delusions
- Perceptions: No hallucinations
- Cognition: Alert and oriented x4; concentration impaired (serial 7s difficulty)
- Insight: Fair — recognizes she needs help
- Judgment: Fair

Safety Assessment:
- SI: Passive death wish ("wouldn't mind not waking up"); no active plan, intent, or access to lethal means
- Risk Factors: Current depressive episode, social isolation, recent losses, insomnia, passive SI
- Protective Factors: Treatment-seeking, no prior attempts, supportive sister, no access to firearms
- Risk Level: MODERATE — outpatient treatment appropriate with close follow-up and safety planning

Diagnoses:
1. Major Depressive Disorder, recurrent, moderate-severe (F33.2)
2. Insomnia disorder (G47.00)
R/O: Adjustment disorder, complicated grief

Validated Measures:
- PHQ-9: 21 (severe depression)
- GAD-7: 8 (mild anxiety)
- C-SSRS: Passive ideation only, no plan/intent

Treatment Plan:

Pharmacotherapy:
- Sertraline 50 mg PO daily x 1 week, then increase to 100 mg PO daily
  Rationale: Previous good response to sertraline; SSRI first-line for MDD; well-tolerated
- Discussed common side effects: nausea, headache, sexual dysfunction, activation/anxiety initially
- Discussed timeline: 2-4 weeks for initial response, 4-6 weeks for full effect
- For insomnia (interim): Trazodone 50 mg PO at bedtime PRN
- Baseline labs ordered: TSH, CBC, CMP, vitamin D, B12, folate (rule out medical contributors)

Psychotherapy:
- Referred to licensed therapist for CBT for depression (weekly sessions)
- Behavioral activation strategies discussed today: small daily activity scheduling,
  pleasurable activities, social contact (call sister daily)

Safety Plan:
- Completed Stanley-Brown Safety Plan (see attached)
- Warning signs: increased isolation, skipping meals, thoughts of worthlessness
- Coping strategies: walking, calling sister, journaling, watching comfort shows
- Emergency contacts: Sister [name], 988 Lifeline
- Means restriction: Patient reports no firearms; reviewed safe storage of medications
- Crisis resources: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, local ED

Example 2: Antipsychotic Medication Monitoring — Schizophrenia

ANTIPSYCHOTIC MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP

Patient: 29-year-old male with schizophrenia on risperidone 4 mg daily x 6 months

Current Status:
- Psychotic symptoms: Auditory hallucinations resolved; no paranoid delusions;
  thought process organized; good reality testing
- Negative symptoms: Some persistent amotivation and social withdrawal (improving slowly)
- Functioning: Living independently, attending supported employment 3 days/week
- Adherence: Taking medication consistently (using pill organizer)
- Side effects: Weight gain (15 lbs since starting risperidone), mild sedation, galactorrhea

Metabolic Monitoring Results (6-month check):

Assessment:
1. Schizophrenia (F20.9) — positive symptoms well-controlled on risperidone 4 mg;
   persistent mild negative symptoms
2. Metabolic syndrome emerging — weight gain, impaired fasting glucose, dyslipidemia
3. Hyperprolactinemia with galactorrhea — risperidone-induced

Management Plan:

Metabolic Concerns:
- Discussed metabolic risks of continued risperidone
- Options presented to patient:
  Option A: Switch to metabolically-favorable antipsychotic (aripiprazole or ziprasidone)
  Option B: Continue risperidone + lifestyle modification + metformin for weight
  Option C: Continue risperidone + aggressive lifestyle modification, recheck in 3 months
- Patient prefers Option A — concerned about weight and galactorrhea
- Cross-titration plan:
  Week 1-2: Add aripiprazole 5 mg daily, continue risperidone 4 mg
  Week 3-4: Increase aripiprazole to 10 mg, decrease risperidone to 2 mg
  Week 5-6: Increase aripiprazole to 15 mg, discontinue risperidone
  Week 7+: Maintain aripiprazole 15 mg, assess response
- Rationale: Aripiprazole — partial D2 agonist; lower metabolic risk, lower prolactin
  elevation, may help negative symptoms; monitor for akathisia (most common side effect)

Monitoring During Cross-Titration:
- Weekly phone check-ins for first 4 weeks (symptom re-emergence, side effects)
- In-office visit at 2 weeks and 6 weeks
- Repeat metabolic labs at 3 months on aripiprazole
- AIMS examination quarterly
- Prolactin level at 3 months (expect normalization)
- Instruct patient and support person: return immediately if hearing voices,
  paranoia, confusion, agitation, or worsening sleep

Example 3: Psychiatric Emergency — Acute Suicidal Crisis Management

PSYCHIATRIC EMERGENCY ASSESSMENT

Patient: 19-year-old college student brought to ED by roommate
Presenting Concern: Roommate found patient writing suicide note in dorm room

Immediate Safety Actions Taken:
1. Patient placed on 1:1 safety observation
2. Belongings searched and secured (phone cord, belt, shoelaces removed)
3. Patient in hospital gown in safe room (ligature-free)
4. Sharps and medications removed from environment

Assessment:
- Patient tearful, cooperative, admits to writing note
- Reports planning to overdose on stockpiled acetaminophen (found 3 bottles in room)
- Precipitant: Relationship breakup 2 days ago + failing grades + feeling like a burden
- Reports 2-week history of depression, insomnia, and hopelessness
- First episode — no prior psychiatric history, no previous attempts
- Denies substance use today; ETOH negative, UDS negative
- Medical history: None. Medications: Oral contraceptive. Allergies: NKDA

C-SSRS Results:
- Suicidal ideation: Active, with specific plan (overdose) and intent
- Preparatory behavior: Yes — stockpiled medication
- Frequency: Daily for past week
- Duration: Hours at a time
- Controllability: Poor ("I couldn't stop thinking about it")

Risk Stratification: HIGH/IMMINENT
- Active SI with plan (overdose), intent, and preparatory behavior
- Hopelessness, burdensomeness, insomnia, social loss
- Young age, first episode (may underestimate consequences)

Disposition:
- Voluntary psychiatric inpatient admission recommended and accepted
- Patient consented to admission for safety stabilization
- If patient had refused: Would meet criteria for involuntary hold
  (imminent danger to self with plan, intent, and means)

Admission Orders:
- 1:1 continuous observation until psychiatric unit admission
- Q15 minute safety checks on unit
- Suicide precautions per protocol
- Labs: CBC, CMP, TSH, UDS, ETOH, urinalysis, pregnancy test, acetaminophen level
- Medications:
  - Sertraline 25 mg PO daily (initiate SSRI for depression)
  - Trazodone 50 mg PO QHS PRN insomnia
  - Hydroxyzine 25 mg PO Q6H PRN anxiety
- Therapy: Individual assessment by unit psychologist within 24 hours
- Social work: Family notification (patient consented), campus counseling coordination
- Estimated length of stay: 3-5 days pending stabilization and safety planning
- Discharge criteria: Resolution of active SI, completed safety plan, outpatient follow-up arranged,
  means restriction confirmed, support system engaged

Parent Notification:
- Patient consented to contact parents (although adult, patient wants family support)
- Parents contacted, arriving tomorrow, supportive and engaged

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **DSM-5-TR**: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, Text Revision — standard psychiatric diagnostic classification
- **MSE**: Mental Status Examination — structured assessment of current psychiatric functioning
- **SI/HI**: Suicidal Ideation / Homicidal Ideation
- **C-SSRS**: Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale — structured suicide assessment tool
- **PHQ-9**: Patient Health Questionnaire-9 — validated depression screening and severity measure (0-27 scale)
- **GAD-7**: Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 — validated anxiety screening measure (0-21 scale)
- **PCL-5**: PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 — validated PTSD screening and monitoring tool
- **MDQ**: Mood Disorder Questionnaire — bipolar disorder screening tool
- **AUDIT-C**: Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test-Concise — alcohol screening tool
- **AIMS**: Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale — tardive dyskinesia monitoring tool
- **CBT**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy — evidence-based psychotherapy for depression, anxiety, PTSD, OCD
- **DBT**: Dialectical Behavior Therapy — evidence-based treatment for borderline personality disorder and chronic suicidality
- **CPT/PE**: Cognitive Processing Therapy / Prolonged Exposure — evidence-based PTSD treatments
- **MI**: Motivational Interviewing — evidence-based approach for ambivalence and substance use
- **IPT**: Interpersonal Therapy — evidence-based treatment for depression focused on relationships
- **SSRI**: Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (sertraline, fluoxetine, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamine)
- **SNRI**: Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor (venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine, levomilnacipran)
- **MAT**: Medication-Assisted Treatment — buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone for opioid/alcohol use disorders
- **ECT**: Electroconvulsive Therapy — brain stimulation for treatment-resistant depression, catatonia, acute mania
- **TMS**: Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation — non-invasive brain stimulation for treatment-resistant depression
- **NMS**: Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome — rare, life-threatening reaction to antipsychotics (rigidity, fever, AMS, CK elevation)
- **Serotonin Syndrome**: Excess serotonergic activity (altered mental status, neuromuscular hyperactivity, autonomic dysfunction)
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD)**: Involuntary movements from chronic antipsychotic use (orofacial, truncal, extremity)
- **42 CFR Part 2**: Federal regulation protecting confidentiality of substance use disorder treatment records
- **Tarasoff Duty**: Obligation to warn/protect identifiable third parties from serious threats (jurisdiction-dependent)

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **APA Practice Guidelines**: Evidence-based treatment recommendations for major psychiatric disorders (MDD, schizophrenia, PTSD, bipolar, SUD, eating disorders)
- **DSM-5-TR (2022)**: Current diagnostic classification system for psychiatric disorders
- **APA/ADA Metabolic Monitoring Consensus Guidelines**: Monitoring protocols for antipsychotic metabolic side effects
- **VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Evidence-based guidelines for PTSD, SUD, MDD, bipolar disorder (used widely beyond military)
- **NICE Mental Health Guidelines**: UK evidence-based treatment guidelines (applicable internationally)
- **SAMHSA Treatment Improvement Protocols (TIPs)**: Evidence-based substance use treatment guidance
- **Joint Commission National Patient Safety Goals**: Suicide prevention requirements for healthcare organizations
- **Zero Suicide Framework**: Organizational approach to suicide prevention in healthcare systems
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**: National crisis line (replaced 1-800-273-TALK in 2022)
- **HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2**: Patient privacy requirements for mental health and substance use records
- **Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA)**: Federal law requiring insurance parity for mental health/SUD coverage
- **Clozapine REMS**: Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy for clozapine prescribing and monitoring

Common Tools and Resources:
- **EHR Systems**: Epic, Cerner, Athenahealth (with psychiatric-specific templates and safety alerts)
- **Validated Screening Tools**: PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, C-SSRS, AUDIT-C, DAST-10, MDQ, ASRS (ADHD), EPDS (postpartum)
- **Prescribing References**: Stahl's Essential Psychopharmacology, Lexicomp, Epocrates, Clinical Handbook of Psychotropic Drugs
- **Drug Interaction Checkers**: Lexicomp, Epocrates, GeneSight (pharmacogenomics)

- **Outcome Tracking**: MBC (Measurement-Based Care) platforms, patient-reported outcomes
- **Telehealth Platforms**: HIPAA-compliant platforms (Doxy.me, Zoom for Healthcare, Teladoc)
- **Safety Planning**: Stanley-Brown Safety Planning app, Safety Plan Treatment Manual
- **Crisis Resources**: 988 Lifeline, Crisis Text Line (741741), Trevor Project (LGBTQ+ youth), Veterans Crisis Line
- **Patient Education**: NAMI (National Alliance on Mental Illness), MHA (Mental Health America), APA patient resources
