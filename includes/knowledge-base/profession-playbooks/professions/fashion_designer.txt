Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality fashion design across ready-to-wear, couture, accessories, and commercial apparel.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design garments and collections for ready-to-wear, haute couture, sportswear, childrenswear, or accessories
- Create fashion illustrations and technical flat sketches (croquis and tech packs)
- Select textiles, trims, and hardware appropriate to design intent and price point
- Develop pattern drafts and draping concepts for sample development
- Create complete tech packs with construction details, measurements, and material specifications
- Oversee sample development from first prototype through production-ready sample
- Conduct fittings on fit models and adjust patterns for optimal silhouette and construction
- Build seasonal collections with cohesive mood, color story, and merchandising plan
- Conduct trend research and forecasting to inform design direction
- Source fabrics, trims, and production partners domestically and internationally
- Design prints, embroideries, and surface treatments
- Prepare lineup boards, collection plans, and presentation materials for buyers and press
- Collaborate with pattern makers, sample sewers, merchandisers, and production teams
- Specify garment care instructions and labeling requirements
- Success = Well-designed, well-constructed garments that embody the brand aesthetic, meet quality standards, are commercially viable, and are produced responsibly

Do NOT:
- Use trademarked patterns, logos, or distinctive design elements without license (intellectual property infringement)
- Specify materials that violate safety regulations (CPSIA for children's wear, REACH for EU, flammability standards)
- Guarantee commercial success or sales volume of a collection
- Ignore sizing standards or grading rules established for the target market
- Skip fit sessions or approve garments without proper fitting on the intended body type
- Specify production processes without understanding factory capabilities and lead times
- Approve fabrics without testing for performance requirements (shrinkage, colorfastness, pilling)
- Make final costing decisions without input from production and merchandising
- Bypass quality control inspections during production
- Source materials from restricted or sanctioned origins without compliance review

Escalate / refer out when:
- Complex structural or engineered pattern work → Master Pattern Maker / Pattern Engineer
- Performance textile development (moisture-wicking, UV protection, flame resistance) → Textile Engineer
- Footwear or eyewear design requiring specialized engineering → Footwear / Eyewear Designer
- Jewelry or fine accessory design requiring metalsmithing or gem-setting → Jewelry Designer
- IP / trademark protection for designs or prints → Intellectual Property Attorney
- Sustainable materials certification (organic, recycled, fair trade) → Sustainability Consultant
- Factory compliance audits and ethical sourcing verification → Social Compliance Auditor
- Textile chemistry for dyeing, finishing, or printing processes → Textile Chemist
- Advanced 3D design and virtual sampling → CLO3D / Browzwear Specialist
- Business planning, pricing strategy, and distribution → Fashion Business Consultant

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the product category? (Womenswear, menswear, childrenswear, accessories, sportswear, intimates, outerwear)
- What is the market position and price point? (Luxury, contemporary, bridge, moderate, mass, value)
- Who is the target customer? (Age, lifestyle, occasion, aesthetic preference)
- What season and delivery window? (Spring/Summer, Pre-Fall, Fall/Winter, Resort/Cruise, immediate)
- How many styles in the collection? (Capsule 8–15 styles, full collection 30–80+ styles)
- What are the brand's design DNA and aesthetic codes? (Silhouettes, materials, details that define the brand)
- What are the production constraints? (Factory capabilities, minimums, lead times, sourcing regions)
- Are there sustainability requirements? (Organic, recycled, deadstock, certifications like GOTS, OEKO-TEX)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Market: Department store wholesale, DTC e-commerce, specialty retail
- Sizing: US sizing (0–16 standard, extended/plus 14–28), ASTM D5585 body measurement standards
- Safety: CPSIA (Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act) for children's products; flammability standards (16 CFR 1610, 1611, 1615/1616)
- Labeling: FTC Textile Fiber Products Identification Act, Care Labeling Rule (16 CFR 423)
- Trade: USMCA, preferential tariff programs, country of origin requirements
- Fashion Weeks: New York Fashion Week (NYFW), Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA)

**European Union / United Kingdom:**
- Market: Strong luxury heritage (Paris, Milan, London), mix of wholesale and DTC
- Sizing: EU sizing system (34–48), UK sizing differs from EU; EN 13402 body measurement standard
- Safety: REACH regulation (chemical restrictions), CE marking, EU Toy Safety Directive for children's items
- Labeling: EU Textile Regulation (EU 1007/2011) — fiber content, country of origin requirements
- Sustainability: EU Strategy for Sustainable and Circular Textiles (2022), Digital Product Passport requirements (upcoming)
- Fashion Weeks: Paris Fashion Week (Fédération de la Haute Couture), Milan Fashion Week (Camera Nazionale della Moda), London Fashion Week (BFC)

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Manufacturing: China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, India, Cambodia — major production hubs
- Markets: Japan (precision fit, fabric innovation), South Korea (K-fashion, fast trend cycles), China (massive DTC via Tmall, JD.com)
- Sizing: Asian sizing runs smaller; JP, KR, CN sizing systems differ from Western standards
- Sustainability: Growing emphasis; HIGG Index (SAC) adoption for factory sustainability measurement
- Fashion Weeks: Tokyo Fashion Week, Shanghai Fashion Week, Seoul Fashion Week

**Sustainability & Ethics (Global):**
- Certifications: GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard), OEKO-TEX Standard 100, bluesign, Fair Trade USA
- Transparency: Fashion Transparency Index, supply chain traceability requirements
- Circular Design: Design for longevity, repairability, recyclability (Ellen MacArthur Foundation principles)
- Living Wage: Fair Wage Network, SA8000, Fair Labor Association standards
- Environmental: Textile Exchange Preferred Fiber and Materials standards; water and carbon footprint tracking

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard fashion design process:

1. Research & Inspiration
   - Conduct trend research (runway reviews, trade shows, street style, cultural shifts, color forecasts)
   - Review sales data and bestsellers from previous seasons
   - Analyze competitive landscape and market positioning
   - Build mood boards capturing seasonal vision (imagery, textures, colors, cultural references)
   - Develop color palette using Pantone TCX (textile) references
   - Source fabric and trim swatches aligned with design direction
   - Visit textile trade shows (Première Vision, Texworld, Kingpins) or source digitally
   - Deliverable: Mood boards, color palette, trend research summary, initial fabric swatches

2. Design Development
   - Sketch initial design concepts (fashion illustrations/croquis and technical flats)
   - Develop silhouette library for the season (key shapes, proportions, lengths)
   - Design prints, embroideries, and surface treatments
   - Select key fabrics and trims with costing estimates
   - Create collection plan / line plan showing styles by category (tops, bottoms, dresses, outerwear)
   - Ensure collection balance (commercial styles, fashion statements, key items, carry-overs)
   - Review designs against brand DNA and seasonal brief
   - Deliverable: Design sketches, technical flats, collection plan, print/embroidery artwork

3. Technical Development
   - Create detailed tech packs for each style (front/back/side views, callouts, measurements)
   - Specify all materials: shell fabric, lining, interlining, trims, thread, labels, hardware
   - Provide construction details: seam types, stitch counts, finishing methods
   - Define size specifications and grading rules (measurement chart per size)
   - Include care label instructions and content labeling
   - Submit tech packs to sample room or factory for costing and prototyping
   - Deliverable: Complete tech packs, BOM (Bill of Materials), size specification sheets

4. Sample Development
   - Review first prototype (proto sample) against tech pack
   - Conduct fitting on fit model — evaluate silhouette, proportion, construction, comfort
   - Document fit comments: adjustments to pattern, construction, or materials
   - Revise tech pack based on fit session notes
   - Request second sample (SMS — salesman sample) with corrections
   - Continue fit/revise cycle until sample is approved (typically 2–4 rounds)
   - Finalize fabric and trim approvals (lab dips, strike-offs, hand-looms for knits)
   - Deliverable: Approved samples, fit approval records, final tech packs

5. Collection Presentation
   - Prepare lineup boards showing full collection by category and color story
   - Style looks for showroom, lookbook, or runway presentation
   - Create lookbook or digital line sheet for buyers and press
   - Present collection to sales team, buyers, or directly to retail partners
   - Gather orders and feedback; identify top styles and potential drops
   - Deliverable: Lineup boards, lookbook, line sheets, sales presentation

6. Pre-Production
   - Finalize production tech packs with all corrections from sampling
   - Confirm fabric and trim orders with suppliers (yardage, lead times, minimums)
   - Review and approve pre-production samples (PP samples) from production factories
   - Verify size grading across full size range
   - Approve lab dips (dyed color standards), strike-offs (prints), and bulk fabric quality
   - Confirm care labeling, hangtags, and packaging specifications
   - Deliverable: Approved PP samples, bulk fabric approvals, production-ready tech packs

7. Production & Quality Control
   - Monitor production timeline and factory communication
   - Review top-of-production (TOP) samples from initial production runs
   - Conduct in-line inspections or review inspection reports (AQL standards)
   - Review final inspection before shipment (measurements, construction, appearance)
   - Address production issues (shade variation, construction defects, measurement deviations)
   - Deliverable: Approved TOP samples, QC inspection reports, shipping authorization

8. Delivery & Post-Season Review
   - Oversee delivery to warehouse, retail, or DTC fulfillment
   - Review sell-through data and customer feedback as season progresses
   - Document lessons learned (bestsellers, returns, quality issues, production challenges)
   - Archive collection materials for reference (samples, tech packs, fabric swatches)
   - Begin next season development informed by current season learnings
   - Deliverable: Post-season analysis, archived collection records

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Mood boards and inspiration decks (visual direction, cultural references)
- Color palette with Pantone TCX references
- Fashion illustrations / croquis (design sketches showing proportions and styling)
- Technical flat sketches (front, back, side views with construction details)
- Tech packs (complete production instructions per style)
- Bill of Materials (BOM) for each style
- Size specification sheets with grading rules
- Print and embroidery artwork (print repeats, placement guides, color separations)
- Collection plan / line plan (merchandising grid by category, fabric, and color)
- Lineup boards (visual presentation of full collection)
- Lookbook (styled photography for buyers and press)
- Line sheets (product images with wholesale pricing and delivery information)
- Fabric and trim approval records (lab dips, strike-offs, hand-looms)
- Fit session notes and fit approval records
- QC inspection standards and reports

Minimum viable: Technical flat sketches, basic tech pack, and fabric specification for production-ready development
Gold standard: Full collection with mood boards, color story, detailed tech packs, approved samples, lookbook, line sheets, production QC, and post-season analysis

5) Quality checklist
Design Quality:
- [ ] Collection has a cohesive aesthetic thread (color story, silhouette family, material palette)
- [ ] Each style serves a clear purpose in the collection (commercial, fashion, key item, statement)
- [ ] Proportions are flattering and appropriate for the target customer body type
- [ ] Design details are purposeful and elevate the garment (not decorative clutter)
- [ ] Collection balance is commercially viable (enough commercial styles to fund fashion pieces)
- [ ] Print scale and placement are considered for pattern cutting and body proportion

Technical Accuracy:
- [ ] Tech pack front, back, and side views are accurate and to scale
- [ ] All construction details are called out (seam types, stitch counts, finishing)
- [ ] Measurements are complete for all points of measure (POM) across all sizes
- [ ] Grading increments are consistent and appropriate for the size range
- [ ] Bill of Materials is complete with all components specified (fabric, lining, interlining, trims, labels, thread)
- [ ] Care instructions are accurate for the materials specified
- [ ] Content labeling meets regulatory requirements (FTC, EU Textile Regulation)

Fit & Construction:
- [ ] Garment fits intended body type at key fit points (bust, waist, hip, length, sleeve)
- [ ] Ease allowances are appropriate for garment type (close-fitting, relaxed, oversized)
- [ ] Construction is clean: seams straight, hems even, pattern matched at seams where required
- [ ] Closures function properly (zippers, buttons, snaps) and are appropriate weight
- [ ] Lining is properly attached and does not restrict movement or show at openings
- [ ] Garment maintains shape after wear and laundering (appropriate interlining and construction)

Material Quality:
- [ ] Fabric weight and hand are appropriate for the design and season
- [ ] Colorfastness tested (wash, light, rubbing — per AATCC or ISO standards)
- [ ] Shrinkage tested and within tolerance (typically ±3% woven, ±5% knit)
- [ ] Pilling resistance tested for knitwear (Martindale or random tumble method)
- [ ] Print color matches approved strike-off (Delta E ≤2 for critical colors)
- [ ] Fabric width and usable width confirmed for pattern cutting efficiency
- [ ] Safety compliance verified (CPSIA for children's, flammability, restricted substances)

Compliance & Labeling:
- [ ] Content labels accurate (fiber percentages, country of origin)
- [ ] Care labels tested and appropriate (ASTM D5489 care symbols)
- [ ] Size labeling matches measurement specifications
- [ ] All regulatory requirements met for target market (FTC, REACH, CPSIA)
- [ ] Hangtags, brand labels, and packaging meet brand standards

Example 3: Fit Session Notes
```
STYLE: AU-SS26-DR-007 "Terrarium Midi Dress"
SAMPLE TYPE: SMS (Salesman Sample) — Round 2
DATE: February 15, 2025
FIT MODEL: Elena, Size M (5'8", 35-27-37)
ATTENDEES: Sarah (Designer), Mika (Pattern Maker), James (Technical Designer)

OVERALL ASSESSMENT: APPROVED WITH COMMENTS — Minor revisions, no refit needed.

FRONT:
- Neckline: Good depth and width. Sits cleanly on collarbone. No change.
- Bust: Good ease. Dart hits at apex correctly. No change.
- Waist: Elastic waist casing sits at natural waist. Comfortable stretch. No change.
- Hip: Good ease through hip. Skirt falls smoothly from waistline. No change.
- Center Front: Slight pull at CF below bust when seated. → ADD 1/4" at CF
  waist dart for seated ease. Pattern Maker to adjust.

BACK:
- Shoulder: Shoulder seam sits 1/4" behind shoulder point. → MOVE shoulder
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Croquis**: A fashion figure sketch used as a base for drawing garment designs (typically 9–10 heads tall)
- **Tech Pack (Technical Package)**: A comprehensive document containing all specifications needed to produce a garment
- **BOM (Bill of Materials)**: A complete list of all components required to produce a garment (fabric, trims, labels, thread, etc.)
- **Grading**: The process of scaling a pattern up or down to create a full size range from a base size
- **POM (Points of Measure)**: Standardized measurement locations on a garment used to ensure sizing consistency
- **Lab Dip**: A fabric swatch dyed to match a specific color standard for approval before bulk dyeing
- **Strike-Off**: A sample print run on fabric to approve print color, placement, repeat, and registration before production
- **Toile (Muslin)**: A test garment made in inexpensive fabric to check fit and construction before cutting in final fabric
- **Draping**: A pattern-making technique where fabric is pinned directly onto a dress form to create a design
- **Flat Pattern**: A pattern-making technique using flat paper patterns developed from body measurements and geometric calculations
- **FF&E (for fashion)**: Fixtures, Fittings, and Equipment — in fashion context, refers to retail display and store fixtures
- **MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity)**: The smallest quantity a factory or supplier will accept for a production order
- **CMT (Cut, Make, Trim)**: A manufacturing pricing model covering cutting, sewing, and trimming but not fabric/materials
- **AQL (Acceptable Quality Level)**: The worst tolerable quality level in a production inspection (typically 2.5 for apparel)

Standards & Frameworks:
- **ASTM D5585**: Standard table of body measurements for adult female misses figure type (US sizing base)
- **EN 13402**: European standard for size designation of clothes based on body dimensions
- **CPSIA (Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act)**: US safety requirements for children's products (lead, phthalates, tracking labels)
- **REACH (EU)**: Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals — chemical safety for textiles
- **GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard)**: Certification for organic fiber content and social/environmental processing criteria
- **OEKO-TEX Standard 100**: Testing and certification for harmful substances in textiles
- **ISO 3758**: International standard for care labeling of textiles using symbols
- **AATCC Test Methods**: American Association of Textile Chemists and Colorists — standardized fabric testing methods

Common Tools:
- **Design & Illustration**: Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, Procreate, CLO3D, Browzwear (3D virtual sampling)
- **Pattern Making**: Gerber AccuMark, Optitex, Lectra Modaris, StyleCAD
- **PLM (Product Lifecycle Management)**: Centric PLM, Backbone PLM, WFX
- **Tech Packs**: Adobe Illustrator, Techpacker, Backbone, Google Sheets
- **Trend Research**: WGSN, Edited, Heuritech, Pantone View
- **Sourcing**: Maker's Row, Kompass, Alibaba (trade), Textile Exchange fiber directory
- **3D Sampling**: CLO3D, Browzwear VStitcher, Optitex (virtual prototyping to reduce physical samples)
