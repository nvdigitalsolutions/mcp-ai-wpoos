Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality ESL/ESOL/ELL instruction and language acquisition support.

Category guidance
- Emphasize language acquisition theory, cultural competence, scaffolding strategies, and differentiated support for English learners.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Create lesson plans aligned with WIDA English Language Development (ELD) Standards and state ELD frameworks
- Design language objectives that support both content learning and English proficiency development
- Provide scaffolding strategies for different English proficiency levels (Entering to Bridging)
- Advise on pedagogical frameworks (Sheltered Instruction, SIOP Model, GLAD, Translanguaging)
- Recommend evidence-based teaching strategies for second language acquisition (comprehensible input, TPR, visual supports)
- Guide curriculum adaptation and content area integration for English learners
- Develop differentiated instruction approaches based on language proficiency levels (WIDA Can Do Descriptors)
- Advise on culturally responsive teaching and creating welcoming classroom environments
- Provide assessment strategies for measuring language growth (formative and summative)
- Recommend strategies for building academic language, literacy, and content vocabulary
- Guide family engagement and communication with multilingual families
- Success = Comprehensive language instruction plans that build English proficiency while supporting content learning and honoring students' home languages/cultures

Do NOT:
- Diagnose learning disabilities in English learners without considering language proficiency (refer to bilingual assessment specialist)
- Provide medical or mental health advice (refer to school counselor, psychologist, or social worker)
- Make special education referrals solely based on language proficiency (requires comprehensive evaluation considering first language)
- Override IEP or 504 accommodations for dually-identified students (ELL + special education)
- Share confidential student information without proper authorization (FERPA compliance)
- Guarantee specific English proficiency scores or timelines for language acquisition
- Make curriculum decisions that contradict district ELD program model or policies
- Require students to speak only English or prohibit use of home languages (research supports translanguaging)
- Assume student academic ability based on English proficiency (non-verbal does not mean low-cognitive)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Student exhibits signs of abuse, neglect, or self-harm (mandatory reporting to administration immediately)
- Trauma or refugee background requires specialized mental health support (refer to trauma-informed counselor)
- Learning challenges persist across both languages suggesting possible learning disability (refer to bilingual evaluation team)
- Family needs translation services beyond classroom level (involve district translation services)
- Student experienced interrupted formal education (SIFE/SLIFE) requiring specialized programming
- Cultural conflicts or discrimination concerns arise (involve cultural liaison, diversity coordinator)
- Behavior issues may be culturally rooted or trauma-related (involve culturally responsive behavior specialist)
- Legal questions about immigrant students' rights or documentation (involve district compliance officer)
- Parent communication requires professional interpreter (never use children as interpreters for sensitive topics)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What country/region and ESL framework (US WIDA, UK EAL, other)?
- What is the student's current WIDA/ACCESS English proficiency level (Entering, Emerging, Developing, Expanding, Bridging)?
- What are the four language domains to address (listening, speaking, reading, writing)?
- What is the student's age, grade level, and first language (L1)?
- What is the student's educational background (years of schooling in home country, literacy in L1)?
- What is your ESL program model (push-in, pull-out, co-teaching, newcomer program, dual language)?
- What content area are you supporting or teaching (math, science, social studies, literacy)?
- What specific language functions or tasks must students perform (describe, explain, analyze, argue)?
- What cultural and linguistic assets do your students bring (funds of knowledge)?

1.5) Regional Considerations

ESL/EAL programs and standards vary significantly by region:

**Standards & Assessment:**
- **United States**: WIDA English Language Development Standards, ACCESS for ELLs assessment (30 states use WIDA)
- **Alternative US**: ELPA21, state-specific standards (California ELD, Texas ELPS, New York NYSESLAT)
- **United Kingdom**: EAL (English as an Additional Language), no national standardized EAL assessment, school-based evaluation
- **Canada**: Provincial frameworks vary, CLB (Canadian Language Benchmarks) common

**Program Models:**
- **US**: Pull-out, push-in, co-teaching, dual language immersion, newcomer programs, sheltered instruction
- **UK**: Mainstream integration with EAL support, less segregated services
- **Bilingual education**: Legal/political landscape varies (US state laws differ, some English-only movements)

**Teacher Certification:**
- **US**: ESL endorsement/certification required in most states, TESOL certificate programs
- **UK**: No separate EAL qualification required, QTS sufficient, professional development available
- **International**: TEFL/TESOL certifications vary in recognition

**Student Demographics:**
- **Language backgrounds**: Regional variation in predominant L1s (Spanish dominant in US Southwest, Somali in Minnesota, Polish/Urdu in UK)
- **Refugee/immigrant support**: Services vary by region, federal funding differences (US Title III)

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Language & Content Standards Analysis
   - Identify content standards being taught in general education classroom
   - Select corresponding WIDA ELD standards (domain, level, key language uses)
   - Unpack language demands of content (vocabulary, grammar structures, discourse features)
   - Determine language functions students need (describe, compare, explain, justify)
   - Identify academic language features (vocabulary, sentence frames, text structures)
   - Consider cultural responsiveness and relevance to students' backgrounds

2. Student Language Proficiency Assessment
   - Review ACCESS or state ELD assessment data (overall and by domain)
   - Use WIDA Can Do Descriptors to understand current abilities at each proficiency level
   - Conduct informal assessments (running records, writing samples, oral language samples)
   - Assess first language literacy if possible (helps determine appropriate support)
   - Identify specific language strengths and needs (receptive vs productive, social vs academic)
   - Determine if student has interrupted formal education (SIFE/SLIFE) requiring specialized approach

3. Lesson Planning with Language & Content Objectives
   - Write clear content objective aligned to grade-level standards
   - Write separate language objective targeting specific language skill (use WIDA stems)
   - Use SIOP or similar framework to ensure comprehensive language support
   - Plan scaffolding differentiated by proficiency level (Entering through Bridging)
   - Select high-interest, culturally relevant materials and texts
   - Incorporate students' home languages and cultures into lesson (translanguaging, bilingual resources)
   - Design visual supports, realia, and hands-on activities for comprehensible input
   - Plan for multiple modalities (listening, speaking, reading, writing practice)

4. Instructional Strategies & Scaffolding
   - Build background knowledge and activate prior knowledge (including cultural knowledge)
   - Pre-teach key vocabulary using visuals, gestures, cognates, and first language connections
   - Provide comprehensible input using clear, slow speech, visuals, gestures, demonstrations
   - Use sentence frames and paragraph frames to support academic language production
   - Implement cooperative learning structures that promote language interaction (think-pair-share, jigsaw)
   - Allow wait time and processing time appropriate for language learners (10+ seconds)
   - Use Total Physical Response (TPR) for beginning students
   - Provide graphic organizers and visual aids to support comprehension and organization
   - Facilitate language practice in low-stress, supportive environments (partner work, small groups)
   - Allow use of home language for clarification, peer support, and thinking (translanguaging)

5. Assessment & Language Growth Monitoring
   - Use formative assessments differentiated by language proficiency (observation, gestures, drawings, one-word responses, phrases, sentences, extended discourse)
   - Assess content knowledge separately from language proficiency when possible
   - Provide multiple ways to demonstrate understanding (performance tasks, portfolios, visuals)
   - Use rubrics aligned to WIDA proficiency levels for language-focused assessments
   - Monitor progress toward annual English proficiency growth targets
   - Document language use in all four domains for ACCESS preparation
   - Track language development over time using authentic work samples
   - Adjust instruction based on ongoing assessment data

4) Deliverables
Common work products for ESL instruction:
- SIOP-style lesson plans with both content and language objectives
- Scaffolded materials differentiated by WIDA proficiency levels (Entering through Bridging)
- Academic vocabulary lists with visual supports, gestures, and sentence frames
- Language assessment rubrics aligned to WIDA performance definitions
- Student language progress monitoring forms and growth documentation
- Culturally responsive classroom materials and displays (multilingual word walls, home language resources)
- Family communication letters translated into home languages
- Co-planning documents for collaboration with content teachers
- Newcomer orientation materials and supports
- Language proficiency level descriptions for IEP teams (for dually-identified students)
- Professional development resources for mainstream teachers on sheltering instruction
- Cultural asset inventory or funds of knowledge documentation

Minimum viable: Lesson plan with clear language objective, differentiated scaffolding by proficiency level, and formative assessment differentiated for language learners
Gold standard: Comprehensive SIOP-aligned unit plan with differentiated materials for all proficiency levels, visual supports, culturally responsive content, home language integration, family engagement component, and authentic performance assessments with WIDA-aligned rubrics

5) Quality checklist
Before delivering ESL instruction, verify:

Language Objectives & Standards:
- [ ] Language objective separate from content objective and explicitly taught
- [ ] Language objective aligned to WIDA ELD standards and appropriate for proficiency levels
- [ ] Language function clearly identified (describe, explain, argue, summarize, etc.)
- [ ] Academic language features identified (vocabulary, grammar, discourse features)
- [ ] Language objective measurable and achievable in one lesson/unit

Scaffolding & Differentiation:
- [ ] Differentiated supports provided for multiple WIDA proficiency levels (minimum 2-3 levels)
- [ ] Visuals, realia, and graphic organizers included to support comprehension
- [ ] Sentence frames and paragraph frames provided to support language production
- [ ] Cooperative learning structures promote language interaction
- [ ] Wait time and processing time built into lesson pacing
- [ ] Background knowledge activated before introducing new content
- [ ] Key vocabulary pre-taught using multiple strategies (visuals, gestures, cognates, L1)

Comprehensible Input:
- [ ] Teacher speech clear, slow, and accompanied by visuals/gestures
- [ ] Complex concepts explained using multiple modalities
- [ ] Demonstr ations and modeling provided for all tasks
- [ ] Text complexity appropriate or adapted for proficiency levels
- [ ] Audio/video resources include captions when available
- [ ] Instructions simplified and chunked into manageable steps

Language Production Opportunities:
- [ ] Multiple opportunities for students to practice speaking (structured talk, partners, small groups)
- [ ] Low-stress environments for language practice (no pressure for public speaking)
- [ ] Academic language practiced in authentic contexts
- [ ] Errors viewed as part of language development, not penalized excessively
- [ ] Variety of response modes available (oral, written, drawing, acting, technology)

Cultural Responsiveness:
- [ ] Content relevant to students' lives, cultures, and experiences
- [ ] Multilingual resources and home languages valued and incorporated
- [ ] Culturally responsive examples and contexts used
- [ ] Students' cultural identities and backgrounds honored and validated
- [ ] Materials represent diverse cultures and avoid stereotypes
- [ ] Classroom environment welcoming to multilingual, multicultural students

Assessment:
- [ ] Formative assessments differentiated by proficiency level
- [ ] Multiple ways to demonstrate content understanding (not just written tests)
- [ ] Content knowledge assessed separately from language proficiency when possible
- [ ] Assessment instructions clear and scaffolded
- [ ] Rubrics aligned to WIDA performance definitions for language-focused assessments
- [ ] Accommodations provided per ACCESS or state testing accommodations

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: SIOP Lesson Plan - Comprehensive (5th Grade Science)
```
SIOP LESSON PLAN - ESL/ESOL
Teacher: Maria Rodriguez
Grade Level: 5th Grade
ESL Proficiency Levels: Developing to Bridging (Mixed group)
Date: December 18, 2024
Unit: Ecosystems and Food Webs

STANDARDS:
Content Standard: NGSS 5-LS2-1 - Develop a model to describe the movement of matter among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment.
WIDA ELD Standard: Standard 4 (Language of Science), Grade 5, Speaking - Explain using academic language

OBJECTIVES:
Content Objective: Students will identify and explain the roles of producers, consumers, and decomposers in a food web by creating a labeled diagram.

Language Objective: Students will orally explain the relationship between organisms in a food web using the sentence frame: "The [organism] is a [producer/consumer/decomposer] because it [action/role]. It [eats/is eaten by/decomposes] ________."

VOCABULARY:
Key Academic Vocabulary:
- Producer: An organism (like plants) that makes its own food using sunlight (photosynthesis)
- Consumer: An organism that eats other organisms for energy
- Decomposer: An organism (like mushrooms and bacteria) that breaks down dead organisms
- Food web: A model showing how energy flows between different organisms in an ecosystem
- Energy flow: How energy moves from one organism to another through eating

Supplementary Vocabulary:
- Herbivore, Carnivore, Omnivore, Predator, Prey, Ecosystem

MATERIALS:
- Large poster with forest ecosystem visual (animals, plants, fungi visible)
- Organism picture cards (laminated, 24 cards total)
- Sentence frame anchor charts (3 sizes for different proficiency levels)
- Graphic organizer: Food Web Diagram (differentiated by level)
- Yarn or string for creating web connections
- Student whiteboards for quick checks
- Bilingual science glossary (Spanish, Arabic, Hmong)
- Ecosystem video with captions (3 minutes)

DIFFERENTIATION BY PROFICIENCY LEVEL:

Developing (Level 3):
- Use sentence frames with word bank
- Provide partially completed food web graphic organizer
- Allow phrases in oral explanations, model extending to full sentences
- Pair with Expanding student for peer support

Expanding (Level 4):
- Use sentence frames with transition words (In addition, Therefore)
- Provide blank food web graphic organizer with category labels
- Expect complete sentences with some academic language
- Extend to writing a short paragraph explanation

Bridging (Level 5):
- Use paragraph frames with multiple sentence starters
- Create food web from scratch, add decomposers and energy flow
- Explain using complex sentences and cause-effect language
- Extension: Predict impact of removing one organism from web

LESSON SEQUENCE:

1. Building Background & Vocabulary (12 minutes)

- ACTIVATE PRIOR KNOWLEDGE:
  * Display forest ecosystem poster
  * Think-Pair-Share: "What do you see? What do these organisms need to survive?"
  * Chart responses in L1 and English (welcome home languages)
  * Connect to students' experiences: "Have you seen these in nature? What did they eat?"

- PRE-TEACH VOCABULARY:
  * Introduce Producer with TPR:
    - Show plant photo, gesture photosynthesis (hands up to sun)
    - "Producer. Plant. Makes own food. From sun." [Point to sun]
    - Spanish cognate: "Productor - makes something!"
    - All students practice gesture and word

  * Introduce Consumer with acting:
    - Show rabbit eating plant, then fox eating rabbit
    - "Consumer. Eats. Gets energy from food."
    - Students act out eating (TPR)
    - "You are consumers! You eat breakfast, lunch, dinner!"

  * Introduce Decomposer with real mushrooms:
    - Show mushrooms and explain breaking down dead leaves
    - "Decomposer. Breaks down. Dead plants and animals."
    - "Nature's recyclers!" Gesture breaking apart

- CHECK UNDERSTANDING:
  * Show pictures, students identify on whiteboards: P, C, or D
  * Partner talk: "Tell your partner: A tree is a ____. A deer is a ____."

2. Comprehensible Input (18 minutes)

- ECOSYSTEM VIDEO (3 min) with captions:
  * Preview question: "What do organisms eat?"
  * Students watch video showing food relationships
  * Quick discussion: "What did you notice?"

- TEACHER MODELING - Food Web Creation:
  * Display large food web diagram (empty)
  * Think-aloud while adding organisms:
    "First, I need producers - plants. They make food from sun."
    "Next, consumers. Hmm, this rabbit eats plants. Arrow FROM grass TO rabbit."
    "This fox eats the rabbit. Arrow FROM rabbit TO fox."
    "When fox dies, decomposer breaks it down. Arrow TO mushroom."
  
  * Emphasize DIRECTION of arrows (energy flow):
    "Arrow shows energy moving. FROM food TO eater. Not backward!"

  * Point to connections: "This is a WEB, not a chain. Why?"
    [Take responses, validate, explain multiple connections]

- LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE MODELING:
  * Model target sentence frame with visuals:
    "The grass is a producer because it makes its own food using sunlight."
    "The rabbit is a consumer because it eats plants. It eats grass and clover."
    "The mushroom is a decomposer because it breaks down dead organisms."

  * Choral practice: All students repeat frames (3 times with different organisms)
  * Partner practice: A describes producer, B describes consumer (switch roles)

- CHECK FOR UNDERSTANDING:
  * Whiteboards: "Draw arrow showing energy from plant to deer" (quick check)
  * Cold-call using equity sticks: "Jose, is a snake a producer or consumer? How do you know?"

3. Guided Practice (20 minutes)

- SMALL GROUP FOOD WEB CREATION (groups of 4, mixed proficiency):
  * Each group receives:
    - Set of 12 organism picture cards
    - Large paper for creating web
    - Yarn for connecting organisms
    - Sentence frame reference sheet

  * Task: "Create a food web showing 10 organisms. Use yarn to show energy flow."
  * Roles assigned:
    - Organizer: Arranges pictures (Bridging student)
    - Artist: Tapes yarn showing connections (Developing student)
    - Explainer: Prepares to explain web to class (Expanding student)
    - Checker: Verifies arrows correct direction (Any level)

  * Teacher circulates:
    - Provide corrective feedback: "Where does energy come FROM?"
    - Prompt academic language: "Instead of 'eat', say 'consume' or 'organism feeds on'"
    - Support Developing students: "Use your sentence frame. The ___ is a ___."
    - Challenge Bridging students: "What happens if we remove the grass?"

- LANGUAGE PRACTICE - Structured Talk:
  * After 15 minutes, groups rotate to view other webs (gallery walk)
  * Students use sentence frames to explain their web to visiting group
  * Listeners ask questions using frame: "Why did you put ____ here?"

4. Independent Practice (15 minutes)

- DIFFERENTIATED FOOD WEB DIAGRAM:
  
  Developing:
  - Graphic organizer with categories (Producer, Consumer, Decomposer) pre-labeled
  - Word bank with 12 organisms
  - Task: Sort organisms into correct category, draw 5 arrows showing energy flow
  - Sentence frames with fill-in-blanks

  Expanding:
  - Blank food web template with space for 8 organisms
  - Task: Draw and label organisms, show energy flow with arrows
  - Write 3 sentences explaining roles using sentence frames

  Bridging:
  - Blank page (create web from scratch)
  - Task: Create food web with at least 10 organisms, label all, show energy flow
  - Write paragraph explaining ecosystem using transition words
  - Extension: "What would happen if decomposers disappeared?"

- TEACHER SUPPORT:
  * Conferences with Developing students individually
  * Spot-check Expanding students' sentences for accuracy
  * Provide extension challenge to early finishers

5. Review & Assessment (10 minutes)

- LANGUAGE OBJECTIVE REVIEW:
  * Partners: A picks organism card, B explains its role using complete sentence frame
  * Whole class: 3 volunteers share using academic language

- EXIT TICKET (differentiated by level):
  
  Developing:
  - Draw and label one producer, one consumer, one decomposer
  - Write one sentence using frame: "A ___ is a ___ because ___."

  Expanding:
  - Draw a simple food chain (4 organisms) with arrows
  - Write 2 sentences explaining energy flow

  Bridging:
  - Explain in writing: "Why are decomposers important in an ecosystem?"
  - Use academic language and at least 3 sentences
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ESL/ESOL/ELL**: English as a Second Language / English for Speakers of Other Languages / English Language Learner - terms for students learning English as an additional language
- **WIDA**: World-Class Instructional Design and Assessment - consortium that develops English Language Development standards and the ACCESS assessment
- **ACCESS for ELLs**: Annual English proficiency assessment measuring English learners' progress in listening, speaking, reading, and writing
- **WIDA English Language Development Standards**: Framework defining language expectations for English learners across content areas (Language of Language Arts, Math, Science, Social Studies, Social & Instructional Language)
- **WIDA Proficiency Levels**: 1-Entering, 2-Emerging, 3-Developing, 4-Expanding, 5-Bridging, 6-Reaching - describes stages of English language acquisition
- **WIDA Can Do Descriptors**: Performance indicators describing what English learners can do at each proficiency level in each language domain
- **SIOP Model**: Sheltered Instruction Observation Protocol - research-based framework for making content comprehensible to English learners through 8 components (lesson preparation, building background, comprehensible input, strategies, interaction, practice/application, lesson delivery, review/assessment)
- **Language Objective**: Explicitly stated goal for language development in a lesson (separate from content objective) - identifies language function, form, and use students will demonstrate
- **Content Objective**: Academic learning goal focused on content standards (what students will learn in math, science, etc.)
- **Scaffolding**: Temporary supports provided to help English learners access grade-level content - includes visuals, graphic organizers, sentence frames, vocabulary support
- **Comprehensible Input**: Language and instruction made understandable to English learners through clear speech, visuals, gestures, demonstrations, context - based on Krashen's theory (i+1)
- **Translanguaging**: Pedagogical practice allowing English learners to draw on all their linguistic resources (home language + English) fluidly for learning - supports meaning-making and identity
- **Sentence Frames/Starters**: Partially completed sentences providing structure for language production - help students use academic language appropriately (e.g., "I agree with ___ because ___.")
- **Academic Language**: Language used in school for learning content - includes vocabulary, grammar structures, and discourse features specific to academic contexts (vs. social language)
- **BICS vs CALP**: Basic Interpersonal Communication Skills (social language) vs Cognitive Academic Language Proficiency (academic language) - Cummins's distinction (social language develops in 6 months-2 years; academic language takes 5-7 years)
- **L1 and L2**: First language (home language) and second language (English for ELLs in U.S. schools)
- **Newcomer**: English learner who recently arrived in the U.S. (typically within past 12 months) with limited or no English proficiency
- **SIFE/SLIFE**: Students with Interrupted Formal Education / Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education - English learners with gaps in schooling requiring specialized support
- **Dual Language/Bilingual Program**: Educational program teaching content in two languages with goal of bilingualism and biliteracy

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **WIDA English Language Development Standards Framework (2020 Edition)**: Current framework organizing language development around Key Language Uses (Narrate, Inform, Explain, Argue) across four language domains
- **TESOL Standards**: Teachers of English to Speakers of Other Languages - professional standards for ESL instruction (6 domains: Language, Culture, Planning/Instruction, Assessment, Professionalism, Learning Environment)
- **Common Core State Standards**: Grade-level content standards in ELA and Math that English learners are expected to meet with appropriate support
- **NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards)**: Science standards emphasizing language-rich practices (arguing from evidence, constructing explanations) - require explicit language support for ELLs
- **Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA)**: Federal law requiring states to assess English proficiency annually, set progress targets, and support English learners' language development and academic achievement
- **Title III**: Federal program funding services for English learners and immigrant students - requires demonstrated progress toward English proficiency
- **SIOP Protocol (2017 Edition)**: Observation tool and professional development resource for sheltered instruction - includes 30 features across 8 components
- **GLAD (Guided Language Acquisition Design)**: Model for making content comprehensible using exploration, inquiry, and metacognitive strategies
- **Danielson Framework Domain 1 (Planning) and Domain 3 (Instruction)**: Teacher evaluation framework elements especially relevant for ESL planning and differentiation

Common Instructional Models:
- **Push-In ESL**: ESL teacher works with English learners inside the general education classroom (co-teaching model)
- **Pull-Out ESL**: English learners leave general education classroom for separate ESL instruction focused on language development
- **Sheltered Instruction/SDAIE**: Content taught in English using strategies to make it comprehensible for English learners
- **Newcomer Programs**: Separate classes or schools for recent arrivals providing intensive English and orientation to U.S. schools
- **Dual Language/Two-Way Immersion**: Program model serving both English learners and native English speakers; instruction in both languages
- **Transitional Bilingual**: Program using students' home language initially, transitioning to all English over time (typically 2-3 years)

Key Assessment Tools:
- **ACCESS for ELLs 2.0**: Annual English proficiency assessment (Kindergarten ACCESS or grades 1-12) - measures Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing; results guide ESL services
- **WIDA Screener**: Placement test to determine initial English proficiency level for newly enrolled English learners
- **WIDA MODEL**: Interim assessment for monitoring English learners' progress during the school year
- **Running Records**: Informal reading assessment documenting reading level, accuracy, fluency, and comprehension - useful for tracking literacy development
- **Language Samples**: Collections of student oral or written language demonstrating proficiency at a point in time - analyzed for grammatical accuracy, vocabulary, complexity

Accommodations for English Learners:
- **Extended Time**: Extra time on assessments to allow for processing in second language
- **Bilingual Dictionary/Glossary**: Access to translation tools during instruction and assessment (excluding ELA reading tests typically)
- **Simplified Language**: Rewording test questions or instructions to reduce linguistic complexity while maintaining content rigor
- **Visual Supports**: Pictures, diagrams, graphic organizers supporting comprehension
- **Read-Aloud**: Test directions or questions read aloud (accommodations vary by state for different test types)
- **Small Group Testing**: Reduced distractions, opportunity for clarification of directions in small group setting
- **Native Language Support**: Allowing use of native language for clarification, note-taking, or assessment in specific contexts (policies vary by state)
