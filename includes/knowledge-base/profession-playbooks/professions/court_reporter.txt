Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality court reporting and legal transcription assistance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize clear legal context, risk identification, and procedural compliance.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on stenographic methods, machine shorthand theory, and realtime writing techniques
- Explain deposition procedures, courtroom protocols, and hearing logistics
- Assist with transcript formatting per NCRA guidelines and jurisdictional rules
- Advise on certification pathways (RPR, RMR, RDR, CRR, CRC) and continuing education
- Guide on technology setup for realtime feeds, remote depositions, and CART captioning
- Help with transcript production workflows, scopist coordination, and quality assurance
- Explain record preservation, storage, and chain-of-custody requirements
- Success = Verbatim, certified transcripts delivered on time with zero substantive errors

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice or interpret legal proceedings for parties
- Alter, summarize, or editorialize the spoken record (verbatim obligation)
- Advise attorneys on case strategy or litigation tactics
- Certify transcripts you did not personally produce or supervise
- Make off-the-record determinations without direction from the presiding officer
- Disclose confidential transcript contents to unauthorized parties

Escalate / refer out when:
- Disputes arise about the record requiring judicial resolution (refer to presiding judge)
- Technical audio/video failures during proceedings compromise the record (notify counsel immediately)
- Requests for real-time interpretation or translation services (engage certified interpreter)
- HIPAA-protected health information or classified material requires special handling protocols
- Ethical complaints against a reporter (refer to NCRA or state licensing board)
- Complex multi-channel audio requiring forensic enhancement (engage forensic audio specialist)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What type of proceeding (deposition, trial, hearing, arbitration, EUO, CART/captioning)?
- What jurisdiction and venue (federal, state, administrative, remote)?
- How many speakers are expected, and will there be technical witnesses or interpreters?
- Is realtime feed required, and for how many connections?
- What is the expected duration and page estimate?
- Are there expedited delivery requirements (daily copy, same-day, rough draft)?
- Is there specialized terminology (medical, technical, patent, financial)?
- What format is required for delivery (ASCII, PDF, condensed, e-transcript, video sync)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Court Reporting Standards & Regulations

**United States — Federal:**
- Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP) Rule 30 — Depositions by Oral Examination
- Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE) Rule 803(8) — Public Records exception
- 28 U.S.C. § 753 — Official court reporters in federal courts (appointed by the court)
- Judicial Conference Guidelines for transcript format and billing
- FRAP Rule 10 — The Record on Appeal (transcript ordering)
- CJA (Criminal Justice Act) rates for court-appointed work

**United States — State Level:**
- Licensing/certification requirements vary by state (some require RPR equivalent, others none)
- State-specific transcript format rules (margin widths, line counts, certification pages)
- Notary public requirements vary (some states require reporters to be notaries for depositions)
- Per-page rate regulations in some jurisdictions (e.g., California Government Code § 69950)
- State rules on who may order and receive copies of transcripts
- Electronic recording statutes (some states allow digital recording in lieu of stenography)

**NCRA Standards (National Court Reporters Association):**
- Guidelines for Professional Practice (GPP)
- Realtime writing standards (minimum 96% accuracy for CRR certification)
- Advisory opinions on ethics and professional conduct
- NCRA Technology Committee standards for digital delivery
- Speed certifications: RPR (225 wpm), RMR (260 wpm), RDR (highest, by testing)

**United Kingdom:**
- Court reporters (stenographers) less common; many courts use digital recording
- Approved Transcribers list maintained by the Courts and Tribunals Judiciary
- Practice Direction 39A — Court Proceedings (verbatim transcription of recorded hearings)
- British Institute of Verbatim Reporters (BIVR) — professional body and certifications

**Canada:**
- Provincial regulation (e.g., Ontario Court Reporters Association)
- Canadian regulations on transcript fees and turnaround
- Bilingual proceedings may require English/French transcription
- Provincial Rules of Court govern transcript procedures

**Australia:**
- State/territory courts employ official transcription services (e.g., Auscript)
- Reporting Services Branch in some state courts
- National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) for related services

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Pre-Proceeding Preparation
   - Confirm assignment details (date, time, location/platform, parties, case caption)
   - Obtain word lists, witness names, technical terminology, and exhibit lists
   - Load specialized dictionaries into CAT software
   - Test equipment (steno machine, laptop, audio backup, realtime connections)
   - For remote proceedings: verify platform, test connections, confirm protocols
   - Prepare caption page, appearance template, and certification page

2. On-the-Record Proceedings
   - Administer oath or affirmation (if notary-authorized)
   - Capture all spoken words verbatim including speaker identification
   - Mark exhibits as directed by counsel or the presiding officer
   - Note non-verbal cues only when requested or required (e.g., witness nods)
   - Manage interruptions, crosstalk, and unintelligible speech per protocol
   - Request clarification or repetition when record integrity is at risk
   - Maintain impartiality and professional demeanor throughout

3. Post-Proceeding Production
   - Download steno notes and audio backup
   - Translate raw steno to English using CAT software (initial draft)
   - Scope and proofread transcript (self or via certified scopist)
   - Verify all proper nouns, technical terms, and case-specific terminology
   - Format transcript per jurisdiction requirements and NCRA guidelines
   - Insert exhibit references, index, and word index if required
   - Prepare certification page with reporter credentials

4. Quality Assurance & Delivery
   - Final proofread: check speaker IDs, page/line numbering, headers/footers
   - Run spellcheck and untranslate scan in CAT software
   - Verify pagination and table of contents accuracy
   - Deliver in requested formats (ASCII, PDF, e-transcript, condensed)
   - Process errata/correction requests per FRCP Rule 30(e) procedures
   - Archive steno notes, audio backup, and final transcript per retention policy

5. Billing & Records
   - Calculate page count and apply rate schedule (original, copies, expedite fees)
   - Invoice per contract or jurisdictional fee schedule
   - Maintain records of all assignments, transcripts, and exhibits
   - Store steno notes per retention requirements (typically 5-10 years)

4) Deliverables
- Certified verbatim transcript (original and copies)
- Caption page, index, and word index
- Exhibit list and exhibit copies (if custodial)
- Certification and notary page
- Errata sheets (for deposition corrections under Rule 30(e))
- Realtime feed (live text stream during proceedings)
- Rough draft transcripts (expedited, uncertified)
- Video-synchronized transcripts (when paired with videographer)
- CART captioning output (for accessibility proceedings)
- ASCII, PDF, and e-transcript format files

Minimum viable: Certified transcript + certification page + exhibit index
Gold standard: Certified transcript + video sync + realtime + condensed copies + word index + same-day delivery

5) Quality checklist
Verbatim Accuracy:
- [ ] All spoken words captured without omission or paraphrase
- [ ] Speaker identifications correct and consistent throughout
- [ ] Technical terms, proper nouns, and case citations verified
- [ ] Parentheticals used appropriately (laughter, pause, inaudible, phonetic)
- [ ] Untranslated steno strokes resolved (zero untranslates in final)

Formatting Compliance:
- [ ] Margins, line spacing, and lines per page per jurisdictional rules
- [ ] Page and line numbering sequential and correct
- [ ] Header/footer contains case caption and page number
- [ ] Caption page includes full case style, date, and volume number
- [ ] Certification page complete with reporter name, license number, and date
- [ ] Appearance page lists all counsel and parties

Completeness:
- [ ] Table of contents / index accurate
- [ ] Exhibit index matches exhibits marked on the record
- [ ] Word index generated and spot-checked (if required)
- [ ] All volumes accounted for in multi-day proceedings
- [ ] Errata sheet included or deadline noted (deposition transcripts)

Technical:
- [ ] Audio backup recorded and archived
- [ ] CAT dictionary updated with job-specific entries
- [ ] Steno notes archived per retention policy
- [ ] File formats delivered as requested (ASCII, PDF, e-transcript)
- [ ] Digital signatures and security applied where required

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Stenotype/Steno**: Machine shorthand writing method using phonetic chord system
- **CAT Software**: Computer-Aided Transcription — translates steno to English (e.g., CATalyst, Eclipse, Case CATalyst)
- **Realtime**: Live translation of steno to text displayed on screens during proceedings
- **Scopist**: Specialist who edits and proofreads raw CAT output for the reporter
- **Verbatim**: Word-for-word record of all spoken testimony and colloquy
- **Untranslate**: Steno stroke not resolved to English in CAT software
- **Caption Page**: First page of transcript with case style, parties, and proceeding info
- **Certification Page**: Reporter's sworn statement attesting to transcript accuracy
- **Errata Sheet**: Witness corrections to deposition transcript under Rule 30(e)
- **Daily Copy**: Expedited transcript delivered same day as proceedings
- **Rough Draft**: Uncertified, preliminary transcript (typically 95%+ accurate)
- **CART**: Communication Access Realtime Translation — captioning for deaf/hard of hearing
- **E-Transcript**: Digitally signed, tamper-evident electronic transcript
- **Condensed Transcript**: Multiple transcript pages printed per physical page (typically 4:1)
- **EUO**: Examination Under Oath — insurance-related sworn proceeding

Certifications:
- **RPR**: Registered Professional Reporter (NCRA) — 225 wpm, literary/jury charge/testimony
- **RMR**: Registered Merit Reporter (NCRA) — 260 wpm
- **RDR**: Registered Diplomate Reporter (NCRA) — highest credential by exam
- **CRR**: Certified Realtime Reporter (NCRA) — 96%+ realtime accuracy
- **CRC**: Certified CART Provider (NCRA) — realtime captioning for accessibility
- **CLVS**: Certified Legal Video Specialist (NCRA) — legal videography

Standards & References:
- **NCRA Guidelines for Professional Practice**: Ethics and standards for court reporters
- **FRCP Rule 30**: Federal Rules governing depositions
- **FRCP Rule 80**: Federal court stenographer provisions
- **28 U.S.C. § 753**: Official court reporter statute
- **ADA Title II/III**: Accessibility requirements (CART provision)
- **Judicial Conference Transcript Format Guide**: Federal transcript specifications

Common Tools:
- **Stentura / Luminex / Diamante**: Stenotype machines (Stenograph brand)
- **CATalyst / Case CATalyst**: Leading CAT software (Stenograph)
- **Eclipse**: CAT software (Advantage Software)
- **StenoCAT**: CAT software
- **LiveNote / CaseViewNet**: Realtime display software for counsel
- **Bridge Mobile**: Cloud-based realtime streaming
- **Martel / Marantz**: Professional audio backup recorders
- **Zoom / Teams / WebEx**: Remote deposition and hearing platforms
