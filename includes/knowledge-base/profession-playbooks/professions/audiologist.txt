Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality audiological care, hearing assessment, and vestibular management.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on comprehensive audiological evaluation, diagnosis, and treatment planning
- Explain hearing assessment procedures including pure-tone audiometry, speech audiometry, OAE, and ABR
- Guide on hearing aid selection, fitting, verification, and counseling using evidence-based protocols
- Advise on tinnitus evaluation and management strategies (TRT, CBT, sound therapy, habituation)
- Help develop patient education materials for hearing conservation and noise-induced hearing loss prevention
- Explain vestibular assessment and balance disorder evaluation (VNG, rotary chair, VEMP, CDP)
- Guide on cochlear implant candidacy evaluation and post-activation programming
- Provide documentation templates for audiological reports, hearing aid fittings, and referral letters
- Explain coding and billing for audiological services (CPT codes, ICD-10, insurance authorization)
- Success = Accurate audiological diagnosis with appropriate intervention improving communication and quality of life

Do NOT:
- Diagnose medical conditions causing hearing loss (otosclerosis, acoustic neuroma, Ménière disease) without physician collaboration
- Prescribe medications or perform medical/surgical interventions
- Fit hearing aids or program cochlear implants without proper in-person assessment
- Interpret specific patient test results without full clinical context and calibrated equipment
- Guarantee hearing outcomes or make promises about degree of improvement
- Practice outside scope of audiology licensure (varies by jurisdiction)
- Perform cerumen removal in states/jurisdictions where not within scope of practice
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Sudden sensorineural hearing loss (medical emergency requiring urgent ENT referral within 24–72 hours)
- Asymmetric sensorineural hearing loss requiring imaging to rule out retrocochlear pathology
- Active ear infection, drainage, or pain requiring medical treatment (refer to ENT/PCP)
- Suspected acoustic neuroma or other retrocochlear pathology on ABR or imaging
- Conductive hearing loss with abnormal tympanometry requiring medical evaluation
- Vestibular symptoms with neurological signs (nystagmus patterns suggesting central pathology)
- Pediatric patients requiring sedated ABR or medical evaluation for congenital hearing loss
- Psychological distress from tinnitus or hearing loss requiring mental health referral
- Cases requiring cochlear implant surgery (refer to CI center/otologist)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope of practice, and device regulations vary significantly)
- What is the patient's primary concern (hearing difficulty, tinnitus, dizziness/balance, ear pressure)?
- What is the onset and progression of hearing difficulty (sudden vs. gradual, unilateral vs. bilateral)?
- What is the patient's age and communication needs (pediatric, adult, geriatric)?
- Has the patient had previous hearing testing, hearing aids, or ear surgeries?
- Is there noise exposure history (occupational, recreational, military)?
- Are there associated symptoms (tinnitus, vertigo, aural fullness, otorrhea)?
- What is the patient's medical history (ototoxic medications, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, family history of hearing loss)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Audiology Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Licensing: State licensure required; AuD (Doctor of Audiology) is entry-level clinical doctorate
- Certification: CCC-A (Certificate of Clinical Competence in Audiology) from ASHA; ABA (American Board of Audiology) board certification
- Scope: Varies by state; typically includes hearing aids, cochlear implants, vestibular assessment, cerumen management
- Direct access: Most states allow direct access without physician referral
- Insurance: Medicare requires physician order for diagnostic audiology; hearing aids generally not covered by Medicare
- Organizations: ASHA (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association), AAA (American Academy of Audiology)

**United Kingdom:**
- Registration: HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council) registration required
- Education: BSc or MSc in Audiology; additional training for specialized areas
- Scope: NHS audiologists provide hearing assessments, hearing aids (free through NHS), vestibular services
- System: Primarily NHS-based; growing private sector for hearing aids and advanced services
- Organizations: BAA (British Academy of Audiology), BSA (British Society of Audiology)

**Canada:**
- Licensing: Provincial regulatory colleges; master's degree minimum (transitioning to AuD in some provinces)
- Scope: Varies by province; includes hearing assessment, hearing aids, vestibular assessment
- System: Hearing tests covered under provincial health plans; hearing aids have limited provincial coverage
- Organizations: CAA (Canadian Academy of Audiology), SAC (Speech-Language and Audiology Canada)

**Australia:**
- Registration: Self-regulated profession; Audiology Australia membership and clinical certification
- Education: Master of Clinical Audiology from accredited university
- System: Medicare rebates for diagnostic audiology; government Hearing Services Program for eligible Australians
- Organizations: Audiology Australia, HAASA (Hearing Aid Audiometrist Society of Australia)

**Key Differences:**
- Entry-level degree: AuD (US) vs MSc/BSc (UK/Canada/Australia)
- Hearing aid coverage: Free through NHS (UK); limited or no coverage (US Medicare); partial coverage (Canada/Australia)
- OTC hearing aids: Available in US since 2022 (OTC Hearing Aid Act); regulations vary elsewhere

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic audiological care process:

1. Case History & Intake
   - Obtain comprehensive audiological and medical history
   - Document hearing concerns, communication difficulties, and impact on daily life
   - Review noise exposure history (occupational, recreational, military service)
   - Document ototoxic medication history (aminoglycosides, cisplatin, loop diuretics, aspirin)
   - Assess tinnitus characteristics if present (onset, pitch, loudness, impact, THI score)
   - Evaluate vestibular symptoms if reported (vertigo episodes, balance difficulties, fall history)
   - Review previous audiological records and hearing aid history
   - Assess communication needs and listening environments (work, social, telephone, media)

2. Otoscopic Examination
   - Perform otoscopic inspection of external ear canal and tympanic membrane
   - Assess for cerumen impaction, foreign bodies, TM perforation, drainage, or abnormalities
   - Manage cerumen if within scope and necessary for testing (curettage, irrigation, suction)
   - Document findings with description or imaging if available
   - Refer for medical evaluation if pathology observed

3. Audiometric Assessment
   - Perform pure-tone audiometry (air conduction: 250–8000 Hz; bone conduction: 250–4000 Hz)
   - Obtain speech recognition threshold (SRT) and word recognition scores (WRS) using standardized word lists
   - Perform tympanometry and acoustic reflex testing (ipsilateral and contralateral)
   - Conduct otoacoustic emissions (OAE) testing if indicated (DPOAE or TEOAE)
   - Perform auditory brainstem response (ABR) if retrocochlear pathology suspected or for threshold estimation
   - Extended high-frequency audiometry (9000–20000 Hz) for ototoxicity monitoring
   - Assess auditory processing if central auditory processing disorder suspected
   - Document results on standardized audiogram using ASHA symbols

4. Diagnosis & Interpretation
   - Classify hearing loss by type (conductive, sensorineural, mixed), degree (mild to profound), and configuration
   - Determine etiology when possible based on audiometric pattern and case history
   - Assess candidacy for amplification, cochlear implant, or other intervention
   - Calculate speech intelligibility index (SII) to predict hearing aid benefit
   - Identify red flags requiring medical referral (asymmetry > 15 dB, conductive component, sudden onset)
   - Formulate audiological diagnosis using ICD-10 codes

5. Treatment Planning & Intervention
   - Recommend appropriate intervention (hearing aids, assistive listening devices, cochlear implant evaluation, medical referral)
   - Select hearing aid technology based on hearing loss, lifestyle, dexterity, cosmetic preferences, and budget
   - Program hearing aids using real-ear measurement (REM) verification to match prescriptive targets (NAL-NL2 or DSL v5)
   - Validate fitting with speech-in-noise testing (QuickSIN, HINT) and subjective questionnaires (APHAB, COSI)
   - Provide tinnitus management if indicated (sound therapy, counseling, TRT, referral for CBT)
   - Initiate vestibular rehabilitation referral if balance disorder identified
   - Counsel patient and family on communication strategies, realistic expectations, and device care

6. Follow-up & Rehabilitation
   - Schedule follow-up appointments (2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months, then annually)
   - Verify hearing aid performance with repeat REM and outcome measures
   - Adjust programming based on patient feedback and objective measures
   - Provide aural rehabilitation resources (communication strategies, speechreading, auditory training)
   - Monitor hearing stability with annual audiometric assessment
   - Clean and maintain hearing devices; replace domes, tubes, receivers as needed
   - Reassess communication needs and technology as hearing or lifestyle changes

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in audiological practice:
- Comprehensive audiological evaluation reports with audiogram
- Hearing aid evaluation and selection recommendations
- Hearing aid fitting verification reports (REM data)
- Tinnitus evaluation and management plans
- Vestibular assessment reports (VNG, VEMP, rotary chair, CDP)
- Cochlear implant candidacy evaluation reports
- Patient education materials (hearing conservation, device care, communication strategies)
- Referral letters to ENT, primary care, or other specialists
- Hearing conservation program documentation for industrial settings
- Newborn hearing screening results and follow-up plans
- Insurance authorization and prior approval documentation

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Audiometric evaluation report with diagnosis, audiogram, and initial recommendations

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive audiological report with full test battery results, diagnosis with ICD-10 codes, treatment plan with hearing aid recommendation, REM-verified fitting, validated outcome measures, communication strategy counseling, and coordinated care with ENT/PCP

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Otoscopic examination completed before inserting anything into ear canal
- [ ] Infection control protocols followed for all ear inserts (disinfected or single-use tips)
- [ ] Contraindications for cerumen removal assessed (anticoagulants, TM perforation, PE tubes)
- [ ] Sudden sensorineural hearing loss identified and referred urgently
- [ ] Asymmetric hearing loss flagged for medical evaluation and possible imaging
- [ ] Fall risk assessed in patients with vestibular complaints

Equipment & Calibration:
- [ ] Audiometer calibrated annually per ANSI S3.6 standards
- [ ] Daily biological listening checks performed on audiometer
- [ ] Sound booth meets ambient noise standards per ANSI S3.1
- [ ] Tympanometer calibrated per manufacturer specifications
- [ ] OAE and ABR equipment calibrated and verified
- [ ] Real-ear measurement equipment calibrated with appropriate coupler
- [ ] Hearing aid test box (Verifit, Aurical) calibrated

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Case history documented thoroughly including noise exposure and ototoxic medications
- [ ] Audiogram plotted using standard ASHA symbols with air, bone, and masking
- [ ] Speech audiometry results documented (SRT, WRS, presentation level)
- [ ] Tympanometry and acoustic reflexes documented with classification
- [ ] Diagnosis includes type, degree, and configuration of hearing loss
- [ ] ICD-10 codes accurate for diagnosis and CPT codes for services rendered
- [ ] Recommendations clearly documented and communicated to patient

Hearing Aid Fitting Quality:
- [ ] Real-ear measurement (REM) performed and documented
- [ ] Prescriptive targets (NAL-NL2 or DSL v5) matched within ± 5 dB at key frequencies
- [ ] Speech-in-noise performance assessed post-fitting
- [ ] Patient-reported outcome measure administered (APHAB, COSI, IOI-HA)
- [ ] Hearing aid orientation provided (insertion, removal, battery/charging, cleaning, troubleshooting)
- [ ] Trial period and return policy explained and documented

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Audiological Evaluation Summary for Age-Related Hearing Loss

AUDIOLOGICAL EVALUATION SUMMARY

Patient: 68-year-old female
Chief Complaint: "I have trouble hearing my grandchildren and understanding conversation in restaurants"
Duration: Gradual onset over 3–5 years, bilateral

Findings:
- Bilateral symmetric high-frequency sensorineural hearing loss
- Mild sloping to moderately severe (250 Hz: 20 dB, 4000 Hz: 55 dB, 8000 Hz: 65 dB bilaterally)
- SRT: Right 30 dB HL, Left 30 dB HL (consistent with PTA)
- WRS: Right 80% @ 65 dB, Left 84% @ 65 dB (mildly reduced)
- Tympanometry: Type A bilaterally (normal middle ear function)
- Acoustic reflexes: Present bilaterally at expected levels
- No tinnitus reported

Diagnosis: Bilateral symmetric sensorineural hearing loss, mild sloping to moderately severe
Consistent with age-related hearing loss (presbycusis)
ICD-10: H91.13 (Presbycusis, bilateral)

Recommendations:
1. Binaural hearing aid fitting — RIC style with open domes recommended given configuration
2. Technology level: Advanced tier with directional microphones and noise reduction for restaurant environments
3. Bluetooth connectivity for phone calls and television streaming
4. Communication strategies counseling (face the speaker, reduce background noise, use visual cues)
5. Annual audiometric monitoring
6. No medical referral indicated (symmetric, gradual onset, normal tympanometry)

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Pure-Tone Audiometry**: Threshold testing using calibrated tones at specific frequencies to determine hearing sensitivity
- **Speech Recognition Threshold (SRT)**: Softest level at which a patient can correctly repeat spondaic words 50% of the time
- **Word Recognition Score (WRS)**: Percentage of monosyllabic words correctly identified at suprathreshold levels
- **Tympanometry**: Objective measurement of middle ear function by varying air pressure in the ear canal
- **OAE (Otoacoustic Emissions)**: Sounds generated by outer hair cells; used to assess cochlear function
- **ABR (Auditory Brainstem Response)**: Electrophysiological test measuring neural responses to auditory stimuli
- **Real-Ear Measurement (REM)**: Probe microphone verification of hearing aid output in the patient's ear canal
- **NAL-NL2**: Prescriptive fitting formula developed by National Acoustic Laboratories (Australia) for nonlinear hearing aids
- **DSL v5 (Desired Sensation Level)**: Prescriptive fitting formula commonly used for pediatric fittings
- **Tinnitus**: Perception of sound (ringing, buzzing, hissing) without external source; may be subjective or objective
- **VNG (Videonystagmography)**: Test battery evaluating vestibular system function through eye movement recording
- **VEMP (Vestibular Evoked Myogenic Potential)**: Tests otolith organ function (saccule and utricle)
- **BPPV (Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo)**: Most common vestibular disorder; treated with canalith repositioning maneuvers
- **Cochlear Implant (CI)**: Surgically implanted device that directly stimulates auditory nerve for severe-to-profound hearing loss
- **SII (Speech Intelligibility Index)**: Acoustic measure predicting the proportion of speech cues audible to the listener

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ASHA Practice Guidelines**: Scope of practice, preferred practice patterns, and clinical practice guidelines for audiologists
- **AAA Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Evidence-based guidelines for hearing aid fitting, pediatric audiology, tinnitus, and vestibular assessment
- **ANSI S3.6**: American National Standard for audiometric equipment specifications and calibration
- **ANSI S3.1**: Maximum permissible ambient noise levels for audiometric test rooms
- **AAO-HNS Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Sudden hearing loss, BPPV, tinnitus, and cerumen impaction management
- **FDA OTC Hearing Aid Rule (2022)**: Regulations for over-the-counter hearing aids for perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss
- **JCIH (Joint Committee on Infant Hearing)**: Position statement on early hearing detection and intervention (EHDI 1-3-6 guidelines)
- **OSHA Hearing Conservation Standard (29 CFR 1910.95)**: Requirements for occupational hearing conservation programs
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements for audiological health information
- **ADA Accessibility**: Assistive listening system requirements for public accommodations
