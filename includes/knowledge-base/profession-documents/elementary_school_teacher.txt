Knowledge Base Notes
-------------------
### Elementary Education Fundamentals
- Teach children in grades K-5 (typically ages 5-11)
- Focus on foundational skills: reading, writing, mathematics, science, social studies
- Adapt instruction to developmental stages and learning needs
- Create safe, inclusive, and nurturing classroom environment
- Foster social-emotional development alongside academic growth
- Integrate technology appropriately for age group
- Follow state and national curriculum standards (Common Core, Next Generation Science Standards)

### Child Development (Ages 5-11)
- **K-1 (Ages 5-7)**: Concrete thinking, short attention spans (10-15 min), learning through play, developing fine motor skills, beginning to read and write
- **Grades 2-3 (Ages 7-9)**: Building reading fluency, improving attention span (15-25 min), developing logical thinking, peer relationships becoming important
- **Grades 4-5 (Ages 9-11)**: Abstract thinking emerges, increased independence, longer attention span (25-35 min), self-awareness developing, complex social dynamics

### Literacy and Reading Instruction
- **Phonemic Awareness**: Sound recognition and manipulation (phonics)
- **Phonics**: Letter-sound relationships, decoding strategies
- **Fluency**: Reading accuracy, rate, and expression
- **Vocabulary**: Word knowledge and academic language development
- **Comprehension**: Understanding and analyzing text
- **Writing Development**: From letter formation to paragraph structure
- **Guided Reading**: Small group instruction at instructional level
- **Independent Reading**: Building stamina and choice
- **Balanced Literacy**: Read aloud, shared reading, guided reading, independent reading

### Mathematics Instruction
- **Number Sense**: Counting, place value, number operations
- **Operations**: Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division (fact fluency and conceptual understanding)
- **Fractions and Decimals**: Part-whole relationships, equivalence
- **Geometry**: Shapes, spatial reasoning, measurement
- **Problem-Solving**: Multiple strategies, word problems, real-world applications
- **Math Manipulatives**: Concrete tools for abstract concepts (base-10 blocks, fraction bars, counters)
- **Math Talk**: Discourse and explaining mathematical thinking

### Classroom Management Strategies
- **Positive Behavior Support**: Clear expectations, consistent routines, positive reinforcement
- **Classroom Rules**: Simple, positively stated, co-created with students (3-5 rules)
- **Transitions**: Smooth movement between activities (timers, signals, routines)
- **Attention Strategies**: Call-and-response, hand signals, countdown, "freeze"
- **Seating Arrangements**: Flexible seating, strategic grouping for learning and behavior
- **Time Management**: Daily schedule, visual timers, chunking activities
- **Reward Systems**: Individual and class-wide incentives (when developmentally appropriate)

### Differentiated Instruction
- **Readiness-Based**: Adjust complexity, support, and challenge level
- **Interest-Based**: Offer choices that connect to student interests
- **Learning Profile**: Address learning styles, strengths, and preferences
- **Flexible Grouping**: Whole class, small groups, partners, independent work
- **Tiered Assignments**: Same concept, different levels of complexity
- **Learning Centers/Stations**: Multiple activities addressing different needs
- **Accommodations**: Extra time, modified assignments, assistive technology
- **Interventions**: Targeted support for struggling learners (RTI model)

### Social-Emotional Learning (SEL)
- **Self-Awareness**: Identifying emotions, self-confidence, self-efficacy
- **Self-Management**: Impulse control, stress management, goal setting
- **Social Awareness**: Empathy, perspective-taking, appreciating diversity
- **Relationship Skills**: Communication, cooperation, conflict resolution
- **Responsible Decision-Making**: Problem-solving, ethical choices, consequences
- **SEL Programs**: Second Step, PATHS, Zones of Regulation, PBIS

### Assessment and Progress Monitoring
- **Formative Assessment**: Exit tickets, observations, questioning, quick checks
- **Summative Assessment**: Unit tests, projects, performance tasks
- **Diagnostic Assessment**: Identifying strengths and needs (beginning of year)
- **Running Records**: Reading assessment (accuracy, fluency, comprehension)
- **Math Fact Fluency Checks**: Timed assessments, fact practice
- **Progress Monitoring**: Regular data collection to track growth
- **Report Cards and Conferences**: Communicating student progress to families

### Parent and Family Communication
- **Regular Communication**: Weekly newsletters, class websites, communication apps
- **Parent-Teacher Conferences**: Scheduled check-ins, progress reports
- **Positive Phone Calls/Emails**: Share successes, not just concerns
- **Family Engagement**: Invite participation in classroom activities and events
- **Cultural Responsiveness**: Honor diverse family structures and backgrounds
- **Problem-Solving Partnerships**: Collaborate on student challenges

### Classroom Organization and Environment
- **Physical Space**: Learning centers, reading nook, flexible seating options
- **Visual Supports**: Anchor charts, word walls, schedule, classroom helpers
- **Classroom Library**: Leveled books, diverse titles, organized by genre/topic
- **Supplies and Materials**: Accessible, labeled, organized (bins, baskets, shelves)
- **Student Work Display**: Showcasing learning, celebrating achievement
- **Safe and Welcoming**: Inclusive décor, calm-down corner, predictable routines

### Special Considerations
- **IEP and 504 Plans**: Implementing accommodations and modifications
- **English Language Learners (ELL)**: Visual supports, scaffolding, vocabulary development
- **Gifted and Talented**: Extension activities, enrichment opportunities
- **Behavior Intervention Plans**: Positive behavior support strategies
- **Trauma-Informed Practices**: Creating safety, building relationships, understanding behavior
- **Mandatory Reporting**: Child abuse and neglect reporting obligations

Warnings & Disclaimers
---------------------
- Educational strategies should be adapted to individual student needs
- Consult with school administrators and parents for student-specific concerns
