# Disciplinary Process for Security Policy Violations
## Open Operator System (NV oOS) WordPress Plugin

**Document Classification:** Internal  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-05  
**Review Date:** 2026-04-05  
**Document Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a formal disciplinary process for security policy violations within the NV oOS project. The purpose is to:

- Ensure consistent and fair enforcement of security policies
- Deter future policy violations through clear consequences
- Protect organizational information and assets
- Demonstrate commitment to information security
- Comply with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Control A.6.4
- Balance corrective action with organizational needs

---

## 2. Scope

This disciplinary process applies to:
- All employees, contractors, consultants, and third-party personnel
- All contributors to the NV oOS project (with access privileges)
- All security policy violations, including but not limited to:
  - ISMS Policy violations
  - Acceptable Use Policy violations
  - Access Control policy violations
  - Data protection violations
  - Incident reporting failures
  - Procedural non-compliance

---

## 3. Principles

### 3.1 Guiding Principles

**Fairness:**
- Consistent application of disciplinary measures
- Consideration of individual circumstances and intent
- Right to explanation and appeal
- Proportionate response to violation severity

**Transparency:**
- Clear communication of policies and consequences
- Documented disciplinary process
- Explanation of decisions to affected individuals

**Corrective Focus:**
- Emphasis on learning and improvement, not punishment
- Opportunity for corrective action
- Support for behavioral change

**Consistency:**
- Similar violations treated similarly
- No preferential treatment
- Documented precedents guide future decisions

### 3.2 No Punishment for Good Faith Actions

**Protected Activities:**
- Good faith security incident reporting (including self-reporting)
- Identification and reporting of vulnerabilities
- Questioning or seeking clarification on policies
- Reasonable mistakes made while attempting to comply

**Exception:** Gross negligence or deliberate disregard is not protected.

---

## 4. Violation Severity Levels

### 4.1 Level 1 - Minor Violations

**Characteristics:**
- Unintentional, first-time violation
- No security impact or data exposure
- No malicious intent
- Easily correctable

**Examples:**
- Forgot to lock screen once
- Weak password (not yet compromised)
- Late completion of security training (first time)
- Minor deviation from procedure without impact
- Accidental brief storage of low-sensitivity data in wrong location

**Typical Consequence:** Verbal warning, retraining

---

### 4.2 Level 2 - Moderate Violations

**Characteristics:**
- Repeated minor violations after warning
- Potential security risk created (but not realized)
- Negligent behavior
- Violation of multiple policies simultaneously

**Examples:**
- Repeated failure to lock screen after warnings
- Sharing credentials with coworker
- Installing unauthorized software
- Disabling security tools without authorization
- Accessing data outside of assigned responsibilities without clear business need
- Failure to report security incident within required timeframe
- Repeated late security training completion

**Typical Consequence:** Written warning, mandatory retraining, access restrictions

---

### 4.3 Level 3 - Serious Violations

**Characteristics:**
- Deliberate policy violation
- Significant security risk or actual incident resulted
- Gross negligence
- Violation of law or regulation
- Damage to organizational reputation

**Examples:**
- Intentionally bypassing security controls
- Unauthorized access to confidential or restricted data
- Sharing confidential data with unauthorized parties
- Failure to report security incident after becoming aware
- Destroying evidence of policy violation
- Repeated moderate violations after written warning
- Lying during investigation
- Attempting to cover up violation

**Typical Consequence:** Final written warning, privilege suspension, possible termination

---

### 4.4 Level 4 - Critical Violations

**Characteristics:**
- Intentional malicious activity
- Major security breach or significant data loss
- Illegal activity (theft, fraud, sabotage)
- Severe damage to organization or others
- Criminal conduct

**Examples:**
- Data theft or exfiltration for personal gain or malicious purposes
- Intentional introduction of malware or ransomware
- Sabotage of systems or data
- Industrial espionage
- Fraud or embezzlement
- Hacking or unauthorized access with malicious intent
- Sale of confidential information
- Serious harassment or threats

**Typical Consequence:** Immediate termination, legal action, law enforcement notification

---

## 5. Disciplinary Action Matrix

### 5.1 Standard Disciplinary Actions

| Violation Level | First Offense | Second Offense | Third Offense |
|----------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Level 1 (Minor)** | Verbal warning + Retraining | Written warning | Final written warning |
| **Level 2 (Moderate)** | Written warning + Retraining | Final written warning + Access restriction | Termination consideration |
| **Level 3 (Serious)** | Final written warning + Privilege suspension | Termination | Immediate termination |
| **Level 4 (Critical)** | Immediate termination + Legal action | N/A | N/A |

**Note:** This matrix provides guidelines. Actual disciplinary action depends on specific circumstances, including intent, impact, and mitigating/aggravating factors.

### 5.2 Disciplinary Action Types

**Verbal Warning:**
- Informal discussion with individual
- Explanation of violation and policy
- Reminder of expected behavior
- No formal documentation in personnel file (but logged by manager)
- Retraining may be required

**Written Warning:**
- Formal written notice of violation
- Documentation in personnel file
- Clear description of violation and policy violated
- Expected corrective action
- Consequences of repeated violation
- Signature required (acknowledgment of receipt, not necessarily agreement)
- Retraining typically required

**Final Written Warning:**
- Formal final warning before termination
- Documented in personnel file
- Strong language about termination risk
- Performance improvement plan (if applicable)
- Close monitoring period
- Signature required

**Access Restriction/Privilege Suspension:**
- Temporary or permanent reduction of access privileges
- Removal of privileged access (administrative rights, etc.)
- Restriction to read-only access
- Suspension of remote access
- Duration specified (temporary) or permanent

**Unpaid Suspension:**
- Time away from work without pay (where legally permitted)
- Typically 1-5 days for serious violations
- Used for reflection and as deterrent
- Documented in personnel file

**Termination:**
- End of employment or contractor relationship
- Immediate or with notice period (depending on severity and local law)
- All access revoked immediately
- Offboarding process initiated
- Documentation in personnel file
- May affect references (depending on jurisdiction)

**Legal Action:**
- Civil lawsuit (for damages, injunctions, etc.)
- Criminal complaint (for illegal activity)
- Reporting to law enforcement
- Cooperation with legal authorities
- Pursuit of damages or restitution

## 6. Disciplinary Process Steps

### 6.1 Step 1: Incident Detection and Reporting

**Detection Methods:**
- Security monitoring and alerting
- Manager observation
- Peer reporting
- Self-reporting by violator
- Internal audit findings
- External report (user, customer, security researcher)

**Immediate Actions:**
- Secure evidence (logs, screenshots, files)
- Prevent further damage or continued violation (if ongoing)
- Notify manager, CISO, and HR
- Do not confront individual prematurely (preserve evidence)

**Documentation:**
- Incident report with date, time, description
- Evidence collected
- Witnesses identified
- Initial severity assessment

---

### 6.2 Step 2: Investigation

**Investigation Team:**
- **Assigned Investigator:** Manager, CISO, or HR (depending on violation)
- **Support:** IT/Security for technical investigation
- **Oversight:** CISO for policy interpretation, HR for employment matters

**Investigation Activities:**
1. **Evidence Gathering:**
   - Review audit logs and system logs
   - Collect screenshots, files, communications
   - Interview witnesses
   - Document timeline of events

2. **Individual Interview:**
   - Notify individual of investigation (unless criminal activity suspected)
   - Interview individual to get their account
   - Allow individual to explain circumstances
   - Maintain confidentiality during investigation

3. **Analysis:**
   - Determine what happened (facts)
   - Assess policy violation (which policy, how violated)
   - Evaluate intent (intentional, negligent, accidental)
   - Identify impact (actual or potential damage)
   - Consider mitigating and aggravating factors

**Investigation Timeline:**
- Minor violations: 1-3 business days
- Moderate violations: 1-2 weeks
- Serious violations: 2-4 weeks
- Critical violations: Immediate (with ongoing legal investigation)

**Documentation:**
- Investigation report with findings
- Evidence appendix
- Interview notes
- Conclusion and severity determination

---

### 6.3 Step 3: Determination

**Decision Makers:**
- **Level 1 Violations:** Manager (with CISO consultation if needed)
- **Level 2 Violations:** Manager + CISO (or HR)
- **Level 3 Violations:** CISO + HR + Management
- **Level 4 Violations:** Management + Legal Counsel

**Determination Considerations:**
1. **Violation Severity:** Based on criteria in Section 4
2. **Intent:** Intentional vs. negligent vs. accidental
3. **Impact:** Actual damage caused or potential damage
4. **Prior History:** Prior violations or clean record
5. **Cooperation:** Cooperation during investigation
6. **Mitigating Factors:**
   - Self-reporting
   - Genuine remorse
   - Corrective action taken independently
   - Extenuating circumstances
   - First-time offender
7. **Aggravating Factors:**
   - Deliberate or malicious intent
   - Cover-up attempts or lying
   - Repeated violations
   - Significant impact
   - Failure to cooperate with investigation

**Decision:**
- Violation severity level confirmed
- Appropriate disciplinary action determined (per matrix in 5.1, adjusted for factors)
- Corrective actions required (retraining, process changes, etc.)
- Monitoring or follow-up plan

**Documentation:**
- Determination memorandum
- Rationale for decision
- Disciplinary action to be taken
- Approvals obtained

---

### 6.4 Step 4: Action Implementation

**Notification Meeting:**
- Manager (or CISO/HR) meets with individual
- Present findings and determination
- Explain policy violated and specific violation
- State disciplinary action being taken
- Provide written documentation
- Allow individual to respond (listen, but decision is final unless appealed)
- Obtain signature acknowledging receipt (not agreement)

**Immediate Actions:**
- Implement access restrictions (if applicable)
- Schedule retraining (if required)
- Update personnel file
- Notify relevant parties (IT, security, management) on need-to-know basis

**Documentation:**
- Disciplinary action form signed by individual and decision maker
- Placed in personnel file (confidential)
- Copy provided to individual
- Logged in disciplinary action tracking system

---

### 6.5 Step 5: Follow-Up and Monitoring

**Monitoring Period:**
- **Minor Violations:** 3 months monitoring
- **Moderate Violations:** 6 months monitoring
- **Serious Violations:** 12 months monitoring

**Follow-Up Activities:**
- Verify retraining completion (if required)
- Monitor for repeated violations (closer supervision)
- Check-in meetings (monthly or as appropriate)
- Performance improvement tracking (if part of action plan)
- Confirm corrective actions implemented

**Successful Completion:**
- No further violations during monitoring period
- All corrective actions completed
- Monitoring period ends
- Documentation of successful completion in file
- Warning may be expunged after specified period (policy dependent)

**Further Violations:**
- Escalate to next disciplinary level
- Reassess determination based on new information
- Consider termination if serious or repeated

---

### 6.6 Step 6: Appeals Process

**Right to Appeal:**
- Individual may appeal disciplinary determination
- Appeal must be submitted in writing within 5 business days of notification
- Appeal must state grounds for appeal (new evidence, procedural error, disproportionate action)

**Appeals Review:**
- **Reviewer:** Higher-level manager or management representative (not involved in original decision)
- **Process:**
  - Review investigation report and determination
  - Review appeal grounds
  - Interview appellant if needed
  - Consult with legal counsel if appropriate
  - Make independent decision

**Appeal Outcomes:**
- **Uphold:** Original decision stands
- **Modify:** Disciplinary action reduced or increased (if new information justifies)
- **Overturn:** No disciplinary action (if violation not substantiated)

**Appeal Decision Timeline:**
- Decision within 10 business days of appeal submission
- Decision is final (no further internal appeal)

**Documentation:**
- Appeal submission
- Appeal review notes
- Appeal decision with rationale
- All placed in personnel file

## 7. Special Circumstances

### 7.1 Self-Reporting

**Encouragement:**
- Organization encourages self-reporting of policy violations
- Self-reporting is a significant mitigating factor

**Consideration:**
- May result in reduced disciplinary action (e.g., warning instead of suspension)
- Level 1 and Level 2 violations: Typically verbal or written warning if self-reported promptly
- Level 3 violations: May avoid termination with self-reporting and corrective action
- Level 4 violations: Self-reporting considered but may not prevent termination or legal action

**Requirements for Leniency:**
- Prompt self-reporting (before discovery by others)
- Full disclosure and cooperation
- Genuine remorse and commitment to not repeat
- Corrective action taken (if possible)

### 7.2 Multiple Violations

**Simultaneous Violations:**
- If single incident violates multiple policies, treat as single violation (most severe level)
- Consider all policies violated in determination

**Pattern of Violations:**
- Multiple unrelated violations over time
- Each evaluated separately but history considered
- Pattern may indicate disregard for policies, leading to higher severity classification

### 7.3 Contributory Factors

**Organizational Factors:**
- Unclear policies or inadequate training may mitigate individual responsibility
- If violation reveals policy gap, focus on policy improvement, not just individual discipline
- Systemic issues addressed separately from individual discipline

**External Factors:**
- Emergency situations, extenuating personal circumstances considered
- Coercion or pressure from others (still may result in discipline, but mitigated)

### 7.4 Resignation During Investigation

**Pending Resignation:**
- Investigation continues even if individual resigns
- Findings documented in personnel file
- May affect references or future rehire eligibility

**Resignation to Avoid Discipline:**
- Resignation accepted, but findings documented
- May note "resigned in lieu of termination" if applicable
- Legal action may still be pursued for serious violations

## 8. Confidentiality

**Investigation Confidentiality:**
- Investigations conducted confidentially
- Information shared on need-to-know basis only
- Personnel file information confidential (access restricted to HR, management, CISO)

**Post-Action Confidentiality:**
- Disciplinary actions not discussed publicly
- Individual privacy respected
- May communicate policy violation occurred and corrective action taken (without identifying individual) for awareness purposes

**Exceptions:**
- Legal or regulatory reporting requirements
- Law enforcement investigations
- Need-to-know for operational purposes (e.g., revoking access)

---

## 9. Records and Documentation

### 9.1 Required Documentation

**For All Disciplinary Actions:**
- Incident report (triggering the investigation)
- Investigation report (findings and evidence)
- Determination memorandum (decision and rationale)
- Disciplinary action form (action taken, signed by individual)
- Follow-up and monitoring notes
- Appeal documentation (if applicable)

**Storage:**
- Confidential personnel file (HR)
- Secure digital records (encrypted, access controlled)
- Retention per organizational records retention policy

### 9.2 Retention Periods

**Retention:**
- **Verbal Warnings:** 12 months, then may be expunged
- **Written Warnings:** 24 months in active file, then archived
- **Final Warnings:** Permanent in personnel file
- **Terminations:** Permanent

**Purging:**
- Expunged records removed from active file but may be archived
- Individual may request expungement after specified clean period

## 10. Training and Awareness

### 10.1 Policy Communication

**All Personnel:**
- Receive copy of this policy during onboarding
- Acknowledge understanding and acceptance
- Refresher during annual security training

**Managers:**
- Special training on disciplinary process and their role
- Guidance on recognizing violations and conducting initial investigation
- Consultation resources available (CISO, HR)

### 10.2 Preventive Approach

**Focus on Prevention:**
- Clear policies and procedures
- Regular training and awareness programs
- Ongoing communication about security expectations
- Support for personnel to comply (tools, guidance)

**Security Culture:**
- Encourage questions and seeking clarification
- No-blame culture for good faith mistakes
- Recognition for good security practices
- Leadership modeling of security-conscious behavior

## 11. Continuous Improvement

### 11.1 Process Review

**Regular Review:**
- Annual review of disciplinary process effectiveness
- Quarterly review of disciplinary action trends
- Adjust process based on experience and feedback

**Metrics:**
- Number of violations by severity level
- Types of violations (identify patterns)
- Disciplinary actions taken
- Repeat violations (effectiveness of corrective actions)
- Appeal outcomes

### 11.2 Policy Improvement

**Identifying Policy Gaps:**
- Violations may reveal unclear or inadequate policies
- Policy improvements to prevent future violations
- Training enhancements based on common violations

**Feedback Loop:**
- Lessons learned from investigations
- Process improvements implemented
- Communication of policy updates
