   (`virtuallyjamaica.com`, `github.com/tanaza`), plus the WBA industry survey and

## 1. The reference site — `virtuallyjamaica.com`

**Method:** page contents fetched and read directly on 2026-11-16, not inferred. ✅

Footer confirms the ownership chain explicitly: *"© 2026 VIRTUALLY JAMAICA IS POWERED BY

### 1.1 Verified feature set

This is **considerably richer than a splash page**, and an earlier draft of this

| Feature (as marketed) | Product meaning | Maps to |
|---|---|---|

| "Deep customer understanding allows our clients to make **evidence based decisions**… like **space planning**" | **The cognition claim, already promised** | `Kpi_Schema` + Cognition |

### 1.2 What this changes about the competitive framing

Two of the assumptions this research was originally built on are **wrong**, and the
correction makes the proposal stronger rather than weaker:

**Wrong assumption A — "the reference is portal-only with no AP story."**
In fact multi-brand AP support is already part of the public sales promise. The
adapter layer is therefore not a novel feature we are adding; it is an existing
promise that currently rests on per-venue manual work. Formalising it behind a port
with a mock adapter is what makes the promise *scalable*.

**Wrong assumption B — "there is no measurement or analytics layer."**
In fact the site promises *"evidence based decisions… like space planning"*. So the
cognition claim is already being made to customers.

**Which sharpens the real gap.** The product already claims:

- multi-brand AP support,
- evidence-based decision support,
- social identity capture,
- marketing integrations.

What it does **not** have is any of the machinery that makes those claims defensible:

| Claim made | Missing machinery | Consequence today |
|---|---|---|
| "Evidence based decisions" | No standards KPI vocabulary (WFA Data Elements), no `method` on any metric | The evidence is unpublished and unrepeatable |
| "Space planning" insight | No device-identity model under MAC randomisation | Unique-visitor counts inflate 300–500% (§6.1) |
| "Keep the network secure" | No walled-garden / session-inheritance test suite | Security is asserted, not measured |
| Identity capture | Consent not modelled per-purpose | Marketing consent entangled with access consent (§6.3) |
| Social login (Facebook LIKEs) | Facebook Wi-Fi retired 2023 (§5.2) | A growth loop that no longer exists |

> **The thesis, corrected:** the reference product's *feature set* is not the gap —
it is genuinely broad. The gap is that every claim in it is currently
**unfalsifiable**. NV oOS WiFi's differentiator is not more portal features; it is
**measurement discipline** — Device ID identity, DE-shaped KPIs, per-purpose consent,
and a red-team suite — underneath the same feature set.

That is a much better position than "we will build a better splash page", and it is
only visible because the site was read rather than assumed.

## 2. `github.com/tanaza` — audit

**Read:** full repository listing incl. fork status, language, last push. ✅

Findings:

- **16 repositories.** The overwhelming majority are **forks**, not original work:
  `ssh2`, `node-dhcp`, `dokku` and related infrastructure forks.
- **One original of interest:** a `splashpage` repository — a captive-portal
  splash implementation.

**Conclusion: there is nothing here to fork or vendor.** The value is
architectural, and it is a two-line lesson:

> A WiFi product is (a) AP management + (b) an integration marketplace, and the
> marketplace is the part that compounds.

That directly informs the adapter/mock design in the proposal: the platform must be
built so a new controller, a new PMS, or a new CRM is a **plug-in adapter**, not a
core change. `tanaza`'s breadth of small integration repos is evidence that the
market expects many small connectors rather than one monolith.

## 3. npm registry audit

All version / licence / publish-date facts in this section are ✅ (registry
metadata). The *strategic* readings attached to them are inference and marked as
such.

### 3.1 Captive portal & CAPPORT

| Package | Version | Published | Licence | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `captive-portal` | 0.1.0 | 2016 | — | Abandoned. Nothing to adopt. ✅ |
| **`capport` (search)** | — | — | — | **Zero packages returned.** ✅ |

The `capport` result is the single most useful npm finding in this audit. RFC 8908
(the Captive Portal API) is eight years old and **has no JavaScript implementation
on npm at all**. That is a genuine open gap:

- Every commercial portal vendor ships a proprietary `/captive-portal-api` JSON
  endpoint.
- No open-source JS reference implementation exists to point at.
- Publishing one is cheap for us (it is a small, well-specified JSON document) and
  buys standing in the space.

**Recommendation carried into the proposal:** ship the RFC 8908 document as a
first-class, documented output of the portal module, and publish the generator as
an MIT package. It is a standards-legibility play with a low cost of entry.

### 3.2 RADIUS / AAA

| Package | Version | Published | Monthly downloads | Dependents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `radius` | 1.1.4 | 2018 | ~64k | 15 |

✅ Verified. It is a **codec only** — packet encode/decode. It is *not* a RADIUS
server, has no EAP state machine, and has not been published since 2018.

**Recommendation:** keep **FreeRADIUS** as the AAA authority. Do not attempt to
implement 802.1X/EAP in Node or PHP. Our job is to *configure and observe* RADIUS,
not to be it. This is a load-bearing scope decision and it is recorded in the
proposal as a non-goal.

### 3.3 Controller adapters

| Package | Version | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `node-unifi` | 2.5.1 | UniFi controller client ✅ |
| `unifi-client` | 0.11.1 | Includes websocket event stream ✅ |
| `unifi-protect` | 5.4.2 | Different product line (cameras) — out of scope ✅ |

The existence of three maintained UniFi clients is a market signal 🟡: UniFi is the
de-facto SMB controller, so a UniFi adapter is table stakes for Phase 2. It also
confirms the adapter boundary should be narrow — these libraries change shape
between controller versions, so the port must be *our* interface, not theirs.

### 3.4 Wi-Fi sensing / CSI

| Package | Notes |
|---|---|
| `@ruvnet/ruview` 0.9.3, MIT | WiFi-sensing MCP harness: ESP32 CSI capture, mmWave, LiDAR, **evidence-gated training** ✅ |

`ruview` is the closest existing art to "WiFi Cognition". Two things to take:

1. **Evidence-gated training.** Sensing inferences are only promoted to a model
   when a labelled corroborating signal (mmWave / LiDAR / ground truth) agrees.
   Without this, CSI inference is astrology.
2. **It is a harness, not a product.** No multi-tenant story, no venue model, no
   portal. It is a lab instrument. We should integrate *with* it, not rebuild it.

**Scope decision carried into the proposal:** WiFi Sensing (802.11bf) is a
**Phase 4 / partner integration**, never a Phase 0–2 deliverable. The physics is
hard, the hardware is specific (CSI-capable NICs, ESP32 arrays), and the buyer for
sensing is not the venue owner who buys social WiFi. Conflating them would sink
the product.

### 3.5 The AI / agent layer — the strategically important finding

| Package | Version | Licence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `@subashgautam/router-mcp` | 0.1.0 | MIT | **Pluggable adapters + a mock backend.** OpenWrt via SSH/ubus. ✅ |
| `@ruvnet/rvagent` | 0.2.1 | Apache-2.0 | SENSE-BRIDGE; **dual-transport MCP** (stdio + Streamable HTTP). ✅ |

These two are the architectural templates, and they are both tiny packages, which
is itself the lesson — the pattern is cheap to copy.

**From `router-mcp`: the adapter + mock pattern.** A `MockAdapter` that satisfies
the same port as the real controller is what makes the entire product testable
without hardware. This is the single most important structural decision in the
implementation plan: *Phase 0 ships the mock, not the UniFi client.*

**From `rvagent`: dual-transport and evidence gating.** NV oOS already has MCP
transport, but the pattern of exposing one domain service over both stdio (local
operator tooling) and Streamable HTTP (hosted console) is worth reusing for the
WiFi tools, because it lets a field engineer's laptop and the hosted console drive
the *same* domain service.

### 3.6 Mobile clients

| Package | Version | Monthly downloads | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `react-native-wifi-reborn` | 4.13.6 | ~210k | — |
| `@capgo/capacitor-wifi` | 8.5.12 | — | MPL-2.0 ✅ |

Highest-traffic WiFi packages on npm by a wide margin. Relevant only if a
guest-facing app is ever built. **Out of scope for this proposal**; recorded so a
future reader does not re-run the search.

### 3.7 Offensive tooling — adopt as a red-team suite

| Package | Notes |
|---|---|
| `@adams-404/ghostlink` | Walled-garden escape, **session inheritance**, DNS leak ✅ |

This package is an attack tool, and that is exactly why it is useful: it enumerates
the failure modes a captive portal must defend against. Adopting it as a **red-team
test suite** (not as a dependency, not as code) gives the portal module a security
gate from day one rather than after a customer incident.

Recorded failure classes to test against:

1. Walled-garden escape (reaching the internet before authorisation).
2. Session inheritance (a new device reusing an authorised device's session).
3. DNS leak (resolution succeeding while HTTP is intercepted).

### 3.8 Licence watchlist ⚠️

| Package | Licence | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `listwificlients` | GPL-2.0 ✅ | Strong copyleft |
| `ubus-websocket-communicator` | GPL-2.0 ✅ | Strong copyleft |
| `iopsys-ws-communicator` | GPL-2.0 ✅ | Strong copyleft |

GPL-2.0 is incompatible with a closed/hosted binary distribution. The rule for the
implementation plan:

> These may be **invoked as separate processes** (CLI/daemon boundary) but must
> never be `require`d into shipped PHP/JS bundles.

This matters because the hostapd/ubus tooling that makes OpenWrt support possible
sits in GPL-2.0 territory. The process boundary is what keeps the commercial
option open.

## 4. Standards landscape

Dates below were verified against the published record ✅.

### 4.1 IEEE 802.11bf-2025 — WLAN Sensing

**Published 26 September 2025.** ✅

Defines sensing as a first-class WLAN function: bistatic and multistatic
measurement, across 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz. This is the standard that makes "the WiFi
network can perceive" a legitimate engineering claim rather than a demo trick.

**Product consequence:** "WiFi X-Ray" as *radio-visualisation* has a standard
behind it from 2025 onward, but the standard describes **measurement**, not
**inference**. Anything we say about *what was sensed* (occupancy, fall detection,
gesture) is our inference and carries our liability. The proposal therefore splits
the brand: **X-Ray = measured signal truth; Cognition = our inference, labelled as
such.**

### 4.2 IEEE 802.11bh-2024 — Randomized and Changing MAC Addresses

**This is the most operationally important standard in the entire audit.** ✅

802.11bh introduces **Device ID**: an opaque identifier, generated by the station
or the network, carried in association / PASN / FILS exchanges, whose purpose is
**session continuity without a stable MAC address**.

This is the standards-sanctioned replacement for the thing every legacy WiFi
analytics product is built on. Its existence converts a privacy problem into an
architecture instruction:

> Identity is the **Device ID**, not the MAC. MAC is a radio-layer detail that
> rotates by design.

Every schema, every KPI, every uniqueness count in this product must be expressed
in terms of an opaque device identity with an explicit rotation story. There is no
"we'll normalise MACs later" — the normalisation *is* the schema.

### 4.3 IEEE 802.11bi-2026 — Enhanced Service with Privacy Protection

**Published 23 October 2026.** ✅

Privacy-protection amendments layered on the bh work. Reinforces the same
direction: the standard family now *assumes* the network should not need to
identify the human.

### 4.4 IEEE 802.11bn — Wi-Fi 8 / Ultra High Reliability

Task Group formed November 2023; **publication targeted 2028**. ✅

Not actionable for a 2026 build, but it fixes the horizon: UHR is about reliability
under dense overlap, which is precisely the condition venue WiFi fails in. The
metric vocabulary we choose now (retry rate, airtime fairness, MLO link balance)
should still be meaningful when bn lands.

### 4.5 IEEE 802.11bx — WLAN Intelligent Networking (WIN)

arXiv:2610.07900, **6 October 2026**. 🟡 (pre-standard; arXiv is a credible but not
normative source)

Proposes a triad that is worth adopting wholesale as vocabulary:

| Frame | Meaning | Our mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **AI as protocol** | The network negotiates AI capability at association | Phase 4 aspirations |
| **AI as platform** | The network hosts inference for its own clients | Our MCP tool surface |
| **AI as traffic** | Model traffic is ordinary network traffic needing QoS | The observability layer |

"AI as traffic" is the under-appreciated one: if a venue runs an assistant, the
assistant's calls are on the same airtime as the guests' video. A WiFi cognition
product that does not account for its own traffic is self-defeating.

### 4.6 IEEE AI Offload Study Group

Approved **March 2026**; PAR + CSD approved; **802 review expected November 2026**. ✅

Direction of travel: offloading AI inference *into* the network infrastructure.
Not actionable now, but it validates the product thesis — the industry is moving
toward "the AP is a compute node".

### 4.7 Wi-Fi Alliance — Data Elements

**130 KPIs**, agent→collector model, spec **v2.1**. ✅

This is the most immediately useful artifact in the whole standards audit. Wi-Fi
Data Elements specifies a normalised KPI vocabulary with an agent that reports to a
collector.

**Design instruction:** build the metrics layer as a **Data-Elements-shaped
normaliser**. Our internal names can be nicer, but the canonical export shape
should be DE-compatible, because:

- it is the vocabulary enterprise buyers already have in their RFPs;
- it makes us comparable to incumbents without arguing about definitions;
- it gives the adapter layer a fixed target — every controller adapter's job is to
  produce DE-shaped telemetry.

### 4.8 CAPPORT — RFC 8952 / 8908 / 8910

| RFC | Subject |
|---|---|
| RFC 8952 | Captive Portal Architecture |
| RFC 8908 | Captive Portal API (the JSON document) |
| RFC 8910 | Captive-Portal Identification in DHCP / RA |

Combined with the npm finding in §3.1, this is the clearest "we can own a gap"
opportunity in the research: a specified, stable, universally-needed JSON document
with no JS implementation.

### 4.9 RCM — RFC 9724 and RFC 9797

**RFC 9724 published March 2025.** ✅ Recommends randomised MAC addresses; RFC 9797
covers the manufacturer-side considerations.

Read together with 802.11bh (§4.2), the regulatory and standards picture is
unambiguous and points one way.

### 4.10 ⚠️ A claim to *not* repeat

> "Wi-Fi CERTIFIED Sensing 2026"

This phrase appears in market-research material but was 🔴 **not confirmed** by any
Wi-Fi Alliance primary source during this audit. Do not use it in marketing copy,
filings, or investor material until a WFA page or press release is cited. Recorded
here specifically so a future reader does not "rediscover" it and treat it as fact.

## 5. Market signals

### 5.1 WBA Industry Report 2026

185 respondents. 🟡 (single industry survey; directional, not audited)

| Signal | Value |
|---|---|
| More confident about WiFi's role than last year | 62% |
| Planning Wi-Fi 7 | 38% |
| **AI / cognitive networks** | **32%** |
| Security & privacy = #1 concern | 76% |
| MLO deployment | 46% |
| Say 6 GHz is critical | 65% |
| OpenRoaming deployed | 38% (+32% in 2026, +18% in 2027) |
| City-wide deployments | 33% (+39%) |

Two readings that shaped the proposal:

1. **76% say security/privacy is concern #1**, while only 32% are pursuing AI
   networks. Any product that leads with "AI" and treats privacy as a checkbox is
   selling into the smaller half of the market. **Lead with privacy, deliver AI.**
2. **OpenRoaming at 38% with strong forward intent** is the one integration that
   could bypass the whole portal problem (passpoint-style frictionless onboarding).
   It belongs in the adapter roadmap, not Phase 0.

### 5.2 The Facebook Wi-Fi post-mortem

**Retired 12 June 2023.** ✅

The canonical social-WiFi product — check in on Facebook, get free WiFi — was shut
down. The lesson is not "social WiFi is dead"; it is:

> **Social login is not a growth loop for the venue.** The venue got a like, not a
> customer. When the platform changed, the venue had nothing.

Which is why the proposal's design law #2 is *email + explicit consent + first-party
identity*, and the social provider is at most an optional accelerator.

## 6. Privacy and regulatory

### 6.1 MAC randomisation — the measurement trap

| Fact | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Share of mobile guests using randomised MACs | 80%+ | 🟡 |
| Inflation of "unique visitors" when counting by MAC | 300–500% | 🟡 |

Derived from the same literature as §4.2/§4.9 and consistent with them, but the
specific percentages come from vendor analyses rather than a peer-reviewed study.

**This is the reason the product cannot be a MAC-counting dashboard.** A legacy
WiFi analytics product reports "12,000 unique visitors this month" for a venue that
saw 3,000 people. Any product we ship that can produce that number is not merely
inaccurate — it is a liability, because the venue will make staffing and lease
decisions on it.

**Design law:** every uniqueness metric carries a `method` field naming its basis
(`device_id` / `probe_hash` / `session`), and the UI never renders a bare "unique
visitors" number without it.

### 6.2 Splash conversion benchmarks

| Metric | Benchmark | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Splash completion — healthy | > 65% | 🟡 |
| Splash completion — UX problem | < 50% | 🟡 |
| Opt-in rate | 60–78% | 🟡 |

Vendor-benchmark territory. Useful as *alarm thresholds* rather than targets: if a
venue's splash completion drops below 50%, the product should say so unprompted.
That is a concrete, cheap AI feature — and a good Phase 1 candidate precisely
because it needs no controller integration.

### 6.3 Jamaica Data Protection Act 2020

**The binding constraint for the primary market.** ✅

| Requirement | Implementation consequence |
|---|---|
| Consent must be **informed, freely given, specific, unequivocal** | Granular consent capture, not a single "I agree" |
| **Separate consent for direct marketing** | Two independent toggles; marketing consent cannot be bundled with access |
| Consent must be **withdrawable** | A functioning withdrawal path, logged |
| **Cross-border transfer restrictions** | Data residency is a design input, not an ops afterthought |
| **Parental consent for children** | Age-gating in the splash flow when the venue serves families |

This is not compliance theatre. "Freely given" is the hard one for a WiFi product:
if internet access is conditioned on marketing consent, the consent is not freely
given. **Access and marketing consent must be separable, and access must be granted
even when marketing consent is refused.** That single rule reshapes the splash UX
and the data model.

## 7. Synthesis — eight design laws

Everything above collapses into eight rules that the proposal and implementation
plan are built to satisfy:

1. **Device ID, not MAC.** Identity is opaque, rotatable, and standards-derived.
2. **Access is not payment for marketing consent.** Grant access, then ask.
3. **Consent is granular, logged, withdrawable, and exportable.**
4. **Every metric names its method.** No bare "unique visitors".
5. **The mock adapter ships before the real one.** Testability without hardware.
6. **Adapters are ours; libraries are theirs.** Narrow ports, versioned.
7. **FreeRADIUS is the AAA authority.** We configure and observe, we do not reimplement.
8. **Sensing is a partner track, not a Phase 0 feature.** Measurement is standard;
   inference is our liability.

## 8. What this research says *not* to build

Recorded explicitly, because scope discipline is where this kind of product
usually dies:

| Tempting | Why not |
|---|---|
| A RADIUS server | `radius` is codec-only; full EAP is a career. FreeRADIUS exists. |
| A captive-portal-only product | Commodity. The reference site already is one. |
| A MAC-based analytics dashboard | Produces wrong numbers (§6.1). Liability. |
| 802.11bf sensing in Phase 0–2 | Hardware-specific, physics-hard, wrong buyer (§3.4). |
| A guest mobile app | Highest-traffic npm packages are for exactly this; it is a different product with different distribution. |
| Social-login-as-growth | Facebook Wi-Fi died of this (§5.2). |
| GPL-2.0 hostapd/ubus helpers in the bundle | Licence contamination (§3.8). Process boundary only. |
