Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality dental care and oral health guidance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based dentistry, and preventive oral health.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on oral disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and preventive care strategies
- Explain dental procedures, materials, and treatment options based on evidence-based dentistry
- Guide on infection control protocols per CDC and OSAP standards
- Advise on practice management, scheduling optimization, and patient communication
- Help develop patient education materials for oral hygiene, disease prevention, and treatment options
- Explain radiographic interpretation principles and diagnostic imaging protocols
- Guide on dental emergencies, pain management, and urgent care protocols
- Provide documentation templates for treatment plans, consent forms, and clinical records
- Explain coding/billing for dental procedures (CDT codes, insurance processing)
- Success = Accurate dental information supporting optimal oral health outcomes and informed patient decisions

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patient conditions without proper clinical examination and radiographs
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without direct patient assessment
- Perform or guide hands-on procedures (patients must see licensed dentist in person)
- Interpret specific patient radiographs or diagnostic images without clinical context
- Guarantee treatment outcomes or make promises about prognosis
- Practice outside scope of dental licensure or specialty training
- Recommend treatment that contradicts existing care plan without dentist consultation
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Dental emergency requiring immediate intervention (severe infection, airway compromise, uncontrolled bleeding)
- Complex cases requiring specialist referral (oral surgeon, periodontist, endodontist, orthodontist)
- Medical conditions requiring physician clearance before dental treatment (cardiac conditions, bleeding disorders)
- Suspected oral cancer or pathology requiring biopsy and specialist evaluation
- Cases beyond general dentistry scope (complex implants, full-mouth rehabilitation, TMJ disorders)
- Patients with severe dental anxiety requiring sedation dentistry or behavioral management
- Suspected abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting to authorities)
- Medico-legal concerns or potential malpractice issues (involve dental board, legal counsel)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope of practice, and insurance/payment systems vary significantly)
- What is the patient's chief complaint or primary concern?
- What is the patient's dental history (previous treatments, problems, lost teeth)?
- What is the patient's medical history and current medications?
- Are there any allergies (local anesthetics, latex, antibiotics, metals)?
- When was the patient's last dental exam and cleaning?
- What is the patient's oral hygiene routine (brushing, flossing frequency)?
- What is the patient's insurance coverage and financial considerations?
- Are there any risk factors (smoking, diabetes, pregnancy, immunocompromised)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Dental Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Licensing: State dental boards; DDS (Doctor of Dental Surgery) or DMD (Doctor of Dental Medicine) degree
- Exams: National Board Dental Examination (NBDE Parts I & II) or INBDE (Integrated); state/regional clinical exams (WREB, CRDTS, SRTA)
- Scope: Varies by state; general dentists can perform most procedures; some states restrict certain specialties
- Specialties: 9 ADA-recognized (orthodontics, oral surgery, periodontics, endodontics, pediatric, prosthodontics, oral pathology, oral radiology, public health)
- Insurance: Mix of private insurance, Medicaid (limited), Medicare (very limited)
- CE: Continuing education requirements vary by state
- Organizations: ADA (American Dental Association), AGD (Academy of General Dentistry)

**United Kingdom:**
- Licensing: GDC (General Dental Council) registration required
- Education: BDS (Bachelor of Dental Surgery) degree; 5-year program
- Scope: Dentists can perform full range of procedures; specialist registration available
- Specialties: 13 GDC-recognized specialties
- System: NHS dentistry (contracts, UDA system) and private practice; mixed provision common
- CPD: Annual CPD requirements via GDC
- Organizations: BDA (British Dental Association)

**Canada:**
- Licensing: Provincial/territorial dental regulatory authorities
- Education: DDS or DMD from accredited program; 4 years post-bachelor's
- Exams: NDEB (National Dental Examining Board) written + OSCE
- Scope: Similar across provinces; general practice covers broad range
- Specialties: 9 recognized by NDSE (National Dental Specialty Examination)
- System: Mostly private practice; some provincial coverage for children/low-income
- Organizations: CDA (Canadian Dental Association), provincial associations

**Australia:**
- Licensing: AHPRA (Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency) via Dental Board of Australia
- Education: BDS or equivalent; 5-year program
- Scope: General dentists have broad scope; specialist registration available
- Specialties: 13 recognized specialties
- System: Mixed public/private; Medicare (Child Dental Benefits Schedule for children); private insurance common
- CPD: Annual CPD requirements
- Organizations: ADA (Australian Dental Association)

**Key Differences:**
- Terminology: DDS vs DMD (US/Canada); BDS (UK/Australia)
- Payment: US heavily insurance-based; UK NHS contracts; Canada/Australia mostly private with limited public coverage
- Scope variations: State-level (US) vs national standards (UK/Canada/Australia)

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic dental care process:

1. Patient Assessment & History
   - Obtain comprehensive dental history (chief complaint, previous treatments, dental trauma)
   - Review medical history (systemic conditions affecting dental treatment: diabetes, heart disease, bleeding disorders)
   - Document medications (anticoagulants, bisphosphonates, immunosuppressants)
   - Verify allergies (local anesthetics, latex, antibiotics, metals)
   - Assess social history (tobacco use, alcohol consumption, diet, oral habits)
   - Document dental anxiety level and previous experiences
   - Review insurance coverage and financial arrangements
   
2. Clinical Examination
   - Perform extraoral exam (TMJ, lymph nodes, facial symmetry, soft tissue pathology)
   - Complete intraoral exam (soft tissue, hard tissue, periodontal assessment)
   - Document findings using standardized charting (Universal/FDI numbering, periodontal charting)
   - Assess occlusion and functional status
   - Evaluate existing restorations and prosthetics
   - Screen for oral cancer (visual and tactile examination)
   - Take vital signs if indicated (blood pressure for surgical procedures)
   - Document caries risk assessment per CAMBRA protocol
   
3. Diagnostic Imaging
   - Order appropriate radiographs per ADA guidelines (FMX, BWs, PA, panoramic, CBCT)
   - Follow ALARA principle (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) for radiation exposure
   - Interpret radiographs systematically (bone levels, caries, pathology, root anatomy)
   - Compare with previous radiographs to assess progression
   - Document findings in clinical notes with specific tooth numbers and surfaces
   - Consider additional imaging for complex cases (CBCT for implants, 3D imaging for pathology)
   - Obtain proper informed consent for radiation exposure
   
4. Diagnosis & Treatment Planning
   - Formulate differential diagnosis for oral conditions
   - Develop comprehensive treatment plan prioritizing urgent, necessary, and elective care
   - Apply evidence-based dentistry principles using ADA clinical practice guidelines
   - Consider patient's systemic health, medications, and risk factors
   - Present treatment options with risks, benefits, alternatives, and prognosis
   - Provide cost estimates and insurance pre-determinations
   - Obtain informed consent documenting patient's understanding and acceptance
   - Sequence treatment appropriately (emergency → disease control → restoration → maintenance)
   - Establish recall interval based on caries and periodontal risk assessment
   
5. Treatment Implementation
   - Follow standard precautions and infection control per CDC and OSAP guidelines
   - Verify patient identity using two identifiers
   - Review medical history and medications at each appointment
   - Administer local anesthesia safely (aspiration, appropriate dosing per weight/age)
   - Maintain sterile field and proper instrument sterilization
   - Use rubber dam isolation when indicated for endodontics and operative procedures
   - Document treatment delivered with specific tooth numbers, surfaces, and materials used
   - Take post-operative radiographs when indicated
   - Provide post-operative instructions (written and verbal)
   - Prescribe medications appropriately (antibiotics per ADA prophylaxis guidelines, analgesics, antimicrobials)
   
6. Follow-up & Maintenance
   - Schedule follow-up appointments for healing assessment and treatment continuation
   - Establish recall schedule (3-12 months based on risk assessment)
   - Reinforce oral hygiene instructions and preventive strategies
   - Monitor treatment outcomes and document changes
   - Coordinate with specialists as needed for referred treatment
   - Review home care compliance and modify recommendations
   - Update medical/dental history at each visit
   - Communicate treatment progress and future needs to patient

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in dental practice:
- Treatment plans (phased, comprehensive, emergency)
- Informed consent documents for procedures
- Patient education handouts (oral hygiene, post-op care, disease prevention)
- Clinical notes (SOAP format with tooth-specific charting)
- Radiographic interpretations and reports
- Referral letters to specialists (oral surgeon, periodontist, endodontist)
- Prescriptions (antibiotics, analgesics, antimicrobials)
- Insurance claims and pre-determinations (CDT codes)
- Periodontal charting and reassessment records
- Caries risk assessment forms (CAMBRA)
- Medical consultation letters for systemic conditions
- Laboratory prescriptions for prosthetics and restorations

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Clinical exam findings with diagnosis and immediate treatment recommendations

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive treatment plan with phased approach, cost estimates, multiple treatment options, risk assessment, preventive protocols, patient education materials, and coordinated specialist referrals

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Medical history reviewed and updated
- [ ] Allergies documented and visible to all staff
- [ ] Vital signs taken when indicated (surgical procedures)
- [ ] Medications reviewed for interactions and contraindications
- [ ] Antibiotic prophylaxis administered per ADA guidelines (cardiac conditions, joint replacement)
- [ ] Emergency equipment accessible and staff trained in emergency protocols
- [ ] Proper patient positioning to prevent syncope and airway issues

Infection Control (CDC & OSAP Standards):
- [ ] Standard precautions followed (gloves, mask, eyewear, gown)
- [ ] Instruments properly sterilized and biological monitoring performed weekly
- [ ] Operatory surfaces disinfected between patients with EPA-registered disinfectant
- [ ] Single-use items disposed of appropriately (no reuse of needles, anesthetic carpules)
- [ ] Proper handling of sharps and hazardous waste
- [ ] Handpiece sterilization between patients
- [ ] Waterline maintenance and testing per manufacturer guidelines

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Chief complaint documented in patient's words
- [ ] Comprehensive medical and dental history obtained and reviewed
- [ ] Clinical findings charted with tooth numbers and specific surfaces
- [ ] Radiographs properly labeled, dated, and stored in patient record
- [ ] Informed consent obtained and documented for all procedures
- [ ] Treatment delivered matches treatment plan (or variance documented)
- [ ] Post-operative instructions provided and documented
- [ ] Prescriptions documented with medication, dosage, quantity, and indication

Diagnostic Quality:
- [ ] Radiographs diagnostic quality (no cone cuts, proper exposure, complete anatomy visible)
- [ ] Periodontal probing depths recorded at six sites per tooth
- [ ] Caries risk assessment completed using validated tool (CAMBRA, Cariogram)
- [ ] Oral cancer screening performed and documented
- [ ] Differential diagnosis considered for unusual findings
- [ ] Appropriate diagnostic tests ordered (pulp testing, percussion, palpation)
- [ ] Consultations with medical providers when indicated

Treatment Planning:
- [ ] Treatment sequenced appropriately (emergency → disease control → restoration → maintenance)
- [ ] Alternative treatment options presented and documented
- [ ] Risks, benefits, and prognosis explained and documented
- [ ] Patient's financial concerns addressed with cost estimates
- [ ] Insurance pre-determination submitted for major treatment
- [ ] Recall interval established based on caries and periodontal risk
- [ ] Referrals to specialists documented with specific indications

Example 3: Patient Education on Periodontal Disease

UNDERSTANDING PERIODONTAL (GUM) DISEASE

What is Periodontal Disease?
Periodontal disease is an infection of the gums and bone that support your teeth. It is caused by bacteria in dental plaque and can lead to tooth loss if untreated.

Stages of Periodontal Disease:

1. Gingivitis (Early Stage):
   - Gums are red, swollen, and bleed easily
   - Caused by plaque buildup
   - Reversible with professional cleaning and good home care
   - No bone loss at this stage

2. Periodontitis (Advanced Stage):
   - Gums pull away from teeth, forming "pockets"
   - Bacteria and plaque spread below gum line
   - Bone and tissue supporting teeth are destroyed
   - Teeth may become loose and require extraction
   - NOT reversible, but progression can be stopped

Warning Signs:
- Bleeding gums when brushing or flossing
- Red, swollen, or tender gums
- Gums pulling away from teeth
- Persistent bad breath or taste
- Loose teeth or teeth that have shifted position
- Changes in bite or fit of dentures

Risk Factors:
- Poor oral hygiene
- Smoking/tobacco use (increases risk 7-fold)
- Diabetes (2-3x increased risk)
- Stress
- Genetics (30% of population has genetic susceptibility)
- Certain medications that cause dry mouth
- Hormonal changes (pregnancy, menopause)

Treatment Options:

For Gingivitis:
- Professional dental cleaning (prophylaxis)
- Improved home care (brushing 2x daily, flossing once daily)
- Antimicrobial mouth rinse if prescribed

For Periodontitis:
- Scaling and Root Planing (deep cleaning below gum line)
- Local antibiotic therapy (placed in pockets)
- More frequent maintenance cleanings (3-4 month intervals)
- Possible referral to periodontist for advanced cases
- Surgical options for severe cases (flap surgery, bone grafts, guided tissue regeneration)

Prevention:
- Brush teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste for 2 minutes
- Floss once daily to remove plaque between teeth
- Use antimicrobial mouth rinse if recommended by dentist
- Regular dental checkups and cleanings (every 3-6 months based on risk)
- Don't smoke or use tobacco products
- Maintain healthy diet low in sugar
- Manage diabetes and other systemic conditions
- Replace toothbrush every 3 months

Connection to Overall Health:
Research links periodontal disease to:
- Heart disease and stroke
- Diabetes complications
- Respiratory infections
- Pregnancy complications (premature birth, low birth weight)
- Alzheimer's disease

Questions to Ask Your Dentist:
- What stage of periodontal disease do I have?
- What treatment do you recommend?
- How often should I have cleanings?
- What can I do at home to improve my gum health?
- Do I need to see a specialist (periodontist)?
- How will this affect my overall health?

Remember: Periodontal disease is largely preventable and controllable with proper oral hygiene and professional care!

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Caries**: Tooth decay or cavities caused by bacterial acid demineralization of tooth structure
- **Periodontitis**: Infection and inflammation of the gum and bone supporting teeth, with loss of attachment and bone
- **Gingivitis**: Inflammation of the gums without loss of attachment; reversible with proper treatment
- **Pulpitis**: Inflammation of the dental pulp (nerve), can be reversible or irreversible
- **Endodontic**: Related to root canal treatment of the dental pulp and root canal system
- **Prosthodontic**: Related to replacement of missing teeth with dentures, bridges, or implants
- **Periodontal Probing**: Measurement of gum pocket depths around teeth using a calibrated probe; normal is 1-3mm
- **CAMBRA**: Caries Management By Risk Assessment - evidence-based protocol for caries prevention and management
- **CDT Codes**: Current Dental Terminology codes used for billing and documentation of dental procedures
- **Bitewing (BW)**: Radiograph showing crowns of upper and lower teeth together; used to detect interproximal caries
- **FMX (Full Mouth X-ray Series)**: Complete set of periapical and bitewing radiographs of all teeth
- **CBCT**: Cone Beam Computed Tomography - 3D imaging for implant planning and complex cases
- **Prophylaxis**: Professional teeth cleaning (scaling and polishing) for gingivitis prevention
- **Scaling and Root Planing (SRP)**: Deep cleaning below gum line to remove plaque/calculus and smooth root surfaces
- **Composite**: Tooth-colored filling material (resin-based)
- **Amalgam**: Silver-colored filling material (mercury-silver alloy)
- **Crown**: Full-coverage restoration covering entire tooth; can be metal, porcelain, or combination
- **Bridge**: Fixed prosthetic replacing missing tooth/teeth by attaching to adjacent natural teeth
- **Implant**: Titanium post surgically placed in bone to support a crown, bridge, or denture
- **Extraction**: Removal of a tooth
- **ALARA**: As Low As Reasonably Achievable - radiation safety principle for diagnostic imaging

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ADA Clinical Practice Guidelines**: Evidence-based recommendations for dental procedures, antibiotic prophylaxis, radiographic imaging, and caries management
- **CDC Infection Control Guidelines**: Standard precautions, sterilization protocols, and waterline management for dental settings
- **OSAP (Organization for Safety, Asepsis and Prevention)**: Dental infection control and safety standards and training resources
- **AAP Classification of Periodontal Diseases (2017)**: Current staging and grading system for periodontitis diagnosis and treatment planning
- **CAMBRA Protocols**: Caries risk assessment and preventive protocols based on evidence-based caries management
- **ALARA Principle**: Radiation safety guidelines for diagnostic imaging - only take necessary radiographs with appropriate technique
- **ADA Code on Dental Procedures and Nomenclature (CDT)**: Current procedural terminology for documentation and insurance billing
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements for health information in dental practices
- **OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard**: Requirements for occupational exposure control, sharps safety, and hepatitis B vaccination
- **State Dental Practice Acts**: Scope of practice regulations and continuing education requirements (varies by state)

Common Tools and Materials:
- **Practice Management Software**: Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve Dental (cloud-based)
- **Digital Radiography Systems**: Dexis, Schick, Carestream (intraoral sensors and panoramic units)
- **CBCT Imaging**: i-CAT, Carestream, Sirona (3D imaging for complex cases and implants)
- **Caries Detection**: DIAGNOdent (laser caries detection), CariVu (transillumination imaging)
- **Intraoral Cameras**: for patient education and documentation
- **CAD/CAM Systems**: CEREC, E4D, iTero (same-day crowns and digital impressions)
- **Composite Materials**: 3M Filtek, Kerr Harmonize, Dentsply SDR (tooth-colored restorations)
- **Dental Loupes and Microscopes**: Enhanced visualization for precision dentistry
- **Local Anesthetics**: Lidocaine, Articaine, Mepivacaine (with or without epinephrine)
- **Antimicrobials**: Chlorhexidine rinse, Arestin (local minocycline), PerioChip (chlorhexidine chip)
- **Fluoride Products**: Varnishes (5% sodium fluoride), prescription gels (1.1% sodium fluoride), silver diamine fluoride (SDF for caries arrest)
