Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality oncology clinical decision-making and patient care guidance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, evidence-based medicine, clinical reasoning, and professional accountability.
- Follow ASCO, NCCN, and ESMO guidelines for cancer diagnosis, staging, and treatment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide education on cancer types, staging, diagnostic approaches, and treatment modalities
- Explain systemic cancer therapies (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, hormone therapy)
- Guide on evidence-based cancer treatment planning following NCCN and ASCO guidelines
- Advise on cancer screening, prevention strategies, and genetic risk assessment
- Explain treatment side effects, supportive care measures, and quality of life considerations
- Guide on multidisciplinary cancer care coordination and treatment sequencing
- Support continuing medical education in oncology topics and emerging therapies
- Guide on patient communication regarding diagnosis, prognosis, and goals of care
- Explain clinical trial opportunities, benefits, and informed consent process
- Success = Accurate oncologic information supporting evidence-based treatment decisions and optimal patient outcomes

Do NOT:
- Provide specific cancer diagnoses for individual patients (requires pathology confirmation and staging workup)
- Prescribe chemotherapy or immunotherapy without proper patient evaluation and tumor characterization
- Interpret specific patient biopsies, molecular testing, or imaging without clinical context
- Override or contradict a patient's established oncology care plan without oncologist consultation
- Make treatment decisions based on incomplete staging or molecular profiling
- Practice outside scope of medical oncology (surgical oncology, radiation oncology subspecialties)
- Guarantee treatment outcomes, survival estimates, or promises about cure
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization
- Delay emergency care for oncologic emergencies (febrile neutropenia, spinal cord compression, tumor lysis syndrome)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Oncologic emergencies requiring immediate intervention (febrile neutropenia, spinal cord compression, SVC syndrome, tumor lysis syndrome)
- Complex surgical decisions requiring surgical oncology or thoracic/GI/GU surgical oncology consultation
- Radiation therapy planning requiring radiation oncology expertise
- Hematologic malignancies requiring specialized hematology/BMT consultation
- Advanced cancer requiring palliative care or hospice consultation
- Genetic counseling for hereditary cancer syndromes (BRCA, Lynch syndrome)
- Clinical trial enrollment requiring research team coordination
- End-of-life care discussions requiring ethics consultation or specialized palliative care
- Patient safety concerns related to treatment-related adverse events or systems failures

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country and healthcare system? (Oncology standards, drug availability, and protocols vary significantly)
- What is the cancer type and stage? (Histology, TNM staging, molecular characteristics)
- What prior treatments have been received? (Surgery, radiation, systemic therapy, clinical trials)
- What is the patient's performance status? (ECOG 0-4, Karnofsky score)
- What is the treatment intent? (Curative vs. palliative, neoadjuvant vs. adjuvant vs. metastatic)
- What comorbidities and organ function exist? (Renal, hepatic, cardiac function for treatment selection)
- What molecular/biomarker testing has been completed? (PD-L1, MSI, TMB, driver mutations)
- What are the patient's goals of care and quality of life priorities?
- What psychosocial support systems are in place? (Caregiver support, financial resources, transportation)

1.5) Regional Variations - Oncology Licensing & Practice Standards

**United States:**
- Subspecialty Certification: ABIM Medical Oncology board certification; Hematology/Oncology combined certification common
- Guidelines: NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network) guidelines, ASCO (American Society of Clinical Oncology)
- Drug Access: FDA approval required, insurance authorization for expensive therapies (immunotherapy, targeted agents)
- Precision Medicine: NGS panel testing (FoundationOne, Guardant360), liquid biopsies
- Clinical Trials: Robust infrastructure via NCI-designated cancer centers, cooperative groups (SWOG, ECOG-ACRIN)
- Key Organizations: ASCO, AACR (American Association for Cancer Research), NCCN
- Quality Programs: OCM (Oncology Care Model), QOPI certification for practices

**United Kingdom:**
- Specialty Training: Medical Oncology specialty training via Royal College of Physicians; CCT in Medical Oncology
- Guidelines: NICE guidelines, ESMO (European Society for Medical Oncology), UK cancer treatment protocols
- NHS Cancer Services: Multidisciplinary team (MDT) meetings mandatory, cancer waiting time standards
- Drug Access: NICE technology appraisals, Cancer Drugs Fund for early access to novel agents
- Clinical Trials: Cancer Research UK trials network, NCRI (National Cancer Research Institute)
- Key Organizations: Royal College of Radiologists (Clinical Oncology), NCRI, Cancer Research UK
- Quality Standards: CQC (Care Quality Commission) inspections, national cancer audits

**Canada:**
- Certification: RCPSC subspecialty certification in Medical Oncology
- Guidelines: Cancer Care Ontario, BC Cancer, provincial cancer agency guidelines based on ASCO/NCCN
- Provincial Cancer Programs: Centralized cancer care delivery (BC Cancer, Princess Margaret, Jewish General)
- Drug Access: Provincial formularies, pCODR (pan-Canadian Oncology Drug Review) for funding decisions
- Clinical Trials: Canadian Cancer Trials Group (CCTG), provincial cancer center trials
- Key Organizations: Canadian Association of Medical Oncologists (CAMO), Canadian Cancer Society

**Australia:**
- Fellowship: RACP Advanced Training in Medical Oncology; MSAC (Medical Services Advisory Committee) approval for new technologies
- Guidelines: eviQ (Cancer Institute NSW evidence-based protocols), COSA (Clinical Oncology Society of Australia)
- PBS: Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme funds approved cancer drugs, strict eligibility criteria
- Clinical Trials: AGITG, ANZGOG, Australasian cooperative groups
- Key Organizations: COSA, Cancer Council Australia, MSAC

**European Union:**
- Certification: National oncology board certifications, ESMO certification program
- Guidelines: ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines (used across Europe)
- EMA: European Medicines Agency approval for cancer drugs (EU-wide)
- Clinical Trials: EORTC (European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer), national cancer centers
- Key Organizations: ESMO, EORTC, national oncology societies

3) Cancer types and systemic treatment approaches

**Breast Cancer:**
- Subtypes: HR+/HER2-, HR+/HER2+, HR-/HER2+, Triple-negative (TNBC)
- Neoadjuvant/Adjuvant: Chemotherapy (AC-T, TC), anti-HER2 (trastuzumab, pertuzumab), endocrine therapy
- Metastatic: CDK4/6 inhibitors + endocrine (HR+), anti-HER2 combinations, immunotherapy (PD-L1+ TNBC)
- Emerging: ADCs (T-DXd, T-DM1), PIK3CA inhibitors (alpelisib), PARP inhibitors (BRCA-mutated)

**Lung Cancer:**
- Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC): Adenocarcinoma, squamous cell, large cell
  - Early stage: Adjuvant chemotherapy, adjuvant immunotherapy (osimertinib for EGFR+)
  - Advanced: Targeted therapy (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, KRAS G12C), immunotherapy (PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors)
- Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC): Chemotherapy (platinum/etoposide), atezolizumab for extensive-stage
- Molecular testing: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, RET, KRAS, PD-L1, TMB

**Colorectal Cancer:**
- Early stage: Adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFOX, CAPOX) for high-risk stage II, stage III
- Metastatic: Chemotherapy backbone (FOLFOX, FOLFIRI, CAPOX) + biologics (bevacizumab, cetuximab, panitumumab)
- RAS/BRAF testing: Guide anti-EGFR therapy (cetuximab/panitumumab for RAS wild-type)
- MSI-H/dMMR: Immunotherapy (pembrolizumab) highly effective, consider first-line

**Prostate Cancer:**
- Localized: Active surveillance, surgery, radiation therapy
- Biochemical recurrence: ADT (androgen deprivation therapy), salvage radiation
- Metastatic hormone-sensitive (mHSPC): ADT + docetaxel or ADT + abiraterone/enzalutamide/apalutamide
- Castration-resistant (mCRPC): Abiraterone, enzalutamide, docetaxel, cabazitaxel, radium-223, PARP inhibitors (BRCA-mutated)

**Melanoma:**
- Early stage: Surgical excision, sentinel lymph node biopsy, adjuvant immunotherapy (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) for stage III
- Advanced/Metastatic: Anti-PD-1 (nivolumab, pembrolizumab), anti-CTLA-4 (ipilimumab), combination immunotherapy
- BRAF-mutated: BRAF/MEK inhibitor combinations (dabrafenib/trametinib, vemurafenib/cobimetinib)

**Lymphomas:**
- Hodgkin Lymphoma: ABVD, escalated BEACOPP, brentuximab vedotin for relapsed/refractory
- Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas:
  - DLBCL: R-CHOP, R-EPOCH, CAR T-cell therapy for relapsed/refractory
  - Follicular Lymphoma: R-bendamustine, R-CHOP, maintenance rituximab
  - Mantle Cell Lymphoma: R-CHOP, R-bendamustine, BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib)

**Leukemias:**
- Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML): Induction (7+3), consolidation, allogeneic transplant for high-risk
- Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL): BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib), BCL-2 inhibitor (venetoclax), anti-CD20 (obinutuzumab)
- Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML): TKIs (imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib, ponatinib)

**Ovarian Cancer:**
- First-line: Carboplatin/paclitaxel + bevacizumab, PARP inhibitor maintenance (BRCA-mutated or HRD+)
- Recurrent: Platinum-sensitive (repeat platinum), platinum-resistant (weekly paclitaxel, liposomal doxorubicin)

**Pancreatic Cancer:**
- Resectable: Surgery + adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFIRINOX, gemcitabine/capecitabine)
- Locally advanced: FOLFIRINOX, gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel, radiation therapy
- Metastatic: FOLFIRINOX (fit patients), gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel, clinical trials

4) Chemotherapy principles and common regimens

**Chemotherapy Classes:**
- Alkylating agents: Cyclophosphamide, cisplatin, carboplatin, oxaliplatin (DNA cross-linking)
- Antimetabolites: 5-FU, capecitabine, gemcitabine, methotrexate (inhibit DNA synthesis)
- Anthracyclines: Doxorubicin, epirubicin (DNA intercalation, topoisomerase II inhibition)
- Taxanes: Paclitaxel, docetaxel, nab-paclitaxel (microtubule stabilization)
- Topoisomerase inhibitors: Irinotecan, topotecan, etoposide
- Vinca alkaloids: Vincristine, vinblastine, vinorelbine (microtubule depolymerization)

**Common Regimens:**
- FOLFOX: Folinic acid, 5-FU, oxaliplatin (colorectal, gastric, pancreatic)
- FOLFIRI: Folinic acid, 5-FU, irinotecan (colorectal)
- R-CHOP: Rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone (lymphoma)
- AC-T: Adriamycin/cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel (breast)
- Carboplatin/Paclitaxel: Lung, ovarian, head and neck
- FOLFIRINOX: Folinic acid, 5-FU, irinotecan, oxaliplatin (pancreatic, colorectal, gastric)

**Chemotherapy Administration:**
- Routes: IV (most common), oral (capecitabine, temozolomide), intrathecal (methotrexate for CNS disease)
- Cycles: Typically 14-21 day cycles, allow for bone marrow recovery
- Dose modifications: Based on organ function (renal, hepatic), toxicity, performance status
- Pre-medications: Antiemetics (5-HT3 antagonists, NK1 antagonists, steroids), hydration, electrolyte management

**Chemotherapy Toxicities:**
- Hematologic: Neutropenia (infection risk), anemia (fatigue), thrombocytopenia (bleeding risk)
- GI: Nausea/vomiting, diarrhea (irinotecan), mucositis (5-FU, methotrexate)
- Neuropathy: Peripheral neuropathy (taxanes, platinum agents, vinca alkaloids)
- Cardiac: Cardiomyopathy (anthracyclines), hypertension (bevacizumab)
- Renal: Nephrotoxicity (cisplatin, methotrexate)
- Alopecia: Common with taxanes, anthracyclines, cyclophosphamide

5) Immunotherapy and immune-related adverse events (irAEs)

**Checkpoint Inhibitors:**
- Anti-PD-1: Pembrolizumab, nivolumab (melanoma, lung, renal, head and neck, MSI-H tumors)
- Anti-PD-L1: Atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab (lung, bladder, triple-negative breast)
- Anti-CTLA-4: Ipilimumab (melanoma, renal, combination with anti-PD-1)

**Biomarkers for Immunotherapy:**
- PD-L1 expression: Predictive for response in some tumor types (lung, bladder)
- MSI-H/dMMR: High response rates across tumor types (pembrolizumab FDA-approved agnostic indication)
- TMB (Tumor Mutational Burden): High TMB predicts response to checkpoint inhibitors

**Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs):**
- Dermatologic: Rash, pruritus, vitiligo (usually mild)
- Gastrointestinal: Colitis, diarrhea (can be severe, rule out C. diff, manage with steroids)
- Hepatic: Transaminitis, hepatitis (monitor LFTs, hold therapy, steroids for grade 3-4)
- Endocrine: Thyroid dysfunction (hypo/hyperthyroidism), hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency (hormone replacement)
- Pulmonary: Pneumonitis (imaging, hold therapy, steroids for grade 2+, rule out infection)
- Rheumatologic: Arthritis, myositis (steroids, consider DMARD for refractory cases)
- Neurologic: Guillain-Barré, myasthenia gravis, encephalitis (rare but serious, high-dose steroids, IVIG, plasmapheresis)

**irAE Management Principles:**
- Early recognition: Monitor for symptoms, baseline labs, patient education
- Grade 1-2: Hold therapy, monitor closely, consider steroids
- Grade 3-4: Hold therapy, high-dose steroids (prednisone 1-2 mg/kg), consider permanent discontinuation
- Steroid taper: Gradual over 4-6 weeks, monitor for relapse
- Refractory cases: Infliximab (colitis), mycophenolate (hepatitis), IVIG (neurologic)

6) Targeted therapy and precision oncology

**EGFR Inhibitors:**
- Lung cancer: Osimertinib (preferred for EGFR exon 19 del, L858R), afatinib, erlotinib
- Colorectal cancer: Cetuximab, panitumumab (RAS wild-type only)
- Head and neck: Cetuximab

**ALK/ROS1 Inhibitors:**
- Lung cancer: Alectinib (preferred), brigatinib, lorlatinib, crizotinib, ceritinib

**HER2-Targeted Therapy:**
- Breast cancer: Trastuzumab, pertuzumab, T-DM1, T-DXd (trastuzumab deruxtecan), neratinib, tucatinib
- Gastric cancer: Trastuzumab, trastuzumab deruxtecan

**BRAF/MEK Inhibitors:**
- Melanoma: Dabrafenib/trametinib, vemurafenib/cobimetinib, encorafenib/binimetinib
- Lung cancer: Dabrafenib/trametinib (BRAF V600E)
- Colorectal cancer: Encorafenib/cetuximab (BRAF V600E)

**VEGF Inhibitors:**
- Bevacizumab: Colorectal, lung, ovarian, glioblastoma, renal
- TKIs: Sunitinib, pazopanib, axitinib, cabozantinib (renal, thyroid, HCC)

**CDK4/6 Inhibitors:**
- Breast cancer (HR+/HER2-): Palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib + endocrine therapy

**PARP Inhibitors:**
- Ovarian cancer (BRCA-mutated, HRD+): Olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib
- Breast cancer (BRCA-mutated): Olaparib, talazoparib
- Prostate cancer (BRCA/HRR-mutated): Olaparib, rucaparib

**KRAS G12C Inhibitors:**
- Lung cancer: Sotorasib, adagrasib (KRAS G12C-mutated NSCLC)

**BTK Inhibitors:**
- CLL, mantle cell lymphoma: Ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib

7) Oncologic emergencies and acute management

**Febrile Neutropenia:**
- Definition: ANC <500 or <1000 with expected decline, temperature ≥38.3°C or ≥38°C for >1 hour
- Immediate management: Blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics (cefepime, piperacillin/tazobactam, carbapenem)
- Risk assessment: High-risk (prolonged neutropenia, hemodynamic instability) → hospitalization
- G-CSF: Consider for primary prophylaxis (high-risk regimens), secondary prophylaxis (prior febrile neutropenia)

**Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS):**
- Risk: High tumor burden, rapidly proliferating tumors (Burkitt lymphoma, ALL, high-grade NHL)
- Labs: Hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, hypocalcemia, elevated LDH, renal insufficiency
- Prevention: IV hydration, allopurinol (or rasburicase for high-risk), monitor electrolytes
- Management: Aggressive hydration, rasburicase, treat electrolyte abnormalities, consider dialysis

**Spinal Cord Compression:**
- Presentation: Back pain, weakness, sensory loss, bowel/bladder dysfunction
- Imaging: Urgent MRI spine (whole spine to identify all compression sites)
- Management: High-dose dexamethasone (10-100 mg IV), urgent radiation therapy or neurosurgical decompression
- Prognosis: Early diagnosis and treatment critical for neurologic recovery

**Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome:**
- Presentation: Facial/upper extremity edema, dyspnea, cough, headache, dilated chest veins
- Imaging: CT chest with contrast
- Management: Elevate head of bed, dexamethasone, urgent radiation therapy or chemotherapy (depending on histology)
- Stenting: Consider for refractory cases or life-threatening symptoms

**Hypercalcemia of Malignancy:**
- Symptoms: Confusion, lethargy, nausea, constipation, polyuria
- Labs: Corrected calcium, PTHrP, vitamin D levels
- Management: IV hydration, bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid), calcitonin (rapid onset), denosumab (bisphosphonate-refractory)

**Malignant Pleural/Pericardial Effusions:**
- Pleural: Thoracentesis (diagnostic and therapeutic), pleurodesis (talc, doxycycline), indwelling catheter
- Pericardial: Pericardiocentesis, pericardial window for recurrent effusions
- Monitor: Signs of tamponade (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds)

**Brain Metastases:**
- Imaging: MRI brain with contrast (more sensitive than CT)
- Management: Dexamethasone (reduce edema), whole brain radiation therapy, stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS), neurosurgical resection
- Systemic therapy: Consider if CNS-penetrant agents available (osimertinib, alectinib, tucatinib)

8) Supportive care and symptom management

**Nausea and Vomiting:**
- Acute: 5-HT3 antagonists (ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron)
- Delayed: NK1 antagonists (aprepitant, rolapitant) + dexamethasone + 5-HT3 antagonist
- Breakthrough: Metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, olanzapine, lorazepam
- Anticipatory: Benzodiazepines, behavioral therapy

**Pain Management:**
- WHO analgesic ladder: Non-opioids (acetaminophen, NSAIDs) → weak opioids (tramadol) → strong opioids (morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl)
- Breakthrough pain: Short-acting opioids (immediate-release morphine, fentanyl buccal)
- Neuropathic pain: Gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, tricyclic antidepressants
- Interventional: Radiation therapy for bone metastases, nerve blocks, palliative surgery

**Fatigue:**
- Evaluation: Rule out anemia, hypothyroidism, depression, poor nutrition
- Management: Conserve energy, light exercise (as tolerated), psychosocial support
- Medications: Limited evidence; consider methylphenidate, modafinil for severe cases

**Anemia:**
- Transfusion: For symptomatic anemia or Hgb <7-8 g/dL (higher threshold for cardiac disease)
- ESAs: Limited role, risk of thrombosis and tumor progression; use cautiously if at all

**Neutropenia:**
- G-CSF: Primary prophylaxis (high-risk regimens), secondary prophylaxis (prior febrile neutropenia)
- Dose delays/reductions: For prolonged or severe neutropenia

**Diarrhea:**
- Chemotherapy-induced: Loperamide, hydration, electrolyte replacement
- Immunotherapy-induced colitis: Rule out infection, steroids for grade 2+, infliximab for refractory

**Mucositis:**
- Prevention: Good oral hygiene, cryotherapy (for short-infusion chemotherapy)
- Treatment: Topical anesthetics (viscous lidocaine, "magic mouthwash"), systemic pain control

**Thrombosis:**
- Cancer-associated thrombosis: LMWH (enoxaparin, dalteparin) preferred over warfarin
- DOACs: Emerging data (rivaroxaban, apixaban), avoid in GI/GU cancers due to bleeding risk

9) Clinical trials and research participation

**Types of Clinical Trials:**
- Phase I: Dose-finding, safety, pharmacokinetics (first-in-human studies)
- Phase II: Preliminary efficacy, further safety assessment
- Phase III: Randomized controlled trials comparing new therapy to standard of care
- Phase IV: Post-marketing surveillance, long-term safety and efficacy

**Trial Enrollment Considerations:**
- Eligibility: Inclusion/exclusion criteria (performance status, organ function, prior therapies)
- Informed Consent: Detailed discussion of risks, benefits, alternatives, randomization
- Patient Preferences: Willingness to participate, logistical considerations (travel, frequent visits)
- Insurance Coverage: Routine care costs typically covered, investigational drug provided by sponsor

**ClinicalTrials.gov:**
- Search tool for available trials by cancer type, location, eligibility criteria
- Encourage patients to discuss trial options with their oncology team

10) Palliative care and end-of-life discussions

**Goals of Care Discussions:**
- Early integration: Initiate conversations at diagnosis of metastatic/incurable cancer
- Explore values: What is most important to the patient? Quality vs. quantity of life?
- Treatment preferences: Aggressive interventions vs. comfort-focused care
- Advance directives: Healthcare proxy, living will, POLST/MOLST forms

**Palliative Care Consultation:**
- Indications: Symptom management, goals of care discussions, prognostic uncertainty, caregiver distress
- Team approach: Palliative care physicians, nurses, social workers, chaplains
- Not end-of-life care: Can be concurrent with active cancer treatment

**Hospice Care:**
- Eligibility: Prognosis <6 months, focus on comfort rather than curative treatment
- Services: Home hospice, inpatient hospice, respite care, bereavement support
- Medicare/Insurance: Typically covered; patient forgoes curative-intent treatments

**Advance Care Planning:**
- Healthcare proxy: Designated decision-maker if patient unable to make decisions
- DNR/DNI: Do not resuscitate, do not intubate orders
- POLST/MOLST: Provider orders for life-sustaining treatment (portable medical orders)

11) Cancer survivorship and long-term follow-up

**Surveillance:**
- Cancer-specific guidelines: Imaging, labs, physical exams based on cancer type and stage
- Recurrence risk: Highest in first 2-5 years, varies by cancer type
- Secondary cancers: Increased risk in some cancer survivors (e.g., lymphoma after Hodgkin's)

**Late Effects of Treatment:**
- Cardiotoxicity: Anthracyclines, trastuzumab (monitor with echocardiography)
- Neuropathy: Platinum agents, taxanes (may be chronic)
- Infertility: Alkylating agents, pelvic radiation (discuss fertility preservation pre-treatment)
- Cognitive impairment: "Chemo brain" (supportive management, cognitive rehabilitation)
- Secondary malignancies: Increased risk with radiation, alkylating agents

**Survivorship Care Plans:**
- Treatment summary: Diagnoses, treatments received, side effects experienced
- Follow-up recommendations: Surveillance schedule, specialist referrals
- Health promotion: Diet, exercise, smoking cessation, cancer screening
- Psychosocial support: Support groups, counseling, financial resources

12) Multidisciplinary cancer care

**Tumor Board / MDT Meetings:**
- Participants: Medical oncology, surgical oncology, radiation oncology, pathology, radiology
- Purpose: Discuss complex cases, treatment planning, ensure guideline adherence
- Frequency: Weekly or biweekly, case-by-case as needed

**Care Coordination:**
- Nurse navigators: Guide patients through cancer care continuum, coordinate appointments
- Social work: Financial assistance, transportation, psychosocial support
- Nutritionists: Dietary counseling, manage treatment-related nutrition issues
- Physical/occupational therapy: Rehabilitation, lymphedema management, functional restoration

**Genetic Counseling:**
- Indications: Family history of cancer, young age at diagnosis, specific cancer types (ovarian, pancreatic, early-onset breast)
- Testing: BRCA1/2, Lynch syndrome genes, other hereditary cancer syndromes
- Implications: Risk-reducing strategies, cascade testing for family members, treatment selection (PARP inhibitors)

13) Documentation and quality metrics

**Essential Documentation:**
- Cancer diagnosis: Histology, stage (TNM), grade, molecular markers
- Treatment plan: Regimen, intent (curative vs. palliative), duration, expected side effects
- Informed consent: Risks, benefits, alternatives discussed
- Performance status: ECOG or Karnofsky score at each visit
- Toxicity assessment: Grade adverse events per CTCAE (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events)

**Quality Metrics (QOPI):**
- Appropriate chemotherapy selection: Guideline-concordant regimens
- Toxicity management: Antiemetic prophylaxis, G-CSF for high-risk regimens
- Survivorship care: Provision of treatment summaries and care plans
- Palliative care: Early integration for metastatic cancer patients

14) Professional development and evidence-based updates

**Continuing Medical Education:**
- ASCO Annual Meeting: Largest oncology conference, latest trial results and practice-changing data
- ESMO Congress: European oncology conference, international perspective
- Disease-specific conferences: ASH (hematology), SABCS (breast), WCLC (lung)

**Landmark Trials:**
- Immunotherapy: CheckMate, KEYNOTE, IMpower trials
- Targeted therapy: FLAURA (osimertinib), ALEX (alectinib), SOLO (olaparib)
- Chemotherapy: MOSAIC (FOLFOX adjuvant), FOLFIRINOX trials

**Journal Review:**
- High-impact journals: NEJM, JCO, Lancet Oncology, JAMA Oncology, Nature Medicine
- Subsp ecialty journals: Blood, Annals of Oncology, European Journal of Cancer

---

**Summary:**
This oncologist playbook emphasizes evidence-based cancer care, adherence to NCCN/ASCO/ESMO guidelines, patient safety, and comprehensive management of cancer from diagnosis through survivorship or end-of-life care. Success is defined by accurate cancer diagnosis and staging, appropriate use of systemic therapies (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy), management of treatment-related toxicities, multidisciplinary care coordination, and patient-centered care focused on quality of life and informed decision-making.
