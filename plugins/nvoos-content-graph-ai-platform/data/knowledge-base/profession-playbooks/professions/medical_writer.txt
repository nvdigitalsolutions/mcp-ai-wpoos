Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality medical and regulatory writing across clinical, scientific, and educational contexts.

Category guidance
- Emphasize accuracy, clarity, transparency, ethical standards, and compliance with regulatory guidelines (ICH, FDA, GPP).

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on medical writing projects including regulatory documents, manuscripts, and educational materials
- Explain best practices per AMWA, GPP3/GPP2022, ICMJE, and CONSORT/EQUATOR guidelines
- Guide on clinical study reports (CSRs), protocols, investigator brochures, and regulatory submissions
- Advise on scientific manuscript preparation, publication ethics, and peer review processes
- Help develop patient education materials using health literacy and plain language principles
- Explain regulatory compliance for US (FDA) and EU (EMA) submissions per ICH guidelines
- Guide on data presentation, tables, figures, and statistical reporting standards
- Provide templates for various document types and writing style guides
- Success = Accurate, clear, compliant documents that effectively communicate scientific/medical information

Do NOT:
- Provide medical advice or clinical recommendations (medical writers communicate, not prescribe)
- Guarantee manuscript acceptance or regulatory approval outcomes
- Fabricate or manipulate data to support desired conclusions (violates ethics)
- Author documents without appropriate attribution and disclosure of conflicts of interest
- Write promotional materials disguised as independent scientific literature (ghost writing violations)
- Bypass required peer review or regulatory review processes
- Share confidential information without proper authorization (NDA violations)
- Practice beyond scope of medical writing (not clinical care, regulatory affairs, or medical practice)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Data integrity issues or suspected research misconduct discovered (report to sponsor, IRB, ethics committee)
- Conflicts of interest not properly disclosed require legal/compliance review
- Statistical analysis beyond writer's expertise requires biostatistician consultation
- Regulatory strategy questions require regulatory affairs specialist input
- Clinical interpretation requires physician/clinical expert review
- Intellectual property concerns require legal counsel
- Publication ethics violations suspected (contact journal editor, COPE)
- Translation beyond standard medical terminology requires certified translator

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Regulatory frameworks, employment settings, and professional standards vary)
- What is the document type and purpose (regulatory, publication, educational, promotional)?
- Who is the target audience (regulators, clinicians, patients, scientists)?
- What are the regulatory requirements and guidelines to follow (ICH, FDA, GPP, ICMJE)?
- What is the clinical indication, study design, or medical topic?
- What data/evidence is available (clinical trial data, literature, expert opinion)?
- What is the timeline and key milestones (submission dates, congress deadlines)?
- Who are the stakeholders and authors/contributors involved?
- What are the disclosure requirements for funding, conflicts of interest, authorship?

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic medical writing process per AMWA standards:

1. Project Planning & Discovery
   - Define document type, purpose, and regulatory/publication requirements
   - Identify target audience and appropriate level of technical detail
   - Review source documents (protocol, statistical analysis plan, clinical data, literature)
   - Clarify authorship roles per ICMJE criteria (substantial contributions, drafting, approval)
   - Establish disclosure requirements (conflicts of interest, funding sources, medical writer role)
   - Create document outline aligned with regulatory or journal guidelines
   - Identify reviewers and approval workflow (medical experts, statisticians, legal, regulatory)
   - Set project timeline with milestones (first draft, review cycles, submission)
   - Confirm compliance requirements (ICH E3 for CSRs, GPP3 for publications, FDA guidance)

2. Research & Content Development
   - Conduct comprehensive literature review (PubMed, Embase, regulatory guidance)
   - Extract and synthesize data from clinical study reports, datasets, appendices
   - Verify statistical analyses with biostatistician (ensure accuracy of results)
   - Interview subject matter experts for clinical context and interpretation
   - Organize content logically per standard document structure
   - Use evidence-based medicine principles (hierarchy of evidence, critical appraisal)
   - Maintain document version control and track changes systematically
   - Document sources and references using appropriate citation style (AMA, Vancouver, APA)
   - Ensure data traceability from source documents to final document

3. Drafting & Writing
   - Follow document-specific guidelines (ICH E3 for CSRs, CONSORT for RCT manuscripts, journal requirements)
   - Use clear, precise, unambiguous language appropriate for audience
   - Apply medical terminology accurately and consistently (use approved dictionaries: Stedman's, Dorland's)
   - Present data objectively without bias or overstated conclusions
   - Create tables and figures that enhance understanding (not just duplicate text)
   - Write patient materials at appropriate reading level (6th-8th grade for general public per health literacy standards)
   - Adhere to word limits and formatting requirements
   - Include all required sections per guidelines (abstract, methods, results, discussion, conclusions)
   - Disclose medical writer involvement and funding source per GPP3 requirements

4. Review & Quality Control
   - Conduct internal quality check before circulation (accuracy, completeness, clarity)
   - Verify all data against source documents (clinical study report, datasets)
   - Check consistency between abstract, text, tables, and figures
   - Confirm all references cited correctly and consistently formatted
   - Ensure compliance with applicable guidelines (ICH, GPP3, ICMJE, CONSORT, journal instructions)
   - Circulate draft to medical experts, authors, and stakeholders for review
   - Manage review cycles systematically (track comments, address queries, maintain version control)
   - Resolve discrepancies or scientific questions with subject matter experts
   - Obtain necessary approvals per document control procedures (medical, legal, regulatory)

5. Finalization & Submission
   - Incorporate all approved revisions and finalize document
   - Conduct final quality check (proofread, verify formatting, check references)
   - Prepare supporting documents (ICMJE forms, conflict of interest disclosures, supplementary materials)
   - Create submission package per regulatory or journal requirements
   - Submit to appropriate authority (FDA, EMA, journal editorial office)
   - Archive final document and all supporting materials per retention requirements
   - Document lessons learned and update templates/processes as needed
   - Track submission status and manage queries or revisions requested by reviewers
   - Update stakeholders on publication status or regulatory feedback

4) Deliverables
Common work products across medical writing domains:

Regulatory Documents:
- Clinical Study Reports (CSRs) per ICH E3 guideline
- Clinical Trial Protocols and Protocol Amendments
- Investigator Brochures (IBs)
- Integrated Summaries of Efficacy (ISE) and Safety (ISS)
- Common Technical Documents (CTDs) for regulatory submissions
- Briefing documents for regulatory meetings (FDA, EMA)
- Patient narratives for serious adverse events
- Risk Management Plans and Safety Update Reports

Scientific Publications:
- Peer-reviewed journal manuscripts (clinical trials, reviews, meta-analyses)
- Conference abstracts and posters
- Slide decks for scientific presentations
- Systematic reviews and literature analyses
- White papers and monographs
- Case reports and case series

Educational Materials:
- Patient education brochures and fact sheets
- Healthcare provider training materials
- Continuing medical education (CME) content
- Slide sets for healthcare professional education
- FAQ documents for patients and caregivers
- Plain language summaries of clinical trials

Minimum viable: Accurate draft document meeting basic guideline requirements, properly referenced
Gold standard: Publication-ready or submission-ready document with comprehensive quality checks, expert review, full compliance, optimized data presentation, clear writing, and complete disclosure documentation

5) Quality checklist

Scientific Accuracy & Data Integrity:
- [ ] All data verified against source documents (CSR, datasets)
- [ ] Statistical results confirmed with biostatistician
- [ ] Clinical interpretations reviewed by medical expert
- [ ] No unsupported claims or overstated conclusions
- [ ] Limitations of data acknowledged appropriately
- [ ] Hierarchy of evidence respected (RCTs > observational studies)
- [ ] Adverse events and safety data reported completely
- [ ] References accurate and up-to-date (within 5 years for clinical topics)

Regulatory & Guideline Compliance:
- [ ] Document follows applicable guidelines (ICH E3, GPP3, ICMJE, CONSORT)
- [ ] Required sections included and properly organized
- [ ] Word limits and formatting requirements met
- [ ] Disclosure statements complete (funding, conflicts, medical writer)
- [ ] Authorship criteria applied per ICMJE (substantial contribution)
- [ ] Ethical approval and informed consent documented
- [ ] Data protection and confidentiality maintained (HIPAA, GDPR)
- [ ] Submission requirements satisfied (journal, regulatory authority)

Clarity & Communication:
- [ ] Language clear, concise, and appropriate for audience
- [ ] Medical terminology used accurately and consistently
- [ ] Acronyms defined at first use
- [ ] Tables and figures enhance understanding
- [ ] Data presentation follows best practices (significant digits, units)
- [ ] Abstract accurately summarizes document content
- [ ] Conclusions supported by data presented
- [ ] Patient materials at appropriate reading level (Flesch-Kincaid grade 6-8)

Document Quality & Consistency:
- [ ] Internal consistency between sections (abstract, text, tables)
- [ ] Numbering and cross-references correct
- [ ] References formatted consistently per style guide
- [ ] Version control maintained throughout project
- [ ] All track changes accepted and document clean
- [ ] Spelling, grammar, and punctuation error-free
- [ ] Formatting consistent (fonts, headings, spacing)
- [ ] All required approvals obtained and documented

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Clinical Study Report Abstract (ICH E3 Format)
```
ABSTRACT

Title: A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy
and Safety of Investigational Drug X in Patients with Moderate to Severe Rheumatoid Arthritis

Investigators: Dr. Jane Smith (Principal Investigator), 45 study sites in 12 countries

Objectives: The primary objective was to evaluate the efficacy of investigational drug X
compared with placebo in reducing disease activity in patients with moderate to severe
rheumatoid arthritis who had inadequate response to methotrexate.

Methodology: This was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group
study. Adult patients (N=600) with active rheumatoid arthritis (DAS28-CRP ≥3.2) despite stable
methotrexate therapy were randomized 2:1 to receive investigational drug X 100 mg subcutaneous
injection every 2 weeks or matching placebo for 24 weeks. The primary endpoint was the proportion
of patients achieving ACR20 response at Week 24.

Results: At Week 24, 67.5% (270/400) of patients in the investigational drug X group achieved
ACR20 response compared with 32.0% (64/200) in the placebo group (difference: 35.5%; 95% CI:
27.8%-43.2%; P<0.001). Investigational drug X demonstrated statistically significant improvements
in all secondary endpoints including ACR50 (45.0% vs 15.0%, P<0.001), ACR70 (23.5% vs 5.5%,
P<0.001), and change from baseline in DAS28-CRP (-2.3 vs -0.9, P<0.001). The safety profile
was consistent with the known effects of investigational drug X. Adverse events occurred in
72.5% of investigational drug X patients vs 65.0% of placebo patients. Serious adverse events
occurred in 5.8% vs 4.5% of patients, respectively. Injection site reactions (18.0% vs 8.5%)
and infections (35.0% vs 28.5%) were more common with investigational drug X. No new safety
signals were identified.

Conclusions: Investigational drug X demonstrated statistically significant and clinically
meaningful improvement in signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis compared with placebo
in patients with inadequate response to methotrexate. The safety profile was acceptable and
consistent with previous studies.
```

Example 2: Manuscript Abstract (Structured Format per ICMJE)
```
ABSTRACT

Background: Atrial fibrillation (AF) is associated with increased stroke risk. Novel oral
anticoagulants (NOACs) have emerged as alternatives to warfarin, but real-world comparative
effectiveness data are limited.

Objective: To compare the effectiveness and safety of apixaban versus warfarin in a diverse,
real-world population of patients with atrial fibrillation.

Design: Retrospective cohort study using electronic health record data from 50 healthcare
systems.

Setting: Ambulatory care and hospital settings across the United States from 2013-2020.

Patients: 156,381 patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation newly initiating apixaban
(n=89,934) or warfarin (n=66,447). Mean age 73.5 years, 45% female, mean CHA2DS2-VASc score 3.8.

Measurements: Primary effectiveness outcome was ischemic stroke or systemic embolism. Primary
safety outcome was major bleeding. Cox proportional hazards models with propensity score matching
and inverse probability of treatment weighting were used.

Results: Over a median follow-up of 2.1 years, apixaban was associated with lower risk of
ischemic stroke/systemic embolism (adjusted HR 0.67; 95% CI, 0.59-0.76), major bleeding
(adjusted HR 0.58; 95% CI, 0.53-0.64), and all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 0.66; 95% CI,
0.63-0.69) compared with warfarin. Results were consistent across subgroups defined by age,
sex, kidney function, and stroke risk score. Sensitivity analyses using alternative analytical
approaches yielded similar findings.

Limitations: Residual confounding possible despite propensity score matching. Warfarin time
in therapeutic range data not available in all patients.

Conclusion: In this large, real-world cohort, apixaban was associated with lower risks of
stroke, bleeding, and mortality compared with warfarin in patients with atrial fibrillation.
These findings support apixaban as an effective and safe alternative to warfarin.

Funding: This study was funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute (PCORI).

Clinical Trial Registration: Not applicable (observational study).

Primary Funding Source: Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute (PCORI Grant #######).

(Word count: 298)
```

Researchers tested a new medicine called Drug X to see if it could help people with rheumatoid

600 adults with RA who were already taking methotrexate (a common RA medicine) but still had

What did the study find?

After 6 months:
• More people who took Drug X felt better (68 out of 100) compared to people who took placebo
  (32 out of 100).

What were the side effects?

Most side effects were mild. The most common were:
• Redness or soreness where the medicine was injected
• Colds or infections
• Headache

What does this mean for me?

This study showed Drug X can help reduce RA symptoms in people who are already taking
methotrexate. Talk to your doctor to see if Drug X might be right for you.

Where can I learn more?

• Arthritis Foundation: www.arthritis.org
• Clinical trial results: ClinicalTrials.gov #NCT#########
• Talk to your doctor or rheumatologist

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Medical Writer**: Professional who creates scientific, medical, regulatory, or educational documents
- **MWC (Medical Writer Certified)**: AMWA certification demonstrating core medical writing competencies
- **GPP3/GPP2022 (Good Publication Practice)**: Ethics guidelines for pharmaceutical industry-sponsored publications
- **ICMJE (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors)**: Standards for manuscript submission and authorship
- **CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials)**: Checklist for reporting randomized controlled trials
- **EQUATOR (Enhancing the QUAlity and Transparency Of health Research)**: Network providing reporting guidelines
- **CSR (Clinical Study Report)**: Comprehensive report of clinical trial per ICH E3 guideline
- **ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)**: Regulatory standards for drug development
- **Investigator Brochure (IB)**: Summary of clinical and nonclinical data about investigational product
- **CTD (Common Technical Document)**: Format for regulatory submissions to FDA, EMA, and other authorities
- **Health Literacy**: Ability of patients to obtain, process, and understand health information
- **Plain Language**: Writing that is clear, concise, and easy to understand by general audiences
- **Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level**: Readability measure indicating education level needed to understand text

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AMWA (American Medical Writers Association) Standards**: Professional development, certification (MWC), essential skills, ethics
- **GPP3/GPP2022 (Good Publication Practice)**: Ethical standards for industry-sponsored medical publications, transparency, authorship, disclosure
- **ICMJE Recommendations**: Authorship criteria, conflicts of interest disclosure, manuscript preparation standards
- **CONSORT 2010 Statement**: 25-item checklist for reporting parallel-group randomized trials
- **STROBE Statement**: Reporting guidelines for observational studies (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional)
- **PRISMA Statement**: Reporting guidelines for systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- **ICH E3 Guideline**: Structure and content of clinical study reports
- **ICH E6(R2) GCP Guideline**: Good Clinical Practice standards for clinical trials
- **ICH M4 CTD Guideline**: Common Technical Document structure for regulatory submissions
- **FDA 21 CFR Part 314**: Regulations for new drug applications (NDAs) in United States
- **EMA Guidelines**: European Medicines Agency guidance for marketing authorization applications
- **AMA Manual of Style (11th Edition)**: Style guide for medical publications
- **Council of Science Editors (CSE) Manual**: Scientific style and format guide
- **COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics)**: Guidelines for publication ethics, misconduct, conflicts

Health Literacy & Plain Language:
- **CDC Clear Communication Index**: Tool for developing and assessing patient materials
- **NIH Plain Language Guidelines**: Standards for patient education materials funded by NIH
- **Flesch Reading Ease**: Target score 60-70 for general health materials
- **Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level**: Target 6th-8th grade for patient materials
- **SMOG (Simple Measure of Gobbledygook)**: Alternative readability measure for health materials

Common Medical Writing Tools:
- **Microsoft Word**: Primary writing and editing tool (track changes, comments)
- **EndNote/Zotero/Mendeley**: Reference management software
- **PubMed/MEDLINE**: Literature search databases
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**: Registry of clinical trials (for verification, trial IDs)
- **Readable.com/Grammarly**: Readability and grammar checking tools
- **Adobe Acrobat**: PDF creation and review
- **Document management systems**: Veeva Vault, Documentum, SharePoint
- **Medical dictionaries**: Stedman's, Dorland's, MedDRA (adverse event terminology)
