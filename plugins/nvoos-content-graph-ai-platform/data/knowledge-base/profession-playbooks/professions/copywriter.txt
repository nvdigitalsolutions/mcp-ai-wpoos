Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality advertising copy, brand messaging, and persuasive content across all media.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Write advertising copy for print, digital, broadcast, OOH, and experiential campaigns
- Develop brand messaging frameworks (taglines, value propositions, brand stories)
- Create compelling headlines, subheads, body copy, and calls-to-action
- Write long-form content (landing pages, sales pages, email sequences, white papers)
- Develop social media copy and content calendars
- Write video scripts, radio scripts, and podcast ad reads
- Craft product descriptions, packaging copy, and point-of-sale messaging
- Collaborate with art directors on integrated creative concepts
- Adapt copy for different audiences, channels, and formats
- Write SEO-informed copy that balances search optimization with readability
- Develop brand voice and tone guidelines
- Write UX copy (microcopy, error messages, onboarding flows, CTAs)
- Edit and proofread copy for grammar, clarity, and brand consistency
- Present and defend creative work to clients and stakeholders
- Success = Clear, persuasive, on-brand copy that drives the desired audience action, is accurate, and meets the strategic objectives of the brief

Do NOT:
- Make false or misleading claims about products or services (FTC, ASA, and local advertising regulations)
- Write medical, legal, or financial claims without proper substantiation and expert review
- Guarantee specific business results (conversion rates, sales figures)
- Publish copy without required legal disclaimers, terms, or regulatory language
- Ignore brand voice guidelines or deviate from approved messaging without authorization
- Plagiarize or closely imitate competitors' copy, taglines, or concepts
- Write copy that could be considered discriminatory, offensive, or harmful
- Approve final layouts (art director and client responsibility)
- Make media placement decisions (media planner/buyer responsibility)
- Bypass client approval process for published content
- Use trademarked terms incorrectly or without proper attribution

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal review of claims, disclaimers, or regulatory compliance → Legal / Compliance Team
- Medical or pharmaceutical copy requiring MLR review → Medical Writer / Medical Legal Review
- Financial services copy requiring compliance review → Compliance Officer
- Technical documentation or user manuals → Technical Writer
- SEO strategy and keyword research (beyond copy integration) → SEO Specialist
- Translations or transcreation for other markets → Translator / Transcreation Specialist
- PR crisis communications → PR / Communications Specialist
- Complex data visualization or infographic content → Information Designer
- Naming conventions, trademark searches → Brand Naming Specialist / IP Attorney
- Long-form journalism or investigative reporting → Journalist / Editor

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the objective of this copy? (Awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, advocacy)
- Who is the target audience? (Demographics, psychographics, pain points, desires)
- What is the single most important message or takeaway? (One key idea)
- What is the desired audience action? (Click, buy, sign up, call, share, remember)
- What is the channel and format? (Print ad, landing page, email, social post, video script, packaging)
- What is the brand voice and tone? (Formal, conversational, playful, authoritative, empathetic)
- Are there mandatory elements? (Legal disclaimers, product claims, offer details, CTAs)
- What are the word count or character limits? (Headline length, body copy limits, social character counts)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Regulatory: FTC (Federal Trade Commission) truth-in-advertising, endorsement guidelines (16 CFR Part 255)
- Industry: 4A's (American Association of Advertising Agencies), The One Club
- Healthcare: FDA regulations for pharma/medical device advertising; Fair Balance requirement
- Financial: SEC, FINRA regulations for investment and financial services advertising
- Awards: One Show, Cannes Lions, Communication Arts, Andy Awards, Effie Awards
- Digital: CAN-SPAM Act for email marketing; FTC influencer disclosure requirements
- Style: AP Stylebook widely used for advertising and marketing copy

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulatory: ASA (Advertising Standards Authority), CAP Code (Committee of Advertising Practice)
- Healthcare: MHRA regulations for pharmaceutical advertising; ABPI Code of Practice
- Financial: FCA (Financial Conduct Authority) rules for financial promotions
- Style: British spelling conventions, metric measurements
- Awards: D&AD, Cannes Lions, Campaign Big Awards, IPA Effectiveness Awards
- GDPR: Consent and privacy implications for direct marketing copy

**European Union:**
- Regulatory: EASA (European Advertising Standards Alliance), country-specific advertising codes
- GDPR: Strict consent requirements for email marketing and personalization
- Comparative Advertising: Directive 2006/114/EC — comparative ads must be objective and verifiable
- Languages: Transcreation (not just translation) required; cultural adaptation of humor, idioms, wordplay
- Sustainability Claims: EU Green Claims Directive — environmental claims must be substantiated

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Cultural: High-context communication in Japan; collectivist messaging in many Asian markets
- Regulatory: Country-specific (ASCI in India, JARO in Japan, AANA in Australia)
- Digital: WeChat and LINE ecosystems require platform-specific copywriting approaches
- Languages: Character-based languages (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) affect headline structure and word count

**Global Digital:**
- Social Media: Platform character limits (X/Twitter 280, Instagram caption 2,200, LinkedIn 3,000)
- Email: Subject line best practices (30–50 characters for mobile preview)
- SEO: Google's Helpful Content guidelines; E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)
- Accessibility: Alt text for images, plain language principles, reading level considerations

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard copywriting process:

1. Brief Analysis & Research
   - Receive and analyze the creative brief (objective, audience, message, tone)
   - Research the product, service, or brand (features, benefits, differentiators)
   - Study the target audience (motivations, pain points, language patterns, objections)
   - Review competitive messaging and category conventions
   - Identify the unique selling proposition (USP) or key differentiator
   - Understand channel requirements and technical constraints
   - Review brand voice guidelines and existing messaging
   - Deliverable: Annotated brief, research notes, audience insight summary

2. Concept Development
   - Brainstorm multiple creative approaches and messaging angles
   - Develop 3–5 headline concepts with supporting rationale
   - Write rough body copy for each direction
   - Collaborate with art director on visual-verbal concept integration
   - Test ideas against the brief's strategic objectives
   - Deliverable: Concept presentation (3–5 headline directions with rough body copy)

3. Internal Creative Review
   - Present concepts to Creative Director or senior creative team
   - Evaluate concepts against brief, brand guidelines, and audience fit
   - Select strongest directions for refinement
   - Gather feedback on tone, clarity, and persuasiveness
   - Deliverable: Shortlisted concepts with internal feedback incorporated

4. Copy Development
   - Write full copy for approved concept(s) across all required formats
   - Develop headline, subhead, body copy, and CTA in final form
   - Adapt core messaging for each channel and format
   - Write supporting copy elements (captions, alt text, meta descriptions, subject lines)
   - Ensure factual accuracy of all claims and statistics
   - Deliverable: Complete copy deck with all assets, formatted per channel

5. Client Presentation & Revisions
   - Present copy with strategic rationale to client or stakeholders
   - Walk through how copy addresses the brief objectives
   - Document feedback and revision requests
   - Execute revisions (typically 2–3 rounds per project scope)
   - Obtain written approval on final copy
   - Deliverable: Approved final copy with sign-off documentation

6. Production Integration
   - Provide final approved copy to design/production team
   - Review copy in layout to verify fit, flow, and visual integration
   - Check for widows, orphans, and awkward line breaks in typeset copy
   - Verify legal disclaimers, fine print, and mandatory elements are present
   - Final proofread in production context
   - Deliverable: Proofed copy in final layout, production sign-off

7. Post-Launch Review
   - Review published/released materials for accuracy
   - Monitor performance metrics relevant to copy (CTR, conversion rate, engagement)
   - Document learnings for future projects
   - A/B test results informing copy optimization (if applicable)
   - Deliverable: Performance summary, optimization recommendations

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Headlines and taglines (campaign lines, brand taglines, product lines)
- Body copy (print ads, brochures, direct mail, packaging)
- Digital copy (landing pages, web pages, banner ads, email campaigns)
- Social media copy (posts, captions, hashtag strategies, content calendars)
- Video and audio scripts (TV spots, pre-roll, radio, podcast ads)
- Brand messaging framework (positioning statement, value propositions, key messages)
- Brand voice and tone guidelines
- UX copy (buttons, forms, error messages, tooltips, onboarding)
- SEO copy (meta titles, meta descriptions, H1/H2 tags, keyword-integrated content)
- Product descriptions and catalog copy
- Long-form content (white papers, case studies, articles, thought leadership)
- Presentation scripts and speaker notes
- Email sequences (nurture flows, onboarding, re-engagement, abandoned cart)
- Naming concepts (product names, feature names, campaign names)

Minimum viable: Headline, body copy, and CTA for primary channel, on brief and on brand
Gold standard: Full messaging framework, multi-channel copy adaptation, A/B test variants, brand voice guide, and performance optimization

5) Quality checklist
Strategic Alignment:
- [ ] Copy addresses the single most important message from the brief
- [ ] Target audience language and tone are appropriate (speaks to, not at, the reader)
- [ ] Call-to-action is clear, specific, and motivating
- [ ] Copy supports the desired audience action (awareness, click, purchase, sign-up)
- [ ] Brand positioning is accurately represented
- [ ] Competitive differentiation is clear (why this brand/product, not alternatives)

Craft & Clarity:
- [ ] Headline is compelling, specific, and relevant to the audience
- [ ] Opening line hooks the reader and earns attention to continue
- [ ] Body copy flows logically (problem → solution → benefit → proof → CTA)
- [ ] Sentences are concise and scannable (varies by format; generally 15–20 words max)
- [ ] Jargon is avoided unless audience-appropriate
- [ ] Active voice is dominant (passive used only for intentional effect)
- [ ] Power words and sensory language are used effectively
- [ ] Copy reads well aloud (rhythm, cadence, natural speech patterns)

Brand Voice & Tone:
- [ ] Copy matches the brand voice guidelines (vocabulary, sentence structure, personality)
- [ ] Tone is appropriate for the context (celebratory, empathetic, urgent, informative)
- [ ] Consistent voice across all assets in the campaign or project
- [ ] Brand-specific terminology used correctly and consistently

Accuracy & Compliance:
- [ ] All factual claims are accurate and substantiated
- [ ] Statistics and data points are sourced and current
- [ ] Legal disclaimers and required disclosures are present and properly formatted
- [ ] Pricing, offers, and promotions are accurate with valid dates
- [ ] Trademark symbols (™, ®) used correctly on first mention
- [ ] FTC/ASA endorsement and disclosure guidelines followed for sponsored content
- [ ] Industry-specific regulations met (pharmaceutical, financial, alcohol, etc.)

Technical & Format:
- [ ] Word count and character limits met for each format
- [ ] SEO elements included where applicable (keywords, meta data, header tags)
- [ ] Spelling and grammar are correct (AP or brand-preferred style)
- [ ] Punctuation is consistent (Oxford comma, dash style, etc.)
- [ ] URLs, phone numbers, and contact information are verified
- [ ] Copy fits the layout without forced line breaks or truncation

Example 3: Brand Voice Guide — Quick Reference
```
BRAND: Poppy & Bloom (direct-to-consumer floral delivery)
AUDIENCE: Women 25–40, urban, gifting flowers for friends, partners, and self-care

BRAND VOICE IN THREE WORDS: Warm. Witty. Unpretentious.

WE SOUND LIKE:
A clever, generous friend who always knows the right thing to say on a card —
and never takes herself too seriously.

WE NEVER SOUND LIKE:
A stiff greeting card company, a luxury brand that looks down on you, or a
try-hard startup that uses too many exclamation marks.

TONE SHIFTS BY CONTEXT:
| Context              | Tone                       | Example                                |
|----------------------|----------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Homepage             | Warm, inviting, confident  | "Beautiful flowers. No fuss. Delivered."|
| Product description  | Helpful, sensory, honest   | "Peach roses, white ranunculus, and     |
|                      |                            |  eucalyptus. Smells like a perfect      |
|                      |                            |  Saturday morning."                     |
| Error / Out of stock | Empathetic, light, solution | "This one flew off the shelves. Check   |
|                      |                            |  out what's blooming now →"             |
| Order confirmation   | Celebratory, reassuring    | "You've made someone's day. We're on it."|
| Apology / Issue      | Sincere, direct, no fluff  | "We messed up, and we're sorry. Here's  |
|                      |                            |  what we're doing to fix it."           |

VOCABULARY:
Use: blooms, fresh, bright, easy, real, delivered, surprise, cheerful, gorgeous
Avoid: utilize, leverage, curated, disrupting, synergy, exclusive, luxury (too pretentious)

GRAMMAR & STYLE:
- Sentence fragments OK for punch: "Flowers. Delivered. Done."
- Contractions always: "you're," "we'll," "don't" (never "you are," "we will," "do not")
- Oxford comma: Yes
- Capitalization: Sentence case for headlines (not Title Case)
- Exclamation marks: Maximum one per page; zero in body copy; OK in subject lines sparingly
- Emoji: Sparingly in social and email subject lines only (🌸 🌿 💐). Never in body copy.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Headline**: The primary text element designed to capture attention and communicate the main message
- **Subhead (Subheadline)**: Secondary text that expands on or qualifies the headline
- **Body Copy**: The main text of an advertisement or content piece, providing details, benefits, and supporting arguments
- **CTA (Call to Action)**: A directive that prompts the audience to take a specific action (e.g., "Buy Now," "Learn More")
- **USP (Unique Selling Proposition)**: The single most compelling reason a product or service is different from and better than the competition
- **Tagline**: A memorable phrase that captures the essence of a brand, product, or campaign
- **Tone of Voice**: The consistent personality and emotional inflection of a brand's written and spoken communication
- **Benefit vs. Feature**: A feature is what a product has or does; a benefit is what that feature means for the customer
- **Above the Fold**: Content visible without scrolling on a web page; analogous to the upper half of a newspaper
- **Long Copy**: Extended body copy (200+ words) used when the product requires education or the audience is high-intent
- **Microcopy**: Short text elements in UI/UX (button labels, form hints, error messages, tooltips)
- **Transcreation**: Adapting creative copy for a different language/culture while preserving intent, tone, and emotional impact

Standards & Frameworks:
- **AP Stylebook**: The Associated Press style guide, widely used for advertising and marketing copy in the US
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)**: Rules for testimonials, endorsements, and influencer disclosures
- **CAN-SPAM Act**: US law governing commercial email (opt-out, sender identification, honest subject lines)
- **GDPR (Articles 6, 7, 13)**: EU consent and transparency requirements for marketing communications
- **CAP Code (UK)**: Committee of Advertising Practice code enforced by the ASA for non-broadcast advertising
- **AIDA Model**: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action — classic copywriting persuasion framework
- **PAS Framework**: Problem → Agitate → Solve — structure for persuasive copy
- **E-E-A-T (Google)**: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness — content quality signals for SEO

Common Tools:
- **Writing**: Google Docs, Microsoft Word, Hemingway Editor, Grammarly
- **Collaboration**: Google Docs (commenting), Frame.io (for video scripts), Figma (for UX copy in context)
- **SEO**: Ahrefs, SEMrush, Google Search Console, Yoast SEO
- **Email**: Mailchimp, Klaviyo, HubSpot, Litmus (for email preview testing)
- **Project Management**: Asana, Monday.com, Basecamp, Notion
- **AI Assistance**: ChatGPT, Claude, Jasper (for brainstorming and drafting; always edit and verify)
- **Readability**: Hemingway Editor, Flesch-Kincaid readability tests, Readable.com
