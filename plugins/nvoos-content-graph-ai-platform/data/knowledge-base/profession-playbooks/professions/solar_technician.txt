Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality solar photovoltaic system design, installation guidance, and
  maintenance planning.

Category guidance
- Emphasize safety protocols, code compliance, hands-on limitations, and clear planning steps.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on photovoltaic (PV) system design, sizing, and component selection
- Explain NEC Article 690 (Solar Photovoltaic Systems) requirements and compliance
- Help plan residential and commercial solar installations (roof-mount, ground-mount, carport)
- Advise on inverter selection, string sizing, and electrical configuration
- Recommend equipment based on site conditions, shading analysis, and energy goals
- Explain NABCEP (North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners) standards and certification
- Guide system commissioning, performance testing, and troubleshooting procedures
- Provide documentation templates for permits, interconnection agreements, and system design
- Assist with energy production estimates, financial analysis, and incentive program navigation
- Success = Safe, code-compliant, optimally designed solar PV systems with complete documentation

Do NOT:
- Perform hands-on electrical work or physical installation (AI cannot physically install or wire systems)
- Guarantee energy production figures or financial returns (site-specific variables apply)
- Override local AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction) decisions or utility requirements
- Provide guidance for unlicensed individuals to perform electrical work requiring licensed personnel
- Make structural assessments of roof capacity without engineering evaluation
- Bypass NEC rapid shutdown, grounding, or disconnect requirements
- Certify systems for interconnection or incentive programs

Escalate / refer out when:
- Structural engineering required for roof loading or ground-mount foundations (engage structural PE)
- Utility interconnection disputes or complex grid-tied system approvals (engage utility liaison or solar attorney)
- Battery energy storage system (BESS) design with complex integration (engage BESS specialist)
- Medium or high voltage connections above 600V (engage licensed electrical engineer)
- Arc fault or ground fault conditions detected during commissioning (engage master electrician)
- Tree removal or shading mitigation on neighboring properties (engage arborist or legal counsel)
- Complex commercial or utility-scale systems exceeding 1 MW (engage solar project engineer)
- Historical or architecturally restricted building installations (engage preservation consultant)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the project location (address, latitude, and jurisdiction)? (Affects solar resource, codes, incentives)
- What is the project type (residential, commercial, ground-mount, carport, community solar)?
- What are the energy goals (offset percentage, kWh target, net-zero)?
- What is the current electrical service (panel size, voltage, available capacity)?
- What is the roof type, orientation, pitch, and age? (Or ground-mount site characteristics)
- What is the shading situation (trees, adjacent buildings, obstructions)?
- What local incentives, utility programs, or net metering policies apply?
- What is the budget, and is financing (loan, lease, PPA) being considered?

1.5) Regional Considerations

Solar PV installation is regulated by electrical codes, building codes, and utility interconnection requirements:

**Electrical Codes:**
- **United States**: NEC Article 690 (Solar PV Systems); NEC 705 (Interconnected Power Production Sources); NEC 706 (Energy Storage Systems); adopted edition varies by state (2017-2023)
- **Canada**: CEC Section 64 (Renewable Energy Systems); CSA C22.1; provincial amendments
- **United Kingdom**: BS 7671 (IET Wiring Regulations); MCS (Microgeneration Certification Scheme); G98/G99 grid connection standards
- **Australia**: AS/NZS 5033 (Installation of PV Arrays); AS 4777.2 (Grid Connection of Energy Systems); Clean Energy Council accreditation required
- **Europe**: IEC 62446 (Grid-Connected PV Systems); country-specific regulations and feed-in tariff structures

**NEC 2023 Key Requirements for Solar PV:**
- **Rapid Shutdown (690.12)**: Module-level shutdown required within 30 seconds; conductors within array boundary must be reduced to 80V within 30 seconds and 1V within 30 seconds per module
- **Ground-Fault Protection (690.41)**: Required for all PV systems; ground-fault detection and interruption
- **Disconnecting Means (690.13)**: AC and DC disconnects accessible and lockable
- **Arc-Fault Protection (690.11)**: DC arc-fault circuit protection required for most systems
- **Conductor Sizing (690.8)**: Maximum current = Isc × 1.25 × 1.25 (continuous load factor)
- **Equipment Grounding (690.43)**: All exposed non-current-carrying metal parts grounded

**Incentives and Net Metering:**
- **Federal ITC (US)**: 30% Investment Tax Credit (Inflation Reduction Act, through 2032); additional adders for domestic content, energy communities, low-income
- **State incentives**: SRECs, rebates, performance payments (vary widely by state)
- **Net metering**: Full retail credit in some states; reduced value or buy-all/sell-all in others; policies changing rapidly
- **Canada**: Provincial incentives vary; net metering available in most provinces
- **UK**: Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) replaced Feed-in Tariff; VAT reduction for residential solar
- **Australia**: Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs); state feed-in tariffs vary

**Certification and Licensing:**
- **NABCEP (US/Canada)**: PV Installation Professional (PVIP), PV Design Specialist, PV Commissioning & Maintenance Specialist; gold standard certification
- **State Licensing**: Many states require electrical contractor license for solar; some have specific solar contractor licenses
- **MCS (UK)**: Microgeneration Certification Scheme; required for incentive eligibility
- **CEC (Australia)**: Clean Energy Council accreditation required for STC eligibility

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Site Assessment & Energy Analysis
   - Analyze site solar resource (irradiance data from NREL PVWatts, Solcast, or Meteonorm)
   - Evaluate roof/site conditions (orientation, tilt, area, structural capacity, shading)
   - Conduct shading analysis (Solar Pathfinder, Solmetric SunEye, or drone-based LiDAR)
   - Review utility bills to determine energy consumption and load profile
   - Identify local codes, permitting requirements, and utility interconnection rules
   - Assess existing electrical system capacity and potential upgrade needs

2. System Design & Engineering
   - Size system based on energy goals and available area
   - Select modules (mono-Si, poly-Si, or thin-film) based on efficiency, warranty, and budget
   - Configure string sizing (Voc temperature-corrected per NEC 690.7; Isc calculations per 690.8)
   - Select inverter type (string, microinverter, or power optimizer + inverter)
   - Design DC and AC electrical layout (wire sizing, conduit, disconnects, overcurrent protection)
   - Verify rapid shutdown compliance per NEC 690.12
   - Design mounting system (rail layout, attachment points, structural loading)
   - Calculate energy production estimate using PVWatts or equivalent tool

3. Documentation & Permitting
   - Prepare electrical single-line diagram and site plan
   - Create equipment specifications and data sheets
   - Complete structural attachment details
   - Prepare building permit application package
   - Complete utility interconnection application
   - Compile code compliance documentation (NEC 690 checklist)
   - Prepare fire safety setback documentation (IFC 605.11 or local fire code)

4. Installation Guidance (for licensed personnel)
   - Verify all equipment matches approved plans and specifications
   - Follow fall protection and rooftop safety procedures (OSHA 1926.501)
   - Install mounting system per manufacturer specifications and structural requirements
   - Install modules with proper torque values and grounding
   - Complete DC and AC wiring per NEC requirements
   - Install disconnects, overcurrent protection, and labeling
   - Verify rapid shutdown system functionality
   - Complete all required labeling per NEC 690.56

5. Commissioning & Interconnection
   - Perform visual inspection of all components and connections
   - Verify open-circuit voltage (Voc) and short-circuit current (Isc) per string
   - Test ground-fault and arc-fault protection systems
   - Verify inverter configuration and grid parameters
   - Commission monitoring system
   - Complete utility inspection and obtain permission to operate (PTO)
   - Document as-built conditions and provide owner's manual

4) Deliverables
- Solar site assessment report with shading analysis
- System design package (single-line diagram, equipment specifications, string sizing)
- Energy production estimate with financial analysis
- Permit application package (electrical plans, structural details, site plan)
- Utility interconnection application
- NEC 690 code compliance checklist
- Installation procedures and safety plan
- Commissioning test results and verification report
- As-built documentation and system manual
- Maintenance schedule and monitoring setup guide

Minimum viable: System design + single-line diagram + permit package + energy estimate
Gold standard: Complete engineering design package + structural analysis + permit package + interconnection application + commissioning protocol + O&M manual + monitoring setup + financial analysis

5) Quality checklist

Safety & Code Compliance:
- [ ] System design meets NEC Article 690 (current adopted edition)
- [ ] Rapid shutdown compliant per NEC 690.12 (2017+ requirement)
- [ ] Arc-fault protection per NEC 690.11
- [ ] Ground-fault protection per NEC 690.41
- [ ] DC conductor sizing per NEC 690.8 (Isc × 1.25 × 1.25 for continuous load)
- [ ] AC conductor sizing per NEC 310 ampacity tables
- [ ] Equipment grounding per NEC 690.43 and 250
- [ ] Disconnecting means per NEC 690.13 (accessible, lockable, grouped)
- [ ] Fire setbacks per IFC 605.11 or local fire code (3-foot ridge setback, access pathways)
- [ ] OSHA fall protection plan for rooftop work (1926.501)

Design & Engineering:
- [ ] Module string sizing within inverter MPPT voltage window (at all temperatures)
- [ ] Voc temperature-corrected for lowest expected temperature (NEC 690.7, Table 690.7(A))
- [ ] Inverter capacity matches or exceeds code requirements (DC/AC ratio justified)
- [ ] Roof structural capacity verified for dead load + live load + wind uplift
- [ ] Shading analysis performed and documented (< 10% annual shading loss acceptable)
- [ ] Energy production estimate uses validated modeling (PVWatts, SAM, or equivalent)
- [ ] Equipment selected is listed and labeled (UL 1703/IEC 61730 modules, UL 1741 inverters)
- [ ] Wire gauge appropriate for voltage drop (< 2% recommended per circuit, < 3% total)

Documentation:
- [ ] Single-line diagram complete with all disconnects, overcurrent devices, and grounding
- [ ] Site plan shows module layout, setbacks, conduit routing, and equipment locations
- [ ] Equipment specifications include manufacturer, model, ratings, and listing information
- [ ] String sizing calculations documented (Voc, Isc, Vmp, Imp at temperature extremes)
- [ ] All required NEC labels specified (690.56: DC disconnect, utility disconnect, rapid shutdown)
- [ ] Interconnection agreement submitted and approved
- [ ] Building and electrical permits obtained

Commissioning & Quality:
- [ ] String Voc within 5% of expected value (indicates proper series connections)
- [ ] String Isc within expected range (indicates no shading or module issues)
- [ ] Insulation resistance test passed (megger test > 1 MΩ)
- [ ] Inverter communicating with all modules/strings
- [ ] Monitoring system operational and reporting accurately
- [ ] Rapid shutdown tested and verified functional
- [ ] All labels installed per NEC 690.56 requirements
- [ ] Customer trained on system operation and monitoring

Example 3: Troubleshooting Low Production
```
SOLAR PV TROUBLESHOOTING: LOW PRODUCTION DIAGNOSIS

ISSUE: System producing 30% below expected output for past 2 weeks
System: 8.5 kW string inverter system, installed 2021
Inverter: SMA Sunny Boy 7.7-US
Modules: 20 × LG NeON 2 425W, 2 strings of 10

DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURE:

Step 1: Review Monitoring Data
- Compare current production to same period last year
- Check weather data (was it unusually cloudy/rainy?)
- Review inverter error logs for fault codes
- Result: Production down 30% vs. last year; weather similar; no error codes

Step 2: Visual Inspection
- Check modules for soiling, debris, bird droppings, or physical damage
- Inspect for new shading sources (tree growth, new construction)
- Verify no modules are visibly damaged or discolored
- Result: Heavy pollen accumulation on modules; one new tree branch
  creating partial shade on String 2 afternoon hours

Step 3: Electrical Measurements
                    | Expected    | Measured    | Status
String 1 Voc       | 478V        | 472V        | OK (within 2%)
String 1 Isc       | 10.8A       | 10.2A       | OK (soiling reduces Isc)
String 2 Voc       | 478V        | 430V        | LOW (10% below expected)
String 2 Isc       | 10.8A       | 7.1A        | LOW (34% below expected)

Step 4: Analysis
- String 1: Minor production loss from soiling; cleaning should restore
- String 2: Voc drop indicates possible bypassed module(s);
  Isc drop indicates significant shading impact
- One shaded module affects entire string in string inverter configuration

Step 5: Recommended Actions
1. CLEAN all modules (soft brush and water; schedule professional cleaning)
   Expected improvement: 5-8% production recovery
2. TRIM tree branch causing afternoon shade on String 2
   Expected improvement: 20-25% production recovery
3. INSPECT String 2 modules individually:
   - Use IR camera to identify hot spots (bypassed diodes or cell damage)
   - Measure individual module Voc to isolate underperforming module
4. If individual module fault found → replace under warranty (LG 25-year)
5. CONSIDER adding power optimizers to String 2 for module-level MPPT
   (mitigates partial shading impact on string performance)

PREVENTION:
- Schedule semi-annual cleaning (spring pollen, fall leaves)
- Monitor monthly production vs. expected; set alerts for > 15% deviation
- Annual tree assessment for potential shading growth
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Azimuth**: Compass direction a solar array faces, measured in degrees from true north (180° = due south in Northern Hemisphere)
- **BOS (Balance of System)**: All components other than PV modules (inverters, racking, wiring, disconnects)
- **DC/AC Ratio**: Ratio of DC array capacity to AC inverter capacity (typical 1.1-1.3 for residential)
- **GFDI (Ground Fault Detection and Interruption)**: Required protection for PV systems per NEC 690.41
- **Inverter**: Device converting DC power from PV modules to AC power for grid or loads
- **Irradiance**: Solar power per unit area, measured in W/m²; 1000 W/m² = standard test conditions (STC)
- **MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking)**: Inverter function that optimizes DC operating point for maximum power
- **NABCEP**: North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners; industry-standard certification body
- **Net Metering**: Utility billing mechanism crediting solar customers for excess energy exported to grid
- **NEC 690**: National Electrical Code article governing solar photovoltaic system installation
- **PTO (Permission to Operate)**: Utility approval to interconnect and operate a grid-tied solar system
- **Rapid Shutdown**: NEC 690.12 requirement to de-energize PV conductors within 30 seconds for firefighter safety
- **STC (Standard Test Conditions)**: Testing conditions for PV modules: 1000 W/m², 25°C cell temperature, AM 1.5 spectrum
- **String**: Series-connected group of PV modules; voltage adds, current stays same
- **Voc (Open-Circuit Voltage)**: Maximum voltage from a PV module or string with no load connected
- **Isc (Short-Circuit Current)**: Maximum current from a PV module or string with output shorted

Standards & Frameworks (2024):
- **NEC Article 690 (2023)**: Solar Photovoltaic Systems — primary installation standard for PV
- **NEC Article 705**: Interconnected Electric Power Production Sources
- **NEC Article 706**: Energy Storage Systems (for battery-coupled solar)
- **UL 1703 / IEC 61730**: PV module safety and performance standards
- **UL 1741 / IEEE 1547**: Inverter interconnection and safety standards
- **UL 3741**: PV Hazard Control for rapid shutdown systems
- **IEC 62446**: Grid-connected PV systems — documentation, commissioning, and inspection
- **ASCE 7**: Minimum design loads (wind, snow, seismic for racking design)
- **IFC 605.11**: International Fire Code — solar panel fire setback requirements
- **OSHA 1926.501-503**: Fall protection for rooftop installation work

Design Tools:
