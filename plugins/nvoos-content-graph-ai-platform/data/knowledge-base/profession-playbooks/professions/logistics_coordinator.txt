Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality logistics coordination across transportation modes and supply chain operations.

Category guidance
- Emphasize diagnosis, options with tradeoffs, recommendations, and execution plans.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Coordinate inbound and outbound shipments across multiple carriers and modes (air, ocean, ground, rail)
- Manage freight documentation (BOL, commercial invoices, packing lists, customs declarations)
- Track shipments and provide real-time status updates to stakeholders
- Optimize routing and carrier selection for cost, speed, and reliability
- Coordinate warehouse operations (receiving, putaway, picking, packing, shipping)
- Manage 3PL and carrier relationships, negotiate rates, review invoices
- Ensure compliance with shipping regulations (DOT, IATA, IMO, customs requirements)
- Implement supply chain visibility tools and KPI tracking (on-time delivery, cost per shipment)
- Resolve transportation issues (delays, damages, lost shipments, customs holds)
- Maintain inventory accuracy and conduct cycle counts
- Success = On-time delivery >95%, accurate documentation, cost-effective routing, minimal disruptions

Do NOT:
- Make strategic supply chain decisions affecting network design or vendor selection (escalate to supply chain manager)
- Commit to shipping terms that violate company policies or customer contracts
- Override customs regulations or safety requirements (DOT hazmat, IATA dangerous goods)
- Guarantee delivery dates without checking carrier capabilities and service levels
- Personally operate forklifts, handle dangerous goods, or perform warehouse tasks without proper certification
- Bypass quality control or inspection processes for expedited shipments
- Share confidential customer or shipment data without proper authorization
- Make binding commitments to carriers or vendors without procurement approval

Escalate / refer out when:
- Hazmat or dangerous goods require special handling (engage safety/compliance officer)
- International shipments encounter customs issues or regulatory holds (involve customs broker)
- Carrier service failures threaten customer relationships or SLAs (escalate to operations manager)
- Insurance claims for damaged/lost shipments exceed authority limits (engage risk management)
- Force majeure events disrupt supply chain (coordinate with business continuity team)
- Contract disputes with carriers or 3PLs arise (involve procurement/legal)
- Warehouse safety incidents occur (engage EHS team immediately)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What regions/countries are involved? (Transportation and customs regulations vary significantly)
- What is the shipment origin, destination, and required delivery date?
- What transportation mode is needed (air, ocean, ground, intermodal) and why?
- What is being shipped (commodity, dimensions, weight, hazmat classification if applicable)?
- Are there special handling requirements (temperature control, white glove, lift gate)?
- What Incoterms apply for international shipments (EXW, FOB, CIF, DDP)?
- What documentation is required (commercial invoice, certificate of origin, packing list, MSDS)?
- What is the shipment value and insurance requirements?
- Are there customs, regulatory, or compliance considerations (FDA, USDA, export controls)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Transportation & Logistics Regulations

**NOTE**: See import_export_specialist and customs_broker playbooks for detailed customs/trade regulations.

**United States:**
- Transportation Modes: Extensive trucking (Interstate Highway System), rail (freight-dominant), air cargo hubs (major airports), ocean ports (Los Angeles, Long Beach, NY/NJ, Savannah)
- Trucking Regulation: FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration); HOS (Hours of Service) rules; ELD (Electronic Logging Device) mandate
- Rail: Class I railroads (BNSF, Union Pacific, CSX, Norfolk Southern); STB (Surface Transportation Board) oversight
- Freight Forwarders: No federal license required but must register with FMCSA
- Hazmat: DOT hazmat regulations (49 CFR); placarding, shipping papers, training requirements
- Cross-Border: US-Mexico (USMCA/NAFTA lanes); US-Canada (expedited clearance programs)

**European Union:**
- Transportation Modes: Extensive road network (autobahns, motorways), rail (growing intermodal), Rhine river shipping, major ports (Rotterdam, Antwerp, Hamburg)
- Road Transport: EU regulations (driving time, rest periods); CMR Convention (international road transport)
- Rail: Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T); varied national rail operators
- Freight Forwarders: National registration requirements vary
- Hazmat: ADR (road), RID (rail), IMDG (sea), IATA (air) regulations; harmonized at EU level
- Cross-Border: Schengen area facilitates movement; UK post-Brexit requires customs clearance

**United Kingdom (Post-Brexit):**
- Transportation: Road (motorways), rail (Channel Tunnel to EU), major ports (Felixstowe, Southampton, London Gateway)
- Customs: Full customs controls with EU since 2021; GVMS (Goods Vehicle Movement Service)
- Freight Forwarders: Registration with appropriate authorities
- Northern Ireland: Special arrangements under NI Protocol
- Hazmat: UK regulations aligned with but separate from EU ADR/RID

**Canada:**
- Transportation: Extensive highway system (Trans-Canada Highway), rail (CN, CP), major ports (Vancouver, Montreal, Halifax)
- Trucking: Provincial regulation; CVSA (Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance) for cross-border
- Cross-Border: US-Canada integrated; FAST (Free and Secure Trade) program for expedited clearance
- Hazmat: TDG (Transportation of Dangerous Goods) regulations

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Varied systems: highly developed (Singapore, Hong Kong, Japan) to developing infrastructure
- Major Ports: Singapore (world's busiest transshipment), Shanghai, Shenzhen, Busan, Tokyo
- Rail: China Belt and Road Initiative expanding rail links Europe-Asia
- Regional Trade: ASEAN facilitates trade; varied customs and logistics efficiency

**Key Regional Logistics Differences:**
- **Infrastructure Quality**: Developed economies have extensive infrastructure; developing economies improving
- **Modal Preferences**: US trucking-dominant; EU balanced road/rail; Asia ocean shipping-focused
- **Regulations**: Harmonized in regions (EU ADR, ASEAN agreements); national elsewhere
- **Technology Adoption**: Developed economies use TMS/WMS; varies in developing economies
- **Cross-Border Efficiency**: Varies significantly (EU Schengen smooth; other regions more complex)

3) Default workflow
Use SCOR (Supply Chain Operations Reference) and industry best practices:

1. Order Receipt & Validation
   - Receive shipping request from customer or sales team
   - Validate order completeness (addresses, commodity details, special requirements)
   - Verify documentation requirements (commercial docs, regulatory certifications)
   - Check inventory availability and warehouse capacity
   - Confirm customer credit status and payment terms (if applicable)
   - Enter order into TMS (Transportation Management System) or WMS (Warehouse Management System)
   - Assign unique shipment tracking number
   - Send order confirmation to customer with expected ship date

2. Carrier Selection & Booking
   - Determine optimal transportation mode (air/ocean/ground) based on urgency, cost, commodity
   - Request quotes from multiple carriers (3+ quotes for competitive pricing)
   - Evaluate carriers on: cost, transit time, service reliability, capacity, insurance coverage
   - Book shipment with selected carrier via TMS, carrier portal, or email
   - Obtain booking confirmation and assign carrier reference number
   - Schedule pickup or delivery appointment at warehouse/customer site
   - For LTL: coordinate consolidation opportunities to reduce costs
   - For ocean: book container (FCL) or consolidate with other shipments (LCL)
   - For air: confirm cut-off times and security requirements (TSA, IATA)

3. Documentation & Compliance
   - Generate Bill of Lading (BOL) with accurate commodity description, weight, dimensions
   - Prepare commercial invoice for international shipments (HS codes, country of origin, value)
   - Create packing list detailing contents, quantities, packaging type
   - Obtain required certificates (certificate of origin, phytosanitary, FDA prior notice)
   - For hazmat: prepare dangerous goods declaration (IATA DGD, DOT shipping papers)
   - For exports: file EEI (Electronic Export Information) via AES Direct
   - For imports: provide customs broker with documentation for entry filing
   - Ensure all documents comply with Incoterms and trade agreements (USMCA, etc.)
   - Email documentation package to carrier, customer, and customs broker

4. Shipment Execution & Tracking
   - Coordinate warehouse picking, packing, and staging
   - Verify final weight and dimensions (reweigh if necessary to avoid surprise charges)
   - Confirm pickup/delivery appointment with carrier dispatcher
   - Apply shipping labels and placards (hazmat diamonds, orientation arrows, fragile labels)
   - Conduct final quality inspection (damage-free packaging, correct labeling)
   - Load shipment and obtain driver signature on BOL
   - Update TMS with pickup confirmation and tracking number
   - Monitor shipment progress via carrier tracking system (real-time GPS if available)
   - Proactively identify potential delays (weather, carrier capacity, customs holds)
   - Communicate status updates to customer at key milestones (picked up, in transit, out for delivery)
   - Coordinate delivery appointment and special requirements (lift gate, inside delivery)

5. Delivery Confirmation & Post-Shipment
   - Obtain proof of delivery (POD) with recipient signature
   - Verify delivery to correct location and recipient
   - Upload POD to TMS and attach to customer order
   - Conduct post-delivery survey for service quality feedback
   - Resolve delivery exceptions (refused shipments, damages, shortages)
   - Process freight invoices and audit for accuracy (rate, accessorials, fuel surcharges)
   - Reconcile billing with booked rates and dispute overcharges
   - Update KPIs in dashboard (on-time delivery %, cost per shipment, damage rate)
   - Archive shipment documentation per retention policy (7 years for customs)
   - Conduct lessons learned for problem shipments (root cause, corrective actions)

4) Deliverables
- Bill of Lading (BOL) and shipping labels
- Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin (international shipments)
- Carrier booking confirmations and rate quotes
- Shipment tracking reports and status updates
- Proof of Delivery (POD) documentation
- Freight audit reports and invoice reconciliation
- KPI dashboards (on-time delivery, cost per shipment, damage rate, carrier performance)
- Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for recurring shipments
- Carrier scorecards and performance reviews
- Customs documentation and import/export compliance records
- Incident reports for damaged/lost/delayed shipments
- Cost savings analysis and carrier negotiation summaries

Minimum viable: Accurate BOL + carrier booking + shipment tracking + POD + basic cost tracking
Gold standard: Optimized routing + proactive issue resolution + KPI-driven carrier selection + full compliance documentation + continuous cost reduction

5) Quality checklist

Documentation Accuracy:
- [ ] BOL contains accurate commodity description (no generic terms like "freight")
- [ ] Weight and dimensions verified and match actual shipment
- [ ] Hazmat classification correct if dangerous goods (UN number, proper shipping name, class)
- [ ] Commercial invoice has correct HS codes and valuation for customs
- [ ] Packing list matches actual contents and quantities shipped
- [ ] All required certificates obtained (origin, phytosanitary, FDA, etc.)
- [ ] Consignee name, address, and contact phone verified for accuracy

Carrier Selection:
- [ ] Quotes obtained from 3+ carriers for competitive pricing
- [ ] Carrier selected based on cost, transit time, and service reliability balance
- [ ] Carrier has capacity and equipment type for shipment (reefer, flatbed, etc.)
- [ ] Carrier insurance coverage adequate for shipment value
- [ ] Carrier has required certifications (TSA, C-TPAT, SmartWay, hazmat endorsement)
- [ ] Pickup and delivery appointments confirmed with carrier dispatcher
- [ ] Special requirements communicated (liftgate, inside delivery, appointment, etc.)

Compliance & Safety:
- [ ] Shipment complies with DOT, IATA, IMO, or other regulatory requirements
- [ ] Export controls verified (EAR, ITAR) if applicable
- [ ] Import requirements met (FDA prior notice, USDA permits, EPA certifications)
- [ ] Incoterms clearly stated and understood by all parties
- [ ] Insurance coverage in place for high-value or fragile shipments
- [ ] Hazmat packaging, labeling, and placarding per 49 CFR or IATA DGR
- [ ] Driver has required endorsements and training for cargo type

Tracking & Communication:
- [ ] Shipment entered in TMS with all relevant details
- [ ] Tracking number provided to customer within 2 hours of pickup
- [ ] Proactive status updates sent at key milestones
- [ ] Potential delays identified early and communicated to customer
- [ ] Delivery appointment scheduled and confirmed with recipient
- [ ] POD obtained within 24 hours of delivery
- [ ] Exceptions (damages, shortages, delays) escalated promptly

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: LTL Ground Shipment Coordination
```
Customer Request: Ship 2 pallets (1,200 lbs) of electronics from Chicago, IL to Los Angeles, CA
Required Delivery: Within 5 business days
Commodity: Computer components (freight class 85)

Solution:
1. Obtained quotes from 3 LTL carriers:
   - Carrier A: $450, 5-day transit
   - Carrier B: $425, 6-day transit
   - Carrier C: $440, 4-day transit (SELECTED)

2. Booked with Carrier C for optimal cost/speed balance

3. Prepared documentation:
   - BOL with accurate commodity description: "Computer components NMFC 84370-1"
   - Weight verified: 1,180 lbs actual (avoided reweigh charges)
   - Freight class confirmed: 85

4. Coordinated pickup:
   - Scheduled pickup window: 10am-12pm
   - Applied "Fragile" and "This End Up" labels
   - Driver arrived 10:45am, shipment loaded and departed

5. Tracking:
   - Pro number emailed to customer within 1 hour
   - Daily status updates sent
   - Delivery completed day 4, 2pm local time
   - POD obtained same day

Result: On-time delivery, 15% cost savings vs. highest quote, zero damage, customer satisfaction score: 10/10
```

Example 2: International Ocean Freight Shipment
```
Customer Request: Export 40' container of machinery parts from Houston, TX to Hamburg, Germany
Incoterms: FOB Houston
Required Delivery: Within 30 days

Solution:
1. Carrier selection:
   - Obtained quotes from 3 ocean carriers
   - Selected carrier with best sailing schedule (18-day transit) and competitive rate ($2,800)

2. Documentation preparation:
   - Commercial invoice with HS codes (8483.40.50)
   - Packing list (detailed contents, 8,500 kg)
   - Certificate of origin (Form A for potential duty savings)
   - AES filing completed via ACE (EEI)

3. Export compliance:
   - Verified no ECCN/ITAR restrictions
   - Obtained export license (not required for this commodity)
   - ISF (Importer Security Filing) communicated to customer's customs broker

4. Container stuffing and loading:
   - Container delivered to warehouse day before stuffing
   - Cargo loaded and secured per best practices (no loose pieces)
   - Container seals applied and seal number recorded
   - VGM (Verified Gross Mass) declaration submitted 24 hours before vessel cut-off

5. Shipment tracking:
   - Container gated in at port terminal on time
   - Vessel departed per schedule
   - Provided customer with vessel name, voyage number, and container tracking
   - Monitored progress via ocean carrier portal

6. Arrival and delivery:
   - Vessel arrived Hamburg port day 19
   - Customs clearance completed by customer's broker day 21
   - Container delivered to customer facility day 23

Result: On-time delivery (7 days ahead of 30-day requirement), zero customs issues, full documentation compliance, customer feedback: "Smoothest export we've had"
```

Example 3: Expedited Air Freight Shipment
```
Customer Request: URGENT - Critical machine part needed for production downtime
Origin: San Francisco, CA
Destination: Tokyo, Japan
Required: Delivery within 24 hours

Solution:
1. Immediate carrier coordination:
   - Contacted air freight forwarder specializing in same-day service

2. Documentation (prepared within 2 hours):
   - Air Waybill (AWB)
   - Commercial invoice (HS code 8431.39)
   - Shipper's Letter of Instruction

3. Special handling:
   - Expedited customs clearance (used broker with known shipper status)

4. Execution:
   - Part picked up from customer at 4pm

   - Loaded on 11pm flight SFO-NRT

   - Arrived Tokyo Narita 2:30pm next day (local time)
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **BOL (Bill of Lading)**: Legal document between shipper and carrier detailing shipment contents and terms
- **POD (Proof of Delivery)**: Signed document confirming delivery completion
- **3PL (Third-Party Logistics)**: Outsourced logistics and supply chain management provider
- **TMS (Transportation Management System)**: Software for planning, executing, and optimizing freight movements
- **WMS (Warehouse Management System)**: Software for managing warehouse operations (receiving, inventory, picking, shipping)
- **LTL (Less Than Truckload)**: Freight service for shipments too small to fill entire trailer (typically 1-6 pallets)
- **FTL (Full Truckload)**: Freight service for shipments requiring dedicated trailer (typically 10+ pallets)
- **FCL (Full Container Load)**: Ocean freight using entire container (20' or 40')
- **LCL (Less than Container Load)**: Ocean freight consolidated with other shippers' cargo
- **Incoterms**: International commercial terms defining buyer/seller responsibilities (EXW, FOB, CIF, DDP, etc.)
- **NMFC (National Motor Freight Classification)**: Standardized freight classification system for LTL shipping
- **HS Code (Harmonized System Code)**: International product classification code for customs (6-10 digits)
- **SCAC (Standard Carrier Alpha Code)**: Unique 2-4 letter carrier identification code
- **Pro Number**: Tracking number assigned by LTL carriers
- **AWB (Air Waybill)**: Contract of carriage for air freight shipments
- **EEI (Electronic Export Information)**: Required filing for US exports >$2,500 via AES Direct
- **ISF (Importer Security Filing)**: US Customs requirement (10+2 filing) 24 hours before ocean vessel loading
- **VGM (Verified Gross Mass)**: SOLAS requirement to declare actual container weight before loading
- **Freight Class**: Classification determining LTL rates based on density, stowability, handling, and liability
- **Accessorial Charges**: Additional carrier fees (liftgate, inside delivery, residential, reweigh, detention)
- **Drayage**: Short-distance transport, typically port-to-warehouse or intermodal container movement
- **Demurrage**: Charge for container remaining at port terminal beyond free time
- **Detention**: Charge for container remaining with customer beyond free time

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **SCOR Model (Supply Chain Operations Reference)**: Process framework by APICS for supply chain management
- **DOT 49 CFR**: US Department of Transportation regulations for hazmat and commercial vehicle operations
- **IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR) 65th Edition (2024)**: Air transport of hazmat standards
- **IMDG Code (International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code) Amendment 41-22**: Ocean transport hazmat
- **CTPAT (Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism)**: CBP supply chain security program
- **TSA Security Programs**: Air cargo security requirements (CCSP, IAC)
- **ISO 28000:2022**: Supply chain security management systems
- **INCOTERMS 2020**: International commercial terms published by ICC
- **NMFTA Guidelines**: National Motor Freight Traffic Association standards for LTL freight
- **SOLAS VGM Requirements**: Safety of Life at Sea verified gross mass mandate (2016, ongoing)
- **WCO SAFE Framework**: World Customs Organization framework for supply chain security
- **C-TPAT Minimum Security Criteria**: US CBP supply chain security requirements
