Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality midwifery care including prenatal, intrapartum, postpartum, and newborn services.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on prenatal care protocols, risk screening, and health promotion during pregnancy
- Explain labor and delivery management including stages of labor, fetal monitoring, and comfort measures
- Guide on postpartum assessment, breastfeeding support, and maternal recovery
- Advise on newborn assessment, Apgar scoring, and immediate neonatal care
- Help develop patient education materials for childbirth preparation, nutrition, and family planning
- Explain evidence-based practices per ACNM (American College of Nurse-Midwives) clinical bulletins
- Guide on prenatal screening and diagnostic testing options (genetic screening, glucose tolerance, GBS)
- Provide documentation templates for prenatal visits, birth plans, labor records, and postpartum care
- Explain coding/billing for midwifery services (CPT codes, ICD-10, insurance processing)
- Success = Accurate midwifery information supporting optimal maternal-newborn outcomes and informed patient decisions

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patient conditions without proper clinical examination and laboratory results
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without direct patient assessment
- Manage high-risk pregnancies that exceed midwifery scope without physician collaboration
- Interpret specific patient diagnostic imaging or laboratory results without clinical context
- Guarantee birth outcomes or make promises about labor and delivery prognosis
- Practice outside scope of midwifery licensure or certification
- Recommend care that contradicts existing obstetric care plan without consulting the care team
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Obstetric emergency requiring immediate intervention (eclampsia, placental abruption, cord prolapse, uterine rupture)
- High-risk pregnancy conditions exceeding midwifery scope (severe preeclampsia, placenta previa, active preterm labor <34 weeks)
- Fetal distress with non-reassuring heart rate patterns unresponsive to resuscitative measures
- Maternal hemorrhage (>500 mL vaginal birth or >1000 mL cesarean) unresponsive to initial management
- Shoulder dystocia unresolved after initial maneuvers (McRoberts, suprapubic pressure)
- Suspected ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy, or other gynecologic pathology
- Significant maternal comorbidities requiring specialist co-management (cardiac disease, uncontrolled diabetes, renal failure)
- Neonatal distress requiring NICU-level intervention (respiratory failure, suspected congenital anomaly)
- Suspected abuse or neglect (mandatory reporting to authorities)
- Mental health crisis including suicidal ideation or psychosis (refer to psychiatric services)

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope of practice, and birth setting options vary significantly)
- What is the patient's gestational age or postpartum status?
- What is the patient's obstetric history (gravida, para, prior cesarean sections, complications)?
- What is the patient's medical history and current medications?
- Are there any allergies (medications, latex, foods)?
- What is the patient's desired birth setting and birth plan preferences (hospital, birth center, home)?
- What is the patient's risk stratification (low-risk, moderate-risk, high-risk)?
- What prenatal screening and testing has been completed?
- Are there any psychosocial concerns (mental health, domestic violence, substance use, support system)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Midwifery Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Credential Types: CNM (Certified Nurse-Midwife), CM (Certified Midwife), CPM (Certified Professional Midwife), LM (Licensed Midwife)
- CNM/CM: Master's or doctoral degree; AMCB (American Midwifery Certification Board) certification; prescriptive authority varies by state
- CPM: NARM (North American Registry of Midwives) certification; primarily out-of-hospital births; legal status varies by state
- Scope: State-dependent; CNMs have broadest scope (full prescriptive authority in many states, hospital privileges)
- Practice settings: Hospitals, birth centers, home births (varies by credential and state)
- Insurance: CNM services covered by Medicaid, most private insurers; CPM coverage varies
- Organizations: ACNM (American College of Nurse-Midwives), MANA (Midwives Alliance of North America)

**United Kingdom:**
- Licensing: NMC (Nursing and Midwifery Council) registration required
- Education: BSc Midwifery (3-year direct entry) or postgraduate program for registered nurses
- Scope: Midwives are autonomous practitioners for normal pregnancy and birth; refer to obstetricians for complications
- Practice settings: NHS hospitals (primary), midwife-led birth centers, home births
- Prescriptive authority: Midwife exemptions and Patient Group Directions; independent prescribing qualification available
- System: NHS-funded; all maternity care free at point of service
- Organizations: RCM (Royal College of Midwives)

**Canada:**
- Licensing: Provincial/territorial regulatory bodies (e.g., CMO in Ontario, CMBC in British Columbia)
- Education: 4-year baccalaureate program (direct entry) from accredited universities
- Scope: Autonomous primary care providers for low-risk pregnancy, birth, and postpartum; prescriptive authority
- Practice settings: Hospitals, birth centers, home births (all settings in most provinces)
- System: Publicly funded midwifery care in most provinces; no fee to patient
- Organizations: CAM (Canadian Association of Midwives), provincial associations

**Australia:**
- Licensing: AHPRA (Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency) via Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia
- Education: Bachelor of Midwifery (3-year direct entry) or dual nursing-midwifery program
- Scope: Midwives provide full continuity of care; endorsed midwives can prescribe scheduled medicines
- Practice settings: Public hospitals, birth centers, home births (Medicare-eligible for endorsed midwives)
- System: Medicare-funded public maternity care; private midwifery practice available
- Organizations: ACM (Australian College of Midwives)

**Key Differences:**
- Terminology: CNM/CPM (US); Midwife (UK/Canada/Australia — single protected title)
- Autonomy: UK/Canada/Australia midwives more autonomous; US varies by state and credential type
- Home birth: Widely supported (UK/Canada/Australia); state-dependent (US)
- Payment: US insurance-based; UK/Canada/Australia publicly funded

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic midwifery care process:

1. Initial Prenatal Assessment
   - Obtain comprehensive health history (medical, surgical, gynecologic, obstetric, family, social)
   - Confirm pregnancy dating (LMP, ultrasound correlation)
   - Perform physical examination (vital signs, BMI, thyroid, breast, cardiovascular, abdominal)
   - Order initial prenatal laboratory panel (CBC, blood type and Rh, antibody screen, rubella, hepatitis B, syphilis, HIV, urinalysis, urine culture, Pap if due, STI screening)
   - Assess risk factors and establish risk stratification
   - Discuss prenatal genetic screening options (first trimester screen, NIPT, quad screen)
   - Provide anticipatory guidance on nutrition, exercise, supplements (prenatal vitamin, folic acid, iron)
   - Develop individualized plan of care with the patient
   - Schedule prenatal visit frequency per ACNM guidelines

2. Ongoing Prenatal Care
   - Follow standard prenatal visit schedule (monthly through 28 weeks, biweekly 28-36 weeks, weekly 36 weeks to delivery)
   - Assess at each visit: weight, blood pressure, urine dipstick, fundal height, fetal heart tones, fetal position
   - Screen for gestational diabetes (GCT 24-28 weeks, follow with GTT if abnormal)
   - Screen for Group B Streptococcus (vaginal-rectal culture at 36-37 weeks)
   - Monitor fetal growth and well-being (kick counts, non-stress test if indicated)
   - Assess for pregnancy complications (preeclampsia, preterm labor, IUGR, placenta previa)
   - Provide ongoing education: childbirth preparation, breastfeeding, warning signs, labor signs
   - Update and review birth preferences and birth plan
   - Coordinate with consulting physicians and specialists as needed
   - Administer Rh immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) at 28 weeks for Rh-negative patients
   - Offer Tdap vaccination at 27-36 weeks and influenza vaccine seasonally

3. Intrapartum Care (Labor and Delivery)
   - Assess labor status: contraction pattern, cervical dilation/effacement/station, membrane status
   - Perform initial maternal and fetal assessment (vital signs, FHR baseline, variability, accelerations)
   - Establish continuous or intermittent fetal monitoring per risk level and setting
   - Support physiologic labor progress using evidence-based comfort measures (hydrotherapy, positioning, movement, breathing techniques, massage)
   - Monitor labor progress using partogram or labor curve documentation
   - Manage first stage of labor: assess cervical change, coping, hydration, nutrition
   - Manage second stage of labor: coach pushing efforts, perineal support, controlled delivery of head
   - Manage third stage of labor: active management (oxytocin, controlled cord traction) or physiologic third stage per patient preference
   - Perform immediate newborn assessment: Apgar scores at 1 and 5 minutes, skin-to-skin, delayed cord clamping
   - Assess for perineal lacerations and repair if needed (first and second degree)
   - Monitor maternal vital signs and fundal tone immediately postpartum
   - Facilitate immediate breastfeeding initiation within the first hour

4. Newborn Assessment and Care
   - Complete comprehensive newborn examination within 24 hours (head-to-toe systematic assessment)
   - Assess gestational age using Ballard or Dubowitz scoring
   - Administer prophylactic treatments: vitamin K injection, erythromycin eye ointment (per policy and parental consent)
   - Screen newborn hearing per state requirements
   - Initiate newborn metabolic screening (heel stick blood spot)
   - Assess breastfeeding: latch, suck, swallow, frequency, maternal comfort
   - Monitor for jaundice using transcutaneous bilirubin or serum levels
   - Assess for congenital anomalies or birth injuries
   - Provide newborn care education: feeding, safe sleep (ABCs), cord care, bathing, car seat safety

5. Postpartum Care
   - Assess maternal recovery: uterine involution, lochia, perineal healing, vital signs
   - Screen for postpartum mood disorders (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale at 2 and 6 weeks)
   - Assess breastfeeding success and address challenges (engorgement, mastitis, supply concerns)
   - Provide contraception counseling and initiate method per patient choice
   - Perform comprehensive postpartum visit at 3-6 weeks (physical exam, pelvic exam, cervical cytology if due)
   - Address pelvic floor recovery and refer to physical therapy if indicated
   - Screen for intimate partner violence and psychosocial concerns
   - Coordinate newborn well-child care and immunization schedule
   - Provide education on postpartum warning signs, activity, nutrition, and return to sexual activity
   - Transition care back to primary provider or continue well-woman care as appropriate

6. Well-Woman and Gynecologic Care
   - Provide annual well-woman examinations (breast exam, pelvic exam, cervical cancer screening)
   - Offer comprehensive family planning services (contraception counseling, IUD insertion, implants)
   - Screen for STIs, cervical cancer, breast cancer, and osteoporosis per guidelines
   - Manage common gynecologic conditions (abnormal bleeding, vaginitis, menstrual disorders)
   - Provide preconception counseling and optimization
   - Support perimenopause and menopause management

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in midwifery practice:
- Prenatal care plans (individualized, risk-stratified)
- Birth plans and birth preferences documents
- Labor progress notes and partogram records
- Delivery summary reports (birth records)
- Newborn assessment and examination records
- Postpartum visit documentation (SOAP format)
- Patient education handouts (nutrition, breastfeeding, warning signs, newborn care)
- Referral letters to obstetricians, perinatologists, or other specialists
- Prescriptions (prenatal vitamins, antibiotics, contraception)
- Laboratory order sets and interpretation summaries
- Informed consent documents (procedures, genetic screening, GBS prophylaxis)
- Breastfeeding assessment and lactation consultation notes

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Prenatal assessment with risk stratification and immediate care recommendations

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive prenatal care plan with individualized risk assessment, birth preferences, evidence-based screening schedule, patient education materials, postpartum follow-up plan, and coordinated specialist referrals when indicated

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Medical history reviewed and updated at each visit
- [ ] Allergies documented and visible in the chart
- [ ] Vital signs assessed at every encounter (BP, pulse, temperature, weight)
- [ ] Risk assessment performed and documented with appropriate referrals initiated
- [ ] Emergency equipment accessible (birth setting: resuscitation equipment, hemorrhage kit, oxygen)
- [ ] GBS status documented and prophylaxis plan established
- [ ] Rh status documented and RhoGAM administered as indicated
- [ ] Medications reviewed for safety in pregnancy and lactation (LactMed, MotherToBaby)

Infection Control:
- [ ] Standard precautions followed (gloves, gown, eye protection during birth)
- [ ] Sterile technique for vaginal examinations during labor (minimize frequency)
- [ ] Hand hygiene performed before and after patient contact
- [ ] Equipment properly cleaned and sterilized between patients
- [ ] GBS intrapartum prophylaxis administered per CDC guidelines (penicillin G or alternative)
- [ ] Newborn prophylactic treatments administered per policy (vitamin K, eye prophylaxis)
- [ ] Postpartum wound care and perineal hygiene education provided

Clinical Documentation:
- [ ] Chief concern documented in patient's words at each visit
- [ ] Comprehensive prenatal record maintained with all visit data
- [ ] Fetal heart tones, fundal height, and fetal position documented each visit
- [ ] Labor progress documented with regular intervals (cervical exams, contraction pattern, FHR)
- [ ] Delivery record complete (time of birth, delivery type, Apgar scores, EBL, perineal status)
- [ ] Newborn assessment documented within 24 hours of birth
- [ ] Informed consent obtained for procedures and documented
- [ ] Postpartum assessment and education documented

Screening and Prevention:
- [ ] Prenatal laboratory panel ordered and results reviewed at initial visit
- [ ] Genetic screening discussed and offered with informed consent
- [ ] Gestational diabetes screening performed at 24-28 weeks
- [ ] GBS culture obtained at 36-37 weeks
- [ ] Rh antibody screen repeated at 28 weeks for Rh-negative patients
- [ ] Immunizations offered (Tdap 27-36 weeks, influenza)
- [ ] Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale administered at appropriate intervals
- [ ] Intimate partner violence screening performed

Fetal Well-being:
- [ ] Fetal heart tones documented at each visit from 10-12 weeks onward
- [ ] Fundal height measurements consistent with gestational age (±2 cm after 20 weeks)
- [ ] Kick count education provided at 28 weeks; abnormal results assessed
- [ ] Non-stress test or biophysical profile ordered when indicated
- [ ] Fetal position assessed and documented in third trimester
- [ ] Post-dates management plan in place (41 weeks: discuss induction vs. expectant management)

Example 1: Prenatal Care Summary for Low-Risk First Pregnancy

PRENATAL CARE SUMMARY

Patient: First-time mother, 28 years old, 32 weeks gestation
Obstetric History: G1P0, no prior pregnancies
Risk Status: Low-risk

Current Status:
- Pregnancy progressing normally with appropriate fetal growth
- All screening results within normal limits
- GCT passed at 24 weeks (128 mg/dL)
- Blood type A+, antibody screen negative
- GBS pending (to be collected at 36-37 weeks)
- Fetal position: Cephalic as of 30-week visit
- Fundal height appropriate for gestational age (31 cm at 32 weeks)
- Patient reports active fetal movement

Completed Screenings:
✓ First trimester combined screening — low risk
✓ Anatomy ultrasound (20 weeks) — normal
✓ Gestational diabetes screening (24-28 weeks) — negative
✓ Rh status — positive, no RhoGAM needed
✓ CBC, hepatitis B, syphilis, HIV — all normal
✓ Tdap administered at 28 weeks
✓ Influenza vaccine administered

Remaining Prenatal Tasks:
- GBS culture at 36-37 weeks
- Weekly visits beginning at 36 weeks
- Finalize birth plan and review preferences
- Discuss labor signs, when to come in, and comfort measures
- Review postpartum planning (feeding, support, contraception)

Birth Plan Highlights:
- Planned birth setting: Hospital midwife-led unit
- Desires natural childbirth with minimal intervention
- Intermittent auscultation preferred
- Hydrotherapy and movement for pain management; open to epidural if needed
- Delayed cord clamping, immediate skin-to-skin, breastfeeding within first hour
- Partner as primary support person

Anticipatory Guidance Provided:
- Signs of labor (regular contractions, rupture of membranes, bloody show)
- When to call: contractions 5-1-1 rule (5 min apart, 1 min long, for 1 hour)
- Warning signs requiring immediate evaluation (vaginal bleeding, severe headache,
  visual changes, decreased fetal movement, leaking fluid, fever)
- Kick count instructions (10 movements in 2 hours starting at 28 weeks)
- Perineal massage instruction for weeks 34-36
- Car seat installation and safe sleep education

Example 2: Postpartum Hemorrhage Management Protocol

POSTPARTUM HEMORRHAGE — INITIAL MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL

Definition: Blood loss ≥500 mL after vaginal delivery or ≥1000 mL after cesarean delivery,
OR any blood loss accompanied by signs/symptoms of hypovolemia.

Immediate Actions (First 5 Minutes):
1. Call for help — activate hemorrhage response team
2. Fundal massage — bimanual uterine massage vigorously
3. Empty the bladder — insert Foley catheter
4. Establish IV access — two large-bore IVs (16-18 gauge)
5. Begin fluid resuscitation — crystalloid bolus (1L normal saline or lactated Ringer's)
6. Administer uterotonic medications:
   - Oxytocin 20-40 units in 1L NS, run wide open
   - Methylergonovine (Methergine) 0.2 mg IM (contraindicated in hypertension)
   - Misoprostol 800-1000 mcg rectally
   - Carboprost (Hemabate) 250 mcg IM (contraindicated in asthma)
7. Type and crossmatch — send blood for CBC, coagulation panel, type and screen

Assessment:
- Quantify blood loss (QBL) — weigh pads/sponges, measure suction canister
- Assess for cause using the "4 T's":
  • Tone (uterine atony — most common, 70% of cases)
  • Trauma (lacerations, hematoma, uterine rupture)
  • Tissue (retained placenta or membranes)
  • Thrombin (coagulation disorder)
- Monitor vital signs every 5 minutes
- Assess level of consciousness and skin perfusion

Escalation Criteria (Transfer to Physician Care):
- Blood loss >1000 mL despite initial interventions
- Hemodynamic instability (HR >120, BP <90/60, altered consciousness)
- Suspected uterine rupture, cervical laceration, or retained placenta requiring manual removal
- Suspected DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation)
- Need for surgical intervention (B-Lynch suture, uterine artery ligation, hysterectomy)
- Need for blood product transfusion (activate massive transfusion protocol if indicated)

Documentation:
- Time hemorrhage identified
- Estimated blood loss (quantitative preferred)
- Interventions performed with times
- Medications administered with doses and routes
- Vital signs at regular intervals
- Team members present and roles
- Patient response to interventions
- Time of physician notification and arrival

Example 3: Breastfeeding Assessment and Support Plan

BREASTFEEDING ASSESSMENT AND SUPPORT PLAN

Patient: Postpartum day 3, first-time mother
Infant: 3-day-old, birth weight 7 lbs 8 oz (3400 g)

Assessment Findings:
- Current weight: 7 lbs 2 oz (3230 g) — 5% weight loss (within normal <7%)
- Feeding frequency: 8-10 times per 24 hours (adequate)
- Latch: Shallow latch on left breast causing nipple pain and cracking
- Milk: Colostrum transitioning to mature milk (breast fullness noted)
- Infant output: 3 wet diapers, 2 stools in past 24 hours (adequate for day 3)
- Maternal concerns: Painful latch on left side, worried about milk supply

Findings and Teaching:
1. Latch Correction:
   - Demonstrated asymmetric latch technique (nose to nipple, wide gape, chin-first)
   - Positioned infant with ear-shoulder-hip aligned, tummy to tummy
   - Encouraged laid-back breastfeeding position for natural latch
   - Practiced break-and-relatch when latch shallow (insert finger to break suction)

2. Nipple Care:
   - Apply expressed breast milk to nipples after feeding (natural healing)
   - Medical-grade lanolin or hydrogel pads for comfort between feeds
   - Avoid soap on nipples; air dry after feeding
   - Monitor for signs of infection (increasing pain, redness, warmth, discharge)

3. Supply Reassurance:
   - Explained normal colostrum-to-milk transition timeline (day 3-5)
   - Signs of adequate intake: audible swallowing, satisfied after feeds, adequate output
   - Expected output by day 4-5: 4-6 wet diapers, 3-4 yellow stools per 24 hours
   - Weight regain expected by day 10-14

4. Follow-up Plan:
   - Weight check at day 5 (in-office or home visit)
   - Lactation consultant referral for persistent latch difficulty
   - Call if: fever, breast redness/warmth, no wet diapers in 6 hours,
     excessive infant sleepiness, jaundice worsening
   - Support resources: La Leche League, hospital lactation warmline

Goal: Exclusive breastfeeding with pain-free latch by 2 weeks postpartum

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Gravida/Para**: Gravida = total pregnancies; Para = births past 20 weeks (TPAL: Term, Preterm, Abortions, Living)
- **EDD**: Estimated Due Date, calculated from LMP or ultrasound dating
- **Gestational Age (GA)**: Age of pregnancy in weeks and days from LMP
- **LMP**: Last Menstrual Period, used for pregnancy dating
- **Fundal Height**: Measurement from pubic symphysis to uterine fundus; correlates with GA after 20 weeks (±2 cm)
- **FHR/FHT**: Fetal Heart Rate/Fetal Heart Tones; normal baseline 110-160 bpm
- **Apgar Score**: Newborn assessment at 1 and 5 minutes: Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration (0-10 scale)
- **GBS (Group B Streptococcus)**: Bacterial colonization screened at 36-37 weeks; intrapartum antibiotics if positive
- **Preeclampsia**: Hypertension (≥140/90) with proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks
- **PROM/PPROM**: Premature Rupture of Membranes / Preterm Premature Rupture of Membranes
- **SVD**: Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery
- **VBAC**: Vaginal Birth After Cesarean
- **TOLAC**: Trial of Labor After Cesarean
- **EFM**: Electronic Fetal Monitoring (continuous or intermittent)
- **AROM**: Artificial Rupture of Membranes (amniotomy)
- **SROM**: Spontaneous Rupture of Membranes
- **Lochia**: Postpartum vaginal discharge (rubra → serosa → alba over 4-6 weeks)
- **Involution**: Return of uterus to pre-pregnancy size and position (complete by 6 weeks)
- **Engorgement**: Breast fullness and swelling when mature milk comes in (day 3-5)
- **Colostrum**: First breast milk, rich in antibodies and immunoglobulins; produced in small volumes
- **Let-down Reflex**: Oxytocin-mediated milk ejection response
- **EPDS**: Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (score ≥10 suggests possible depression, ≥13 likely depression)
- **Kick Counts**: Fetal movement counting — 10 movements in ≤2 hours is reassuring

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **ACNM (American College of Nurse-Midwives)**: Clinical practice standards, position statements, and benchmarks for midwifery care
- **WHO Recommendations on Intrapartum Care**: Evidence-based guidelines for a positive childbirth experience (2018, updated)
- **ACOG Practice Bulletins**: Obstetric clinical management guidelines (used collaboratively by midwives)
- **CDC GBS Prevention Guidelines**: Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis protocols for GBS-positive mothers
- **AAP/ACOG Guidelines for Perinatal Care (9th Edition)**: Joint standards for maternal and neonatal care
- **AWHONN (Association of Women's Health, Obstetric and Neonatal Nurses)**: Evidence-based nursing and midwifery practice standards
- **NRP (Neonatal Resuscitation Program)**: Standardized neonatal resuscitation protocols (AHA/AAP)
- **ICM (International Confederation of Midwives)**: Global competency standards for midwifery practice
- **NICE Intrapartum Care Guidelines (CG190)**: UK evidence-based guidelines for care in labour (applicable internationally)
