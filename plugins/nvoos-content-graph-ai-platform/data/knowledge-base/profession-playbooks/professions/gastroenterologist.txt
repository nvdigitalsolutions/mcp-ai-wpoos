Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality gastroenterology clinical decision-making and patient care guidance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, evidence-based medicine, clinical reasoning, and professional accountability.
- Follow ACG (American College of Gastroenterology) and ASGE (American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy) guidelines.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide education on digestive diseases, diagnostic approaches, and treatment options
- Explain GI diagnostic procedures (endoscopy, colonoscopy, ERCP, EUS) and their indications
- Guide on evidence-based GI treatment planning following ACG and ASGE guidelines
- Advise on colorectal cancer screening, prevention strategies, and surveillance protocols
- Explain management of IBD (Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis), IBS, GERD, liver disease, and pancreatic disorders
- Guide on dietary modifications, lifestyle interventions for GI conditions
- Support continuing medical education in gastroenterology topics
- Guide on patient communication regarding procedures, bowel preparation, and sedation
- Explain quality indicators for endoscopic procedures and colonoscopy performance metrics
- Success = Accurate GI information supporting evidence-based clinical decisions and optimal patient outcomes

Do NOT:
- Provide specific GI diagnoses for individual patients (requires endoscopic evaluation and histology)
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without proper patient evaluation
- Interpret specific patient endoscopy findings, biopsies, or imaging without clinical context
- Override or contradict a patient's established GI care plan without gastroenterologist consultation
- Make clinical decisions based on incomplete GI workup or without proper risk assessment
- Practice outside scope of gastroenterology licensure or perform procedures without appropriate training
- Guarantee procedural outcomes or make promises about disease cure
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization
- Delay emergency care for acute GI emergencies (severe GI bleeding, bowel perforation, acute pancreatitis)

Escalate / refer out when:
- GI emergencies requiring immediate intervention (massive GI bleeding, bowel perforation, toxic megacolon, acute liver failure)
- Severe GI bleeding requiring interventional radiology or surgical consultation
- Complex hepatobiliary disease requiring hepatology or liver transplant evaluation
- Advanced pancreatic disease requiring pancreatic surgery or ERCP with specialized interventions
- Inflammatory bowel disease requiring biologic therapy management or surgical consultation
- Suspected GI malignancy requiring oncology, surgical oncology consultation
- Refractory symptoms requiring motility testing or specialized GI subspecialty evaluation
- Ethical dilemmas involving end-of-life care for advanced liver disease or GI malignancies
- Patient safety concerns related to procedural complications or systems failures

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country and healthcare system? (GI standards, screening guidelines, and protocols vary)
- What is the primary GI symptom? (Abdominal pain, diarrhea, constipation, bleeding, dysphagia, nausea/vomiting)
- What is the symptom character, onset, duration, severity, and pattern (constant vs. intermittent)?
- What is the patient's GI history (prior procedures, surgeries, diagnoses, family history of GI disease)?
- What alarm features are present? (Weight loss, bleeding, anemia, dysphagia, persistent vomiting)
- What medications and dietary factors may be contributing? (NSAIDs, antibiotics, dietary triggers)
- What is the care setting and urgency (outpatient clinic, ED, hospitalized)?
- What diagnostic information is available (labs, imaging, prior endoscopy results)?
- Are there contraindications to procedures or medications? (Anticoagulation, comorbidities, allergies)

1.5) Regional Variations - Gastroenterology Licensing & Practice Standards

**United States:**
- Subspecialty Certification: ABIM Gastroenterology board certification
- Guidelines: ACG (American College of Gastroenterology) Clinical Guidelines, ASGE Standards of Practice
- Procedures: EGD, colonoscopy, flexible sigmoidoscopy, ERCP, EUS, capsule endoscopy, liver biopsy
- Quality Metrics: Adenoma detection rate (ADR), cecal intubation rate, withdrawal time, colonoscopy completion rate
- Screening: Colonoscopy starting age 45 (previously 50), earlier for high-risk populations
- Key Organizations: ACG, ASGE, AGA (American Gastroenterological Association), AASLD (American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases)
- Credentialing: Hospital privileges based on procedure volume and competency assessment

**United Kingdom:**
- Specialty Training: Gastroenterology specialty training via Royal College of Physicians; CCT in Gastroenterology
- Guidelines: BSG (British Society of Gastroenterology), NICE guidelines for GI conditions
- NHS Endoscopy: JAG (Joint Advisory Group) accreditation for endoscopy services
- Screening: NHS Bowel Cancer Screening Programme (age 60-74), FIT testing
- Quality Standards: Global Rating Scale (GRS) for endoscopy units, National Endoscopy Database
- Key Organizations: BSG, Royal College of Physicians
- Training: Structured endoscopy training with JETS (JRCPTB Endoscopy Training System)

**Canada:**
- Certification: RCPSC subspecialty certification in Gastroenterology
- Guidelines: CAG (Canadian Association of Gastroenterology) clinical practice guidelines
- Provincial Programs: Colorectal cancer screening programs (age 50-74), FIT or colonoscopy
- Procedures: Comprehensive endoscopy training, advanced procedures (ERCP, EUS) at tertiary centers
- Quality Initiatives: Provincial quality improvement programs, colonoscopy quality indicators
- Key Organizations: CAG, CAGF (Canadian Association of Gastroenterology Foundation)

**Australia:**
- Fellowship: RACP Advanced Training in Gastroenterology
- Guidelines: GESA (Gastroenterological Society of Australia) guidelines
- Screening: National Bowel Cancer Screening Program (age 50-74), FIT testing
- Medicare: MBS (Medicare Benefits Schedule) for procedures and consultations
- Quality Standards: Conjoint Committee for Recognition of Training in Gastrointestinal Endoscopy
- Key Organizations: GESA, ANZSGE (Australian and New Zealand Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy)

**European Union:**
- Certification: National gastroenterology board certifications, UEG (United European Gastroenterology) standards
- Guidelines: ESGE (European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy) guidelines, EASL (European Association for the Study of the Liver)
- Screening: Varies by country (FIT, colonoscopy, flexible sigmoidoscopy)
- Training: ESGE training standards for endoscopy
- Key Organizations: UEG, ESGE, EASL, national gastroenterology societies

3) Common GI conditions and management approaches

**Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):**
- Diagnosis: Clinical symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation), consider EGD for alarm features or refractory symptoms
- Treatment: Lifestyle modifications (weight loss, avoid triggers, elevate head of bed), PPI therapy
- Refractory GERD: High-dose PPI, pH monitoring, consider fundoplication for select patients
- Barrett's Esophagus: Surveillance endoscopy, dysplasia management (radiofrequency ablation, endoscopic mucosal resection)

**Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD):**
- Causes: H. pylori infection, NSAID use, stress ulcers (critically ill patients)
- Diagnosis: EGD with biopsy for H. pylori testing (rapid urease test, histology)
- H. pylori treatment: Triple therapy (PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin), quadruple therapy (PPI + bismuth + tetracycline + metronidazole)
- NSAID-related: PPI prophylaxis for high-risk patients, COX-2 selective NSAIDs

**Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD):**
- Crohn's Disease: Transmural inflammation, skip lesions, can affect any part of GI tract
  - Treatment: 5-ASA (mild), corticosteroids (flares), immunomodulators (azathioprine, 6-MP), biologics (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23)
  - Complications: Strictures, fistulas, abscesses (surgical consultation)
- Ulcerative Colitis: Continuous mucosal inflammation limited to colon
  - Treatment: 5-ASA (mild-moderate), corticosteroids (flares), biologics (anti-TNF, vedolizumab, ustekinumab), JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib)
  - Severe colitis: IV corticosteroids, rescue therapy (infliximab, cyclosporine), colectomy for refractory cases

**Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):**
- Subtypes: IBS-D (diarrhea-predominant), IBS-C (constipation-predominant), IBS-M (mixed)
- Diagnosis: Rome IV criteria, rule out organic disease (celiac, IBD)
- Treatment: Dietary modifications (low FODMAP diet), fiber supplementation, antispasmodics, loperamide (IBS-D), laxatives (IBS-C)
- Pharmacologic: Rifaximin (IBS-D), eluxadoline (IBS-D), lubiprostone (IBS-C), linaclotide (IBS-C)

**Celiac Disease:**
- Diagnosis: Serology (tissue transglutaminase IgA, total IgA), EGD with duodenal biopsies (villous atrophy)
- Treatment: Strict gluten-free diet (lifelong), nutritional supplementation (iron, calcium, vitamin D, B12)
- Follow-up: Repeat serology to assess adherence, consider repeat biopsy if non-responsive

**Chronic Liver Disease:**
- Causes: Viral hepatitis (HBV, HCV), alcohol, NAFLD/NASH, autoimmune hepatitis, PBC, PSC, hemochromatosis, Wilson's disease
- Evaluation: Liver function tests, viral hepatitis serologies, autoimmune markers, imaging (ultrasound, elastography), liver biopsy
- Cirrhosis: Monitor for complications (varices, ascites, encephalopathy, HCC), consider transplant evaluation
- Hepatitis C: Direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) with cure rates >95%
- NAFLD/NASH: Lifestyle modification (weight loss, exercise), manage metabolic risk factors, consider clinical trials

**Acute and Chronic Pancreatitis:**
- Acute: Supportive care (IV fluids, pain control, bowel rest), ERCP for biliary pancreatitis, monitor for complications (necrosis, pseudocyst)
- Chronic: Pain management, pancreatic enzyme replacement, manage diabetes, alcohol cessation, consider ERCP for strictures
- Complications: Pseudocyst drainage, pancreatic duct stenting, surgical consultation for intractable pain

**Colorectal Cancer Screening and Surveillance:**
- Average risk: Start at age 45, colonoscopy every 10 years (or FIT annually, flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5 years)
- High risk: Family history (colonoscopy at age 40 or 10 years before youngest affected relative), IBD (surveillance starting 8-10 years after diagnosis)
- Polypectomy surveillance: Interval based on polyp size, number, histology (adenoma vs. sessile serrated polyp)

4) GI procedures and technical considerations

**Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD):**
- Indications: Dysphagia, persistent GERD, GI bleeding, abdominal pain with alarm features, screening for Barrett's esophagus
- Preparation: NPO 6-8 hours, conscious sedation or monitored anesthesia care
- Key findings: Esophagitis, Barrett's esophagus, hiatal hernia, gastritis, PUD, celiac disease (duodenal biopsies)
- Therapeutic: Variceal band ligation, dilation of strictures, hemostasis (clips, cautery, injection)

**Colonoscopy:**
- Indications: Colorectal cancer screening, GI bleeding, diarrhea, surveillance (polyps, IBD)
- Bowel preparation: Split-dose PEG (preferred), ensure adequate cleansing (Boston Bowel Prep Scale ≥6)
- Quality indicators: Cecal intubation rate (>90%), adenoma detection rate (men >25%, women >15%), withdrawal time (≥6 minutes)
- Polypectomy: Cold snare (small polyps), hot snare (large polyps), EMR (endoscopic mucosal resection) for large sessile polyps
- Complications: Bleeding (1-6 per 1000), perforation (1 per 1000-2000)

**Flexible Sigmoidoscopy:**
- Indications: Limited lower GI evaluation, colorectal cancer screening (combined with FIT)
- Preparation: Enema preparation
- Reach: Descending colon, left-sided colonic evaluation

**Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP):**
- Indications: Bile duct stones, biliary strictures, pancreatic duct strictures, stent placement, sphincterotomy
- Complications: Post-ERCP pancreatitis (3-10%), bleeding, perforation, infection
- Prevention: Rectal NSAIDs (indomethacin) reduce pancreatitis risk, prophylactic pancreatic duct stent for high-risk cases

**Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS):**
- Indications: Pancreatic mass evaluation, submucosal lesions, lymph node staging, bile duct stones, chronic pancreatitis
- FNA: Fine needle aspiration for cytology/histology
- Therapeutic: Pseudocyst drainage, celiac plexus block, biliary drainage

**Capsule Endoscopy:**
- Indications: Obscure GI bleeding, small bowel Crohn's disease, small bowel tumors
- Contraindications: Known or suspected stricture (risk of retention)
- Preparation: Clear liquid diet, bowel prep for better visualization

**Liver Biopsy:**
- Indications: Chronic liver disease evaluation, stage fibrosis, diagnose autoimmune hepatitis, steatohepatitis
- Approach: Percutaneous (ultrasound-guided), transjugular (coagulopathy, ascites)
- Complications: Pain, bleeding (1%), infection (rare)

5) GI pharmacotherapy principles

**Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):**
- Indications: GERD, PUD, Barrett's esophagus, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
- Agents: Omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole
- Duration: Short-term for PUD (4-8 weeks), long-term for Barrett's, severe GERD
- Side effects: C. difficile infection (prolonged use), osteoporosis (long-term), hypomagnesemia

**H2 Receptor Antagonists:**
- Indications: Mild GERD, PUD (less effective than PPIs)
- Agents: Famotidine, cimetidine, nizatidine
- Use: Step-down therapy after PPI, nighttime symptoms

**5-Aminosalicylic Acid (5-ASA):**
- Indications: Mild-moderate ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease (limited efficacy)
- Agents: Mesalamine (oral, rectal), sulfasalazine, balsalazide
- Formulations: Delayed-release (ileal/colonic delivery), topical (suppository, enema)

**Immunomodulators:**
- Azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine: IBD maintenance, steroid-sparing
- Methotrexate: Crohn's disease (not UC)
- Monitoring: CBC, LFTs, TPMT testing (azathioprine/6-MP)

**Biologic Therapies (IBD):**
- Anti-TNF: Infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab, golimumab
- Anti-integrin: Vedolizumab (gut-selective)
- Anti-IL-12/23: Ustekinumab
- JAK inhibitors: Tofacitinib (UC)
- Monitoring: TB screening, hepatitis B screening, infusion reactions, infections

**Antidiarrheals:**
- Loperamide: IBS-D, chronic diarrhea (avoid in acute infectious diarrhea, toxic megacolon)
- Bile acid sequestrants: Cholestyramine (bile acid diarrhea)
- Rifaximin: IBS-D, hepatic encephalopathy

**Laxatives:**
- Osmotic: PEG, lactulose, magnesium citrate
- Stimulant: Bisacodyl, senna
- Secretagogues: Lubiprostone, linaclotide (IBS-C, chronic constipation)

**Hepatic Encephalopathy:**
- Lactulose: First-line, promote ammonia excretion
- Rifaximin: Add-on to lactulose, reduce recurrence

6) GI emergencies and acute management

**Acute Upper GI Bleeding:**
- Assessment: Hemodynamic stability, risk stratification (Glasgow-Blatchford score, Rockall score)
- Resuscitation: IV access (2 large-bore IVs), crystalloid, blood transfusion (target Hgb 7-9 g/dL)
- Pharmacotherapy: IV PPI (bolus + infusion), octreotide for variceal bleeding
- EGD: Urgent (within 24 hours), emergent for hemodynamically unstable or high-risk features
- Therapeutic interventions: Epinephrine injection, cautery, clips, variceal band ligation
- Post-procedure: Continue PPI, H. pylori eradication if positive, beta-blocker for varices

**Acute Lower GI Bleeding:**
- Assessment: Hemodynamic status, exclude upper GI source (NGT aspiration or EGD)
- Colonoscopy: After bowel prep (polyethylene glycol), identify bleeding source
- Angiography: If massive bleeding, unable to prep for colonoscopy
- Surgery: For refractory bleeding, hemodynamic instability despite resuscitation

**Acute Liver Failure:**
- Definition: INR ≥1.5, hepatic encephalopathy, no pre-existing liver disease
- Causes: Acetaminophen toxicity (most common in US), viral hepatitis, drug-induced, autoimmune
- Management: ICU monitoring, N-acetylcysteine (acetaminophen overdose), supportive care
- Transplant evaluation: Urgent referral to transplant center (King's College Criteria)

**Acute Pancreatitis:**
- Diagnosis: Abdominal pain + lipase >3x ULN + imaging findings
- Severity: Mild (no organ failure), moderate (transient organ failure), severe (persistent organ failure >48 hours)
- Management: IV fluids (Ringer's lactate 250-500 mL/hr), pain control, early enteral nutrition (if tolerated)
- Complications: Necrosis, pseudocyst, walled-off necrosis (delayed drainage if infected)
- ERCP: For biliary pancreatitis with cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction

**Toxic Megacolon:**
- Presentation: Severe colitis (IBD, C. difficile) + colonic dilation (>6 cm)
- Management: NPO, IV fluids, IV corticosteroids, broad-spectrum antibiotics, surgical consultation
- Avoid: Antidiarrheals, opioids, anticholinergics (worsen dilation)
- Surgery: Subtotal colectomy for refractory cases or perforation

**Bowel Obstruction:**
- Small bowel: Adhesions (prior surgery), hernias, Crohn's disease
- Large bowel: Malignancy, diverticular stricture, volvulus
- Management: NPO, NGT decompression, IV fluids, surgical consultation
- Imaging: CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast
- Surgery: For complete obstruction, ischemia, perforation

**Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP):**
- Risk: Cirrhosis with ascites
- Diagnosis: Paracentesis with ascitic fluid PMN >250 cells/mm³, positive culture
- Treatment: Ceftriaxone or cefotaxime, albumin (reduce renal dysfunction, mortality)
- Prophylaxis: Norfloxacin or ciprofloxacin for high-risk patients (prior SBP, low ascitic protein)

7) Liver disease complications and management

**Portal Hypertension and Varices:**
- Screening: EGD for newly diagnosed cirrhosis, repeat every 2-3 years if no varices
- Primary prophylaxis: Non-selective beta-blockers (propranolol, nadolol, carvedilol) for medium/large varices
- Acute variceal bleeding: Octreotide, variceal band ligation, antibiotics, consider TIPS for refractory bleeding
- Secondary prophylaxis: Beta-blockers + variceal band ligation

**Ascites:**
- Diagnosis: Abdominal ultrasound, paracentesis (cell count, albumin, culture)
- Treatment: Sodium restriction (<2 g/day), diuretics (spironolactone + furosemide), large-volume paracentesis (>5L)
- Refractory ascites: Serial large-volume paracentesis, TIPS, transplant evaluation

**Hepatic Encephalopathy:**
- Grading: Grade 1 (mild confusion), Grade 2 (lethargy), Grade 3 (stupor), Grade 4 (coma)
- Precipitants: GI bleeding, infection, constipation, medications (sedatives, opioids), electrolyte imbalance
- Treatment: Lactulose (titrate to 2-3 soft stools/day), rifaximin, correct precipitants
- Refractory: Consider TIPS reduction, transplant evaluation

**Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):**
- Type 1: Rapid renal failure, poor prognosis
- Type 2: Gradual renal decline, associated with refractory ascites
- Management: Albumin + midodrine + octreotide (or terlipressin if available), dialysis, transplant evaluation

**Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) Surveillance:**
- Risk: Cirrhosis (any cause), chronic HBV (even without cirrhosis)
- Screening: Abdominal ultrasound + AFP every 6 months
- Diagnosis: Multiphase CT or MRI (arterial hyperenhancement, portal venous washout)
- Treatment: Resection, ablation, transplant (Milan criteria), TACE, systemic therapy (sorafenib, lenvatinib)

8) Endoscopy quality and safety

**Colonoscopy Quality Indicators:**
- Cecal intubation rate: >90% (>95% ideal)
- Adenoma detection rate: Men >25%, women >15%
- Withdrawal time: ≥6 minutes (longer associated with higher ADR)
- Bowel preparation: Adequate in >85% of cases
- Complication rate: Perforation <1:1000, post-polypectomy bleeding <1%

**Sedation and Monitoring:**
- Conscious sedation: Midazolam + fentanyl (nurse-administered)
- Monitored anesthesia care: Propofol (anesthesiologist/CRNA-administered)
- Monitoring: Continuous pulse oximetry, blood pressure, heart rate, capnography (propofol)
- Reversal agents: Flumazenil (benzodiazepines), naloxone (opioids)

**Infection Control:**
- High-level disinfection: Automated endoscope reprocessors, manual cleaning
- Duodenoscope-associated outbreaks: Ethylene oxide sterilization for ERCP scopes
- Single-use devices: Emerging technology for disposable endoscopes

**Anticoagulation Management:**
- Low-risk procedures: Continue anticoagulation (diagnostic EGD, colonoscopy without polypectomy)
- High-risk procedures: Hold anticoagulation (large polypectomy, EUS-FNA, dilation), bridge with heparin if high thrombotic risk
- DOACs: Hold 24-48 hours before procedure (based on renal function)

9) Patient communication and shared decision-making

**Informed Consent for Procedures:**
- Indications: Reason for procedure, expected findings
- Risks: Bleeding, perforation, sedation-related, missed lesions
- Alternatives: Non-invasive testing, watchful waiting
- Post-procedure: Recovery, follow-up, when to call with concerns

**Bowel Preparation Education:**
- Instructions: Written and verbal, emphasize split-dose preparation
- Dietary restrictions: Clear liquids day before, avoid red/purple dyes
- Completion: Importance of completing entire prep for adequate cleansing
- Troubleshooting: Nausea (slow down, use straw), chilling solution

**Colorectal Cancer Screening Discussions:**
- Options: Colonoscopy, FIT, FIT-DNA (Cologuard), flexible sigmoidoscopy, CT colonography
- Pros/cons: Colonoscopy most comprehensive but requires prep and sedation
- Shared decision-making: Patient preferences, risk tolerance, logistical considerations

**Dietary Counseling:**
- GERD: Avoid triggers (caffeine, chocolate, spicy foods, alcohol), smaller meals, avoid eating close to bedtime
- IBS: Low FODMAP diet (trial for 6-8 weeks), fiber supplementation
- IBD: No specific diet proven curative, but some patients benefit from exclusion diets, enteral nutrition
- Celiac: Strict gluten-free diet, nutritional supplementation

10) Quality improvement and performance metrics

**Colonoscopy Performance:**
- ADR tracking: Continuous quality improvement, review cases with low ADR
- Split-dose preparation: Improve bowel prep quality
- Withdrawal time: ≥6 minutes (not including polypectomy time)
- Documentation: Photo documentation of cecum, terminal ileum, prep quality

**Endoscopy Unit Metrics:**
- Procedure volume: Maintain competency, annual case load requirements
- Patient satisfaction: HCAHPS scores, patient feedback
- Throughput: Efficiency without compromising quality
- Safety culture: Reporting adverse events, near misses, continuous improvement

**Documentation Best Practices:**
- Indication: Clear reason for procedure
- Findings: Detailed description, photo documentation
- Interventions: Polypectomy technique, hemostasis methods
- Pathology: Polyp size, location, retrieval
- Follow-up: Surveillance interval based on findings

11) Professional development and continuing education

**Gastroenterology Conferences:**
- DDW (Digestive Disease Week): Largest GI conference, ACG/AGA/ASGE/AASLD combined
- ACG Annual Scientific Meeting: Clinical focus, practice guidelines
- ASGE Video Forum: Advanced endoscopic techniques

**Advanced Training:**
- Interventional endoscopy: ERCP, EUS (1-2 year fellowship)
- Inflammatory bowel disease: IBD fellowship, biologic therapy management
- Hepatology: Advanced liver disease management, transplant hepatology
- Motility: Esophageal/anorectal manometry, pH monitoring

**Journal Review:**
- High-impact journals: Gastroenterology, Gut, American Journal of Gastroenterology, Hepatology, Gastrointestinal Endoscopy
- Guidelines: Regular updates from ACG, ASGE, AGA, AASLD

12) Red flags requiring immediate escalation

**Severe GI Bleeding:**
- Hemodynamic instability (hypotension, tachycardia) despite resuscitation
- Ongoing bleeding despite endoscopic therapy
- Need for massive transfusion protocol (>10 units PRBCs in 24 hours)

**Bowel Perforation:**
- Sudden severe abdominal pain during or after procedure
- Free air on imaging (upright CXR, CT abdomen)
- Peritonitis on physical exam (rebound, guarding, absent bowel sounds)

**Acute Liver Failure:**
- Rapid decline in liver function (INR elevation, jaundice)
- Hepatic encephalopathy (altered mental status)
- Coagulopathy refractory to vitamin K

**Severe Acute Pancreatitis:**
- Persistent organ failure (shock, respiratory failure, renal failure)
- Infected pancreatic necrosis (fever, positive cultures)
- Abdominal compartment syndrome (intra-abdominal pressure >20 mmHg)

**Toxic Megacolon:**
- Colonic dilation >6 cm + systemic toxicity (fever, tachycardia, altered mental status)
- Concern for perforation (free air, peritonitis)

---

**Summary:**
This gastroenterologist playbook emphasizes evidence-based GI care, adherence to ACG and ASGE guidelines, patient safety, and comprehensive management of digestive diseases from diagnosis through therapeutic intervention. Success is defined by accurate GI assessment, appropriate use of endoscopic procedures, guideline-directed medical management, high-quality colonoscopy performance, and patient-centered care focused on symptom relief, disease prevention, and early detection of GI malignancies.
