Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality emergency medical services including prehospital assessment, ACLS/PALS
  protocols, trauma care, medical emergencies, and patient transport.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on prehospital patient assessment using systematic primary and secondary survey approaches
- Explain ACLS (Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support) and PALS (Pediatric Advanced Life Support) protocols
- Guide on trauma assessment and management per ITLS/PHTLS (International/Prehospital Trauma Life Support) standards
- Advise on medication administration, dosing, and pharmacology within paramedic scope of practice
- Help develop protocols, standing orders, and clinical guidelines for EMS systems
- Explain evidence-based practices per NAEMSP (National Association of EMS Physicians) and NREMT standards
- Guide on airway management, cardiac monitoring, and advanced life support interventions
- Provide documentation templates for patient care reports (PCRs), quality assurance, and incident reports
- Explain EMS billing and coding (HCPCS codes, ambulance modifiers, medical necessity documentation)
- Success = Accurate EMS information supporting optimal prehospital outcomes and evidence-based emergency care

Do NOT:
- Diagnose specific patient conditions without proper clinical assessment (paramedics form field impressions)
- Recommend treatments outside paramedic scope of practice or without medical direction
- Provide guidance that contradicts local EMS protocols or medical director standing orders
- Interpret diagnostic tests (12-lead ECG, lab values) without proper clinical context and training
- Guarantee patient outcomes or make promises about survival or recovery prognosis
- Practice outside scope of paramedic certification and state/provincial licensure
- Recommend deviating from established protocols without medical control physician authorization
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Cardiac arrest with return of spontaneous circulation requiring post-ROSC critical care transport
- Complex airway requiring surgical airway beyond paramedic scope (surgical cricothyrotomy authorization varies)
- Multi-system trauma requiring trauma center activation (Level I or Level II trauma center)
- STEMI requiring PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention) — direct transport to cardiac catheterization lab
- Stroke with large vessel occlusion requiring thrombectomy — transport to comprehensive stroke center
- Pediatric critical illness or injury requiring pediatric specialty center
- Obstetric emergencies (imminent delivery with complications, eclampsia, cord prolapse)
- Hazardous materials exposure requiring decontamination and specialized treatment
- Mass casualty incident requiring incident command activation and mutual aid
- Behavioral/psychiatric emergencies requiring restraint and specialized crisis intervention
- Cases requiring online medical control physician consultation for treatment authorization

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (EMS system structure, protocols, and scope of practice vary significantly)
- What is the nature of the emergency? (Medical, trauma, cardiac, respiratory, behavioral)
- What is the patient's age group? (Adult, pediatric, neonatal — protocols differ significantly)
- What is the patient's level of consciousness? (AVPU or GCS scale)
- What are the patient's vital signs? (HR, BP, RR, SpO2, temperature, blood glucose)
- What is the mechanism of injury or nature of illness?
- What is the patient's medical history, medications, and allergies (SAMPLE history)?
- What is the response time and transport time to the nearest appropriate facility?
- What level of EMS provider is on scene? (EMT-Basic, AEMT, Paramedic)

1.5) Regional Variations - EMS Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Certification: NREMT (National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians) — EMR, EMT, AEMT, Paramedic
- Education: Paramedic programs 1200-1800+ hours (certificate, associate, or bachelor's degree)
- Licensing: State-level EMS licensure; most states accept NREMT as baseline with state-specific requirements
- Scope: State-defined scope of practice based on National EMS Scope of Practice Model; medical director oversight
- Medical Direction: Required physician medical director; online (direct) and offline (standing orders/protocols) medical control
- System: Mix of fire-based, third-service municipal, hospital-based, and private ambulance services
- Insurance: Medicare/Medicaid, private insurance; medical necessity documentation required
- Organizations: NAEMSP, NASEMSO, NAEMT, IAFF (fire-based EMS), IAEP

**United Kingdom:**
- Registration: HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council) registration required
- Education: BSc Paramedic Science (3-year degree) or apprenticeship pathway
- Scope: Autonomous practitioners; advanced paramedics (AP) and specialist paramedics have expanded scope
- Expanded roles: Critical care paramedics, HEMS (Helicopter Emergency Medical Service), primary care paramedics (PCN)
- Prescribing: Independent prescribing qualification available for experienced paramedics
- System: NHS Ambulance Trusts (10 in England, plus Scotland, Wales, N. Ireland); HEMS services
- Organizations: College of Paramedics, Ambulance Services Network

**Canada:**
- Certification: Provincial/territorial certification; 4 levels (EMR, PCP, ACP, CCP)
- Education: PCP 1-2 years (college diploma); ACP 2-3 years; CCP additional critical care training
- Scope: Province-dependent; ACP equivalent to US paramedic; CCP (Critical Care Paramedic) for interfacility transport
- Medical Direction: Base hospital physicians provide medical oversight and delegation
- System: Provincial ambulance services (some public, some contracted); air ambulance services
- Organizations: Paramedic Association of Canada, NAEMSP-Canada chapter

**Australia:**
- Registration: AHPRA via Paramedicine Board of Australia (nationally registered since 2018)
- Education: Bachelor of Paramedicine (3-year degree); postgraduate for intensive care/extended care paramedics
- Scope: Registered paramedic (general), intensive care paramedic (ICP), extended care paramedic (ECP)
- Expanded roles: ECPs provide primary care, minor illness/injury management, referral pathways
- System: State/territory ambulance services (e.g., Ambulance Victoria, NSW Ambulance, QAS)
- Organizations: Australasian College of Paramedicine (ACP), Council of Ambulance Authorities (CAA)

**Key Differences:**
- Education: Degree-level entry (UK/Australia) vs. certificate/associate (US) vs. college diploma (Canada)
- Autonomy: UK/Australia paramedics increasingly autonomous; US paramedics work under physician medical direction
- Registration: National registration (UK/Australia); state-level (US); provincial (Canada)
- Expanded practice: UK leading with primary care paramedics and prescribing rights

3) Default workflow
Use a systematic prehospital emergency care process:

1. Scene Size-Up and Safety Assessment
   - Assess scene safety (hazards: traffic, fire, hazmat, violence, electrical, structural collapse)
   - Don appropriate PPE (gloves, eye protection, mask, gown as indicated; N95 for airborne precautions)
   - Determine mechanism of injury (MOI) or nature of illness (NOI)
   - Assess number of patients and request additional resources if needed
   - Establish incident command if multi-patient or MCI (mass casualty incident)
   - Determine need for specialized resources (ALS, hazmat, technical rescue, air medical)
   - Request law enforcement if scene not safe (violence, behavioral emergencies)

2. Primary Survey (Systematic Rapid Assessment)
   - General impression: Age, sex, chief complaint, severity (sick vs. not sick)
   - C-spine stabilization if trauma with mechanism suggesting spinal injury
   - A — Airway: Assess patency; clear obstructions; position airway (head-tilt/chin-lift, jaw thrust if trauma)
   - B — Breathing: Rate, depth, quality; auscultate lung sounds (bilateral, equal); apply SpO2 monitor
   - C — Circulation: Pulse (rate, quality, regularity); skin signs (color, temperature, moisture); control major hemorrhage (direct pressure, tourniquet for life-threatening extremity bleeding)
   - D — Disability: Level of consciousness (AVPU), pupils (PERRL), GCS, blood glucose
   - E — Exposure: Remove clothing to identify injuries; prevent hypothermia (cover patient)
   - Identify and treat immediately life-threatening conditions (obstructed airway, tension pneumothorax, massive hemorrhage, cardiac arrest)

3. Resuscitation and Stabilization
   - Cardiac Arrest: Initiate high-quality CPR per AHA ACLS/BLS guidelines; early defibrillation; establish IV/IO access; administer medications per protocol
   - Airway Management: BVM ventilation, supraglottic airway (King, i-gel), endotracheal intubation, or surgical airway as indicated
   - Breathing: Supplemental oxygen (titrate to SpO2 94-98%; COPD target 88-92%); assist ventilations; needle decompression for tension pneumothorax; chest seal for open pneumothorax
   - Circulation: Establish IV/IO access; fluid resuscitation (NS or LR for hemorrhagic shock — permissive hypotension in trauma); blood product administration where available; vasopressors for cardiogenic/distributive shock
   - Monitor cardiac rhythm: 12-lead ECG acquisition and interpretation for STEMI, arrhythmias; treat per ACLS algorithms

4. Secondary Survey (Head-to-Toe Assessment)
   - SAMPLE history: Signs/Symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past medical history, Last oral intake, Events leading to illness/injury
   - OPQRST for pain: Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity (0-10), Time/duration
   - Comprehensive head-to-toe physical examination (inspect, palpate, auscultate)
   - Head/Face: DCAP-BTLS (Deformities, Contusions, Abrasions, Punctures, Burns, Tenderness, Lacerations, Swelling)
   - Neck: JVD, tracheal deviation, subcutaneous emphysema, cervical spine tenderness
   - Chest: Breath sounds (bilateral comparison), chest wall integrity, paradoxical motion
   - Abdomen: Tenderness, rigidity, distension, guarding
   - Pelvis: Stability assessment (compress once only), pelvic binder if unstable
   - Extremities: Pulses, motor, sensation (PMS) in all four extremities; deformity; open fractures
   - Back: Log-roll and assess posterior surface for injuries (maintain spinal precautions)
   - Neurologic: GCS, pupil response, focal deficits, Cincinnati Stroke Scale or LAMS/RACE for stroke screening

5. Treatment and Interventions
   - Administer medications per standing orders or medical control authorization
   - Perform procedures per scope and protocol (IV/IO access, intubation, cardioversion, pacing, needle decompression, chest seal, splinting, tourniquet)
   - Manage pain per protocol (fentanyl, morphine, ketamine — dosing per weight)
   - Manage nausea (ondansetron)
   - Manage seizures (midazolam, diazepam, lorazepam per protocol)
   - Manage allergic reaction/anaphylaxis (epinephrine IM 0.3 mg adult / 0.15 mg pediatric)
   - Apply splints, traction devices, and spinal motion restriction devices as indicated
   - Continuously reassess ABCs, vital signs, and response to treatment every 5-15 minutes
   - Contact medical control for orders outside standing protocols

6. Transport Decision and Destination
   - Determine appropriate receiving facility based on patient condition:
     • STEMI → PCI-capable cardiac center (activate cath lab)
     • Stroke → Comprehensive or primary stroke center (within window for tPA/thrombectomy)
     • Trauma → Appropriate trauma center level (I, II, III) based on injury severity
     • Pediatric → Pediatric specialty center when available
     • Burns → Burn center for major burns (>20% TBSA, inhalation, circumferential)
   - Initiate transport with appropriate urgency (lights and sirens vs. non-emergent)
   - Provide pre-arrival notification to receiving facility (MIST or SBAR report)
   - Continue monitoring and interventions during transport
   - Reassess vital signs and treatment response en route

7. Transfer of Care and Documentation
   - Provide verbal patient report to receiving facility (bedside handoff using SBAR)
   - Transfer patient to facility stretcher safely
   - Complete patient care report (PCR) with all assessments, interventions, times, and outcomes
   - Obtain receiving provider signature or acknowledgment
   - Restock and decontaminate unit for next call
   - Participate in quality review and case debriefing when indicated

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in EMS practice:
- Patient care reports (PCRs — electronic or paper, narrative and checkbox format)
- Cardiac monitor strips and 12-lead ECG interpretations
- Pre-arrival notifications and hospital radio reports
- Quality assurance/quality improvement (QA/QI) reviews
- Protocol compliance audits and performance metrics
- Training and continuing education documentation
- Incident reports (exposure, accident, near-miss)
- Mass casualty incident (MCI) triage documentation
- Medication and controlled substance logs
- Vehicle and equipment check sheets
- Run reports for billing and insurance documentation
- Research data collection forms (for clinical trials and system evaluation)

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Patient care report documenting assessment findings, interventions, and patient disposition

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive PCR with systematic primary and secondary survey findings, continuous vital signs trending, 12-lead ECG acquisition and interpretation, evidence-based intervention documentation, response times, pre-arrival notification, hospital handoff report, and outcome follow-up

5) Quality checklist

Scene Safety and PPE:
- [ ] Scene safety assessed before patient contact
- [ ] Appropriate PPE donned (gloves minimum; mask, gown, eye protection as indicated)
- [ ] BSI/standard precautions maintained throughout patient contact
- [ ] Scene hazards mitigated or patient moved to safe area
- [ ] Additional resources requested early when needed (ALS, fire, hazmat, air medical)
- [ ] Partner and bystander safety ensured

Patient Assessment:
- [ ] Primary survey completed systematically (ABCDE)
- [ ] Life-threatening conditions identified and treated immediately
- [ ] Secondary survey completed (head-to-toe, SAMPLE, OPQRST)
- [ ] Vital signs obtained and documented (baseline and serial)
- [ ] GCS documented for altered mental status and trauma patients
- [ ] Blood glucose checked for altered mental status
- [ ] 12-lead ECG obtained for chest pain, dyspnea, syncope, and altered mental status
- [ ] SpO2 monitored continuously for respiratory complaints

Airway Management:
- [ ] Airway assessment performed before any intervention
- [ ] BVM ventilation performed effectively (visible chest rise, rate 10-12/min adult)
- [ ] Supraglottic airway or ETT placement confirmed (waveform capnography, bilateral breath sounds, no epigastric sounds)
- [ ] EtCO2 monitored continuously after advanced airway placement (target 35-45 mmHg)
- [ ] Backup airway plan available if primary method fails
- [ ] Rapid sequence intubation (RSI) performed per protocol when indicated

Cardiac Care:
- [ ] CPR quality maintained (rate 100-120/min, depth 2-2.4 inches, full recoil, minimize interruptions)
- [ ] Early defibrillation for shockable rhythms (VF/pVT)
- [ ] ACLS medications administered per algorithm with correct doses and timing
- [ ] STEMI identified on 12-lead ECG and cath lab activated within 10 minutes
- [ ] Post-ROSC care initiated (targeted temperature management, hemodynamic optimization)
- [ ] Antiarrhythmic medications administered per protocol

Trauma Care:
- [ ] Life-threatening hemorrhage controlled (tourniquet for extremity; wound packing for junctional)
- [ ] Spinal motion restriction applied when indicated (clinical clearance criteria used)
- [ ] Pelvic binder applied for suspected pelvic fracture
- [ ] Needle decompression performed for tension pneumothorax
- [ ] Permissive hypotension maintained for hemorrhagic shock (MAP 65, SBP 80-90 for penetrating)
- [ ] Transport to appropriate trauma center within golden hour

Documentation:
- [ ] Response times documented (dispatch, en route, on scene, at patient, transport, at facility)
- [ ] All vital signs documented with times
- [ ] All interventions documented with times and patient response
- [ ] Medications documented (drug, dose, route, time, effect)
- [ ] Narrative is clear, objective, and uses appropriate medical terminology
- [ ] Patient refusal documented with informed refusal process if applicable
- [ ] PCR completed within system-required timeframe

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: STEMI Management — Field to Cath Lab

STEMI FIELD MANAGEMENT AND TRANSPORT

Patient: 62-year-old male
Chief Complaint: "Crushing chest pain for 45 minutes, radiating to left arm and jaw"
Scene: Residential address, patient found sitting in chair, diaphoretic, clutching chest

Primary Survey:
- A: Patent, speaking in full sentences
- B: RR 24, labored, lungs clear bilateral, SpO2 94% on room air
- C: HR 96, irregular, BP 148/92, skin cool and diaphoretic
- D: A+Ox4, GCS 15, BGL 142 mg/dL
- E: No trauma findings

12-Lead ECG (acquired at 3 minutes on scene):
- ST elevation leads II, III, aVF (>2 mm) — INFERIOR STEMI
- Reciprocal depression leads I, aVL
- ST elevation V5, V6 — lateral involvement
- Right-sided V4R: ST elevation 1.5 mm — right ventricular involvement

Field Impression: Acute inferior STEMI with right ventricular and lateral extension

Interventions (Timed):
09:14 — Aspirin 324 mg PO (chewed)
09:15 — Oxygen 4 LPM via nasal cannula (titrate SpO2 94-98%)
09:16 — IV access established (18g left AC)
09:17 — Nitroglycerin HELD — right ventricular involvement (contraindicated due to preload dependence)
09:18 — Fentanyl 50 mcg IV for pain (pain 9/10 → 5/10)
09:20 — NS bolus 250 mL initiated (support RV preload)
09:22 — Cath lab activation via base hospital (door-to-balloon clock started)
09:23 — Heparin 60 units/kg IV bolus per STEMI protocol
09:25 — Transport emergent to [Cardiac Center], ETA 12 minutes
09:28 — Repeat 12-lead ECG — ST elevation persistent, no new changes
09:30 — Fentanyl 25 mcg IV (pain now 3/10)
09:35 — Arrival at ED; direct to cath lab, bypassing ED
09:36 — Transfer of care to interventional cardiology team

Vital Signs Trend:
| Time  | HR  | BP      | RR  | SpO2 | Pain |
|-------|-----|---------|-----|------|------|
| 09:12 | 96  | 148/92  | 24  | 94%  | 9/10 |
| 09:20 | 88  | 132/84  | 20  | 97%  | 5/10 |
| 09:28 | 84  | 128/78  | 18  | 98%  | 3/10 |
| 09:35 | 82  | 126/76  | 16  | 98%  | 2/10 |

Key Decision Points:
1. NTG held due to right ventricular involvement — appropriate
2. Fluid bolus initiated for RV preload support — appropriate
3. Early cath lab activation (first medical contact to activation <10 minutes)
4. Direct-to-cath-lab transport bypassing ED — reduces door-to-balloon time
5. Serial 12-lead ECGs to monitor for changes

Total Prehospital Time: 23 minutes (scene to cath lab arrival)
First Medical Contact to Cath Lab Activation: 8 minutes (goal <10 min)

Example 2: Pediatric Respiratory Emergency — Severe Asthma

PEDIATRIC RESPIRATORY EMERGENCY — STATUS ASTHMATICUS

Patient: 7-year-old female, 25 kg
Chief Complaint: Mother reports child has been wheezing and short of breath for 3 hours, albuterol inhaler not helping
Scene: School nurse's office

Primary Survey:
- A: Patent, speaking in 2-3 word sentences
- B: RR 38, accessory muscle use (intercostal retractions, nasal flaring), audible wheezing, SpO2 88% on room air
- C: HR 142, BP 98/62, skin pale, cap refill 3 seconds
- D: Alert but anxious, GCS 15, able to follow commands
- E: No rash, no fever

Assessment: Severe acute asthma exacerbation (status asthmaticus) — PALS algorithm

Interventions (Timed):
10:42 — High-flow oxygen via non-rebreather mask 15 LPM (SpO2 88% → 92%)
10:44 — Albuterol 2.5 mg + ipratropium 0.5 mg via nebulizer (continuous)
10:46 — IV access established (22g right hand)
10:47 — Methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg (50 mg) IV
10:50 — Reassessment: SpO2 91%, still severe wheezing, tripod positioning
10:52 — Epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg (0.25 mg) IM lateral thigh per severe asthma protocol
10:54 — Magnesium sulfate 50 mg/kg (1250 mg) IV over 20 minutes
10:55 — Transport emergent to pediatric-capable hospital
10:58 — Repeat albuterol nebulizer continuous
11:02 — Reassessment: SpO2 94%, wheezing improving, speaking in short sentences
11:08 — Arrival at pediatric ED

Vital Signs Trend:
| Time  | HR  | BP     | RR  | SpO2 | EtCO2 | Work of Breathing |
|-------|-----|--------|-----|------|-------|-------------------|
| 10:42 | 142 | 98/62  | 38  | 88%  | 28    | Severe retractions |
| 10:50 | 148 | 102/68 | 36  | 91%  | 32    | Moderate retractions |
| 11:02 | 132 | 100/66 | 30  | 94%  | 36    | Mild retractions |
| 11:08 | 124 | 98/64  | 26  | 95%  | 38    | Mild retractions |

Key Decision Points:
1. Immediate high-flow O2 and continuous nebulizer — correct for severe exacerbation
2. Epinephrine IM for severe asthma unresponsive to initial bronchodilators — per PALS
3. Early corticosteroids (within 1 hour of presentation) — reduces admission rates
4. Magnesium sulfate IV — indicated for severe asthma per PALS
5. Continuous EtCO2 monitoring — rising EtCO2 indicates worsening (impending respiratory failure)
6. Transport to pediatric-capable facility — appropriate for age and severity

Caution Flags:
- Quiet chest (no wheezing) in a severe asthma patient = ominous sign (air movement too poor)
- Rising EtCO2 (>45 mmHg) indicates fatigue and possible need for intubation
- Prepare for RSI if continued deterioration despite max medical therapy

Example 3: Trauma Assessment — Multi-System Trauma

MULTI-SYSTEM TRAUMA — FIELD MANAGEMENT

Patient: 34-year-old male, unrestrained driver, high-speed MVC
Mechanism: T-bone collision, driver side impact at approximately 45 mph, significant vehicle intrusion, airbag deployment, prolonged extrication (15 minutes)

Primary Survey:
- C-Spine: Manual in-line stabilization by partner
- A: Patent, maintaining airway, blood in oropharynx (suctioned)
- B: RR 28, shallow, decreased breath sounds left side, SpO2 91%, trachea midline
- C: HR 128, thready radial pulses, BP 88/54, skin pale/cool/diaphoretic, active bleeding from left thigh laceration
- D: GCS 12 (E3 V4 M5), left pupil 4mm sluggish, right pupil 3mm reactive
- E: Deformity left chest wall (flail segment suspected), left femur angulated mid-shaft, left thigh laceration with active hemorrhage

Field Impression: Multi-system trauma — hemorrhagic shock (Class III), left-sided flail chest, suspected left hemothorax, left femur fracture with hemorrhage, possible TBI

Interventions (Timed):
11:32 — Direct pressure and tourniquet applied left thigh (hemorrhage controlled)
11:33 — C-collar applied, extricated to long board
11:34 — High-flow O2 via NRB 15 LPM (SpO2 91% → 94%)
11:35 — Two large-bore IVs (16g bilateral AC)
11:36 — NS bolus 1L initiated (permissive hypotension — target SBP 80-90)
11:37 — Fentanyl 100 mcg IV (pain management for femur fracture)
11:38 — Traction splint applied to left femur
11:39 — Left chest: Decreased breath sounds — clinical decision: load and go
11:40 — Transport emergent to Level I Trauma Center, ETA 14 minutes
11:41 — Trauma activation called: "Level 1 trauma activation, ETA 13 minutes"
11:42 — Reassessment: SpO2 93% with O2, breath sounds still decreased left
11:44 — Needle decompression left chest (2nd ICS MCL, 14g catheter) — rush of air, SpO2 improves to 96%
11:46 — TXA 1g IV over 10 minutes per massive hemorrhage protocol
11:50 — Second NS bolus 500 mL (BP improved to 92/60)
11:54 — Arrival at Level I Trauma Center; direct to trauma bay

Total Prehospital Time: 22 minutes (transport decision to trauma bay)

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **ACLS**: Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support — AHA-standardized protocols for cardiac emergencies
- **PALS**: Pediatric Advanced Life Support — AHA-standardized protocols for pediatric emergencies
- **PHTLS/ITLS**: Prehospital/International Trauma Life Support — systematic trauma assessment and management
- **GCS**: Glasgow Coma Scale — neurologic assessment (Eye 1-4, Verbal 1-5, Motor 1-6; total 3-15)
- **AVPU**: Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive — rapid level of consciousness assessment
- **SAMPLE**: Signs/Symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past history, Last intake, Events — patient history mnemonic
- **OPQRST**: Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Time — pain assessment mnemonic
- **DCAP-BTLS**: Deformities, Contusions, Abrasions, Punctures, Burns, Tenderness, Lacerations, Swelling — trauma exam mnemonic
- **ROSC**: Return of Spontaneous Circulation — restoration of heartbeat after cardiac arrest
- **STEMI**: ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction — heart attack requiring emergent catheterization
- **EtCO2**: End-Tidal Carbon Dioxide — capnography measurement for ventilation and CPR quality monitoring
- **SpO2**: Peripheral Oxygen Saturation via pulse oximetry
- **RSI**: Rapid Sequence Intubation — pharmacologically-facilitated endotracheal intubation
- **IO**: Intraosseous — vascular access route when IV access unobtainable
- **TXA**: Tranexamic Acid — antifibrinolytic for hemorrhage (administer within 3 hours of injury)
- **MCI**: Mass Casualty Incident — event with patients exceeding available resources
- **START Triage**: Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment — MCI triage algorithm (RPM: Respirations, Perfusion, Mental status)
- **PCR**: Patient Care Report — legal document of the prehospital encounter
- **MIST**: Mechanism, Injuries, Signs, Treatment — prehospital handoff mnemonic
- **SBAR**: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation — structured communication format
- **BLS**: Basic Life Support — foundational emergency care (CPR, AED, choking)

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AHA ACLS/PALS/BLS Guidelines (2020, updated 2023-2024)**: Evidence-based cardiac arrest, arrhythmia, and resuscitation protocols
- **NREMT (National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians)**: National certification standards and psychomotor skills testing
- **National EMS Scope of Practice Model**: Defines four EMS provider levels and their authorized interventions
- **NAEMSP Position Statements**: Evidence-based position papers on EMS clinical topics
- **PHTLS / ITLS**: Prehospital trauma life support standards and algorithms
- **NAEMT TCCC (Tactical Combat Casualty Care)**: Military-derived hemorrhage control and tactical medicine
- **EMS Agenda 2050**: National strategic vision for the future of EMS in the United States
- **NEMSIS (National EMS Information System)**: Standardized EMS data collection for quality improvement and research
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy requirements for prehospital health information
- **OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard**: Occupational exposure control for EMS providers
- **NFPA 1710/1720**: Response time standards for career and volunteer fire/EMS departments
- **CAMTS (Commission on Accreditation of Medical Transport Systems)**: Standards for air and ground critical care transport

Common Tools and Equipment:
- **Cardiac Monitor/Defibrillator**: Zoll X-Series, Philips HeartStart MRx, Stryker LIFEPAK 15 (12-lead ECG, defibrillation, pacing, capnography)
- **Airway Equipment**: BVM, supraglottic airways (King LT, i-gel), laryngoscope (direct and video — GlideScope, C-MAC), bougie, surgical cricothyrotomy kit
- **Ventilator**: Portable transport ventilator for extended transport (Zoll 731, Hamilton T1)
- **IV/IO Equipment**: IV catheter sets, EZ-IO intraosseous drill, fluid warmers
- **Hemorrhage Control**: CAT/SOFT-T tourniquets, hemostatic gauze (Combat Gauze, Celox), pelvic binder
- **Spinal Motion Restriction**: C-collar, long backboard, scoop stretcher, KED, vacuum splint
- **Splinting**: Traction splint (Hare, Sager), SAM splints, air splints, vacuum splints
- **Medications**: Drug box per protocol (epinephrine, amiodarone, adenosine, atropine, naloxone, midazolam, fentanyl, morphine, ketamine, ondansetron, albuterol, dextrose, diphenhydramine, TXA, RSI medications)
- **ePCR Software**: ESO, ImageTrend, Zoll RescueNet, MACH (electronic patient care report systems)
- **Communication**: Portable radios, MDT (mobile data terminal), CAD (computer-aided dispatch)
