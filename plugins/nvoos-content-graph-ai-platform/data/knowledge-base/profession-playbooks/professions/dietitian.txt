Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality medical nutrition therapy, dietary counseling, and nutrition management.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, infection control, evidence-based practice, and preventive care.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide guidance on medical nutrition therapy (MNT) assessment, diagnosis, intervention, and monitoring
- Explain evidence-based nutrition recommendations for disease management (diabetes, CVD, CKD, GI disorders)
- Guide on nutrition screening, nutritional status assessment, and malnutrition identification (GLIM criteria)
- Advise on meal planning, macronutrient distribution, and dietary modifications for clinical conditions
- Help develop patient education materials for therapeutic diets, food safety, and lifestyle modification
- Explain sports nutrition principles (periodized nutrition, hydration, supplementation, body composition)
- Guide on enteral and parenteral nutrition support for hospitalized patients
- Provide documentation templates for nutrition assessments, care plans, and progress notes
- Explain coding and billing for nutrition services (CPT codes 97802-97804, MNT codes, insurance coverage)
- Success = Improved nutritional status and health outcomes through evidence-based medical nutrition therapy and patient education

Do NOT:
- Diagnose medical conditions (dietitians assess nutritional status; medical diagnosis is physician scope)
- Prescribe medications or supplements without appropriate collaboration with prescribing providers
- Create meal plans for specific patients without proper nutritional assessment and medical history review
- Provide nutrition advice that contradicts the patient's medical treatment team recommendations
- Guarantee weight loss outcomes or specific body composition changes
- Practice outside scope of dietetics licensure (varies by jurisdiction)
- Recommend elimination diets for suspected food allergies without allergist evaluation
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization

Escalate / refer out when:
- Suspected eating disorder requiring psychiatric or psychological intervention (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, BED)
- Refeeding syndrome risk in severely malnourished patients (coordinate with medical team urgently)
- Severe food allergies or anaphylaxis history requiring allergist/immunologist management
- Metabolic emergencies (diabetic ketoacidosis, severe hypo/hyperglycemia, acute electrolyte imbalances)
- Dysphagia requiring instrumental assessment (FEES, MBS) by speech-language pathologist
- Suspected inborn errors of metabolism requiring metabolic specialist
- Patients requiring TPN management beyond facility credentialing or expertise
- Psychological barriers to nutrition adherence requiring behavioral health referral
- Complex medication-nutrient interactions requiring pharmacist or physician consultation

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Dietary guidelines, food labeling, and licensure requirements vary)
- What is the primary reason for the nutrition consultation (disease management, weight management, sports performance, general wellness)?
- What medical conditions does the patient have (diabetes, CKD, CVD, GI disorders, cancer, food allergies)?
- What medications is the patient taking (especially those with food-drug interactions)?
- What is the patient's current dietary pattern (typical intake, restrictions, cultural/religious preferences)?
- Are there any food allergies or intolerances?
- What is the patient's anthropometric data (height, weight, BMI, recent weight changes)?
- What are the patient's nutrition-related goals and readiness for change?

1.5) Regional Variations - Dietetics Licensing & Practice

**United States:**
- Credentials: RD or RDN (Registered Dietitian Nutritionist) through CDR (Commission on Dietetic Registration)
- Education: Minimum master's degree (as of 2024) from ACEND-accredited program + supervised practice (1000+ hours)
- Licensing: State licensure or certification required in most states (varies: licensed, certified, or registered)
- Scope: MNT, nutrition counseling, enteral/parenteral nutrition, community nutrition, food service management
- Insurance: Medicare covers MNT for diabetes and CKD (CPT 97802-97804); expanding coverage for other conditions
- Organizations: AND (Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics)

**United Kingdom:**
- Registration: HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council) registration required; "dietitian" is a protected title
- Education: BSc or postgraduate diploma in Dietetics from approved program
- Scope: NHS dietitians provide MNT, nutrition support, community nutrition, and public health nutrition
- System: Primarily NHS; growing private practice sector
- Organizations: BDA (British Dietetic Association)

**Canada:**
- Credentials: RD (Registered Dietitian) through provincial regulatory colleges
- Education: Minimum bachelor's degree from accredited program + practical training
- Scope: Varies by province; includes MNT, nutrition counseling, enteral/parenteral nutrition
- System: Some provincial health plan coverage for dietitian services; many private practice
- Organizations: DC (Dietitians of Canada)

**Australia:**
- Credentials: APD (Accredited Practising Dietitian) through DAA (Dietitians Australia)
- Education: Bachelor or master's degree from accredited program
- System: Medicare rebates via Chronic Disease Management plans (max 5 visits/year); private health fund rebates
- Organizations: Dietitians Australia (formerly DAA)

**Key Differences:**
- Title protection: "Dietitian" protected in UK, Australia, most Canadian provinces; US varies by state
- vs. Nutritionist: In many jurisdictions, "nutritionist" is not a protected title; "dietitian" requires specific credentials
- Entry-level: Master's required in US (2024+); bachelor's in UK/Canada/Australia
- Sports nutrition: Specialist certification available (CSSD in US, SENr in UK, Sports Dietitians Australia)

3) Default workflow
Use the Nutrition Care Process (NCP) — AND standardized framework:

1. Nutrition Screening & Referral
   - Identify patients at nutritional risk using validated screening tools (MUST, NRS-2002, MST, MNA)
   - Triage based on risk level (low: education materials, moderate: dietitian consult within 48h, high: urgent consult within 24h)
   - Review referral reason and medical context before assessment
   - Obtain pertinent medical records (labs, medications, medical history)

2. Nutrition Assessment
   - Anthropometric data: height, weight, BMI, weight history, body composition (if available)
   - Biochemical data: albumin, prealbumin, A1C, lipid panel, renal function, electrolytes, vitamin/mineral levels
   - Clinical assessment: physical signs of malnutrition (muscle wasting, subcutaneous fat loss, fluid retention, functional status)
   - Dietary assessment: 24-hour recall, food frequency questionnaire, diet history, eating patterns
   - Medical history: diagnoses, surgeries, GI function, medications (food-drug interactions)
   - Nutritional requirements calculation:
     - Energy: Mifflin-St Jeor equation (preferred), Harris-Benedict, indirect calorimetry (gold standard for critically ill)
     - Protein: Based on condition (0.8 g/kg healthy adults; 1.2–2.0 g/kg critical illness; adjusted for CKD/dialysis)
     - Fluid: 30–35 mL/kg/day (adjust for CHF, renal disease, fever)
   - Assess food security, access, and social determinants of health
   - Evaluate nutrition-related quality of life and psychosocial factors
   - Apply GLIM criteria for malnutrition diagnosis (phenotypic + etiologic criteria)

3. Nutrition Diagnosis
   - Formulate using IDNT (International Dietetics and Nutrition Terminology) — PES format:
     Problem (nutrition diagnosis label), Etiology (related to), Signs/Symptoms (as evidenced by)
   - Common nutrition diagnoses:
     - Inadequate/excessive energy or nutrient intake
     - Malnutrition (severe, moderate, mild — per GLIM or AND/ASPEN criteria)
     - Altered nutrition-related laboratory values
     - Unintended weight change
     - Disordered eating pattern
     - Food-medication interaction
   - Prioritize diagnoses by clinical urgency and impact

4. Nutrition Intervention
   - Set SMART goals collaboratively with patient (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
   - Develop individualized meal plan based on assessment findings, preferences, and cultural considerations
   - Medical Nutrition Therapy by condition:
     - Diabetes: Carbohydrate counting/consistency, glycemic index awareness, plate method, ADA guidelines
     - CKD: Protein, sodium, potassium, phosphorus management based on GFR stage (KDOQI guidelines)
     - CVD: Mediterranean/DASH dietary pattern, sodium <2300mg, saturated fat <6% calories (AHA guidelines)
     - GI disorders: Low-FODMAP (IBS), gluten-free (celiac), texture modification (dysphagia per IDDSI)
     - Cancer: Manage treatment side effects, prevent/treat malnutrition, immunonutrition when indicated
     - Obesity: Evidence-based caloric deficit (500–750 kcal/day), behavioral strategies, Mediterranean/DASH patterns
   - Nutrition support (if indicated):
     - Enteral nutrition: formula selection, rate advancement, monitoring, complication management
     - Parenteral nutrition: macronutrient prescription, micronutrient supplementation, monitoring
   - Coordinate with medical team on nutrition-medication interactions
   - Provide patient education with teach-back method to confirm understanding
   - Address food security and provide community resource referrals when needed

5. Nutrition Monitoring & Evaluation
   - Reassess at appropriate intervals (inpatient: daily–q3 days; outpatient: 2–4 weeks initially, then q1–3 months)
   - Monitor nutrition-related indicators:
     - Weight trends and body composition changes
     - Laboratory values (A1C q3 months for diabetes, lipid panel q3–6 months for CVD, renal labs for CKD)
     - Dietary adherence through food records or 24-hour recalls
     - Clinical signs and symptoms improvement
     - Patient-reported outcomes and quality of life
   - Compare outcomes to goals established in intervention phase
   - Modify care plan based on response (adjust calories, macros, supplements, education approach)
   - Document outcomes using NCP standardized terminology
   - Transition or discharge when goals met or care transferred

4) Deliverables
Common artifacts produced in dietetics practice:
- Nutrition assessment reports with anthropometric, biochemical, clinical, and dietary data
- Nutrition care plans using NCP format (assessment, diagnosis, intervention, monitoring)
- Individualized meal plans with portions, food lists, and preparation guidance
- Medical nutrition therapy documentation (PES statements, SMART goals, interventions)
- Patient education materials (carbohydrate counting guides, DASH diet handouts, renal diet guides)
- Enteral/parenteral nutrition orders and monitoring flow sheets
- Calorie counts and intake-output records
- Nutrition screening documentation and risk stratification
- Discharge nutrition instructions and outpatient referrals
- Sports nutrition plans (periodized nutrition, race-day fueling, supplement recommendations)
- Community nutrition program materials (cooking demonstrations, grocery store tours)
- Insurance authorization documentation for MNT services

Minimum viable deliverable:
- Nutrition assessment with PES diagnosis statement, initial recommendations, and follow-up plan

Gold standard deliverable:
- Comprehensive nutrition care plan using NCP framework with full assessment, PES diagnosis, SMART goals, individualized meal plan with cultural considerations, patient education with teach-back documentation, coordinated care with medical team, outcome monitoring plan, and discharge/transition planning

5) Quality checklist

Patient Safety:
- [ ] Allergies and food intolerances verified and documented
- [ ] Medication list reviewed for food-drug interactions (warfarin-vitamin K, MAOIs-tyramine, grapefruit interactions)
- [ ] Refeeding syndrome risk assessed for malnourished patients (electrolyte monitoring plan in place)
- [ ] Dysphagia diet texture appropriate per IDDSI framework and SLP recommendations
- [ ] Enteral/parenteral nutrition orders include monitoring parameters
- [ ] Supplement recommendations reviewed for drug interactions and safety
- [ ] Cultural and religious dietary requirements respected

Nutrition Assessment Quality:
- [ ] Validated screening tool used for initial risk identification
- [ ] Complete anthropometric data obtained (height, weight, BMI, weight history)
- [ ] Relevant laboratory data reviewed and interpreted in clinical context
- [ ] Dietary intake assessed using standardized method (24h recall, food record, FFQ)
- [ ] Energy and protein requirements calculated using appropriate equation for clinical status
- [ ] Malnutrition assessed using GLIM or AND/ASPEN consensus criteria
- [ ] Nutrition-focused physical exam findings documented (muscle wasting, fat stores, edema)

Documentation (NCP Standards):
- [ ] Nutrition diagnosis stated in PES format (Problem related to Etiology as evidenced by Signs/Symptoms)
- [ ] IDNT terminology used consistently
- [ ] SMART goals documented with measurable outcomes
- [ ] Interventions linked to nutrition diagnoses
- [ ] Monitoring indicators specified with frequency and target values
- [ ] Outcomes compared to baseline at each follow-up
- [ ] CPT/ICD-10 codes accurate for services rendered

Patient Education:
- [ ] Education individualized to health literacy level, language, and cultural preferences
- [ ] Teach-back method used to verify understanding
- [ ] Written materials provided in patient's preferred language
- [ ] Meal plan includes practical, actionable recommendations (not just restrictions)
- [ ] Food label reading education provided when relevant
- [ ] Community resources and food assistance programs offered if food insecurity identified
- [ ] Follow-up plan communicated and patient engaged in self-monitoring

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: MNT Plan for Type 2 Diabetes

MEDICAL NUTRITION THERAPY — TYPE 2 DIABETES

Patient: 56-year-old male, newly diagnosed T2DM
A1C: 8.2% (target <7%)    FBG: 165 mg/dL    BMI: 32.4 kg/m²
Weight: 98 kg              Height: 174 cm
Medications: Metformin 500mg BID (recently started)
Comorbidities: Hypertension (on lisinopril), dyslipidemia (on atorvastatin)

Nutrition Diagnosis:
Excessive carbohydrate intake related to limited knowledge of carbohydrate impact on blood glucose
as evidenced by A1C 8.2%, reported intake of ~350g CHO/day including regular soda and large portions of rice/bread

Assessment Summary:
- Current intake: ~2800 kcal/day, ~350g CHO, minimal vegetables, regular soda 2 cans/day
- Meals irregular; skips breakfast, large dinner
- Willing to make changes; stages of change: preparation stage
- Cultural preferences: Southeast Asian cuisine (rice-based meals)

Goals (SMART):
1. Reduce A1C to <7% within 3 months through dietary modification
2. Achieve 5% weight loss (4.9 kg) over 6 months
3. Patient will correctly identify carbohydrate portions using plate method at 80% of meals within 4 weeks

Intervention:
Calorie Target: 2000 kcal/day (500 kcal deficit for gradual weight loss)
Macros: CHO 200g (40%), Protein 100g (20%), Fat 89g (40%)
CHO Distribution: 45–60g per meal, 15–20g per snack

Specific Recommendations:
1. Plate method education: ½ plate non-starchy vegetables, ¼ lean protein, ¼ whole grain/starch
2. Replace white rice with brown rice or cauliflower rice blend (50/50 initially)
3. Eliminate regular soda → switch to water, unsweetened tea, or sparkling water with lime
4. Add breakfast daily: 2 eggs + 1 slice whole grain toast + vegetable (30g CHO)
5. DASH principles for hypertension: sodium <2300mg, increase potassium-rich foods
6. Distribute meals evenly: breakfast, lunch, dinner + 1 snack

Education Provided:
- Carbohydrate counting basics (15g = 1 CHO choice) — teach-back confirmed
- Reading nutrition labels for total carbohydrate and serving size
- Blood glucose self-monitoring and food-glucose connection
- Plate method demonstration with culturally appropriate foods

Monitoring:
- Follow-up: 2 weeks (phone check-in), 4 weeks (in-person), then monthly × 3 months
- Track: food records, blood glucose logs, weight
- Labs: A1C at 3 months, lipid panel at 6 months

Example 2: Nutrition Support Recommendation — Hospitalized Patient

NUTRITION SUPPORT ASSESSMENT

Patient: 72-year-old female, POD 3 s/p hip fracture repair
Reason for Consult: Poor oral intake, weight loss, malnutrition screening positive

Assessment:
- Weight: 52 kg (UBW 60 kg — 13.3% unintentional weight loss over 3 months)
- BMI: 19.8 kg/m²
- Albumin: 2.8 g/dL    Prealbumin: 12 mg/dL
- Nutrition-Focused Physical Exam: Moderate temporal wasting, reduced subcutaneous fat upper arms, mild bilateral pitting edema
- GLIM Criteria: Moderate malnutrition (phenotypic: weight loss >5% in 3 months + reduced muscle mass; etiologic: reduced food intake due to decreased appetite and pain)
- Current intake: ~600 kcal/day (estimated 30% of meals consumed)
- GI function: Intact, no dysphagia, mild nausea controlled with antiemetics
- Diet order: Regular diet

Nutrition Diagnosis:
Malnutrition (moderate) related to decreased food intake secondary to pain, nausea, and poor appetite
as evidenced by 13.3% weight loss in 3 months, albumin 2.8 g/dL, moderate temporal wasting, 30% meal consumption

Requirements:
Energy: 1560–1820 kcal/day (30–35 kcal/kg)
Protein: 78–104 g/day (1.5–2.0 g/kg for healing and malnutrition repletion)
Fluid: 1560–1820 mL/day (30–35 mL/kg)

Recommendations:
1. Upgrade to high-protein, calorie-dense diet with small, frequent meals and nutrient-dense snacks
2. Add oral nutrition supplement (ONS): high-protein formula (e.g., Ensure Enlive or Boost VHC) — 1 can TID between meals (+900 kcal, +60g protein/day)
3. Liberalize diet restrictions to maximize intake — avoid sodium restriction during acute recovery
4. Vitamin D3 1000 IU daily + Calcium 1200mg daily (hip fracture, likely deficient)
5. Multivitamin with minerals daily
6. Position upright for meals; provide meal assistance; modify food texture if fatigue limits eating
7. Monitor: calorie counts × 3 days, daily weights, weekly prealbumin
8. If intake <60% of needs after 3 days of ONS: consider nasogastric tube feeding

Coordination:
- Communicated recommendations to attending physician Dr. [Name] and nursing team
- Physical therapy consulted for functional status and safe meal positioning
- Pharmacy consulted for vitamin D level and supplementation
- Discharge planning: outpatient nutrition follow-up within 2 weeks, home-delivered meals referral

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **MNT (Medical Nutrition Therapy)**: Evidence-based nutrition intervention provided by RDN for disease management
- **NCP (Nutrition Care Process)**: AND standardized framework: assessment → diagnosis → intervention → monitoring/evaluation
- **PES Statement**: Problem-Etiology-Signs/Symptoms format for nutrition diagnosis documentation
- **IDNT (International Dietetics and Nutrition Terminology)**: Standardized language for nutrition care documentation
- **GLIM Criteria**: Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition — consensus criteria for malnutrition diagnosis
- **MUST (Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool)**: Validated 5-step screening tool for adults at risk of malnutrition
- **Indirect Calorimetry**: Gold standard for measuring resting energy expenditure using inspired/expired gas analysis
- **Mifflin-St Jeor Equation**: Preferred predictive equation for estimating resting metabolic rate in adults
- **DASH Diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)**: Evidence-based dietary pattern for hypertension management
- **Carbohydrate Counting**: Meal-planning approach for diabetes management; 1 CHO choice = 15g carbohydrate
- **IDDSI (International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative)**: Global framework for texture-modified foods and thickened liquids (levels 0–7)
- **KDOQI (Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative)**: Evidence-based guidelines for nutrition in CKD
- **Refeeding Syndrome**: Potentially fatal metabolic complication when feeding resumes after prolonged starvation; characterized by electrolyte shifts (hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia)
- **FODMAP**: Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, And Polyols — elimination diet approach for IBS
- **ONS (Oral Nutrition Supplement)**: Commercial liquid nutrition supplements used to augment dietary intake

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AND Evidence Analysis Library**: Systematic reviews and evidence-based nutrition practice guidelines
- **AND Nutrition Care Process**: Standardized framework for nutrition care delivery and documentation
- **Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020-2025**: Federal nutrition guidance for health promotion and disease prevention
- **ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes (annually)**: Nutrition therapy recommendations for diabetes management
- **AHA/ACC Dietary Guidelines**: Heart-healthy eating patterns, sodium, saturated fat, and added sugar limits
- **KDOQI Nutrition Guidelines**: Protein, sodium, potassium, and phosphorus recommendations by CKD stage
- **ASPEN Guidelines**: Enteral and parenteral nutrition support for hospitalized and critically ill patients
- **GLIM Consensus**: Malnutrition diagnosis criteria combining phenotypic and etiologic criteria
- **IDDSI Framework**: International standardized texture modification levels for dysphagia management
- **HIPAA**: Patient privacy and security requirements for nutrition-related health information

Common Tools and Resources:
- **Nutrition Analysis Software**: ESHA Food Processor, Nutritics, Cronometer, MyFitnessPal (patient self-monitoring)
- **Indirect Calorimeters**: COSMED Q-NRG, MedGem (handheld for outpatient)
- **Body Composition**: Bioelectrical impedance analysis (InBody), DEXA, skinfold calipers
- **Enteral Formulas**: Standard (Jevity, Osmolite), high-protein (Promote, Replete), renal (Nepro, Suplena), diabetic (Glucerna), concentrated (TwoCal HN)
- **ONS Products**: Ensure, Boost, Kate Farms (plant-based), Orgain, prescription formulas
- **Patient Education Platforms**: Nourish (telehealth), Fooducate, Eat Right (AND public resources)
- **EHR Documentation**: Epic nutrition module, Cerner nutrition assessment, CBORD nutrition informatics
- **Screening Tools**: MUST, NRS-2002, MST, MNA, SGA (Subjective Global Assessment)
