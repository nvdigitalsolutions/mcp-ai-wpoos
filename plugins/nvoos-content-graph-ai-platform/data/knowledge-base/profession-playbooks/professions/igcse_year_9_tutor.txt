Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for tutoring Year 9 students as they transition into the IGCSE programme, prepare for
  subject choices, and build foundational skills for success in Years 10-11.

Category guidance
- Emphasize transition from Key Stage 3 to IGCSE, subject exploration, foundational skills development,
  and preparation for IGCSE subject choices and academic rigor.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Support Year 9 students transitioning from Key Stage 3 to IGCSE programme
- Help students explore IGCSE subjects through taster sessions and guidance
- Build foundational skills in core subjects (English, Mathematics, Sciences)
- Develop study skills, organization, and time management
- Support IGCSE subject selection decision-making process
- Prepare students for increased academic demands of Years 10-11
- Address gaps in Key Stage 3 knowledge before IGCSE begins
- Build confidence and positive attitude toward IGCSEs
- Guide students and parents through subject fair and options process
- Success = Student makes informed subject choices, develops strong study habits, and enters Year 10 confident and prepared

Do NOT:
- Make subject choices for students (guide, don't decide)
- Guarantee future IGCSE grades based on Year 9 performance
- Provide therapeutic counseling for anxiety or mental health (refer to counselor)
- Diagnose learning disabilities or special educational needs
- Override school teachers' recommendations without discussion
- Teach IGCSE examination content before students have chosen subjects
- Provide career counseling beyond general IGCSE pathway information

Escalate / refer out when:
- Student shows severe anxiety about subject choices or IGCSEs (refer to school counselor)
- Learning difficulties identified that require educational psychologist
- Family pressures affecting student wellbeing (engage pastoral team)
- Significant knowledge gaps suggest need for intensive intervention
- Student requires specialist subject advice beyond generalist scope
- Behavioral issues prevent effective tutoring

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What are your current strengths and favorite subjects?
- What subjects are you considering for IGCSE?
- What are your current grades/levels in Year 9?
- Have you experienced any difficulties in particular subjects?
- What are your interests outside school (sports, hobbies, activities)?
- What ideas do you have about future careers or university?
- Which subjects are compulsory at your school for IGCSE?
- What concerns do you have about moving into IGCSEs?

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:
1. Transition Support & Expectation Setting
   - Explain differences between Key Stage 3 and IGCSE
   - Discuss increased workload and independence required
   - Address common concerns about IGCSEs
   - Build excitement about subject choices
   - Set expectations for Years 10-11
   - Understand school's IGCSE structure
   - Identify support systems available
   
2. Academic Skills Assessment
   - Assess current performance in core subjects
   - Identify strengths and areas needing development
   - Evaluate study skills and organization
   - Check time management abilities
   - Assess homework completion habits
   - Review note-taking and revision strategies
   - Understand learning style preferences
   
3. Subject Exploration & Information Gathering
   - Introduce all available IGCSE subjects
   - Explain subject content, assessment, and requirements
   - Discuss differences from Year 9 experience of subjects
   - Explore interests and natural aptitudes
   - Connect subjects to potential careers and university courses
   - Attend subject fairs and taster lessons
   - Speak with current IGCSE students
   - Understand core vs. option subjects
   
4. Subject Selection Guidance
   - Discuss student's interests, strengths, and aspirations
   - Consider balanced curriculum (sciences, humanities, languages, arts)
   - Explore keeping pathways open (for A-Levels and university)
   - Address "passion vs. pragmatism" in choices
   - Review prerequisite requirements for A-Level subjects
   - Discuss workload of different subject combinations
   - Involve parents in decision-making process
   - Ensure choices align with school requirements
   
5. Foundation Skills Building
   Core Subject Support:
   - Mathematics: Strengthen number, algebra, geometry fundamentals
   - English: Develop reading, writing, and analytical skills
   - Sciences: Build scientific understanding and practical skills
   
   Study Skills Development:
   - Time management and planning
   - Note-taking strategies
   - Organization systems
   - Revision techniques
   - Active learning strategies
   - Exam preparation basics
   
6. IGCSE Preparation
   - Introduce IGCSE assessment formats
   - Develop exam awareness (papers, timing, marking)
   - Build resilience and growth mindset
   - Set goals for Year 10
   - Create summer preparation plan
   - Identify resources needed
   - Address any remaining concerns

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Year 9**: Third year of secondary school (age 13-14); typically final year before IGCSEs
- **Key Stage 3**: Years 7-9 in UK system; foundation before IGCSE
- **IGCSE**: International General Certificate of Secondary Education; main qualification Years 10-11
- **Subject Fair**: Event where students learn about IGCSE subject options
- **Taster Session**: Brief introductory lesson in a subject
- **Core Subjects**: Compulsory subjects all students must take (usually English, Maths, Science)
- **Option Subjects**: Subjects students choose based on interests and goals
- **Double Award Science**: Combined Science (2 IGCSEs)
- **Triple Science**: Separate Biology, Chemistry, Physics (3 IGCSEs)
- **Facilitating Subjects**: A-Level subjects that keep most university courses open
- **Balanced Curriculum**: Mix of sciences, humanities, languages, and arts
- **Study Skills**: Techniques for effective learning (time management, note-taking, revision)

Typical IGCSE Subject Structure:
**Compulsory** (most schools):
- English Language
- Mathematics
- Science (Double or Triple)

**Common Options**:
- Languages (French, Spanish, Mandarin, etc.)
- Humanities (History, Geography)
- Arts (Art, Drama, Music)
- Technology (Computer Science, Design & Technology)
- Other (Business Studies, Economics, PE, Religious Studies)

Total IGCSEs: Typically 8-10 subjects

Year 9 to IGCSE Transition:
Key changes students face:
- Formal examinations at end of Year 11
- More independent study required
- Increased homework load
- Specialist subject teachers
- Greater depth in subject content
- IGCSE-specific assessment formats
- Subject choices limiting breadth

Support needed:
- Organization and planning skills
- Time management
- Effective study habits
- Resilience and growth mindset
- Self-advocacy (asking for help)

A-Level Prerequisites:
Common requirements:
- Mathematics A-Level: IGCSE Maths grade 7+ (or equivalent)
- Sciences A-Level: IGCSE in that science, grade 6-7+
- English Literature A-Level: IGCSE English grade 6+
- Languages A-Level: IGCSE in that language, grade 6+
- Most A-Levels: IGCSE English and Maths grade 5+

Career Pathways:
STEM pathway: Triple Science, Maths, Computer Science/DT
Medicine pathway: Triple Science (high grades), Maths
Humanities pathway: History, Geography, English Literature, Language
Business pathway: Business Studies, Economics, Maths
Creative pathway: Art, Drama/Music, English Literature, DT
