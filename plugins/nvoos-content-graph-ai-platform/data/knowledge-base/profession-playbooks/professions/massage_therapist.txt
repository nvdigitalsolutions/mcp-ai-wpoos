Intent
- Provide actionable guidance for therapeutic massage practice following AMTA/NCBTMB standards.
- Focus on client safety, professional boundaries, evidence-based techniques, and ethical practice.

Category guidance
- Client safety first, informed consent, contraindications screening, professional boundaries, documentation.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide therapeutic soft tissue manipulation for pain relief, stress reduction, and wellness
- Assess musculoskeletal complaints using visual observation and palpation
- Develop treatment plans based on client goals and contraindications screening
- Educate clients on self-care, stretching, and home exercise programs
- Document sessions using SOAP note format (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan)
- Maintain professional boundaries and draping protocols per state regulations
- Screen for contraindications (absolute and relative) before each session
- Provide aftercare recommendations (hydration, rest, activity modifications)
- Collaborate with healthcare providers (physical therapists, chiropractors, physicians)
- Success = Client achieves pain reduction, improved mobility, relaxation, and functional goals safely

Do NOT:
- Diagnose medical conditions (requires physician/healthcare provider license)
- Prescribe medications or supplements (outside scope of practice)
- Perform chiropractic adjustments or joint manipulations
- Treat acute injuries without physician clearance (fractures, severe sprains)
- Massage areas with blood clots, infections, or open wounds
- Continue treatment if client reports severe pain or adverse reactions
- Engage in any sexual conduct or boundary violations (grounds for license revocation)
- Guarantee specific therapeutic outcomes without proper assessment

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client reports chest pain, shortness of breath, or neurological symptoms (call 911)
- Suspected deep vein thrombosis (DVT), blood clot, or severe inflammation
- New lumps, masses, or suspicious tissue changes requiring medical evaluation
- Symptoms suggest fracture, severe sprain, or ligament tear
- Client has active infection, fever, or contagious condition
- Pregnancy complications or high-risk conditions requiring physician clearance
- Client requests services outside scope of practice (diagnosis, prescriptions)
- Suspected abuse, neglect, or trafficking (mandatory reporting)
- Severe chronic pain requiring pain management specialist or physician referral

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Licensing, scope, and title protection vary significantly)
- What are your primary concerns or goals for massage therapy (pain relief, stress, sports recovery)?
- Do you have any medical conditions, recent injuries, or surgeries?
- Are you currently pregnant or have any cardiovascular conditions?
- What medications are you taking (especially blood thinners, muscle relaxants)?
- Do you have any allergies (lotions, oils, latex)?
- What is your pain level and location (0-10 scale)?
- What pressure do you prefer (light, medium, firm, deep tissue)?
- Have you had massage therapy before? What worked well or didn't work?

1.5) Regional Variations - Massage Therapy Regulation

**United States:**
- Licensing: State-based; 46+ states require licensure (LMT, Licensed Massage Therapist)
- Exams: MBLEx (Massage & Bodywork Licensing Examination) via FSMTB
- Education: 500-1000 hours depending on state; COMTA-accredited schools
- Scope: Therapeutic massage, myofascial release, trigger point; no diagnosis or prescribing
- Settings: Spas, clinics, chiropractic offices, sports teams, private practice
- Insurance: Some states allow billing for medical massage (with prescription)
- Organizations: AMTA (American Massage Therapy Association), ABMP (Associated Bodywork & Massage Professionals)

**United Kingdom:**
- Regulation: No statutory regulation; voluntary self-regulation
- Title: "Massage Therapist" not protected; various titles used
- Training: Varied; no national standard (Level 3 Diploma common)
- Professional bodies: CNHC (Complementary and Natural Healthcare Council), FHT (Federation of Holistic Therapists)
- Insurance: Professional indemnity required for practice
- Settings: Spas, health clubs, private practice; limited NHS integration

**Canada:**
- Regulation: Provincial; varies widely
  - Regulated: Ontario (CMTO), BC (CMTBC), Newfoundland (CMTNL)
  - Self-regulated or unregulated: Other provinces
- Title: RMT (Registered Massage Therapist) in regulated provinces
- Education: 2200-3000 hours in regulated provinces; less elsewhere
- Insurance: Extended health benefits cover RMTs; some provincial coverage
- Organizations: RMTAO (Ontario), CMTCA (Canadian), provincial associations

**Australia:**
- Regulation: No national licensing; industry self-regulation
- Associations: AMT (Association of Massage Therapists), AAMT (Australian Association of Massage Therapists)
- Education: Certificate IV or Diploma in Remedial Massage
- Insurance: Private health insurance rebates for remedial massage (extras cover)
- Settings: Clinics, spas, sports, private practice
- Title: "Remedial Massage Therapist" common

**Key Differences:**
- Licensing: Mandatory state licensing (US majority); provincial (Canada select); voluntary (UK/Australia)
- Education: 500-1000 hours (US); 2200+ (Canada regulated provinces); varied (UK/Australia)
- Title protection: LMT (US states); RMT (Canada regulated provinces); not protected (UK/Australia)
- Insurance coverage: Limited medical massage (US); extended health benefits (Canada); private extras (Australia); limited (UK)

3) Default workflow
Use a deterministic, repeatable process:

1. Initial Consultation & Screening (New Clients)
   - Have client complete intake form (health history, medications, allergies)
   - Review contraindications checklist (absolute and relative)
   - Explain treatment approach, modalities, and what to expect
   - Obtain informed consent signature
   - Discuss client goals and treatment preferences
   - Establish pressure preferences and communication signals
   - Review privacy, draping, and professional boundaries
   - Verify insurance coverage if applicable (medical massage)

2. Pre-Session Assessment
   - Conduct brief health check (any changes since last visit?)
   - Assess current pain level and location (0-10 scale)
   - Observe posture, gait, and movement patterns
   - Palpate areas of concern (temperature, texture, tension)
   - Identify restricted range of motion
   - Screen for new contraindications or precautions
   - Confirm treatment goals for today's session
   - Adjust treatment plan based on current presentation

3. Treatment Session
   - Position client comfortably with proper bolstering
   - Ensure appropriate draping at all times
   - Begin with lighter pressure to warm tissues
   - Apply chosen modalities (Swedish, deep tissue, trigger point, etc.)
   - Monitor client responses and adjust pressure/technique
   - Communicate regularly ("Is this pressure okay?")
   - Address primary areas of concern first
   - Work systematically through treatment plan
   - Use proper body mechanics to prevent therapist injury
   - Session typically 30-90 minutes depending on goals

4. Post-Session Care & Documentation
   - Allow client time to reorient and dress
   - Provide water and encourage hydration
   - Give aftercare instructions (ice, heat, stretching, activity modifications)
   - Discuss findings and treatment response
   - Recommend home exercises or self-care techniques
   - Schedule follow-up if appropriate
   - Document session immediately using SOAP format
   - Note any adverse reactions or concerns
   - Update treatment plan for future sessions

5. Follow-Up & Continuity of Care
   - Review progress toward goals at each visit
   - Modify treatment plan based on client response
   - Communicate with referring healthcare providers (with client consent)
   - Refer to other practitioners when indicated
   - Re-assess contraindications periodically
   - Update treatment frequency recommendations
   - Maintain professional liability insurance
   - Complete continuing education requirements for license renewal

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **SOAP Note**: Documentation format (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) standard in healthcare
- **Trigger Point**: Hyperirritable spot in taut band of muscle producing local and referred pain
- **Myofascial Release**: Gentle sustained pressure technique targeting fascial restrictions
- **Contraindication**: Condition that makes massage inappropriate or requires modification
- **Draping**: Appropriate covering of client's body maintaining modesty and professional boundaries
- **Palpation**: Assessment technique using hands to evaluate tissue quality, temperature, texture
- **Modality**: Specific type or technique of massage (Swedish, deep tissue, sports, etc.)
- **Range of Motion (ROM)**: Extent of movement in a joint measured in degrees
- **Adhesion**: Abnormal attachment of tissues due to scar tissue or inflammation
- **Referral Pattern**: Area of pain away from trigger point origin (e.g., TrP in neck refers to headache)
- **ITB (Iliotibial Band)**: Thick fascial band running from hip to knee, common source of runner's knee
- **Bilateral**: On both sides of the body

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **AMTA Standards of Practice (2024)**: Professional ethics, scope of practice, client rights established by American Massage Therapy Association
- **NCBTMB Code of Ethics**: National Certification Board standards for professional conduct and client safety
- **MBLEx**: Massage & Bodywork Licensing Examination required by most states for licensure
- **SOAP Documentation**: Standard healthcare documentation format ensuring comprehensive record-keeping
- **Informed Consent**: Client authorization obtained before treatment explaining risks, benefits, alternatives
- **Universal Precautions**: Infection control standards (hand hygiene, clean linens, equipment sanitation)
- **HIPAA Compliance**: If working in healthcare setting, maintain client privacy per federal regulations

Common Tools & Equipment:
- Massage table: Electric or portable, adjustable height
- Bolsters and pillows: Support positioning (knee bolster, face cradle)
- Lubricants: Massage oils, lotions, creams (unscented or hypoallergenic)
- Hot stone equipment: Heating unit, basalt stones
- Cold therapy: Ice packs, frozen tools
- Stretching aids: Straps, foam rollers, massage balls
- Documentation: EHR software (SOAP Vault, MassageBook, Jane App) or paper forms
- Scheduling software: Online booking, client management

Regulatory Requirements:
- State massage therapy license (varies by state)
- CPR/First Aid certification
- Continuing education (varies by state, typically 12-24 hours/2 years)
- Professional liability insurance
- Business license (if self-employed)
- Health department inspection (if spa/clinic setting)
