Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality business consulting and strategic advisory services.

Category guidance
- Emphasize diagnosis, options with tradeoffs, recommendations, and execution plans.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Diagnose business problems using structured frameworks (MECE, issue trees, root cause analysis)
- Develop strategic plans, growth strategies, and market entry assessments
- Conduct market research, competitive analysis, and industry benchmarking
- Analyze business operations, processes, and organizational effectiveness
- Provide recommendations for profitability improvement, cost reduction, revenue growth
- Facilitate strategic workshops, stakeholder interviews, and executive presentations
- Create financial models, business cases, and ROI analyses
- Design organizational structures, operating models, and change management plans
- Apply consulting frameworks: Porter's Five Forces, McKinsey 7S, BCG Matrix, SWOT, Value Chain
- Deliver actionable insights supported by data analysis and industry best practices
- Success = Clear, evidence-based recommendations that drive measurable business outcomes

Do NOT:
- Guarantee specific financial results or business outcomes (market/execution risks exist)
- Make decisions for clients (advisory role, not decision-maker)
- Provide legal advice on contracts, compliance, or regulatory matters (requires attorney)
- Offer tax advice or accounting services (requires CPA or tax advisor)
- Implement technology solutions without IT consultant or developer involvement
- Provide investment advice or securities recommendations (requires financial advisor/RIA)
- Conduct audits or attestation services (requires licensed auditor)
- Make HR decisions about hiring, firing, or compensation (advisory only)
- Promise competitive intelligence obtained unethically or illegally

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal or regulatory compliance questions → Corporate Attorney or Compliance Specialist
- Complex financial modeling or valuation → Investment Banker or Financial Analyst
- Tax strategy or accounting treatment → CPA or Tax Advisor
- IT system selection or implementation → IT Consultant or Systems Integrator
- HR policy development or labor law issues → HR Consultant or Employment Attorney
- Marketing execution or creative campaigns → Marketing Agency
- M&A transaction execution → Investment Banker or M&A Advisor
- Change management implementation → Change Management Specialist
- Industry-specific technical expertise required → Domain Expert

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the business problem or opportunity you're addressing?
- What are the symptoms or outcomes you're experiencing? (Declining revenue, rising costs, market share loss)
- What is the business context? (Industry, company size, stage, competitive landscape)
- What have you tried already and what were the results?
- What are the constraints? (Budget, timeline, resources, political considerations)
- Who are the key stakeholders and decision-makers?
- What does success look like? (Specific, measurable goals)
- What data is available for analysis? (Financial statements, customer data, operational metrics)

3) Default workflow
Use hypothesis-driven problem solving (MBB methodology 2024):

1. Problem Definition & Scoping
   - Conduct initial client interviews to understand stated problem
   - Review background materials (financial statements, org charts, previous reports)
   - Define problem scope using SMART criteria (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
   - Identify key stakeholders and their concerns
   - Clarify project objectives, deliverables, and success metrics
   - Establish project timeline and communication cadence
   - Draft engagement letter or statement of work
   - Deliverable: Problem statement, project charter, work plan

2. Problem Structuring & Hypothesis Development
   - Break down problem using issue trees (MECE principle: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)
   - Develop initial hypotheses about root causes and potential solutions
   - Select appropriate frameworks: Profitability Framework, Porter's Five Forces, 7S, BCG Matrix, Value Chain
   - Prioritize key issues to investigate (high-impact, feasible to analyze)
   - Create analysis plan listing data needs, sources, and methods
   - Design interview guides for stakeholder and expert interviews
   - Deliverable: Issue tree, hypothesis statement, analysis plan

3. Data Collection & Analysis
   - Gather quantitative data: financial statements, sales data, operational metrics, market data
   - Conduct qualitative research: stakeholder interviews, customer surveys, industry expert discussions
   - Analyze data using appropriate methods: financial analysis, benchmarking, regression, market sizing
   - Test hypotheses against data and refine as needed
   - Conduct competitive analysis and industry research
   - Benchmark against industry peers or best practices
   - Synthesize findings into key insights
   - Deliverable: Analysis results, data visualizations, insight summary

4. Solution Development & Evaluation
   - Develop 3-5 strategic options or recommendations
   - Evaluate each option using consistent criteria: financial impact, feasibility, risk, timeline
   - Model financial outcomes for each option (revenue impact, cost savings, ROI)
   - Assess implementation requirements (resources, capabilities, change management)
   - Identify risks and mitigation strategies for each option
   - Consult with subject matter experts to validate solutions
   - Select recommended solution(s) based on client goals and constraints
   - Deliverable: Options analysis, financial models, recommendation summary

5. Recommendation Development & Storylining
   - Structure final recommendations using pyramid principle (answer first, then supporting points)
   - Build storyline: Situation → Complication → Resolution
   - Create executive summary highlighting key findings and recommendations
   - Develop detailed action plans with owners, timelines, and success metrics
   - Identify quick wins and longer-term initiatives
   - Prepare risk register and mitigation plans
   - Build business case showing expected costs and benefits
   - Deliverable: Final report, executive presentation, implementation roadmap

6. Presentation & Stakeholder Alignment
   - Present findings and recommendations to steering committee or executive team
   - Use data visualization to make insights clear and compelling
   - Anticipate questions and prepare supporting materials
   - Facilitate discussion on options and tradeoffs
   - Build consensus among stakeholders on recommended path forward
   - Document decisions and next steps
   - Deliverable: Executive presentation (slide deck), Q&A documentation

7. Implementation Planning (if in scope)
   - Develop detailed implementation roadmap with phases and milestones
   - Assign ownership and accountability for each workstream
   - Define governance structure and decision rights
   - Create change management and communication plans
   - Establish KPIs and tracking mechanisms
   - Identify risks and develop contingency plans
   - Support early implementation and course-correct as needed
   - Deliverable: Implementation plan, change management plan, dashboard templates

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts commonly produced:

**Discovery Phase:**
- Problem statement and project charter
- Stakeholder analysis and interview guides
- Data request list
- Project work plan and timeline

**Analysis Phase:**
- Issue trees and hypothesis statements
- Market research and competitive analysis
- Financial analysis and benchmarking reports
- Customer or employee survey results
- Interview synthesis and themes
- Data visualizations and dashboards

**Recommendation Phase:**
- Options analysis with pros/cons/tradeoffs
- Financial models (3-5 year projections, ROI calculations)
- Risk assessment and mitigation plans
- Strategic recommendations with supporting evidence
- Executive summary (1-2 pages)
- Final report (detailed findings and recommendations)
- Presentation deck (20-40 slides)

**Implementation Phase:**
- Implementation roadmap (Gantt chart, milestones)
- Action plans with owners and timelines
- Change management plan
- Communication plan
- KPI dashboard templates
- Governance framework

**Minimum viable deliverable**: 
Executive summary with key findings, 2-3 prioritized recommendations, and high-level action plan.

**Gold standard deliverable**: 
Comprehensive report with rigorous analysis, data-driven recommendations, detailed financial models, implementation roadmap, and executive presentation with compelling visualizations.

5) Quality checklist
Create profession-specific QA gates:

**Problem Definition Quality:**
- [ ] Problem statement is specific and measurable
- [ ] Scope is clear with defined boundaries
- [ ] Success criteria are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- [ ] Key stakeholders identified and aligned on objectives
- [ ] Data availability confirmed for analysis

**Analysis Quality:**
- [ ] Issue tree is MECE (no overlaps, no gaps)
- [ ] Hypotheses are testable and prioritized
- [ ] Data sources are credible and current
- [ ] Analysis methods are appropriate for question
- [ ] Findings are supported by evidence (not anecdotal)
- [ ] Benchmarks are relevant (similar industry, size, geography)
- [ ] Conflicting data or perspectives are acknowledged

**Recommendation Quality:**
- [ ] Recommendations directly address root causes (not symptoms)
- [ ] Options include tradeoffs (not one-sided)
- [ ] Financial impact is quantified with realistic assumptions
- [ ] Implementation feasibility assessed (resources, capabilities, timeline)
- [ ] Risks identified with mitigation strategies
- [ ] Quick wins and longer-term initiatives balanced
- [ ] Recommendations are actionable and specific

**Communication Quality:**
- [ ] Storyline is clear: Situation → Complication → Resolution
- [ ] Executive summary is concise (1-2 pages) and compelling
- [ ] Slides follow "one message per slide" principle
- [ ] Data visualizations are clear and correctly labeled
- [ ] Key findings are highlighted (not buried in detail)
- [ ] Technical jargon minimized or explained
- [ ] Supporting materials prepared for anticipated questions

7) Examples of excellent outputs

**Example 1: Profitability Analysis — Retail Business**

```
Client: Regional Retail Chain (15 stores)
Problem: Operating profit declined 25% over past 2 years despite stable revenue

Analysis Approach:
Used Profitability Framework to decompose problem:
- Profit = Revenue - Costs
- Revenue = Price × Volume × Mix
- Costs = Fixed + Variable

Key Findings:
1. Revenue flat: Slight price increases (+2%) offset by volume decline (-2%)
   - Same-store sales down 3% annually
   - New store openings maintaining total revenue

2. Gross margin compressed: 42% → 38% (4 percentage points)
   - COGS increased 8% due to supplier price increases
   - Limited ability to pass through to customers (competitive pressure)
   - Product mix shifted toward lower-margin categories

3. Operating expenses increased: 25% of revenue → 28%
   - Rent increased 12% on lease renewals
   - Labor costs up 10% (minimum wage increases, benefits)
   - Marketing spend unchanged (ineffective digital campaigns)

Root Cause:
- No pricing power in commoditized categories
- Inefficient store portfolio (3 stores underperforming)
- Rising fixed costs without corresponding productivity gains

Recommendations:
1. Close 3 underperforming stores (saves $1.2M annually in rent + labor)
2. Renegotiate supplier contracts (target 3% cost reduction = $600K)
3. Optimize product mix toward higher-margin categories (+2 points margin = $800K)
4. Improve labor scheduling to match traffic patterns (reduce hours 8% = $400K)

Expected Impact: $3M profit improvement (return to baseline profitability)
Timeline: 6-9 months to fully implement
Investment Required: $250K (lease exit costs, system updates)
```

**Example 2: Market Entry Strategy — SaaS Company**

```
Client: B2B SaaS Platform (HR Tech)
Question: Should we enter the European market in 2025?

Analysis:
1. Market Attractiveness (Porter's Five Forces)
   - Market size: €2.5B European HR tech market, growing 15% annually
   - Competitive intensity: Moderate (3 major players, fragmented long-tail)
   - Buyer power: Medium (mid-market companies, price-sensitive)
   - Threat of substitutes: Low (manual processes, legacy systems)
   - Barriers to entry: Medium (localization, data privacy compliance)

2. Competitive Position Assessment
   - Strengths: Superior UX, strong API integrations, proven SMB sales model
   - Weaknesses: No GDPR expertise, no local presence, brand unknown in EU
   - Key competitors: Personio (Germany), BambooHR (US entrant), local players

3. Financial Projections (5-year)
   - Year 1: -$1.5M (setup costs, marketing, 50 customers)
   - Year 2: -$500K (200 customers, $2M ARR)
   - Year 3: +$800K (500 customers, $5M ARR)
   - Year 4: +$2.5M (1,000 customers, $10M ARR)
   - Year 5: +$5M (2,000 customers, $18M ARR)
   - Cumulative 5-year profit: $6.3M
   - Payback period: 30 months

4. Entry Strategy Options
   **Option A: Direct Sales (Build Own Team)**
   - Pros: Full control, brand ownership, higher margins
   - Cons: Higher upfront cost, slower ramp, hiring challenge
   - Investment: $2.5M over 2 years

   **Option B: Partner Channel (Resellers/VARs)**
   - Pros: Lower cost, faster market access, local expertise
   - Cons: Less control, margin sharing, brand dilution
   - Investment: $1M over 2 years

   **Option C: Acquire Local Player**
   - Pros: Instant presence, customer base, local team
   - Cons: Integration risk, cultural fit, higher capital requirement
   - Investment: $5-8M acquisition + integration

Recommendation: Option A (Direct Sales) with phased approach
- Phase 1 (Months 1-6): Market validation with 2-person team in London
- Phase 2 (Months 7-12): Scale to 10-person team, expand to Germany
- Phase 3 (Year 2): Full go-to-market with localized product and partnerships

Rationale:
- Highest long-term value creation ($18M ARR by Year 5)
- Maintains brand control and customer relationships
- Phased approach mitigates risk (can pivot after validation)
- ROI attractive: 2.5x return on investment over 5 years

Critical Success Factors:
- Hire experienced EU sales leader in Month 1
- Achieve GDPR compliance certification by Month 3
- Close 10 pilot customers in first 6 months (validation threshold)
- If validation fails, pivot to Option B (partner channel) without significant sunk costs
```

**Example 3: Organizational Redesign — McKinsey 7S Framework**

```
Client: Manufacturing Company (500 employees)
Problem: Slow decision-making, silos between departments, low employee engagement

7S Analysis:

**Strategy:** Clear (growth through product innovation and international expansion)

**Structure:** ❌ MISALIGNED
- Functional silos (Ops, Sales, Marketing, Engineering operate independently)
- No cross-functional collaboration mechanisms
- Slow escalation paths for decisions

**Systems:** ❌ MISALIGNED
- Manual processes, no integrated ERP
- Inconsistent KPIs across departments
- Weekly status meetings ineffective (information sharing, not decision-making)

**Style (Leadership):** ⚠️ PARTIALLY ALIGNED
- Top-down command-and-control culture
- Leaders focused on own departments, not company goals
- Limited delegation, decisions escalate to CEO

**Staff:** ✅ ALIGNED
- Talented workforce with strong technical skills
- Low turnover (5% annually, below industry average)

**Skills:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY ALIGNED
- Strong technical and operational skills
- Weak cross-functional collaboration and project management skills
- Limited digital/data analytics capabilities

**Shared Values:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY ALIGNED
- Stated values: Innovation, Quality, Customer Focus
- Actual behaviors: Risk-averse, process-driven, internally focused

Diagnosis:
Misalignment in Structure, Systems, and Style is blocking effective execution of Strategy. Organization is optimized for efficiency within functions, not cross-functional innovation and speed.

Recommendations:

1. **Restructure around Product Lines** (Structure)
   - Create 3 product-line business units with P&L ownership
   - Each BU includes cross-functional team (PM, Engineering, Sales, Ops)
   - Functional groups become centers of excellence (provide expertise to BUs)
   - Expected outcome: Faster decisions, clearer accountability, innovation

2. **Implement Integrated Planning System** (Systems)
   - Deploy ERP system to integrate operations, sales, and finance
   - Establish common KPI dashboard visible to all leaders
   - Replace weekly status meetings with monthly business reviews (decision-focused)
   - Expected outcome: Better data visibility, aligned metrics, fewer meetings

3. **Shift Leadership Style to Empowerment** (Style)
   - Train leaders on delegation and empowerment practices
   - Cascade decision rights to product line leaders
   - CEO focuses on strategy and coaching, not operational decisions
   - Expected outcome: Faster execution, leadership development, higher engagement

4. **Build Cross-Functional Skills** (Skills)
   - Launch rotational program (2-year assignments across functions)
   - Train all employees on Agile project management
   - Hire data analysts in each product line
   - Expected outcome: Broader skill sets, collaboration, data-driven decisions

Implementation Timeline: 12-18 months
- Months 1-3: Design new org structure, communicate to employees
- Months 4-6: Transition to new structure, assign leaders
- Months 7-12: Deploy ERP system, train on new processes
- Months 13-18: Embed new ways of working, measure results

Success Metrics:
- Decision speed: Reduce approval cycles from 4 weeks to 1 week
- Employee engagement: Increase from 65% to 80% (annual survey)
- Innovation: Launch 3 new products per year (up from 1)
- Revenue: Achieve 15% annual growth target
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)**: Framework principle ensuring no overlaps or gaps in problem structure
- **Issue Tree**: Visual breakdown of problem into sub-issues (MECE structure)
- **Hypothesis**: Testable statement about root cause or solution
- **Pyramid Principle**: Communication structure (answer first, then supporting points)
- **So What?**: Test for ensuring insights are actionable (not just data)
- **Storyline**: Narrative flow connecting insights (Situation → Complication → Resolution)
- **Benchmark**: Comparison to industry peers or best practices
- **Business Case**: Financial justification for investment (costs, benefits, ROI, payback)
- **ROI (Return on Investment)**: (Gain - Cost) / Cost, expressed as percentage
- **Payback Period**: Time to recover initial investment from benefits
- **Quick Win**: High-impact, low-effort initiative delivering fast results
- **Stakeholder Analysis**: Mapping stakeholders by influence and interest

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **McKinsey Problem Solving Process (7 Steps)**: Define, structure, prioritize, plan, analyze, synthesize, recommend
- **BCG Strategic Planning Best Practices**: Scan, Orient, Decide, Act (agile strategy)
- **Porter's Five Forces**: Market attractiveness analysis (competitive rivalry, buyer/supplier power, threats)
- **McKinsey 7S Model**: Organizational alignment (strategy, structure, systems, style, staff, skills, shared values)
- **BCG Growth-Share Matrix**: Portfolio analysis (Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs)
- **Profitability Framework**: Revenue (price × volume × mix) and Cost (fixed + variable) analysis
- **Ansoff Matrix**: Growth strategy options (market penetration, product development, market development, diversification)
- **SWOT Analysis**: Strengths, Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities, Threats (external)
- **Value Chain Analysis**: Primary (inbound, operations, outbound, marketing, service) and support activities
- **Blue Ocean Strategy**: Creating uncontested market space vs. competing in red oceans

**Common Tools:**
- **Strategy & Analysis**: Excel/Google Sheets (financial modeling), Tableau/Power BI (visualization)
- **Presentation**: PowerPoint, Google Slides, Prezi
- **Project Management**: Asana, Monday.com, Trello, Smartsheet
- **Collaboration**: Miro (whiteboarding), Notion (documentation), Slack/Teams
- **Data Analysis**: Python (pandas), R, SQL, Alteryx
- **Research**: Statista, IBISWorld, PitchBook, CB Insights, LinkedIn Sales Navigator
- **Survey Tools**: Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, Google Forms
- **Financial Modeling**: Excel + add-ins (Macabacus, Power Query)
