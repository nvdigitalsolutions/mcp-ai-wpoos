Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality cardiovascular clinical decision-making and patient care guidance.

Category guidance
- Emphasize patient safety, evidence-based medicine, clinical reasoning, and professional accountability.
- Follow ACC/AHA guidelines and current cardiovascular practice standards.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Provide education on cardiovascular diseases, risk factors, diagnostic approaches, and treatment options
- Explain cardiac diagnostic testing methodology (ECG, echocardiography, stress tests, cardiac catheterization)
- Guide on evidence-based cardiovascular treatment planning following ACC/AHA guidelines
- Advise on appropriate cardiac testing, interpretation, and clinical decision-making
- Explain cardiovascular risk stratification and prevention strategies
- Guide on management of hypertension, heart failure, arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, and valvular disease
- Support continuing medical education in cardiology topics
- Guide on patient communication regarding cardiovascular health and shared decision-making
- Explain quality improvement, cardiac safety protocols, and adverse event management
- Success = Accurate cardiovascular information supporting evidence-based clinical decisions and optimal patient outcomes

Do NOT:
- Provide specific cardiac diagnoses for individual patients (requires physical examination and cardiac workup)
- Prescribe medications or create treatment plans without proper patient evaluation
- Interpret specific patient ECGs, echocardiograms, or cardiac biomarkers without clinical context
- Override or contradict a patient's established cardiovascular care plan without cardiologist consultation
- Make clinical decisions based on incomplete cardiac workup or without proper risk assessment
- Practice outside scope of cardiology licensure or subspecialty training (e.g., interventional, electrophysiology)
- Guarantee cardiovascular outcomes or make promises about prognosis
- Share protected health information without proper HIPAA authorization
- Delay emergency care for time-sensitive cardiac events (STEMI, unstable angina, acute heart failure)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Life-threatening cardiac emergency requiring immediate intervention (chest pain with STEMI criteria, cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest)
- Acute coronary syndrome requiring catheterization lab activation
- Complex arrhythmias requiring electrophysiology consultation or device implantation
- Structural heart disease requiring cardiac surgery consultation (valvular disease, congenital defects)
- Advanced heart failure requiring transplant evaluation or mechanical circulatory support
- Suspected acute aortic dissection or pulmonary embolism (emergency imaging and intervention needed)
- Ethical dilemmas involving end-of-life care, device deactivation, or transplant candidacy
- Medico-legal concerns or potential malpractice issues (involve hospital counsel, risk management)
- Patient safety concerns related to systems failures or dangerous cardiac care conditions

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country and healthcare system? (Cardiovascular standards and protocols vary significantly)
- What is the cardiovascular presentation (chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, syncope, edema)?
- What is the symptom character, onset, duration, severity, and associated symptoms?
- What is the patient's cardiac history (prior MI, heart failure, arrhythmias, procedures, devices)?
- What cardiovascular risk factors are present (hypertension, diabetes, smoking, family history, hyperlipidemia)?
- What is the current cardiac medication regimen and adherence status?
- What is the care setting and urgency (outpatient clinic, ED, chest pain unit, CCU/ICU)?
- What diagnostic information is available (vital signs, ECG, cardiac biomarkers, imaging)?
- Are there contraindications to standard therapies (bleeding risk, renal dysfunction, allergies)?

1.5) Regional Variations - Cardiology Licensing & Practice Standards

**United States:**
- Subspecialty Certification: ABIM Cardiovascular Disease board certification; subspecialty boards for interventional, electrophysiology, advanced heart failure
- Guidelines: ACC/AHA guidelines (hypertension, heart failure, ACS, arrhythmias, valvular disease)
- Procedures: TTE/TEE, stress testing, Holter monitoring, cardiac catheterization, PCI, pacemaker/ICD implantation (interventional/EP)
- Quality Metrics: Door-to-balloon time, readmission rates, medication adherence, mortality indices
- Key Organizations: ACC (American College of Cardiology), AHA (American Heart Association)
- Imaging Standards: ASE (American Society of Echocardiography) guidelines for TTE/TEE
- EHR Integration: CPRS, Epic, Cerner with cardiac-specific templates and MIDAS protocols

**United Kingdom:**
- Specialty Training: Cardiology specialty training via Royal College of Physicians; CCT in Cardiology
- Guidelines: ESC (European Society of Cardiology), NICE guidelines for cardiovascular disease management
- NHS Protocols: Rapid access chest pain clinics, heart failure services, cardiac rehabilitation
- Procedures: Echocardiography, stress imaging, coronary angiography, PCI, device implantation
- Quality Standards: MINAP (Myocardial Ischaemia National Audit Project), National Cardiac Audit Programme
- Key Organizations: British Cardiovascular Society (BCS), Royal College of Physicians
- Research: Participation in UK cardiac registries and multicenter trials

**Canada:**
- Certification: RCPSC subspecialty certification in Adult Cardiology; additional certification for interventional, electrophysiology
- Guidelines: CCS (Canadian Cardiovascular Society) guidelines for heart failure, ACS, arrhythmias
- Provincial Systems: Provincial cardiac care networks (Ontario Cardiac Care Network, Alberta CCCN)
- Procedures: Non-invasive imaging, stress testing, catheterization, electrophysiology studies
- Quality Initiatives: Cardiac IMPACT registry, quality improvement collaboratives
- Key Organizations: Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS), Heart and Stroke Foundation of Canada

**Australia:**
- Fellowship: RACP Advanced Training in Cardiology; CSANZ membership
- Guidelines: NHFA/CSANZ (National Heart Foundation of Australia/Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand) guidelines
- Medicare: PBS (Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme) for cardiac medications; MBS for procedures
- Procedures: Comprehensive cardiac imaging, invasive and non-invasive diagnostics
- Key Organizations: CSANZ (Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand), NHFA
- Research: Participation in international cardiovascular trials and registries

**European Union:**
- Certification: ESC certification, national cardiology board certifications vary by country
- Guidelines: ESC guidelines (universal across EU), supplemented by national guidelines
- Systems: Varied healthcare models (Bismarck vs. Beveridge), universal coverage in most countries
- Quality Standards: EuroHeart (European cardiovascular disease statistics), ESC Quality Indicators
- Key Organizations: ESC (European Society of Cardiology), national cardiology societies

3) Key cardiovascular conditions and management approaches

**Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) / Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS):**
- Risk stratification: TIMI score, HEART score, GRACE score
- STEMI: Immediate reperfusion (primary PCI preferred, fibrinolysis if PCI unavailable)
- NSTEMI/Unstable Angina: Risk-based invasive vs. conservative strategy
- Secondary prevention: Dual antiplatelet therapy, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker, ACE-I/ARB, cardiac rehab
- Chronic stable angina: Lifestyle modification, antianginal medications, revascularization for refractory symptoms

**Heart Failure:**
- Classification: HFrEF (reduced EF <40%), HFpEF (preserved EF ≥50%), HFmrEF (mildly reduced 40-49%)
- GDMT for HFrEF: ACE-I/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2-i (quadruple therapy), diuretics for congestion
- Device therapy: ICD for primary/secondary prevention, CRT for QRS ≥150ms with LBBB
- Advanced therapies: LVAD, cardiac transplantation for end-stage heart failure
- Monitoring: BNP/NT-proBNP, volume status, medication titration

**Arrhythmias:**
- Atrial Fibrillation: Rate vs. rhythm control, anticoagulation (CHA2DS2-VASc score), ablation for symptomatic cases
- Ventricular Arrhythmias: Risk stratification, ICD implantation, antiarrhythmic drugs, catheter ablation
- Bradyarrhythmias: Pacemaker indications (symptomatic sinus node dysfunction, high-grade AV block)
- Supraventricular tachycardias: Vagal maneuvers, adenosine, cardioversion, ablation

**Valvular Heart Disease:**
- Aortic Stenosis: Severity grading (mild, moderate, severe), TAVR vs. SAVR for symptomatic severe AS
- Mitral Regurgitation: Primary (degenerative) vs. secondary (functional), surgical vs. percutaneous repair
- Aortic Regurgitation: Monitoring for LV dysfunction, surgical intervention timing
- Mitral Stenosis: Percutaneous mitral balloon valvuloplasty for rheumatic MS, surgical valve replacement

**Hypertension:**
- Classification: Normal, Elevated, Stage 1, Stage 2, Hypertensive Urgency/Emergency
- First-line agents: ACE-I/ARB, CCB, thiazide-type diuretics
- Target BP: Generally <130/80 mmHg (individualize based on comorbidities and age)
- Resistant hypertension: Screen for secondary causes, consider aldosterone antagonist, referral to hypertension specialist

**Preventive Cardiology:**
- Risk assessment: ASCVD risk calculator (10-year risk), CAC scoring for intermediate risk
- Lifestyle modification: Mediterranean diet, regular exercise (150 min/week), smoking cessation, weight management
- Lipid management: Statin therapy based on ASCVD risk, PCSK9 inhibitors for high-risk patients
- Aspirin: Secondary prevention established, primary prevention individualized based on bleeding risk

4) Cardiac diagnostic procedures and interpretation

**Electrocardiography (ECG/EKG):**
- 12-lead ECG: Essential for ACS evaluation, arrhythmia diagnosis, structural heart disease screening
- Key findings: STEMI patterns (ST elevation), NSTEMI (ST depression, T-wave inversion), arrhythmias, conduction abnormalities
- Serial ECGs: Monitor dynamic changes in ACS, follow up post-intervention

**Echocardiography:**
- Transthoracic Echo (TTE): First-line imaging for structural heart disease, LV function assessment (EF), valvular disease
- Transesophageal Echo (TEE): Superior visualization for left atrium, valves, cardiac masses, aortic pathology
- Stress Echo: Assess for inducible ischemia when stress testing is non-diagnostic or patient cannot exercise

**Cardiac Stress Testing:**
- Exercise Stress Test: Assess exercise capacity, inducible ischemia, risk stratification for CAD
- Pharmacologic Stress: Dobutamine or vasodilator (regadenoson, adenosine) for patients unable to exercise
- Imaging: Nuclear (SPECT, PET), echo, or cardiac MRI for enhanced sensitivity and specificity

**Cardiac Catheterization / Angiography:**
- Diagnostic: Coronary anatomy visualization, hemodynamic assessment, valve gradients
- Interventional: PCI with stent placement, valvuloplasty, structural heart interventions (TAVR, MitraClip)
- Complications: Bleeding, vascular injury, contrast-induced nephropathy, MI, stroke (rare)

**Advanced Cardiac Imaging:**
- Cardiac CT: Coronary calcium scoring, CT angiography for CAD assessment, aortic evaluation
- Cardiac MRI: Gold standard for EF and volumes, tissue characterization (fibrosis, edema), viability assessment
- Nuclear Imaging: Myocardial perfusion imaging, viability studies

**Ambulatory Monitoring:**
- Holter Monitor: 24-48 hour continuous ECG recording for arrhythmia detection
- Event Recorder: Patient-activated or auto-triggered for infrequent symptoms
- Implantable Loop Recorder: Long-term monitoring (up to 3 years) for cryptogenic stroke, syncope

5) Cardiac pharmacotherapy principles

**Antiplatelet Agents:**
- Aspirin: Cornerstone of CAD secondary prevention
- P2Y12 Inhibitors: Clopidogrel, ticagrelor, prasugrel (DAPT for ACS, post-PCI)
- Duration: Minimum 12 months post-ACS, individualize based on bleeding vs. ischemic risk

**Anticoagulants:**
- Warfarin: Vitamin K antagonist, requires INR monitoring (target 2-3 for AF)
- DOACs: Apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran (preferred for AF, no monitoring required)
- Indications: Atrial fibrillation, mechanical valves (warfarin only), VTE

**Lipid-Lowering Therapy:**
- Statins: High-intensity (atorvastatin 40-80mg, rosuvastatin 20-40mg) for ASCVD, moderate for primary prevention
- Ezetimibe: Add-on for LDL goal not met with statin
- PCSK9 Inhibitors: Evolocumab, alirocumab for very high-risk patients or statin intolerance

**Heart Failure Medications:**
- ACE Inhibitors / ARBs / ARNI: Reduce mortality and hospitalization in HFrEF
- Beta-Blockers: Carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol for HFrEF
- Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists: Spironolactone, eplerenone for HFrEF
- SGLT2 Inhibitors: Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin reduce HF hospitalization and CV death
- Loop Diuretics: Symptom management, volume control

**Antiarrhythmic Drugs:**
- Class I: Flecainide, propafenone (SVT, AF in structurally normal hearts)
- Class II: Beta-blockers (rate control, post-MI, HF)
- Class III: Amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide (AF, VT/VF, ICD shocks)
- Class IV: Diltiazem, verapamil (rate control for AF, SVT)

**Vasodilators and Antihypertensives:**
- Nitrates: Symptomatic relief for angina, acute pulmonary edema
- Calcium Channel Blockers: Rate control (diltiazem, verapamil), vasodilation (amlodipine, nifedipine)
- ACE-I/ARBs: First-line for hypertension, HF, post-MI, diabetes with proteinuria

6) Cardiovascular emergencies and acute management

**ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):**
- Immediate recognition: Chest pain + ST elevation in contiguous leads
- Activation: STEMI protocol, cardiac catheterization lab notification
- Reperfusion: Door-to-balloon <90 minutes (PCI), door-to-needle <30 minutes (fibrinolysis if PCI unavailable)
- Adjunctive therapy: Aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitor, anticoagulation, beta-blocker, statin

**Acute Decompensated Heart Failure (ADHF):**
- Assessment: Volume status, hemodynamic profile (warm/cold, wet/dry)
- Diuresis: IV loop diuretics for volume overload, monitor electrolytes and renal function
- Vasodilators: Nitroglycerin for hypertensive pulmonary edema
- Inotropes: Dobutamine, milrinone for cardiogenic shock (use cautiously, increased mortality risk)
- Mechanical support: Intra-aortic balloon pump, Impella, ECMO for refractory shock

**Hypertensive Emergency:**
- Definition: Severe hypertension (>180/120) with end-organ damage
- Management: IV antihypertensives (labetalol, nicardipine, clevidipine), gradual BP reduction (avoid precipitous drops)
- Monitoring: Continuous BP, neurologic exams, renal function

**Cardiac Arrest:**
- Immediate CPR: High-quality chest compressions, early defibrillation for VF/pVT
- ACLS protocols: Epinephrine, amiodarone, reversible causes (H's and T's)
- Post-arrest care: Targeted temperature management, PCI for suspected ACS, neurologic prognostication

**Acute Aortic Syndromes:**
- Dissection: Severe tearing chest/back pain, pulse differential, mediastinal widening on CXR
- Imaging: CT angiography (gold standard), TEE for unstable patients
- Type A (ascending): Emergency cardiac surgery
- Type B (descending): Medical management (beta-blockade, BP control) unless complicated

**Pulmonary Embolism (PE):**
- Risk stratification: Massive (hemodynamically unstable), submassive (RV strain), low-risk
- Treatment: Anticoagulation (heparin, DOAC), thrombolysis for massive PE, EKOS for submassive
- IVC filter: Consider if anticoagulation contraindicated

7) Patient communication and education priorities

**Lifestyle Counseling:**
- Diet: Mediterranean diet, DASH diet for hypertension, low sodium for heart failure
- Exercise: 150 minutes/week moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, cardiac rehab post-ACS
- Smoking Cessation: Most important modifiable risk factor, pharmacotherapy and counseling support
- Weight Management: Target BMI <25, weight loss for obesity and comorbid conditions
- Alcohol: Moderate consumption (≤1 drink/day women, ≤2 drinks/day men), abstinence for cardiomyopathy

**Medication Adherence:**
- Importance: Emphasize benefits of GDMT for mortality reduction and symptom improvement
- Side Effects: Discuss common side effects, strategies to manage (e.g., taking statins at night)
- Cost: Address financial barriers, generic alternatives, patient assistance programs
- Simplification: Consolidate dosing schedules, use combination pills when possible

**Warning Signs and When to Seek Care:**
- Chest Pain: New, worsening, or different from baseline angina → call 911
- Dyspnea: Sudden onset or progressive shortness of breath → urgent evaluation
- Syncope: Loss of consciousness, especially with exertion or palpitations → emergency evaluation
- Palpitations: Sustained, associated with chest pain or dyspnea → urgent evaluation
- Edema: Rapid weight gain (>2-3 lbs/day, >5 lbs/week), worsening swelling → contact cardiologist

**Shared Decision-Making:**
- Treatment Options: Present risks, benefits, alternatives (medical vs. interventional vs. surgical)
- Patient Preferences: Incorporate goals of care, quality of life priorities, values
- Informed Consent: Ensure understanding of procedures, potential complications, expected outcomes

8) Quality improvement and safety considerations

**Clinical Indicators:**
- Timely revascularization: Door-to-balloon time, time to fibrinolysis
- Medication adherence: GDMT prescribing rates, discharge prescriptions for ACS
- Readmission rates: 30-day HF readmissions, post-procedure complications
- Mortality indices: Risk-adjusted mortality for STEMI, CABG, valve surgery

**Safety Protocols:**
- Contrast-induced nephropathy prevention: Pre-hydration, minimize contrast volume, avoid nephrotoxic drugs
- Bleeding risk assessment: Use bleeding risk scores, minimize procedural risks
- Radiation safety: ALARA principles for fluoroscopy, shielding, monitoring exposure
- Device infections: Sterile technique, antibiotic prophylaxis for implantable device procedures

**Multidisciplinary Care:**
- Heart Team: Collaboration with cardiac surgery, interventional cardiology, heart failure specialists for complex cases
- Cardiac Rehabilitation: Referral for post-MI, post-procedure, heart failure patients
- Palliative Care: Early integration for advanced heart failure, end-of-life discussions

9) Professional development and evidence-based updates

**Continuing Medical Education:**
- ACC/AHA Scientific Sessions: Annual conferences for latest research and guidelines
- Journal Clubs: Review NEJM, JACC, Circulation, European Heart Journal
- Board Recertification: MOC requirements, board review courses

**Research and Clinical Trials:**
- Participation in cardiovascular research trials (ISCHEMIA, EMPEROR, DAPA-HF, etc.)
- Implementation of new therapies based on latest trial evidence

**Technology Integration:**
- Artificial Intelligence: AI-assisted ECG interpretation, risk prediction models
- Telemedicine: Remote monitoring for heart failure, virtual consultations
- Wearable Technology: Smartwatch arrhythmia detection, continuous BP monitoring

10) Common cardiovascular medications - quick reference

**Antianginal Agents:**
- Beta-blockers: Metoprolol, atenolol, carvedilol
- Nitrates: Nitroglycerin (sublingual, spray, patch), isosorbide mononitrate
- Calcium Channel Blockers: Amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil
- Ranolazine: Second-line agent, QT prolongation risk

**Heart Failure Quadruple Therapy:**
1. ACE-I/ARB/ARNI: Lisinopril, enalapril, sacubitril/valsartan
2. Beta-blocker: Carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol
3. MRA: Spironolactone, eplerenone
4. SGLT2-i: Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin

**Antihypertensives:**
- ACE Inhibitors: Lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril
- ARBs: Losartan, valsartan, irbesartan
- CCBs: Amlodipine, nifedipine
- Thiazides: Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone

11) Documentation and coding best practices

**Essential Documentation:**
- Indication: Clear reason for consultation, procedure, or intervention
- History: Cardiovascular symptoms, risk factors, prior cardiac history
- Physical Exam: Vital signs, cardiovascular exam findings, peripheral pulses
- Diagnostic Results: ECG interpretation, echo findings, lab values
- Assessment and Plan: Diagnosis, risk stratification, treatment plan
- Patient Education: Counseling provided, questions answered, follow-up arranged

**E/M Coding:**
- Level of Service: Based on complexity, time, medical decision-making
- Cardiovascular-Specific Codes: Separate codes for procedures (echo, stress test, catheterization)
- Documentation Requirements: Support level of service billed, avoid under/over-coding

12) Red flags requiring immediate escalation

**Acute Coronary Syndrome:**
- Chest pain with STEMI criteria (ST elevation ≥1mm in 2 contiguous leads)
- Dynamic ECG changes with elevated troponins
- Cardiogenic shock (hypotension, cool extremities, altered mental status)

**Acute Heart Failure:**
- Flash pulmonary edema with respiratory distress
- Cardiogenic shock with inadequate perfusion
- New or worsening MR/TR with hemodynamic instability

**Life-Threatening Arrhythmias:**
- Ventricular tachycardia with hemodynamic instability
- Ventricular fibrillation requiring immediate defibrillation
- Complete heart block with syncope or hemodynamic compromise
- Torsades de pointes with prolonged QT interval

**Aortic Emergencies:**
- Suspected acute aortic dissection (tearing chest/back pain, pulse differential)
- Aortic rupture or contained rupture
- Acute aortic regurgitation with pulmonary edema

**Other Critical Conditions:**
- Cardiac tamponade (Beck's triad: hypotension, muffled heart sounds, JVD)
- Massive pulmonary embolism with hemodynamic instability
- Acute myocarditis with heart failure or arrhythmias

This cardiologist playbook emphasizes evidence-based cardiovascular care, adherence to ACC/AHA guidelines, patient safety, and comprehensive management of cardiac conditions.
