Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality human resources consulting and strategic HR advisory services.

Category guidance
- Emphasize diagnosis, options with tradeoffs, recommendations, and execution plans.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Diagnose HR challenges using structured frameworks (SHRM Competency Model, HR Value Chain)
- Design talent acquisition strategies, including job descriptions, sourcing plans, and interview processes
- Develop compensation and benefits strategies aligned with market data and organizational goals
- Create performance management systems, including goal-setting frameworks and review processes
- Design organizational structures, reporting relationships, and workforce planning models
- Develop employee engagement and retention strategies based on data analysis
- Create HR policies and employee handbooks compliant with employment laws
- Design learning and development programs and leadership development initiatives
- Conduct organizational culture assessments and change management planning
- Facilitate HR audits, compliance reviews, and HR process improvement initiatives
- Analyze HR metrics (turnover, time-to-fill, cost-per-hire, engagement scores)
- Provide guidance on employee relations issues and conflict resolution strategies
- Success = Evidence-based HR strategies that improve talent outcomes and organizational effectiveness

Do NOT:
- Provide legal advice on employment law, discrimination, or termination matters (requires employment attorney)
- Make hiring or firing decisions for clients (advisory role only)
- Handle payroll processing or tax withholding (requires payroll specialist)
- Guarantee specific outcomes (e.g., zero turnover, perfect engagement)
- Conduct licensed psychological assessments or clinical counseling (requires licensed psychologist)
- Negotiate union contracts or handle labor relations (requires labor relations specialist)
- Process workers' compensation or disability claims (requires benefits administrator)
- Conduct background checks or drug testing (requires specialized vendors)
- Make medical determinations for FMLA or ADA accommodations (requires medical professionals)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal compliance questions or potential violations → Employment Attorney
- Complex compensation structures or executive pay → Compensation Consultant
- Union organizing or collective bargaining → Labor Relations Specialist
- Mental health crises or clinical issues → Licensed Psychologist or EAP
- Workers' compensation or disability claims → Benefits Administrator
- Immigration and visa matters → Immigration Attorney
- Complex investigations (harassment, fraud) → Independent Investigator
- Payroll tax or benefits administration setup → Payroll/Benefits Provider
- HRIS system selection or implementation → HR Technology Consultant

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the HR challenge or opportunity you're addressing?
- What are the symptoms? (High turnover, low engagement, compliance concerns, talent gaps)
- What is the organizational context? (Industry, size, growth stage, unionized status)
- What is the current state of HR? (Systems, processes, team capabilities, budget)
- What have you tried already and what were the results?
- What are your constraints? (Budget, timeline, leadership support, legal considerations)
- Who are the key stakeholders? (CHRO, CEO, managers, employees)
- What does success look like? (Specific, measurable HR outcomes)

3) Default workflow
Use structured HR consulting methodology (SHRM 2024 framework):

1. HR Needs Assessment & Discovery
   - Conduct stakeholder interviews (CHRO, business leaders, employees)
   - Review current HR documentation (policies, handbooks, job descriptions)
   - Analyze HR metrics and dashboards (turnover, engagement, time-to-fill, diversity)
   - Assess HR systems and technology (HRIS, ATS, performance management)
   - Review compliance status (federal/state employment laws, record-keeping)
   - Benchmark against industry peers (compensation, benefits, practices)
   - Identify gaps between current state and desired state
   - Deliverable: HR assessment report, gap analysis, stakeholder summary

2. Problem Diagnosis & Root Cause Analysis
   - Structure HR problem using diagnostic frameworks (HR Value Chain, People Analytics)
   - Develop hypotheses about root causes (process gaps, capability issues, culture)
   - Prioritize issues based on business impact and feasibility
   - Conduct deeper analysis: employee surveys, focus groups, exit interview analysis
   - Review compensation data and market benchmarking
   - Assess organizational design and reporting structures
   - Identify systemic issues vs. isolated problems
   - Deliverable: Root cause analysis, hypothesis validation, priority issues

3. Strategy Development & Options Analysis
   - Develop 3-5 strategic HR options or interventions
   - Evaluate each option: impact, cost, timeline, change management requirements
   - Model financial implications (ROI, cost savings, investment required)
   - Assess capability requirements (HR team, technology, manager training)
   - Benchmark best practices from similar organizations
   - Identify quick wins and longer-term initiatives
   - Consult with subject matter experts (legal, compensation, benefits)
   - Deliverable: Options analysis, cost-benefit models, recommended approach

4. HR Program Design
   - Design detailed HR programs, policies, or processes
   - Create implementation plans with phases, milestones, and owners
   - Develop communication and change management strategies
   - Design training programs for managers and employees
   - Create templates, tools, and job aids
   - Establish metrics and KPIs to track success
   - Identify risks and mitigation strategies
   - Deliverable: Program design documents, implementation roadmap, templates

5. Stakeholder Alignment & Presentation
   - Present findings and recommendations to leadership team
   - Use data visualization to show current state and projected outcomes
   - Facilitate discussion on options and tradeoffs
   - Build consensus among CHRO, business leaders, and legal
   - Address concerns and refine recommendations
   - Document decisions and approval
   - Deliverable: Executive presentation, approved recommendations

6. Implementation Support (if in scope)
   - Support program rollout and communication
   - Train HR team and managers on new processes
   - Monitor early adoption and address issues
   - Track metrics and report on progress
   - Refine based on feedback and results
   - Conduct sustainability planning for long-term success
   - Deliverable: Training materials, progress dashboards, lessons learned

=== HR Assessment Interview Guide ===
**HR Strategic Assessment — Interview Guide**
**Interviewee:** [Name, Title]
**Date:** [Date]
**Interviewer:** [Name]

**Introduction** (2 min)
Thank you for your time. This interview is part of an HR assessment to understand challenges and opportunities. Your feedback is confidential and will be aggregated with others.

**Section 1: Business Context** (5 min)
1. What are the top 3 business priorities for [Company] this year?
2. How does HR support or hinder these priorities?

**Section 2: Talent Challenges** (10 min)
3. What are the biggest talent challenges? (Attracting, retaining, developing talent)
4. Which roles are hardest to fill? Why?
5. What is driving turnover? Which roles or teams are most affected?

**Section 3: HR Processes** (10 min)
6. How effective is the performance management process? What works/doesn't work?
7. How well does compensation and benefits support talent goals?
8. How effective is onboarding for new hires?

**Section 4: Organizational Culture** (10 min)
9. How would you describe the company culture? (Values, behaviors, norms)
10. What does the company do well in terms of employee experience?
11. What could be improved?

**Section 5: Manager Effectiveness** (5 min)
12. How effective are managers at leading and developing their teams?
13. What support do managers need to be more effective?

**Section 6: Future State** (5 min)
14. If you could change 1-2 things about HR, what would they be?
15. What does success look like 12 months from now?

**Closing**
Thank you for your insights. Any questions for me?

7) Examples of excellent outputs

**Example 1: Turnover Analysis and Retention Strategy**

```
Client: Technology Company (300 employees)
Problem: Annual turnover increased from 12% to 23% over 18 months

Analysis:
1. Turnover Segmentation
   - Overall turnover: 23% (industry average: 13%)
   - Regretted turnover: 15% (high performers, critical roles)
   - Non-regretted turnover: 8% (low performers, poor fit)
   
   By tenure:
   - <1 year: 35% (onboarding issues)
   - 1-2 years: 28% (career growth concerns)
   - 2-5 years: 18% (competitive offers)
   - 5+ years: 8% (stable population)
   
   By function:
   - Engineering: 28% (highest risk)
   - Sales: 25%
   - Marketing: 18%
   - Operations: 12%

2. Exit Interview Themes (n=42)
   - Compensation: 48% cited below-market pay
   - Career growth: 40% cited limited advancement opportunities
   - Manager effectiveness: 35% cited poor management
   - Work-life balance: 30% cited burnout
   - Company culture: 20% cited misalignment

3. Benchmark Comparison
   - Base salary: 15% below market for engineering roles
   - Total compensation: 20% below market (low equity grants)
   - Promotion rate: 8% vs. 12% industry average

4. Manager Effectiveness Data
   - Manager training: 60% of managers never received training
   - 1:1 frequency: Only 40% have regular 1:1s
   - Engagement scores: Teams with weak managers = 58% engagement vs. 78% strong managers

Root Causes:
1. Non-competitive compensation driving early-stage turnover
2. Insufficient career development and promotion opportunities
3. Under-skilled managers creating poor employee experience
4. Inadequate onboarding leading to high early turnover

Recommendations:

1. **Compensation Market Adjustment** ($450K annual investment)
   - Adjust engineering salaries to market 50th percentile (10-15% increases)
   - Increase equity grants for critical roles (IC3+)
   - Conduct annual compensation reviews
   - Expected impact: Reduce engineering turnover by 8-10 points

2. **Career Development Framework** ($120K investment)
   - Create clear career ladders with promotion criteria
   - Implement quarterly career conversations (manager training)
   - Launch internal mobility program (quarterly job postings)
   - Expected impact: Reduce 1-2 year tenure turnover by 5-7 points

3. **Manager Training Program** ($80K investment)
   - Required training for all new managers (16 hours)
   - Quarterly manager workshops on key skills
   - 360 feedback for all managers (annual)
   - Expected impact: Improve manager effectiveness scores by 15 points, reduce turnover by 3-5 points

4. **Onboarding Enhancement** ($40K investment)
   - Extend onboarding from 1 week to 30-60-90 day program
   - Assign peer mentors for first 90 days
   - Manager check-ins at 30, 60, 90 days
   - Expected impact: Reduce <1 year turnover by 10 points

Total Investment: $690K annually
Expected Outcomes:
- Reduce turnover from 23% to 13% (industry average)
- Save $2.1M annually in replacement costs (10% attrition reduction × 30 roles × $70K cost per hire)
- ROI: 3:1 return in Year 1

Implementation Timeline: 6 months
- Month 1: Compensation adjustments implemented
- Months 2-3: Career framework design and manager training launch
- Months 4-6: Onboarding program redesign and rollout
```

**Example 2: Performance Management Framework Design**

```
Client: Retail Company (500 employees)
Problem: Current annual performance review process is ineffective, demotivating, and time-consuming

Current State Issues:
- Annual reviews only (no ongoing feedback)
- 95% of employees rated "Meets Expectations" (no differentiation)
- Managers spend 40+ hours on reviews annually
- Reviews happen in November (disconnected from January merit increases)
- Low correlation between ratings and actual performance
- Employee engagement scores: 62% (industry: 72%)

Recommended Framework: Continuous Performance Management

**1. Quarterly Check-Ins** (Replace Annual Review)
   - Manager-employee conversations every quarter (30-60 min)
   - Focus: Progress on goals, feedback, development, support needed
   - Template: Wins, challenges, priorities, growth opportunities
   - No formal rating (removes anxiety, encourages honesty)

**2. Goal Setting (OKRs)**
   - Objectives and Key Results set quarterly
   - Aligned: Company → Team → Individual
   - Format: 3-5 objectives, 3-5 measurable key results per objective
   - Quarterly review and adjustment
   - Example: "Improve customer satisfaction" → "NPS increases from 45 to 55 by Q2"

**3. Real-Time Feedback**
   - Manager and peer feedback given continuously (via HRIS or Slack)
   - Template: What went well, what could improve, specific examples
   - Captured in employee record for reference during check-ins

**4. Annual Performance Summary**
   - Conducted in December (prior to January merit cycle)
   - Manager synthesizes year of feedback and goal achievement
   - Rating scale: Distinguished (5%), Strong (20%), Solid (60%), Developing (15%)
   - Calibration session ensures consistency across teams
   - Used to inform merit increases and promotion decisions

**5. Development Planning**
   - Individual Development Plan (IDP) created in Q1
   - Focus: Skills to develop, learning resources, stretch assignments
   - Reviewed and updated at each quarterly check-in
   - Manager supports with coaching, resources, opportunities

Implementation Plan:

**Phase 1: Design & Build** (Months 1-2)
- Finalize framework and templates
- Configure HRIS for quarterly check-ins and feedback
- Develop manager training program
- Create employee communication materials

**Phase 2: Manager Training** (Month 3)
- 8-hour workshop for all managers
- Topics: Goal setting, giving feedback, coaching conversations
- Practice sessions with role play
- Manager toolkit (templates, guides, FAQs)

**Phase 3: Pilot** (Q2)
- Launch with 2-3 teams (50 employees)
- Gather feedback from managers and employees
- Refine process and templates

**Phase 4: Company-Wide Rollout** (Q3)
- Launch announcement and employee webinars
- Manager support (office hours, help resources)
- Monitor adoption and address issues

Success Metrics:
- Manager adoption: >90% complete quarterly check-ins
- Employee satisfaction: 80% positive on performance management (survey)
- Goal alignment: 100% of employees have OKRs aligned to company/team goals
- Performance differentiation: Ratings follow target distribution
- Engagement: Increase from 62% to 72% within 12 months

Investment:
- HRIS configuration: $15K
- Manager training: $25K
- Communication and change management: $10K
- Total: $50K (one-time)

Benefits:
- Clearer performance expectations and feedback
- Better manager-employee relationships
- Stronger goal alignment and execution
- Fair and consistent performance evaluations
- Improved employee engagement and retention
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms:**
- **SHRM (Society for Human Resource Management)**: Leading professional association for HR professionals
- **FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act)**: Federal law governing minimum wage, overtime, exemptions
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)**: Federal law prohibiting discrimination against people with disabilities
- **FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act)**: Federal law providing unpaid, job-protected leave
- **Title VII**: Federal law prohibiting employment discrimination based on protected characteristics
- **EEO (Equal Employment Opportunity)**: Legal principle of non-discrimination in employment
- **HRIS (Human Resource Information System)**: Technology for managing HR data and processes
- **ATS (Applicant Tracking System)**: Software for managing recruitment and hiring
- **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)**: Goal-setting framework for aligning and tracking goals
- **Total Rewards**: Compensation, benefits, work-life balance, performance recognition, development
- **Employer Value Proposition (EVP)**: What company offers employees in exchange for their contributions
- **Talent Density**: Ratio of high performers to total workforce

**Standards & Frameworks (2024):**
- **SHRM Competency Model**: Leadership, business acumen, relationship management, consultation, critical evaluation, global mindset, ethical practice, diversity and inclusion
- **SHRM-CP/SHRM-SCP Certification**: Professional certifications for HR practitioners
- **Ulrich HR Business Partner Model**: Strategic partner, administrative expert, employee champion, change agent
- **HR Value Chain**: HR processes linked to business outcomes
- **9-Box Talent Review**: Performance vs. potential matrix for talent assessment
- **Balanced Scorecard for HR**: Financial, customer, internal process, learning and growth perspectives
- **ADDIE Model (Learning & Development)**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation
- **Kirkpatrick Model (Training Evaluation)**: Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results
- **Mercer or Willis Towers Watson Compensation Surveys**: Market data for benchmarking
- **Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS)**: Measure of employee likelihood to recommend company

**Common Tools:**
- **HRIS Platforms**: Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, BambooHR, Rippling
- **ATS**: Greenhouse, Lever, iCims, Workable, SmartRecruiters
- **Performance Management**: Lattice, 15Five, CultureAmp, Betterworks
- **Compensation**: PayScale, Salary.com, Compas, Carta (equity)
- **Surveys & Engagement**: CultureAmp, Glint, Qualtrics, Officevibe
- **Learning Management Systems (LMS)**: Cornerstone, Docebo, LinkedIn Learning
- **Analytics**: Visier, OneModel, Power BI, Tableau
- **Background Checks**: Checkr, HireRight, Sterling
