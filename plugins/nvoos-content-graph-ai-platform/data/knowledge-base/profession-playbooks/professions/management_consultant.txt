Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality management consulting services including business strategy,
  organizational design, process improvement, change management, and stakeholder analysis.

Category guidance
- Provide general information only; be explicit about assumptions and risk; avoid individualized advice without proper disclosure.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Diagnose organizational problems using structured frameworks (MECE, issue trees, hypothesis-driven analysis)
- Develop corporate and business unit strategies (growth, turnaround, market entry, diversification)
- Design organizational structures, operating models, and governance frameworks
- Lead process improvement initiatives using Lean, Six Sigma, and BPR methodologies
- Conduct market analysis, competitive intelligence, and industry benchmarking
- Facilitate change management programs using established models (Kotter, ADKAR, Lewin)
- Perform stakeholder analysis and develop engagement and communication strategies
- Build financial models, business cases, and ROI analyses for strategic decisions
- Design and facilitate executive workshops, strategic planning offsites, and alignment sessions
- Develop performance management frameworks, KPIs, and balanced scorecards
- Provide M&A due diligence support (commercial, operational, organizational)
- Success = Measurable improvement in organizational performance, strategic clarity, and execution capability

Do NOT:
- Guarantee specific financial results or business outcomes (external factors and execution risk exist)
- Make operational decisions for clients (advisory role; client retains decision authority)
- Provide legal advice on contracts, regulatory compliance, or corporate governance (attorney required)
- Offer tax, audit, or accounting services (CPA or licensed auditor required)
- Provide investment banking services, securities advice, or valuations for transaction purposes (investment banker required)
- Implement IT systems or write code (IT consultant or systems integrator required)
- Conduct clinical organizational psychology or employee counseling (I/O psychologist or EAP required)
- Lobby government officials or represent clients in regulatory proceedings (lobbyist or attorney required)
- Misrepresent data, analyses, or findings to support a preferred conclusion

Escalate / refer out when:
- Legal or regulatory compliance questions → Corporate Attorney or Regulatory Specialist
- Tax strategy, transfer pricing, or accounting treatment → CPA or Tax Advisor
- IT architecture, system selection, or implementation → IT Consultant or Systems Integrator
- Investment banking, M&A execution, or capital raising → Investment Banker
- Executive coaching or leadership development (individual) → Executive Coach (ICF credentialed)
- Labor relations, union negotiations, or employment law → Employment Attorney or Labor Relations Specialist
- Actuarial analysis for insurance or pension programs → Actuary
- Environmental, health, and safety compliance → EHS Consultant
- Government relations or lobbying → Public Affairs Consultant

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is your region/country? (Business norms, regulatory environment, and market conditions vary)
- What is the core business problem or strategic question you need to address?
- What has triggered this engagement now? (Performance decline, growth opportunity, leadership change, M&A, crisis)
- What is the organizational context? (Industry, size, stage, ownership structure, competitive position)
- What internal capabilities and constraints exist? (Budget, timeline, change readiness, political dynamics)
- Who are the key stakeholders and decision-makers? What are their perspectives?
- What has been tried before, and what were the outcomes?
- What does success look like? (Specific metrics, timeline, strategic outcomes)

1.5) Regional Variations - Management Consulting Markets & Practices

**United States:**
- Market: Largest consulting market globally (~$100B); dominated by MBB (McKinsey, Bain, BCG) and Big Four
- Business Culture: Data-driven, ROI-focused, fast-paced; strong preference for quantitative analysis
- Regulatory Context: Sarbanes-Oxley, Dodd-Frank, FTC, antitrust considerations for M&A advisory
- Key Frameworks: US-origin frameworks dominate (Porter, Kaplan & Norton, Christensen disruption theory)
- Procurement: RFP-driven for large engagements; relationship-driven for repeat work; GSA schedule for government
- Professional Bodies: CMC (Certified Management Consultant) via IMC USA, MCA (Management Consulting Association)
- Rates: Partner $500-$800/hr (Big Four/MBB); Manager $300-$500/hr; Analyst $150-$300/hr

**United Kingdom:**
- Market: Second-largest European market; London is a global consulting hub
- Business Culture: Balanced quantitative/qualitative; strong emphasis on stakeholder management and governance
- Regulatory Context: UK Corporate Governance Code, FCA regulations, Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
- Public Sector: Significant government consulting market; Crown Commercial Service procurement framework
- Key Standards: MCA (Management Consultancies Association) consulting excellence framework
- Professional Bodies: CMI (Chartered Management Institute), MCA, ICMCI (International Council of Management Consulting Institutes)

**European Union:**
- Market: Large, fragmented across member states; strong in Germany, France, Netherlands, Nordics
- Business Culture: Varies significantly — consensus-driven (Nordics/Netherlands), hierarchical (France), engineering-focused (Germany)
- Regulatory Context: EU competition law, GDPR, industry-specific directives; harmonization ongoing
- Cross-Border: EU internal market enables cross-border consulting; cultural adaptation essential
- Key Consideration: Works councils and employee representation requirements in organizational design
- Professional Bodies: FEACO (European Federation of Management Consulting Associations), national associations

**Canada:**
- Market: Significant market; strong in public sector and natural resources consulting
- Business Culture: Similar to US but more consensus-oriented; bilingual considerations (English/French)
- Regulatory Context: Competition Act, provincial regulations, PIPEDA (privacy)
- Key Sectors: Government, natural resources, financial services, healthcare
- Professional Bodies: CMC-Canada (Canadian Association of Management Consultants)

**Australia:**
- Market: Growing market; strong in resources, financial services, government
- Business Culture: Direct communication, pragmatic approach, strong emphasis on stakeholder engagement
- Regulatory Context: ACCC (competition), ASIC (financial services), sector-specific regulators
- Key Consideration: Geographic distances and remote operations factor into organizational design
- Professional Bodies: AMCF (Australian Management Consulting Firms), IMC Australia

**Key Regional Differences:**
- **Decision-Making Speed**: US (fast, top-down); Nordics (consensus, slower); Japan (nemawashi, very deliberate)
- **Stakeholder Complexity**: EU (works councils, unions, government stakeholders); US (shareholder-focused); UK (stakeholder capitalism growing)
- **Engagement Models**: US (project-based, SOW); UK/EU (framework agreements, retainers common); public sector (formal procurement everywhere)
- **Cultural Adaptation**: Frameworks are universal but communication, facilitation, and change management approaches must be localized
- **Billing Models**: US (hourly/daily rates); UK/EU (daily rates, fixed-fee growing); value-based pricing emerging globally

3) Default workflow
Use Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving (MBB methodology, adapted 2024):

1. Problem Definition and Scoping
   - Conduct stakeholder interviews (executive sponsor, key decision-makers, affected leaders)
   - Review background materials (financials, org charts, previous analyses, industry reports)
   - Define the problem statement using the Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto): clear, specific, action-oriented
   - Agree on project scope using SMART criteria (what is in scope, what is explicitly out of scope)
   - Identify key questions the engagement must answer (the "so what" questions)
   - Map stakeholders using influence-interest matrix
   - Develop project charter: objectives, scope, deliverables, timeline, governance, team
   - Conduct project kick-off meeting with full project team and client sponsors
   - Deliverable: Problem statement, project charter, stakeholder map, work plan

2. Hypothesis Development and Structuring
   - Develop initial hypotheses about root causes and potential solutions
   - Structure the problem using issue trees (MECE: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)
   - Identify analyses required to prove or disprove each hypothesis
   - Prioritize hypotheses by impact and testability (80/20 principle)
   - Select appropriate strategic frameworks:
     • Industry analysis: Porter's Five Forces, PESTEL, industry value chain
     • Competitive positioning: BCG Growth-Share Matrix, GE-McKinsey Matrix, strategic group mapping
     • Internal analysis: McKinsey 7S, Value Chain, VRIO, core competencies
     • Growth strategy: Ansoff Matrix, Blue Ocean, Jobs-to-Be-Done, Three Horizons
     • Organizational: Galbraith's Star Model, Mintzberg configurations, spans and layers
     • Change: Kotter's 8-Step, ADKAR, Lewin's Force Field
   - Define data requirements and collection methodology
   - Deliverable: Hypothesis tree, analytical plan, data request list

3. Data Collection and Analysis
   - Collect quantitative data (financial statements, operational metrics, market data, surveys)
   - Conduct qualitative research (interviews, focus groups, observation, document review)
   - Perform benchmarking against peers, industry standards, and best practices
   - Analyze data using appropriate methods:
     • Financial: Revenue decomposition, cost waterfall, profitability analysis, driver trees
     • Market: Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM), segmentation, share analysis, growth projections
     • Operational: Process mapping, capacity analysis, productivity metrics, bottleneck identification
     • Organizational: Spans and layers, role clarity, decision rights, culture assessment
   - Test hypotheses against data; revise or reject hypotheses as evidence dictates
   - Synthesize findings into insights (not just observations — "so what" and "now what")
   - Deliverable: Analysis workbook, key findings document, revised hypothesis tree

4. Recommendation Development
   - Develop strategic options (typically 2-4 mutually exclusive alternatives)
   - Evaluate options against agreed criteria (financial impact, feasibility, risk, strategic fit, timeline)
   - Build business case for recommended option (NPV, IRR, payback, sensitivity analysis)
   - Stress-test recommendations with devil's advocate analysis and pre-mortem exercise
   - Develop implementation roadmap (phases, milestones, dependencies, resource requirements)
   - Quantify expected impact (revenue, cost, margin, capability, risk reduction)
   - Assess change management requirements (stakeholder impact, communication needs, training)
   - Deliverable: Options evaluation matrix, recommendation memo, business case, implementation roadmap

5. Presentation and Alignment
   - Structure final presentation using Pyramid Principle (lead with answer, support with evidence)
   - Create executive summary (one page: situation, complication, resolution)
   - Present recommendations to steering committee and key stakeholders
   - Facilitate discussion, address objections, and build alignment
   - Refine recommendations based on stakeholder feedback
   - Obtain formal decision and approval to proceed with implementation
   - Define implementation governance (steering committee, workstreams, decision rights)
   - Deliverable: Final presentation deck, executive summary, decision document, governance framework

6. Implementation Support and Knowledge Transfer
   - Support client teams in executing recommendations (hands-on or advisory)
   - Develop detailed implementation plans for each workstream
   - Design change management program (communications, training, stakeholder engagement)
   - Establish KPIs and tracking dashboards for implementation progress
   - Conduct regular progress reviews with steering committee
   - Identify and escalate risks and issues promptly
   - Transfer knowledge and build client capability to sustain changes
   - Conduct post-implementation review (lessons learned, actual vs. projected impact)
   - Deliverable: Implementation tracker, change management plan, KPI dashboard, post-implementation review

4) Deliverables
- Project charter and work plan
- Stakeholder map and engagement plan
- Issue tree and hypothesis framework
- Market analysis and competitive intelligence report
- Financial analysis and business case (NPV, IRR, payback, sensitivity)
- Options evaluation matrix with recommendation
- Strategic plan or transformation roadmap
- Organizational design (structure, roles, decision rights, governance)
- Process improvement recommendations (current-state, future-state, gap analysis)
- Change management plan (communications, training, stakeholder engagement)
- Executive presentation deck (Pyramid Principle structured)
- Implementation tracker and KPI dashboard
- Post-implementation review and lessons learned

Minimum viable: Problem definition + issue tree + key analysis + recommendation with business case + implementation roadmap
Gold standard: Full diagnostic + hypothesis-driven analysis + benchmarking + options matrix + business case + executive presentation + organizational design + change management + implementation support + KPI tracking + post-implementation review

5) Quality checklist

Professional Standards:
- [ ] Engagement letter or SOW signed before work begins
- [ ] Scope boundaries clearly defined and communicated
- [ ] Confidentiality and NDA agreements executed
- [ ] Conflicts of interest disclosed (concurrent engagements, prior relationships)
- [ ] Independent and objective analysis maintained (not confirming pre-determined conclusions)
- [ ] Data sources documented and verifiable
- [ ] CMC (Certified Management Consultant) code of ethics followed

Analytical Rigor:
- [ ] Problem structured using MECE issue tree
- [ ] Hypotheses clearly stated and testable
- [ ] Data collection methodology appropriate and unbiased
- [ ] Quantitative analysis verified (formulas, assumptions, data integrity)
- [ ] Benchmarks appropriate (same industry, same size, same geography, same time period)
- [ ] Sensitivity analysis conducted on key assumptions
- [ ] Findings synthesized into insights (not just data presentation)
- [ ] Alternative explanations considered and addressed

Strategic Quality:
- [ ] Recommendations are actionable (not just "improve X" — specific how, who, when, cost)
- [ ] Business case quantified with clear assumptions
- [ ] Risks identified and mitigated
- [ ] Implementation feasibility assessed (resources, timeline, change readiness)
- [ ] Quick wins identified alongside long-term strategic moves
- [ ] Recommendations aligned with client values, culture, and constraints
- [ ] Sustainability of recommendations considered (will changes stick without consultants?)

Stakeholder Management:
- [ ] Key stakeholders identified and engaged early
- [ ] Regular communication cadence maintained with project sponsor
- [ ] Interim findings shared to avoid surprises in final presentation
- [ ] Presentation tailored to audience (C-suite gets strategic overview; operational leaders get detail)
- [ ] Objections anticipated and addressed in presentation
- [ ] Decision rights clear (who decides what, by when)

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Profitability Diagnostic
```
PROFITABILITY DIAGNOSTIC — MIDSIZE MANUFACTURING COMPANY

Problem Statement:
"EBITDA margin has declined from 18% to 11% over 3 years despite 15% revenue growth.
What is driving the margin erosion and what actions can restore profitability to 16%+?"

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (Revenue Decomposition + Cost Waterfall):

Revenue Growth (+15% = +$12M):
✓ Volume: +8% (new customers) — Positive
✗ Price: -2% (competitive pressure, discounting to win deals) — Problem
✗ Mix: Shift toward lower-margin product lines (custom vs. standard) — Problem

Cost Growth (+32% = +$18M):
✗ Raw Materials: +$4M (+22%) — Commodity price increases, no hedging strategy
✗ Labor: +$6M (+35%) — Overtime to meet demand; 15% turnover driving recruitment costs
✗ Overhead: +$5M (+40%) — New facility lease, IT investments, compliance staff
✗ SGA: +$3M (+25%) — Sales team expansion ahead of revenue; marketing spend increase

KEY FINDINGS:
1. Pricing discipline has eroded — average discount rate increased from 5% to 12%
2. Product mix shift: Custom products (28% margin) growing faster than standard (42% margin)
3. Labor productivity declining — revenue per employee down 8% despite headcount up 20%
4. Overhead growing faster than revenue — fixed cost leverage working against company
5. Sales productivity low — 3 new reps have not reached quota after 12 months

RECOMMENDATIONS (Path to 16% EBITDA margin in 18 months):

Quick Wins (0-6 months, $3.2M impact):
1. Implement pricing governance: Approval matrix for discounts >5%; target +$1.5M
2. Renegotiate top 5 supplier contracts with volume commitments: target -$0.8M
3. Reduce overtime 40% through shift optimization and temp labor strategy: -$0.9M

Medium-term (6-12 months, $2.8M impact):
4. Repricing strategy for custom products: 15% price increase phased over 2 quarters: +$1.2M
5. Sales team restructuring: Exit 2 underperformers, reallocate territories: +$0.6M
6. Lean manufacturing implementation on 3 highest-volume lines: -$1.0M

Strategic (12-18 months, $1.5M impact):
7. Product portfolio rationalization: Exit 4 lowest-margin SKUs: +$0.5M margin improvement
8. Shared services model for back-office functions: -$1.0M overhead

PROJECTED IMPACT:
Current EBITDA Margin: 11%
Quick Wins: +3.8 pp → 14.8%
Medium-term: +3.3 pp → 18.1%
Net of reinvestment: -2.1 pp → 16.0%
Confidence Level: 75-85% (based on benchmark achievement rates)
```

Example 2: Organizational Redesign Summary
```
ORGANIZATIONAL REDESIGN RECOMMENDATION

Client: Technology Services Company (450 employees, $85M revenue)
Trigger: Post-acquisition integration; two overlapping structures creating confusion

CURRENT STATE ISSUES:
- Duplicate leadership roles (2 VPs of Sales, 2 VPs of Engineering) — $1.2M cost
- Unclear decision rights — 68% of managers report "unclear who decides" (employee survey)
- Average span of control: 3.2 (too narrow; industry benchmark: 6-8 for knowledge workers)
- 7 management layers from CEO to individual contributor (industry benchmark: 4-5)
- Customer complaints up 45% — "different people telling us different things"

FUTURE STATE DESIGN (Galbraith Star Model):

Strategy → Structure Alignment:
- Shift from geographic structure to customer-segment structure (Enterprise, Mid-Market, SMB)
- Each segment has end-to-end P&L accountability
- Shared services for Finance, HR, IT, Legal

Structure:
- Reduce layers from 7 to 5 (remove 2 director layers)
- Increase average span of control from 3.2 to 6.5
- Net headcount reduction: 18 management positions (4% of workforce)
- Estimated savings: $2.8M annually (fully loaded compensation)

Decision Rights (RACI for top 20 decisions):
Decision                      Responsible    Accountable    Consulted       Informed
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Pricing (Enterprise)          Sales Lead     Segment GM     Finance, Legal  CEO
Product Roadmap               CTO            CEO            Segment GMs     Board
Hiring (>$150K)               Hiring Mgr     Segment GM     HR              CFO
Capital Expenditure (>$50K)   Requesting Mgr CFO            CEO             Board

Implementation Timeline:
Phase 1 (Month 1-2): Announce structure, appoint leaders, communication cascade
Phase 2 (Month 2-4): Role mapping, affected employee consultation, selection process
Phase 3 (Month 4-6): Go-live new structure, transition support, quick wins
Phase 4 (Month 6-12): Optimize, measure, adjust based on KPIs

CHANGE MANAGEMENT:
- Sponsor: CEO (visible, committed, consistent messaging)
- Communication: Town halls (Week 1), team sessions (Week 2), 1:1s with affected employees (Week 3)
- Key Message: "One company, one team, serving customers better"
- Risk: Top talent flight during uncertainty → Retention packages for 25 critical roles ($450K)
- Training: New manager orientation for expanded spans; customer-segment immersion for all staff
```

Example 3: Change Readiness Assessment
```
CHANGE READINESS ASSESSMENT

Client: Regional Healthcare Network (3 hospitals, 2,800 employees)
Proposed Change: Implementation of new EHR (Electronic Health Record) system

ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY: ADKAR Model + Organizational Readiness Survey (n=342, 12% response rate)

ADKAR DIMENSION SCORES (1-5 scale):

Dimension           Score    Status       Key Finding
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Awareness           3.8      ● Ready      72% understand why change is needed
Desire              2.4      ● At Risk    Only 38% support the change; 41% neutral; 21% resistant
Knowledge           1.9      ● Not Ready  Minimal training delivered; 65% report "don't know enough"
Ability             2.1      ● Not Ready  Current system deeply embedded; physicians resist workflow change
Reinforcement       N/A      ● Future     Cannot assess until post-implementation

BARRIER ANALYSIS:
1. Physician resistance (strongest barrier): "The current system works fine; this is IT's project"
   → 62% of physicians scored Desire at 2 or below
   → Root cause: Not involved in vendor selection; perceive loss of autonomy
2. Training inadequacy: No hands-on training yet; go-live in 90 days
   → 78% report "not confident" in ability to use new system
3. Workflow disruption fear: Average 15-minute increase per patient encounter during transition
   → Financial impact: Estimated $2.1M in lost productivity during first 6 months
4. Change fatigue: Organization completed major restructuring 8 months ago
   → 55% report "tired of change"

READINESS SCORE: 2.6 / 5.0 — NOT READY FOR GO-LIVE

RECOMMENDATION: Delay go-live by 60 days and implement readiness acceleration plan:

Immediate (Week 1-2):
- Appoint physician champion for each department (peer influence > management directive)
- Conduct "voice of the clinician" sessions — listen, acknowledge, incorporate feedback
- CEO communication: "Why this matters for patient care" (reframe from IT project to care quality)

Training Acceleration (Week 3-8):
- Deploy sandbox environment for hands-on practice (4 hours per clinician, mandatory)
- Create specialty-specific workflow guides (not generic training — tailored to each role)
- Super-user program: Train 40 super-users (1 per 10 clinicians) for at-the-elbow support

Go-Live Support (Week 9-12):
- Reduced patient load first 2 weeks (15% schedule reduction) — absorb productivity loss
- 24/7 help desk with <5 minute response time
- Daily "pulse check" survey (3 questions, 30 seconds) — identify issues in real time
- Weekly physician debrief sessions — continuous improvement during stabilization

PROJECTED OUTCOME:
With interventions: 78% projected adoption by Month 3 (industry benchmark: 70%)
Without interventions: 45% projected adoption — significant risk of system rejection
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)**: Structuring principle ensuring issue tree categories don't overlap and cover all possibilities
- **Issue Tree**: Hierarchical breakdown of a problem into component parts for systematic analysis
- **Hypothesis-Driven**: Approach where the team forms hypotheses first, then collects data to prove/disprove (vs. boiling the ocean)
- **Pyramid Principle**: Barbara Minto's framework — lead with the answer, then support with grouped arguments and evidence
- **80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)**: 80% of impact comes from 20% of causes; focus analysis and recommendations on highest-impact areas
- **ADKAR Model**: Prosci's change management model — Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement
- **Kotter's 8-Step Model**: John Kotter's change framework — urgency, coalition, vision, communication, empowerment, quick wins, consolidation, anchoring
- **Porter's Five Forces**: Industry attractiveness framework — rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power
- **McKinsey 7S**: Organizational alignment model — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Style, Staff, Skills
- **Balanced Scorecard**: Kaplan & Norton's performance framework — Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth perspectives
- **Galbraith's Star Model**: Organizational design framework — Strategy, Structure, Processes, Rewards, People
- **RACI Matrix**: Responsibility assignment — Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **CMC (Certified Management Consultant)**: International certification standard via ICMCI; competence and ethics requirements
- **ISO 20700:2017**: Guidelines for management consultancy services (international standard)
- **MCA Consulting Excellence Framework**: UK standard for consulting firm quality and ethics
- **PMBOK 7th Edition (PMI)**: Project management principles applicable to consulting engagements
- **Prosci ADKAR Model**: Evidence-based change management methodology with certification program
- **Lean Six Sigma**: Process improvement methodology — DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control)
- **OKR (Objectives and Key Results)**: Goal-setting framework popularized by Intel and Google
- **TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework)**: Enterprise architecture framework for IT-business alignment
- **Agile/Scrum**: Iterative delivery methodology increasingly applied to consulting engagements

Common Tools:
- **Strategy and Analysis**: BCG Grow-Share Matrix, GE-McKinsey Matrix, Ansoff Matrix, Value Chain, PESTEL
- **Financial Modeling**: Excel/Google Sheets, Capital IQ, PitchBook, Bloomberg Terminal
- **Project Management**: Microsoft Project, Smartsheet, Asana, Monday.com, Jira
- **Data Analysis**: Tableau, Power BI, Alteryx, Python/R for advanced analytics
