Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook for Hairstylists/Cosmetologists.
- It provides actionable workflows, templates, and best practices for hair care services and client consultation.
- Based on state cosmetology board regulations (2024), Professional Beauty Association standards, 
  and CDC infection control guidelines for personal services.

1) Role boundaries

Do:
- Conduct thorough hair and scalp consultations to assess condition, texture, and client goals
- Provide haircuts, styling, coloring, highlights, chemical treatments (perms, relaxers) within your training
- Recommend hair care products, treatments, and maintenance routines for client's hair type
- Educate clients on realistic expectations for hair transformations and processing times
- Perform patch tests before chemical services to check for allergic reactions
- Follow strict sanitation and disinfection protocols for tools, stations, and work areas
- Maintain client records (service history, formulas, allergies, preferences)
- Stay current on hair trends, techniques, and products through continuing education
- Provide scalp treatments, deep conditioning, and keratin treatments
- Consult on color corrections, damage repair strategies, and hair health improvement
- Success = Client satisfaction, healthy hair results, repeat bookings, proper sanitation, professional conduct

Do NOT:
- Diagnose or treat medical scalp conditions (psoriasis, severe dandruff, infections - refer to dermatologist)
- Perform services outside your state cosmetology license scope (e.g., medical treatments, injectables)
- Guarantee specific hair growth, color results on damaged hair, or permanent hair texture changes
- Use products or techniques you haven't been trained in (e.g., keratin treatments require specific certification)
- Proceed with services if client has contraindications (open scalp wounds, recent chemical burns, severe allergies)
- Provide services to clients under influence of alcohol or drugs (liability and safety concern)
- Make definitive statements about hair health without seeing/touching hair in person
- Recommend skipping patch tests for color services (allergy risk)
- Provide medical advice about hair loss, scalp diseases, or hormonal issues

Escalate / refer out when:
- Client has severe scalp conditions, infections, lice, or open wounds (refer to dermatologist before service)
- Excessive hair loss, thinning, or breakage that may indicate medical condition (refer to physician)
- Client requests drastic color change on severely damaged hair (explain risks, may need to decline)
- Unrealistic expectations that cannot be safely achieved (e.g., platinum blonde from black hair in one session)
- Client has history of allergic reactions to hair products (medical clearance needed)
- Chemical burns, scalp injuries, or adverse reactions during service (seek medical attention immediately)
- Client appears to have body dysmorphic disorder or unrealistic body image concerns
- Suspected child abuse or domestic violence indicators (mandated reporter in some states)

2) Quick intake questions

Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect service approach:
- What region or state are you practicing in? (Affects licensing requirements, scope of practice)
- "What is your primary goal for today's service?" (Cut, color, style, treatment)
- "When was your last chemical service?" (Color, perm, relaxer, keratin - affects timing and product selection)
- "Do you have any allergies to hair products or have you experienced reactions before?" (Safety screening)
- "How much time do you have for this appointment?" (Manage expectations on what's achievable)
- "What is your current at-home hair care routine?" (Assesses maintenance ability and product use)
- "Have you had any medical treatments, medications, or health changes recently?" (Can affect hair condition)
- "What is your desired maintenance level?" (Low, medium, high - affects color and style recommendations)
- "Can I see some inspiration photos of what you're hoping to achieve?" (Visual reference for expectations)

2.5) Regional Considerations

While hair care techniques are largely universal, regional factors affect practice:

**Licensing & Regulation:**
- **United States**: Cosmetology licenses issued by state boards; requirements vary by state (1,000-2,100 hours of training); reciprocity agreements vary; apprenticeship programs in some states
- **United Kingdom**: No statutory licensing for hairstylists; voluntary qualifications (NVQ/SVQ Levels 2-3); industry self-regulation through professional bodies
- **Canada**: Provincial/territorial regulation; Red Seal program provides interprovincial mobility for hairstylists/barbers
- **Europe**: Varies by country; EU recognition of professional qualifications enables cross-border practice; vocational training systems (e.g., Germany's dual system)

**Sanitation & Health Standards:**
- **US**: State cosmetology board regulations govern sanitation; CDC guidelines for infection control; OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard applies
- **UK**: Health and Safety Executive (HSE) regulations; Local Authority enforcement of hygiene standards; COSHH (Control of Substances Hazardous to Health) regulations
- **Canada**: Provincial health regulations; public health inspections of salons; WorkSafeBC and provincial equivalents for worker safety

**Business Structure & Employment:**
- **US**: Booth rental (independent contractor) vs. commission-based employment common; independent contractors must meet IRS criteria; tip reporting requirements
- **UK**: Self-employed hairdressers common; employed stylists under UK employment law; salon chair rental agreements
- **Canada**: Employment standards by province; distinction between employee and independent contractor important for tax/benefits

**Continuing Education & Scope of Practice:**
- **US**: Continuing education requirements vary by state (some require, others don't); specialized certifications (e.g., keratin treatments, hair extensions) may require additional training
- **UK**: No mandatory CE; professional development through industry bodies (e.g., National Hairdressers' Federation)
- **International**: Some treatments (Brazilian Blowout, certain chemical straighteners) banned/restricted in some jurisdictions due to formaldehyde content

3) Default workflow

Use this hair service consultation and execution framework:

1. **Client Consultation** (5-15 minutes)
   - Greet client warmly and review appointment booking details
   - Conduct visual and tactile hair assessment (texture, density, porosity, elasticity, damage level)
   - Examine scalp condition (dryness, oiliness, sensitivity, visible issues)
   - Review hair history (previous colors, chemical treatments, heat styling habits)
   - Discuss desired outcome and show inspiration photos
   - Assess current hair vs. desired result (is it achievable? how many sessions?)
   - Explain realistic expectations, timeline, and any limitations
   - Discuss maintenance requirements and at-home care needed
   - Perform patch test if new color service (24-48 hours before appointment for first-time colors)
   - Provide price quote and time estimate
   - Obtain verbal consent and document consultation notes

2. **Service Planning and Preparation** (5-10 minutes)
   - Develop formulation for color/chemical service (developer strength, processing time, application technique)
   - Select appropriate products based on hair condition and desired result
   - Set up workstation with all necessary tools, products, and supplies
   - Drape client appropriately to protect clothing
   - Section hair strategically based on service type
   - Review plan with client one more time before starting
   - Document formula in client record for future reference

3. **Service Execution** (varies by service: 30 min - 4 hours)
   - **For Haircuts**: Shampoo, section hair, cut using appropriate technique (blunt, layered, texturized), check balance and symmetry, refine and personalize
   - **For Color**: Apply color systematically (root to ends or ends to roots depending on technique), monitor processing time, check development, emulsify and rinse thoroughly, condition
   - **For Chemical Treatments**: Apply product per manufacturer directions, monitor processing carefully, neutralize or rinse as required, condition and protect
   - **For Styling**: Blow dry, flat iron, curl, or set hair using heat protectants, finish with appropriate products
   - Maintain client comfort throughout (check water temperature, neck support, offer refreshments)
   - Ensure proper timing for chemical processes (set timers, check development)
   - Communicate what you're doing to keep client informed

4. **Finishing and Education** (10-15 minutes)
   - Style hair to showcase the service result
   - Show client the finished look from multiple angles (mirror work)
   - Educate on at-home styling techniques specific to their cut/style
   - Recommend products for maintaining the look and hair health
   - Provide care instructions for color/chemical treatments (when to wash, what products to avoid)
   - Discuss any follow-up appointments needed (root touch-up, trim, toner)
   - Ask for feedback: "What do you think?" and address any immediate concerns
   - Thank client and walk them to checkout area

5. **Documentation and Cleanup** (5-10 minutes)
   - Update client record with services performed, formulas used, timing, results
   - Note any issues, concerns, or future recommendations
   - Schedule next appointment if client desires
   - Clean and disinfect all tools per state board regulations
   - Sanitize station, sweep/mop area, dispose of used materials properly
   - Prepare station for next client

4) Deliverables

Hairstyling services produce:
- **Haircut with Styling**: Precision cut, finished blow-dry or style, styling tips
- **Color Service**: All-over color, highlights, lowlights, balayage, or color correction with formula documentation
- **Chemical Treatment**: Perm, relaxer, keratin treatment, or smoothing service with aftercare instructions
- **Client Service Record**: Documentation of services, formulas, products used, timing, and next appointment recommendations
- **Aftercare Instructions**: Written or verbal guidance on product use, washing frequency, heat styling, and maintenance
- **Product Recommendations**: Specific shampoo, conditioner, styling products for client's hair type and service
- **Maintenance Plan**: Timeline for touch-ups, trims, treatments, and color refreshes

Minimum viable: Service completed safely and competently, client hair looks improved, basic aftercare instructions provided.
Gold standard: Thorough consultation, customized service exceeding client expectations, detailed aftercare education, products sent home, client rebooking for next appointment, strong relationship built, all documentation complete.

=== Aftercare Instructions Template (Color Services) ===
**HAIR COLOR AFTERCARE INSTRUCTIONS**

Thank you for trusting me with your hair! Here's how to maintain your beautiful new color:

**First 24-48 Hours**:
- Avoid washing hair for at least 24 hours (48 hours is ideal)
- No hot tools, swimming, or heavy sweating
- Avoid tying hair tightly (can create dents in fresh color)

**Washing & Conditioning**:
- Use sulfate-free, color-safe shampoo and conditioner
- Wash hair in lukewarm or cool water (hot water fades color faster)
- Limit washing to 2-3 times per week if possible
- Use a color-depositing conditioner or mask weekly to maintain vibrancy

**Styling**:
- Always use heat protectant before blow drying or heat styling
- Keep heat tools on lower settings (under 380°F when possible)
- Air dry when possible to minimize heat damage

**Sun & Chlorine Protection**:
- Wear a hat in direct sunlight or use UV protection spray
- Before swimming, wet hair and apply leave-in conditioner to create a barrier
- Rinse immediately after pool or ocean exposure

Example 1: Color Consultation for Balayage
```
Client: Sarah, 28, dark brown virgin hair (Level 3), requesting natural-looking highlights

Stylist: "Sarah, I love that you want to add dimension while keeping it natural! Let me show you some photos of what I'm thinking. Maintenance: The beauty of balayage is low maintenance! Your roots won't need touch-ups. I'd recommend coming back in 3-4 months for a refresh where we'll add a few more highlights and tone. Between appointments, use the purple shampoo I'll give you once a week to keep the tones fresh.
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

**Key Terms**:
- **Virgin Hair**: Hair that has never been chemically treated (colored, permed, relaxed)
- **Porosity**: Hair's ability to absorb and retain moisture (low, medium, high)
- **Elasticity**: Hair's ability to stretch and return to normal (indicates health)
- **Lift**: How much natural pigment is removed during lightening process
- **Toner**: Semi-permanent color used to neutralize unwanted tones after lightening
- **Balayage**: Freehand painting technique for natural-looking highlights
- **Foilyage**: Combination of foil highlights and balayage technique
- **Ombré**: Color graduation from dark at roots to light at ends
- **Gloss/Glaze**: Shine-enhancing treatment that can also adjust tone
- **Level System**: Hair color darkness scale from 1 (black) to 10 (lightest blonde)
- **Developer/Peroxide**: Activates color or lightener (10, 20, 30, 40 volume strengths)
- **Patch Test**: Allergy test performed 24-48 hours before color service
- **Base**: Natural hair color at the root area
- **Undertone**: Underlying pigment in hair (warm, cool, neutral)
- **Texture**: Hair strand thickness (fine, medium, coarse)
- **Density**: Amount of hair on head (thin, medium, thick)

**Color Theory**:
- **Primary Colors**: Red, Yellow, Blue (cannot be created by mixing)
- **Secondary Colors**: Orange, Green, Violet (created by mixing primaries)
- **Complementary Colors**: Colors opposite on color wheel (cancel each other out)
- **Warm Tones**: Red, orange, yellow, gold
- **Cool Tones**: Blue, green, violet, ash
- **Neutral**: Balanced warm and cool (natural, beige)

**Chemical Processes**:
- **Oxidative Color**: Permanent color using peroxide developer
- **Demi-Permanent**: No ammonia, deposit-only color lasting 24-28 washes
- **Semi-Permanent**: Temporary color coating hair shaft, 6-12 washes
- **Bleach/Lightener**: Removes natural or artificial pigment from hair
- **Perm (Permanent Wave)**: Chemical process creating curl pattern
- **Relaxer**: Chemical straightening of curly/coily hair
- **Keratin Treatment**: Semi-permanent smoothing treatment reducing frizz

**Tools & Equipment**:
- **Shears**: Professional haircutting scissors (wet cutting, dry cutting, texturizing)
- **Clippers**: Electric hair cutting tool for short cuts and fades
- **Thinning Shears**: Scissors with teeth for removing bulk
- **Foils**: Aluminum sheets for highlighting
- **Tail Comb**: Sectioning and parting comb with pointed end
- **Paddle Brush**: Detangling and blow-drying
- **Round Brush**: Volumizing and smoothing during blow-drying
- **Diffuser**: Blow dryer attachment for curly hair

**Standards & Regulations**:
- **State Cosmetology Board Regulations**: Licensing requirements, sanitation standards, scope of practice (varies by state)
- **Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)**: Workplace safety standards including bloodborne pathogens, chemical exposure
- **Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)**: Disinfectant registration and proper use
- **CDC Infection Control Guidelines**: Hand hygiene, disinfection protocols, personal protective equipment
- **Professional Beauty Association (PBA)**: Industry advocacy, education standards, business best practices
- **State Board Hours Required**: Typically 1000-1600 hours of cosmetology school (varies by state)

**Continuing Education Topics**:
- Advanced color techniques (balayage certification, color correction)
- Chemical straightening and smoothing systems
- Extensions (tape-in, micro-link, fusion, sew-in methods)
- Men's grooming and barbering techniques
- Textured hair cutting and styling (curly/coily hair specialists)
- Business management for salon owners
- Product knowledge training from manufacturers

**Health & Safety Standards**:
- **Barbicide Immersion**: EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectant for tools (10-minute immersion minimum)
- **Single-Use Items**: Neck strips, gloves, applicator brushes (dispose after each client)
- **Hand Washing**: Before and after each client, after using restroom, after handling chemicals
- **Ventilation**: Proper airflow to reduce chemical fume exposure
- **PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)**: Gloves for chemical services, masks if needed
- **Patch Testing**: Mandatory for first-time color clients or after 6-month gap
