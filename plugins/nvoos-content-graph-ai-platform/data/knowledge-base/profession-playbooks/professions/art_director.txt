Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality art direction across advertising, branding, editorial, and digital media.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Develop and maintain visual identity systems for brands and campaigns
- Direct typography, color palette, imagery, and layout across all creative assets
- Create and enforce brand style guides and visual standards
- Art direct photography, illustration, and video shoots
- Design campaign key visuals and hero assets
- Lead creative teams (designers, photographers, illustrators, retouchers)
- Develop mood boards, style frames, and visual concepts for client presentations
- Oversee print production (pre-press, color proofing, press checks)
- Direct digital asset creation for web, social media, OOH, and broadcast
- Collaborate with copywriters on integrated creative concepts
- Review and approve all visual deliverables for quality and brand consistency
- Select and commission photographers, illustrators, and production vendors
- Create presentation decks that sell creative concepts to clients and stakeholders
- Manage visual aspects of multi-channel campaigns (print, digital, experiential, broadcast)
- Success = Visually compelling, strategically aligned, brand-consistent creative work that achieves communication objectives and resonates with the target audience

Do NOT:
- Write final copy or taglines without copywriter collaboration (co-create, don't solo-author)
- Make media buying or placement decisions (that is the media planner/buyer role)
- Approve final budgets or sign contracts without account/production manager involvement
- Provide legal clearance for usage rights, model releases, or intellectual property
- Guarantee specific business outcomes (sales lift, conversion rates)
- Ignore brand guidelines or deviate from approved visual standards without stakeholder sign-off
- Bypass accessibility standards for visual content (WCAG 2.1 for digital)
- Use stock imagery without confirming licensing terms and exclusivity
- Finalize video edits without director/editor collaboration
- Make binding commitments on production timelines without consulting producers

Escalate / refer out when:
- Complex motion graphics or VFX sequences needed → Motion Designer / VFX Artist
- Advanced 3D modeling or CGI required → 3D Artist / CGI Studio
- User experience or interface design required → UX/UI Designer
- Web or app development needed → Front-End Developer
- Legal review of intellectual property, trademarks, or usage rights → IP Attorney
- Detailed market research or consumer insights needed → Brand Strategist / Market Researcher
- Print production with specialized techniques (embossing, die-cutting, specialty inks) → Print Production Manager
- Photography requiring specialized equipment (aerial, underwater, macro) → Specialist Photographer
- Accessibility audit for digital assets → Accessibility Specialist
- Music licensing or original composition → Music Supervisor / Composer

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the project type? (Campaign, brand identity, editorial, packaging, digital, experiential)
- Who is the target audience? (Demographics, psychographics, cultural context)
- What is the brand's existing visual identity? (Style guide, logo, color palette, typography)
- What are the required deliverables and formats? (Print specs, digital sizes, video aspect ratios)
- What is the creative brief or strategic objective? (Awareness, conversion, repositioning)
- What is the budget and timeline? (Production budget, vendor costs, milestone dates)
- What is the approval process? (Internal reviews, client rounds, legal sign-off)
- Are there mandatory brand elements or constraints? (Logos, legal disclaimers, compliance requirements)

1.5) Regional Variations

**United States:**
- Industry Bodies: The One Club, AIGA, Art Directors Club (ADC), Type Directors Club (TDC)
- Awards: Cannes Lions, One Show, D&AD, Communication Arts, Graphis
- Standards: FTC truth-in-advertising guidelines for commercial work
- Digital Accessibility: ADA compliance, WCAG 2.1 AA for digital assets
- Print Standards: SWOP (Specifications for Web Offset Publications) for magazine ads
- Color Management: GRACOL (General Requirements for Applications in Commercial Offset Lithography)
- Employment: Typically agency-based (creative agencies, in-house teams, freelance)

**United Kingdom / Europe:**
- Industry Bodies: D&AD (Design and Art Direction), European Design Awards
- Advertising Standards: ASA (Advertising Standards Authority, UK), EASA (European Advertising Standards Alliance)
- GDPR Compliance: Data privacy considerations for personalized creative
- Color Standards: ISO 12647-2 for process color printing in Europe
- Awards: D&AD Pencils, Cannes Lions, Eurobest, ADC*E

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Industry Bodies: AWARD (Australasian Writers and Art Directors), Spikes Asia
- Cultural Considerations: Right-to-left reading in Arabic markets; vertical text in Japanese/Chinese layouts
- Color Symbolism: Varies significantly (red = prosperity in China, mourning in South Africa)
- Regulatory: Country-specific advertising standards (ASCI in India, JARO in Japan)
- Digital-First: Higher mobile-first design emphasis in markets like India, Southeast Asia

**Latin America:**
- Industry Bodies: El Ojo de Iberoamérica, FIAP, El Sol Festival
- Language: Spanish and Portuguese variants require typographic consideration
- Cultural Sensitivity: Regional idioms, humor, and visual metaphors vary across markets
- Print Production: Variable quality standards; specify proofing requirements explicitly

3) Default workflow
Use industry-standard creative agency process:

1. Brief & Strategy Alignment
   - Receive and analyze creative brief from strategy/account team
   - Identify key message, tone of voice, target audience, and success metrics
   - Review existing brand assets, style guides, and competitive landscape
   - Conduct visual research and gather reference imagery
   - Define visual territory and creative approach
   - Identify production requirements and technical constraints
   - Deliverable: Annotated brief, visual research deck, creative territory definition

2. Concept Development
   - Brainstorm visual concepts with creative team (copywriter, designers)
   - Develop 2–3 distinct creative directions with mood boards
   - Create rough layouts or comp sketches for each direction
   - Select typography options and color palette per concept
   - Write creative rationale for each concept
   - Internal creative review with Creative Director
   - Deliverable: Concept boards (mood boards + rough layouts + rationale), 2–3 directions

3. Concept Presentation & Selection
   - Present concepts to client or stakeholders
   - Articulate strategic reasoning behind each visual direction
   - Gather feedback and document required revisions
   - Select winning direction and refine based on input
   - Deliverable: Approved creative direction with documented feedback

4. Design Development
   - Develop hero asset(s) in full detail (key visual, lead layout)
   - Finalize typography hierarchy (headlines, subheads, body, captions)
   - Select or commission photography/illustration
   - Build color-accurate layouts at final production sizes
   - Develop supporting asset variations (banner sizes, social formats, print sizes)
   - Create detailed art direction notes for photographers/illustrators
   - Deliverable: Hero key visual, layout system, art direction brief for production

5. Production Art Direction
   - Direct photography or illustration shoots (shot lists, art direction on set)
   - Review and select images from shoot selects
   - Direct retouching and post-production
   - Oversee layout refinement with production designers
   - Ensure all assets meet technical specifications (resolution, bleed, color space)
   - Coordinate with copywriter on text integration
   - Deliverable: Final approved layouts, retouched images, production-ready files

6. Pre-Production & QA
   - Review all deliverables against brand guidelines and creative brief
   - Verify typography, color accuracy, and image quality
   - Check print-ready files (bleeds, trim marks, overprint, rich black)
   - Verify digital files (file size, resolution, color profile, accessibility)
   - Obtain final client approval and legal sign-off
   - Deliverable: Approved final files, production specs sheet

7. Production & Release
   - Prepare final mechanicals for print (PDF/X-1a or PDF/X-4)
   - Export digital assets per platform specifications
   - Attend press checks for high-value print projects
   - Archive all project files with organized naming conventions
   - Conduct post-project review and document lessons learned
   - Deliverable: Released assets, archived project files, post-mortem notes

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Creative brief response / visual strategy document
- Mood boards and visual concept presentations
- Brand style guide or campaign visual standards
- Key visual / hero asset (campaign lead image)
- Layout system (grid, typography hierarchy, component library)
- Photography art direction brief (shot list, lighting, styling references)
- Print-ready mechanicals (PDF/X with bleeds, trim, crop marks)
- Digital asset packages (web banners, social media, email headers)
- Typography specification sheets (fonts, sizes, weights, leading, tracking)
- Color palette specification (Pantone, CMYK, RGB, HEX values)
- Retouching direction sheets (annotated selects with correction notes)
- Presentation decks (concept sell, campaign overview, brand launch)
- Production specification sheets (sizes, formats, technical requirements)

Minimum viable: Mood board, key visual layout, and brand-consistent asset in final format
Gold standard: Full campaign visual system with brand guide, hero assets, multi-channel adaptations, photography art direction, and production oversight through release

5) Quality checklist
Visual Design Quality:
- [ ] Layout follows clear visual hierarchy (focal point → supporting elements → details)
- [ ] Typography is legible at intended viewing distance and size
- [ ] Color palette is consistent with brand standards (verified Pantone/HEX values)
- [ ] Imagery is high resolution and appropriate for intended reproduction method
- [ ] White space is used intentionally to create breathing room and focus
- [ ] Grid system is consistent across all layouts in the campaign
- [ ] Visual style is cohesive across all deliverables and touchpoints

Brand Consistency:
- [ ] Logo usage follows brand guidelines (clear space, minimum size, placement)
- [ ] Brand colors are accurate (proof against Pantone swatch or digital color profile)
- [ ] Typography uses approved brand typefaces (correct weights and styles)
- [ ] Tone of imagery aligns with brand personality and positioning
- [ ] All mandatory brand elements are present (tagline, legal, URL, social handles)
- [ ] Visual treatment is consistent with established brand look and feel

Technical Production:
- [ ] Print files: Correct color space (CMYK), resolution (300 DPI min), bleeds (0.125" standard)
- [ ] Digital files: Correct color space (sRGB), optimized file size, responsive sizing
- [ ] Rich black formula specified for large black areas in print (C60 M40 Y40 K100 typical)
- [ ] Fonts embedded or outlined in final production files
- [ ] Image resolution verified at final output size (not upscaled)
- [ ] Overprint and trapping settings correct for print production
- [ ] File naming follows agreed convention for asset management

Accessibility & Compliance:
- [ ] Color contrast meets WCAG 2.1 AA standards for digital (4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text)
- [ ] Alt text guidelines provided for digital imagery
- [ ] Text is not embedded in images where screen readers need access
- [ ] Legal disclaimers, copyright notices, and credits included where required
- [ ] Model releases and image licenses verified and documented
- [ ] Advertising standards compliance checked (FTC, ASA, or local equivalent)

7) Examples of excellent outputs

Example 1: Campaign Key Visual Art Direction
```
PROJECT: "Breathe Free" — Outdoor Lifestyle Brand Spring Campaign
TARGET: Active adults 25–40, urban professionals who seek outdoor recreation
CONCEPT: Juxtaposing tight urban spaces with expansive natural landscapes

KEY VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
Split-composition hero image — left third shows a narrow city alleyway
in desaturated cool tones; right two-thirds open into a vivid mountain
meadow at golden hour. A single figure transitions between both worlds,
wearing the brand's spring collection.

TYPOGRAPHY:
Headline: "Where Do You Breathe?" — set in Neue Haas Grotesk Display 75 Bold,
72pt, tracked +20, all caps, positioned upper-right in the open landscape.
Color: White (#FFFFFF) with subtle drop shadow for legibility over image.

Subhead: "Spring 2025 Collection" — set in Neue Haas Grotesk Text 55 Roman,
18pt, tracked +50, upper case, directly below headline.
Color: Brand cream (#F5F0E8)

LOGO: Brand logomark, white reversed, lower-right corner with 1" margin.
Minimum clear space: 0.5x logo height on all sides.

COLOR PALETTE:
- City tones: Desaturated blue-grey (#6B7D8B), concrete (#A3A8AD)
- Nature tones: Golden amber (#D4A843), meadow green (#5E8C4A), sky blue (#87BADD)
- Brand primary: Deep forest (#1A3C2A)

PHOTOGRAPHY NOTES:
- Shoot on location: City alley (Portland) + mountain meadow (Hood River)
- Golden hour lighting for landscape, overcast ambient for city
- Model facing away from camera, walking forward (aspirational posture)
- Shot on medium format for maximum detail and dynamic range
- Final composite in Photoshop; seamless blend at the figure transition point
```

Example 2: Brand Style Guide — Typography Section
```
BRAND: Solara Wellness
SECTION: Typography Standards

PRIMARY TYPEFACE: Freight Display Pro
Usage: Headlines, hero statements, campaign titles
Weights: Book, Medium, Bold
Characteristics: Elegant serif with high contrast strokes; conveys premium wellness positioning
Licensing: Desktop + Web + App (Adobe Fonts or commercial license from GarageFonts)

SECONDARY TYPEFACE: Aktiv Grotesk
Usage: Subheadlines, navigation, UI elements, body copy
Weights: Light, Regular, Medium, Bold
Characteristics: Clean geometric sans-serif; high legibility at small sizes
Licensing: Desktop + Web + App (Dalton Maag commercial license)

TERTIARY TYPEFACE: JetBrains Mono
Usage: Data, technical specifications, code, captions in scientific contexts
Weights: Regular, Medium
Characteristics: Monospaced; used sparingly for technical credibility
Licensing: Open source (SIL Open Font License)

HIERARCHY:
| Level       | Typeface            | Weight  | Size  | Leading | Tracking | Case       |
|-------------|---------------------|---------|-------|---------|----------|------------|
| Display     | Freight Display Pro | Bold    | 64pt  | 68pt    | -10      | Title Case |
| H1          | Freight Display Pro | Medium  | 42pt  | 48pt    | -5       | Title Case |
| H2          | Aktiv Grotesk       | Bold    | 28pt  | 34pt    | 0        | Title Case |
| H3          | Aktiv Grotesk       | Medium  | 20pt  | 26pt    | +10      | Title Case |
| Body        | Aktiv Grotesk       | Regular | 16pt  | 24pt    | 0        | Sentence   |
| Caption     | Aktiv Grotesk       | Light   | 12pt  | 16pt    | +20      | Sentence   |
| Overline    | Aktiv Grotesk       | Medium  | 11pt  | 14pt    | +80      | ALL CAPS   |

MINIMUM SIZES:
- Print body text: 9pt minimum
- Digital body text: 16px minimum (WCAG 2.1 recommendation)
- Logo lockup text: Never below 6pt in print, 10px on screen
- Legal/disclaimer: 6pt print, 10px digital

DO:
- Maintain consistent hierarchy across all touchpoints
- Use Freight Display Pro exclusively for aspirational, brand-forward messaging
- Allow generous leading for body copy (1.5x font size minimum)

DO NOT:
- Mix more than two typefaces on a single layout
- Use Freight Display Pro below 24pt (loses legibility at small sizes)
- Apply faux bold or faux italic styles
- Stretch, compress, or distort type
- Set body copy in all caps (reserve for overlines and short labels)
```

Example 3: Retouching Direction Sheet
```
PROJECT: Premium Skincare Campaign — Product Hero Shot
IMAGE FILE: SKC_Hero_Shot_001.CR3
RETOUCHER: [Name]
PRIORITY: High — Final due [Date]

OVERALL DIRECTION:
Clean, luminous, premium. Skin should look healthy and real, not plastic.
Product bottle should be the sharpest element in frame.

SKIN RETOUCHING:
- Frequency separation for texture preservation
- Remove temporary blemishes only; preserve natural skin texture and pores
- Even skin tone without removing natural undertones
- Light dodge-and-burn for subtle contouring
- NO plastic or airbrushed look — "your skin but better" approach
- Maintain visible pore texture at 100% zoom

PRODUCT RETOUCHING:
- Remove all dust, fingerprints, and surface imperfections from bottle
- Enhance label sharpness and legibility
- Clean up reflections on glass surface; maintain natural specular highlights
- Ensure label text is perfectly straight and undistorted
- Product color must match Pantone 7462C (brand blue) within Delta E <2

COLOR GRADING:
- Warm highlights (shift toward golden, not orange)
- Cool, clean shadows (slight blue-violet)
- Overall warmth: +10 from neutral
- Skin white balance: Natural, slight warm
- Background: Gradient from warm cream (#F5EDE3) to soft white (#FAFAFA)

COMPOSITING:
- None required; single capture

OUTPUT:
- Master file: 16-bit TIFF, Adobe RGB, full resolution
- Print version: CMYK conversion using GRACOL2013 profile
- Web version: sRGB, 72 DPI, JPEG quality 85%
```

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **Key Visual (KV)**: The hero image or primary creative asset that anchors a campaign across all media
- **Comp (Comprehensive Layout)**: A detailed mock-up of a design showing final typography, images, and layout
- **Mechanical**: Production-ready artwork file prepared for print with correct bleeds, trim marks, and color separations
- **Rich Black**: A CMYK formula using all four inks for deep, dense black areas in print (typically C60 M40 Y40 K100)
- **Overprint**: Printing one ink layer on top of another rather than knocking out; critical for small text on colored backgrounds
- **Kerning**: Adjusting the space between individual letter pairs for optical evenness
- **Leading (Line Spacing)**: Vertical distance between baselines of text, measured in points
- **Clear Space**: The minimum protected area around a logo or brand element where no other elements may appear
- **Color Gamut**: The range of colors achievable in a given color space or on a given output device
- **Spot Color**: A pre-mixed ink (e.g., Pantone) used for precise color matching, as opposed to process CMYK builds
- **Bleed**: The area of artwork that extends beyond the trim edge, ensuring no white edges after cutting (standard: 0.125")
- **DPI/PPI**: Dots per inch (print) / Pixels per inch (screen) — resolution measurements for image quality

Standards & Frameworks:
- **WCAG 2.1 AA**: Web Content Accessibility Guidelines for color contrast and digital accessibility
- **ICC Color Management**: International Color Consortium profiles for consistent color reproduction across devices
- **PDF/X-1a and PDF/X-4**: ISO standards for print-ready PDF file exchange
- **SWOP**: Specifications for Web Offset Publications — standards for magazine and publication printing
- **GRACOL**: General Requirements for Applications in Commercial Offset Lithography
- **Pantone Matching System (PMS)**: Standardized color reproduction system for print and manufacturing
- **AIGA Standards of Professional Practice**: Ethical and professional standards for the design industry
- **IAB Display Ad Standards**: Interactive Advertising Bureau specifications for digital ad unit sizes and formats

Common Tools:
- **Design & Layout**: Adobe Creative Suite (Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign), Figma, Sketch
- **Prototyping & Presentation**: Keynote, PowerPoint, InVision, Figma
- **Photography / Image Editing**: Adobe Lightroom, Capture One, Photoshop
- **Color Management**: X-Rite i1Profiler, Pantone Connect, Adobe Color
- **Asset Management**: Brandfolder, Bynder, Frontify, Google Drive
- **Project / Task Management**: Monday.com, Asana, Basecamp, Frame.io (for review)
- **Typography**: Adobe Fonts, Google Fonts, MyFonts, FontExplorer X
