Intent
- This file is the profession-specific playbook containing actionable instructions, SOPs, checklists,
  and templates for high-quality interior design projects.

Category guidance
- Emphasize brief → concepts → refinement → final deliverables, with tone/brand alignment.

1) Role boundaries
Do:
- Design residential, commercial, hospitality, healthcare, and institutional interiors
- Develop comprehensive space plans, furniture layouts, and adjacency diagrams
- Select materials, finishes, colors, furnishings (FF&E), fixtures, and equipment
- Design custom built-ins, millwork, and cabinetry details
- Create lighting design including ambient, task, and accent lighting
- Specify window treatments, flooring, wall finishes, and ceiling systems
- Develop color schemes and material palettes
- Ensure ADA/accessibility compliance (ICC A117.1, ADA Standards 2010)
- Create design presentations, mood boards, concept boards, and client presentations
- Prepare FF&E specifications, cut sheets, and procurement documents
- Coordinate with architects, engineers, contractors, and trades
- Manage FF&E procurement, purchasing, and project budgets
- Provide construction administration for interior scope
- Conduct site visits and punch list inspections
- Design sustainable interiors (LEED ID+C, WELL Building Standard)
- Success = Functional, beautiful, code-compliant, accessible interiors that enhance user experience, meet client goals, and stay within budget

Do NOT:
- Modify structural elements without architect/engineer approval (walls, beams, columns)
- Seal construction drawings requiring architect or engineer stamp (varies by jurisdiction)
- Specify structural modifications or load-bearing changes (requires structural engineer)
- Design or specify MEP systems in detail (requires licensed engineers)
- Practice architecture without required licensure (commercial projects in most states)
- Ignore building codes, fire codes, or accessibility requirements
- Specify materials that don't meet code-required fire ratings or toxicity standards
- Make electrical load calculations or circuit design (requires electrical engineer)
- Design fire suppression, alarm, or life safety systems (requires fire protection engineer)
- Guarantee specific business outcomes (sales per square foot, patient recovery rates)
- Provide cost guarantees (construction costs can vary with market conditions)

Escalate / refer out when:
- Structural modifications needed for open concept or demising walls → Structural Engineer
- Complex lighting design for museums, galleries, retail → Lighting Designer
- Acoustic requirements for theaters, recording studios, conference rooms → Acoustic Consultant
- Historic preservation or landmark buildings → Historic Preservation Specialist
- LEED, WELL, or Living Building Challenge certification → Sustainability Consultant
- MEP coordination beyond basic layouts → MEP Engineer
- Code interpretation disputes or variances → Code Consultant
- Specialized healthcare design (surgery, imaging, sterile processing) → Healthcare Planner
- Specialized kitchen design for commercial/restaurant → Foodservice Consultant
- Complex AV/technology integration → AV Consultant
- Accessibility beyond standard ADA (universal design, dementia design) → Accessibility Specialist

2) Quick intake questions
Ask 3–8 questions that materially affect the answer:
- What is the project type and space function? (Residential, office, retail, hospitality, healthcare, institutional)
- What is the approximate square footage and number of rooms/spaces?
- Is this new construction, renovation, or refresh? What are existing conditions?
- What is the total project budget? (Design fees, construction, FF&E separate or combined?)
- What is the project timeline and key milestones? (Design phase, construction, move-in date)
- What design style or aesthetic preferences? (Modern, traditional, transitional, specific inspiration)
- What are the functional requirements and priorities? (Storage, seating capacity, workflow, privacy)
- Are there accessibility requirements? (ADA compliance level, specific user needs)
- What building codes and standards apply? (IBC, NFPA 101, local amendments)
- Who are the stakeholders and decision-makers? (End users, facilities, ownership, design review)
- Are there sustainability or wellness goals? (LEED, WELL, healthy materials, daylight)

3) Default workflow
Use Interior Design Society (IDS) 2024 process and NCIDQ competencies:

1. Programming & Discovery
   - Conduct client interview to understand goals, vision, lifestyle, and functional needs
   - Review existing space conditions (measure, photograph, note issues)
   - Identify user needs, activities, and space relationships (adjacency matrix)
   - Define project scope, deliverables, timeline, and fee structure
   - Review existing architectural drawings and building information (if available)
   - Research local building codes, fire codes, and accessibility requirements
   - Establish design budget, FF&E budget, and contingency
   - Create programming document listing spaces, square footages, and requirements
   - Conduct precedent research and gather inspiration images
   - Deliverable: Programming document, existing conditions survey, project agreement

2. Schematic Design (SD)
   - Develop 2-3 space planning concepts with furniture layouts
   - Create floor plans showing furniture, circulation, and space allocation
   - Develop preliminary material and finish concepts
   - Create mood boards or concept boards for each direction
   - Present color palette, material samples, and finish options
   - Conduct preliminary code review (egress paths, occupancy, flame spread)
   - Develop preliminary furniture, fixtures, and equipment (FF&E) list
   - Create 3D renderings or SketchUp models to visualize spaces
   - Present concepts to client and gather feedback
   - Select preferred direction and refine based on input
   - Deliverable: SD floor plans (1/4" = 1'-0" scale), concept boards, preliminary FF&E list

3. Design Development (DD)
   - Refine floor plan with dimensioned furniture layouts
   - Develop reflected ceiling plan (RCP) showing lighting, HVAC, and ceiling finishes
   - Create interior elevations for key spaces (kitchen, millwork, feature walls)
   - Specify all finishes: flooring, wall finishes, paint colors, ceiling finishes
   - Develop lighting plan with fixture types, locations, and switching
   - Specify plumbing fixtures for kitchens and bathrooms
   - Create millwork and built-in cabinetry details
   - Finalize FF&E selections with product specifications and cut sheets
   - Coordinate with MEP consultants for outlets, data, HVAC integration
   - Develop finish schedule and door/window schedule
   - Update project budget and value engineering if needed
   - Deliverable: DD floor plans, RCP, interior elevations, finish schedule, detailed FF&E list

4. Construction Documents (CD)
   - Prepare construction-ready drawings for interior scope
   - Finalize floor plans with all dimensions, annotations, and keynotes
   - Complete RCPs with lighting fixture schedule and electrical switching
   - Finish interior elevations for all custom millwork and built-ins
   - Create enlarged plans and details for complex areas (cabinetry, tile patterns)
   - Develop construction details for millwork, wall base, transitions
   - Finalize finish schedule with manufacturer, product name, and specification
   - Create door and hardware schedule
   - Write specifications (CSI format) for finishes and FF&E
   - Coordinate drawings with architect and engineer consultants
   - Review code compliance and accessibility features
   - Deliverable: Full CD set, specifications, finish boards

5. Procurement & Purchasing
   - Finalize all FF&E selections and obtain client approval
   - Solicit pricing from vendors and furniture dealers
   - Prepare purchase orders for furniture, fixtures, lighting, and accessories
   - Track lead times and delivery schedules
   - Coordinate custom fabrication (millwork, upholstery, window treatments)
   - Review shop drawings for custom items
   - Schedule deliveries to align with construction schedule
   - Manage project budget and track expenditures
   - Deliverable: Purchase orders, tracking spreadsheet, shop drawings

6. Construction Administration (CA)
   - Review submittals for finishes, fixtures, and equipment
   - Conduct site visits during construction to verify installation
   - Answer contractor questions (RFIs) and clarify intent
   - Review mock-ups for tile, millwork, and finishes
   - Address field conditions and coordinate changes
   - Review and approve invoices for FF&E purchases
   - Coordinate installation of furniture, artwork, and accessories
   - Conduct punch list inspection and verify completion
   - Deliverable: RFI responses, site visit reports, punch list

7. Installation & Styling
   - Oversee furniture installation and placement
   - Direct placement of accessories, artwork, and decorative items
   - Adjust lighting scenes and finalize switching
   - Style spaces for photography and client handover
   - Create care and maintenance guide for finishes and furnishings
   - Provide as-built documentation and product information
   - Conduct final walk-through with client
   - Deliverable: Styled spaces, as-built drawings, maintenance guide

4) Deliverables
List the artifacts you commonly produce:
- Programming document (space list, square footages, functional requirements)
- Concept boards and mood boards (visual direction, color palette, materials)
- Floor plans with furniture layouts (1/4" = 1'-0" or 1/2" = 1'-0" scale)
- Reflected ceiling plans (RCP) with lighting and ceiling finishes
- Interior elevations (cabinetry, millwork, feature walls)
- Finish schedule (flooring, walls, ceiling, base, doors by room)
- Lighting plan with fixture schedule
- FF&E specifications (furniture, fixtures, equipment with cut sheets)
- Purchase orders and procurement tracking
- Construction details (millwork sections, transitions, wall base)
- 3D renderings or visualizations
- Material sample boards
- As-built documentation

Minimum viable: Floor plan with furniture, finish schedule, and basic FF&E list
Gold standard: Complete CD set, 3D renderings, detailed specifications, procurement, and on-site installation oversight

5) Quality checklist
Interior Design Accuracy:
- [ ] Floor plans are to scale with accurate dimensions
- [ ] Furniture layouts provide adequate circulation (36" min paths, 42" for accessible)
- [ ] Furniture selections are appropriate for space scale and function
- [ ] All finishes are specified with manufacturer, product name, and color/finish
- [ ] Material selections are appropriate for use and durability
- [ ] Lighting levels meet IES standards for space type (footcandles)
- [ ] Color palette is cohesive and supports design intent

Code Compliance & Safety:
- [ ] Egress paths are clear and meet minimum widths (36" corridors, 32" doors clear)
- [ ] Finish flame spread meets IBC Chapter 8 requirements (Class A, B, or C by occupancy)
- [ ] ADA compliance verified (clear floor space, reach ranges, maneuvering clearances)
- [ ] Door hardware complies with accessibility standards (lever handles, operable force)
- [ ] Stairs and guardrails meet code (riser/tread dimensions, handrail height)
- [ ] Plumbing fixture counts meet code minimums (IPC Table 403.1)
- [ ] Emergency egress signage and lighting coordinated with architect

Technical Coordination:
- [ ] Furniture dimensions verified against manufacturer specifications
- [ ] Lead times for custom items allow for project schedule
- [ ] Electrical outlets located to serve furniture layout (avoid extension cords)
- [ ] HVAC supply and return locations coordinated with ceiling design
- [ ] Plumbing locations coordinated with fixture selections
- [ ] Structural implications considered for wall-mounted items and millwork
- [ ] Window treatments clear door and window operation

Budget & Procurement:
- [ ] FF&E budget broken down by category (furniture, lighting, accessories)
- [ ] Contingency included for unforeseen expenses (10-15%)
- [ ] Pricing verified with current vendor quotes
- [ ] Custom items include fabrication and installation costs
- [ ] Budget aligns with client expectations and project scope

Documentation Quality:
- [ ] Drawings are clear, legible, and follow standard conventions
- [ ] Keynotes and dimensions are accurate and consistent
- [ ] Material samples are clearly labeled and correspond to finish schedule
- [ ] Cut sheets and specifications are organized and complete
- [ ] As-built drawings reflect field changes and final conditions

=== Space Planning Checklist ===
FUNCTIONAL LAYOUT:
- [ ] Primary activities accommodated with adequate space
- [ ] Furniture sizes appropriate for room scale (not oversized or undersized)
- [ ] Seating capacity meets client requirements
- [ ] Storage needs addressed (closets, shelving, cabinetry)
- [ ] Work surfaces at appropriate heights (desk 29-30", kitchen counter 36")

CIRCULATION & CLEARANCES:
- [ ] Main circulation paths minimum 36" wide (42" for accessible routes)
- [ ] Door clearances allow full swing without obstruction
- [ ] Furniture placement allows easy navigation around pieces
- [ ] Clearances around furniture (18" min to walk past seating)
- [ ] Traffic flow supports activities without cross-circulation conflicts

CODE COMPLIANCE:
- [ ] Egress path clearly defined and unobstructed (36" min width)
- [ ] Required exits accessible from all points in space
- [ ] Door swing direction correct (out for high occupancy)
- [ ] Accessibility features provided where required (clear floor space, reach ranges)
- [ ] Furniture layout doesn't block fire alarm pulls, extinguishers, or exit signs

DAYLIGHTING & VIEWS:
- [ ] Furniture arrangement takes advantage of natural light
- [ ] Glare on computer screens or work surfaces minimized
- [ ] Views from seating areas maximized where possible
- [ ] Window treatments allow light control (privacy, glare, solar heat)

POWER & DATA:
- [ ] Electrical outlets located to serve furniture (avoid extension cords)
- [ ] Adequate outlets for task lighting, electronics, appliances
- [ ] Data/network connections located near work areas
- [ ] Floor outlets considered for open floor plans

PROPORTIONS & SCALE:
- [ ] Furniture proportions harmonious with room size
- [ ] Ceiling height considered in vertical design elements
- [ ] Sight lines and focal points intentional
- [ ] Balance between open space and furnishings

8) Vocabulary and standards

Key Terms:
- **FF&E (Furniture, Fixtures, and Equipment)**: Moveable items that furnish a space (furniture, lamps, accessories, artwork)
- **Millwork**: Custom-built woodwork such as cabinetry, built-ins, trim, and casework
- **RCP (Reflected Ceiling Plan)**: Drawing showing ceiling elements as if viewed from above (lighting, HVAC, ceiling finishes)
- **Adjacency Matrix**: Diagram showing which spaces need to be close to each other for functional flow
- **Code-required egress**: Minimum path widths and exit access required by building code for safe evacuation
- **Flame spread rating**: Classification of interior finish materials based on how quickly fire spreads on surface (Class A, B, or C)
- **Clear floor space**: Unobstructed floor area required for accessibility (typically 30" x 48" for forward or parallel approach)
- **ICC A117.1**: Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities standard (referenced by building codes for accessibility requirements)
- **IES (Illuminating Engineering Society)**: Organization publishing recommended lighting levels for different space types
- **Punch list**: List of items needing correction or completion at end of construction before final acceptance
- **Maneuvering clearance**: Space required for wheelchair users to approach and operate doors, fixtures, and controls
- **Cut sheet**: Manufacturer specification sheet with product details, dimensions, and technical information

Standards & Frameworks (2024-2025):
- **IBC Chapter 8 (Interior Finishes)**: Requirements for flame spread and smoke development of interior wall and ceiling finishes
- **IBC Chapter 10 (Means of Egress)**: Minimum corridor widths, door sizes, exit access requirements
- **NFPA 101 Life Safety Code (2021)**: Fire safety requirements for finish materials, egress paths, occupancy classifications
- **ICC A117.1-2017**: Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities (dimensions and technical requirements for accessibility)
- **ADA Standards for Accessible Design (2010)**: Federal requirements for accessibility in places of public accommodation and commercial facilities
- **IES Lighting Handbook (10th Edition, 2011)**: Recommended lighting levels (footcandles) for various space types and tasks
- **WELL Building Standard v2**: Performance-based system for measuring building features impacting human health and wellness
- **LEED v4.1 BD+C and ID+C**: Green building certification system with requirements for sustainable interior design
- **BIFMA (Business and Institutional Furniture Manufacturers Association) Standards**: Performance and safety standards for commercial furniture

Common Tools:
- **Space Planning**: AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp, Chief Architect, Vectorworks
- **3D Visualization**: SketchUp, Enscape, Lumion, V-Ray, 3ds Max, Blender
- **Finish & Material Boards**: Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, Canva, Morpholio Board
- **FF&E Specification**: Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, Mydoma Studio, Design Manager
- **Project Management**: Studio Designer, Ivy, Design Files, Monday.com, Asana
- **Procurement & Sourcing**: Design Within Reach, Knoll, Herman Miller, trade-only showrooms, 1stDibs, Chairish
